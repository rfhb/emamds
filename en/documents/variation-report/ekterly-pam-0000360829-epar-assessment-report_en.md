## 1. Introduction

On 6 July 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Ekterly, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that this study \"Open-Label Safety, Pharmacokinetic, and Efficacy Trial of Sebetralstat (KVD900) in Paediatric Patients (Ages 2-11) with Hereditary Angioedema Type I or II (KONFIDENTKID)\" number \"KVD900-303\", is a stand-alone PAM [P46] application with no updates to Product Information expected.

This study, a Phase 3, corresponds to Study 4 in the PIP number EMA/PE/0000233998 last update on 15 May 2025. The indication targeted by this PIP is \"Treatment of hereditary angioedema in adolescents and children from 2 years of age\".

## Consequently, in the current PAM submission [P46], no paediatric formulations, no paediatric dosage, nor any age or weight limits for paediatric patients use, are claimed.

The efficacy and safety of sebetralstat have already been demonstrated in patients aged 12 years and older using a 300 mg film-coated tablets. On Sept 18 2025, Ekterly 300 mg film-coated tablets was approved in Europe for \"symptomatic treatment of acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE) in adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older\" with the hereafter posology:

## Adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older

The recommended dose is one 300 mg tablet of Ekterly administered at the earliest recognition of an attack. A second dose may be taken 3 hours after the first dose if response is inadequate, or if symptoms worsen or recur. No more than two doses should be administered in a 24 hour period.

This first MAA has been authorised based on the main clinical study KVD900-301 a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3, multicenter clinical trial, three-way crossover trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of two dose levels of KVD900 (300 mg and 600 mg), an oral plasma kallikrein inhibitor, for on-demand treatment of angioedema attacks in adolescent (≥12 to &lt;18 years) and adult patients with hereditary angioedema type 1 or 2.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

The investigational medicinal product (IMP) used in this trial is sebetralstat orally disintegrating tablets (ODT / orodispersible tablets), which disintegrate in the mouth before swallowing. ODTs are suitable paediatric formulations. Sebetralstat (KVD900) was available as ODTs in the two dose strengths of 75 mg and 300 mg.

## Description

KONFIDENT-KID (KVD900-303), is an open-label, phase 3, multicenter clinical trial to investigate the safety, pharmacokinetics (PK), and efficacy of sebetralstat in patients aged 2 to 11 years old with HAE Type I or II. The investigational medicinal product (IMP) used in this trial is sebetralstat orally disintegrating tablets (ODT), which disintegrate in the mouth before swallowing.

First Patient Visit 01 August 2024 - Last Patient Visit 15 January 2026.

Estimated duration for each patient was up to one year from the first HAE attack treated with sebetralstat.

KVD900-303 Protocol v1.0, Global, has been approved on 04 March 2024. Subsequently, a large number of amendments were made, either at a global level or specifically for the US, Europe, Japan and Israel. Only amendments at a Global and European levels are summarised hereafter and listed in the table below.

## Global amendments:

- Increased sample size (24 → 36, v2.0), followed by an increase in recruitment to 48 patients (v3.0), to better document seizures and PK data, particularly in the lightest paediatric patients.

## EU amendments (v1.1):

- Changes requested by the European authorities: supervision of the first dose, restriction on grapefruit juice, new biological exclusion criteria, exclusion of pregnant or breastfeeding women, pubertal status, and strengthened justification for dose and sample size.
- The subsequent EU versions (v2.1, v3.1) simply follow the global amendments regarding recruitment.

## Methods

## Study design

Patients were allowed to treat HAE attacks for up to one year following the first HAE attack treated with sebetralstat (i.e. for 52 weeks following the first treatment with IMP for an HAE attack) or until the patient reached 12 years of age or the trial was completed.

Throughout the trial, HAE attacks were initially treated with a single administration of IMP. HAE attacks were considered eligible for treatment with the IMP if at least 48 hours elapsed since the patient used conventional on-demand treatment or IMP to treat an HAE attack.

## Justification of doses - Extract of Protocol 03 September 2025, Version 3.1 (EU Only) -

In the double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial (KVD900-301) in patients 12 years and older with Type I or II HAE, 1 or 2 doses of 300 mg or 600 mg sebetralstat were found to be safe and tolerable, with a comparable safety profile to matching placebo control.

The dose levels were selected using a population-based PK model. Doses for the current trial were selected to ensure simulated PK levels were above those observed with 300 mg in adults and below levels observed with 600 mg in adults. The dose levels planned for use in this trial were selected following pediatric simulations using a population-based PK (PopPK) model.

Within the PopPK model, the PK of sebetralstat was best described by a 2-compartment model with mixed first and zero-order absorption, with absorption lag time on the zero-order process and linear elimination. The model supported interindividual variability on apparent clearance (CL/F), apparent central volume of distribution (Vz/F), first-order absorption rate constant (KA) with a proportional error structure, weight on clearance, and volume using allometric scalars.

Weight groups were created and analyzed to determine the groupings that best resemble exposures of sebetralstat measured in adults. Simulations of sebetralstat exposure in pediatrics were conducted for four weight groups:

- Body weight &lt;20 kg
- Body weight 20 to 30 kg
- Body weight 30 to 45 kg
- Body weight &gt; 45kg

A total of 1,200 pediatric patients were simulated and used for exposure predictions. Single doses of 75 mg, 150 mg, 300 mg, and 600 mg ODTs were used in the simulations and were compared to the mean observed exposures following a single dose of sebetralstat 300 mg film-coated tablet in adults.

## Objective(s)

The primary objective was to evaluate the safety and tolerability of sebetralstat in pediatric patients aged 2 to &lt;12 years of age with HAE Type I or II.

The secondary objectives were to evaluate:

- the PK of sebetralstat in pediatric patients with HAE Type I or II.
- the clinical efficacy of sebetralstat for the on-demand treatment of HAE attacks in pediatric patients with HAE Type I or II.

## Outcomes/endpoints

The primary safety variables in this trial were:

- AEs, including serious treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs)
- Trial discontinuations
- Laboratory test results
- Vital signs

The efficacy variables of interest in this trial were:

- Time to beginning of symptom relief measured by CaGI-C, defined as at least \"a little better\" (2 time points in a row) within 12 hours of the first IMP administration.
- Time to first incidence of decrease from baseline (2 time points in a row) within 12 hours of the first IMP administration, measured by CaGI-S.
- Time to HAE attack resolution defined as \"none\" within 24 hours of the first IMP administration, measured by CaGI-S
- Time to first use of conventional on-demand treatment within 12 hours of the first IMP administration.

## Estimands

## Primary Estimand

The primary estimand is the proportion of pediatric patients with HAE Types I or II who take any sebetralstat dose, who experience any AE(s) (including fatal AEs) during the study, irrespective of uses of other medications and sebetralstat discontinuations for any reason.

## Secondary Estimands

The secondary estimand is the median time to event by sebetralstat-treated attack number for each time-to-event endpoint, in pediatric patients with HAE Types I or II, regardless of prohibited medications and without conventional attack treatment use and as if patients have not discontinued the trial.

## Sample size

Up to 48 patients were planned to be enrolled into the trial to ensure a sufficient number of patients treated at the intended dose level.

The sample size was considered appropriate to evaluate safety, PK, and efficacy in this population. With 36 to 48 pediatric patients exposed to sebetralstat, there was an 84.2% to 91.5% probability of observing at least one event for an AE with an innate AE rate of 5%.

## Blinding

This was an open-label trial where all patients received sebetralstat, therefore blinding is not applicable.

This was an open-label trial where all patients were assigned to dose cohorts based on body weight as per predefined criteria, at the discretion of the Investigator, therefore randomisation is not applicable.

## Statistical Methods

The statistical methods described below are based on SAP v3 (23 Feb 2026). The SAP underwent two amendments following the initial version (v1, 05 Jun 2025), and none of these amendments had an impact on the analyses of the primary or secondary endpoints of the study.

## Analysis populations

The analysis populations were defined as follows:

- Screened Set included all patients who have signed informed consent.
- Safety Set (SAF) included all enrolled patients who received at least one dose of IMP.
- Pharmacokinetic Set (PKS) included all enrolled patients who provided at least one PK sample. The PKS was the population for PK analyses.
- Full Analysis Set (FAS) included all enrolled patients who received at least one dose of IMP for a qualifying HAE attack. The FAS was the population for efficacy analyses.
- Intent to Treat (ITT) Set will include all patients who meet all eligibility criteria.

The analysis populations (Screened Set, SAF, PKS, and FAS) were applied to three cohorts:

- 300 mg ED Cohort: patients consented under Protocol v1.0 - v3.3 (Global);
- 600 mg ED Cohort: patients consented under Protocol v3.4 (US Only) or under Protocol v1.0 - v3.3 (Global) and continued to Protocol v3.4 (US Only); and
- Overall: all patients consented under any protocol version (v1.0 - v3.4).

## Analysis of demographic information

All demographics summaries and listings were performed using the SAF.

## Analysis of efficacy

Descriptive summaries (time in trial, attack counts, treated/untreated attacks, attack characteristics) were provided by FAS. Efficacy analyses were stratified by age group (2-5, 6-11) and weight/dosing group (≥9.5 to &lt;20 kg-20 to 45 kg-&gt;45 kg).

The dose assigned to the patient was based on their weight group. Patients were weighed at enrolment and at each visit, and if their weight group changed, their dose was adjusted to match the new weight group. However, their assignment into weight groups was not updated (if their weight group changed) for the planned analysis for each HAE attack.

A qualifying HAE attack was defined as an IMP-treated HAE attack. Only qualifying HAE attacks were included in all efficacy analyses; non-qualifying attacks appear in listings only.

Time-to-event endpoints were summarized via Kaplan-Meier by IMP-treated attack number. Missing values were not imputed. Endpoints that could not be derived due to missing post-baseline assessments were censored at time 0.

In the sensitivity analysis (ITT Set), patients who discontinued without an IMP-treated attack were counted as having one attack, censored at time 0.

No formal interim analyses were planned.

After database lock and finalization of SAP Version 3.0, post hoc analyses were completed to analyze treatment compliance, time to beginning of symptom relief, time to reduction in severity, and time to complete HAE attack resolution by actual dose of sebetralstat taken for each IMP-treated HAE attack for the FAS (300 mg ED Cohort).

Three patients (8.3%) in the SAF experienced important protocol deviations, all occurring within the 300 mg ED cohort; none were reported in the 600 mg ED cohort.

## Efficacy results

- Characteristics of IMP-treated HAE attacks
- o Characteristics of IMP-treated HAE attacks by age - 300 mg ED Cohort

In total, 211 HAE attacks were treated with IMP across 33 patients. In 2-5 year olds (3 patients), 24 HAE attacks were treated with IMP; in 6-11 year olds (30 patients), 187 HAE attacks were treated with IMP. Most HAE attacks had a baseline [CaGI-S category] of moderate (53.1%) or mild (32.2%); in the 6-11 years age group only 14 HAE attacks had a category of severe (6.6%) and one as very severe (0.5%).

The median (Q1, Q3) time from onset of HAE attack to the first IMP administration was 29.0 (0.0, 109.0) minutes. IMP was administered in &lt;30 minutes from onset of HAE attack for 49.3% of HAE attacks.

The younger patients had a greater proportion of HAE attacks located in the abdomen (62.5% versus 38.5%, respectively) and head/face/neck (29.2% versus 13.9%), but a lower proportion of HAE primary attacks located in the arms/hands (4.2% versus 32.6%).

The baseline primary pooled HAE attack location 1 of abdominal only was more common in the younger patients (62.5% versus 35.3%) and the location of subcutaneous only was less common in the younger patients (37.5% versus 57.2%). The baseline primary pooled HAE attack location 2 of mucosal was more common in the younger patients (62.5% versus 40.6%), and the location of other was less common in the younger patients (37.5% versus 57.2%). The laryngeal attacks are less than 2% whatever the age studied population.

## Characteristics of IMP-treated HAE attacks

In total, 211 HAE attacks were treated with IMP across 33 patients. ## Treatment compliance

Overall, the two age groups showed similar treatment adherence. The majority of HAE attacks were treated with either 1 dose (35.1%) or 2 doses (35.1%), 3 doses (29.9%) and no HAE attacks were treated with ≥4 doses. No significant difference has been observed between age group and weight group. But only 1 in 6 children weighing over 45 kg has received a third dose of the treatment. The 300 mg dose group had more HAE attacks treated with 1 dose of sebetralstat compared with the 75 mg dose and 150 mg dose groups (52.2% of HAE attacks compared with 28.6% and 33.1%, respectively) and fewer HAE attacks treated with 3 doses of sebetralstat than the other dose groups (4.3% of HAE attacks compared with 42.9% and 32.6%, respectively), overall three doses of treatment, particularly for the 75 mg and the 150 mg doses, seem necessary to treat one HAE attack.

## Clinical Laboratory Evaluation

Changes from baseline in hematology parameters, chemistry and coagulation parameters, liver enzymes and urinalysis parameters during the trial were small, within expected ranges for the pediatric population, and not clinically meaningful. No shifts were associated with clinical signs or AEs suggestive of toxicity. Subgroup analyses by age and weight within each dosing cohort did not demonstrate any clinically meaningful differences.

## Vital Signs, Physical Findings, and Other Observations Related to Safety

Vital signs: A review of the vital signs data did not reveal any clinically significant or unexpected findings for blood pressure, pulse rate and respiration rate. No specific vital signs findings were highlighted. There were no meaningful differences between age or weight subgroups. The same trends were observed for the 300 mg ED Cohort and for the 600 mg ED Cohort.

Physical examinations: In total, 94.4% of patients had a normal physical examination. There were no clinically significant physical examination abnormalities reported.

Pubertal development scales : The PDS was only performed for the 14 EU patients. PDS results were analyzed for the 300 mg ED Cohort. At the Screening and Enrollment Visits, the majority of patients were prepubertal (50% or early pubertal (42.9%), and 1 patient was post pubertal (7.1%). Only 13 patients had PDS data at the Bi-Annual Visit, of whom 46.2% were prepubertal and 53.8% were early pubertal. At the Final Visit, 42.9% of patients were prepubertal, 42.9% were early pubertal, 7.1% were midpubertal, and 7.1% were post pubertal. All patients with PDS data in the 2-5 years age group were categorized as either early pubertal or midpubertal across all visits. Similarly, all patients with PDS data in the ≥9.5 to &lt;20 kg weight group were categorized as either early pubertal or midpubertal across all visits, and in the &gt;45 kg weight group were categorized as early pubertal.

## Conclusion on safety

36 patients from 2 to 11 years old with Type I or Type II HAE were exposed to sebetralstat in the KVD900-303 clinical trial, for a total of 454 administered doses.

In total, 55.6% of patients reported at least one TEAE for a total of 46 TEAEs. The MAH mainly presented the 38 most common TEAEs observed in more than one patient (≥5% of patients), but the remaining 8 TEAEs observed in only one patient were consistent with the more common PTs. The TEAE PTs reported in ≥5% of patients were headache, vomiting, and abdominal pain upper, which are adverse reactions observed in the clinical trials in adults and are hence listed in the section 4.8 of the SmPC.

There were no TEAEs considered as related to sebetralstat by the investigators, severe TEAEs, fatal or serious TEAEs or TEAEs leading to trial discontinuation.

The proportions of observed TEAEs were higher in the lower weight and higher weight groups (&lt;20 kg and &gt;45kg) than in the 20 to 45 kg weight group but the substantial difference of group size (3 and 6 patients versus 27 patients) does not facilitate making comparisons between the young and older children.

In the United States, an amendment to the protocol changed the treating dose to 600 mg. In total one patient had an HAE attack treated by a 600 mg equivalent dose and experienced 2 TEAEs. These data do not allow to compare the tolerability between the 300 mg and the 600 mg doses.

There were no clinically meaningful trends in laboratory evaluations or vital signs during the trial.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

The submitted study \"Open-Label Safety, Pharmacokinetic, and Efficacy Trial of Sebetralstat (KVD900) in Paediatric Patients (Ages 2-11) with Hereditary Angioedema Type I or II (KONFIDENT-KID)\" number \"KVD900-303\", is a stand-alone PAM [P46] application with no updates to Product Information expected.

Consequently, in the current PAM submission [P46], no paediatric formulations, no paediatric dosage, nor any age or weight limits for paediatric patients use, are claimed.

Therefore, while the PK data are pivotal for the pediatric extension of indication they cannot be interpreted at this stage since would have to be checked with the applicant's claim.

As expected, given the similarity of the disease between adults and children, the clinical data are only descriptive but could serve as making a comparison of results obtained with those of the main clinical study KVD900-301 used in the MAA Ekterly 300 mg film-coated tablets approved in Europe for \"symptomatic treatment of acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE) in adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older\".
