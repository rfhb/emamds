## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Introduction

This application concerns a generic application of a centrally authorised medicinal product.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The reference product is Jakavi 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets registered by Novartis Europharm Limited. The EU granted marketing authorisation of the reference product Jakavi 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets on 23.08.2012, under numbers EU/1/12/773/004-012 and EU/1/12/773/014-016.

Ruxolitinib is a selective inhibitor of the Janus Associated Kinases (JAKs) JAK1 and JAK2 (IC50 values of 3.3 nM and 2.8 nM for JAK1 and JAK2 enzymes, respectively). These mediate the signalling of a number of cytokines and growth factors that are important for haematopoiesis and immune function.

Ruxolitinib inhibits JAK-STAT signalling and cell proliferation of cytokine-dependent cellular models of haematological malignancies, as well as of Ba/F3 cells rendered cytokine-independent by expressing the JAK2V617F mutated protein, with IC50 ranging from 80 to 320 nM.

JAK-STAT signalling pathways play a role in regulating the development, proliferation, and activation of several immune cell types important for GvHD pathogenesis.

In line with the originator Jakavi 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets, the targeted indication for Ruxolitinib Viatris 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets states as follows:

## Myelofibrosis (MF)

Ruxolitinib Viatris is indicated for the treatment of disease-related splenomegaly or symptoms in adult patients with primary myelofibrosis (also known as chronic idiopathic myelofibrosis), post polycythaemia vera myelofibrosis or post essential thrombocythaemia myelofibrosis.

## Polycythaemia vera (PV)

Ruxolitinib Viatris is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with polycythaemia vera who are resistant to or intolerant of hydroxyurea.

## Graft versus host disease (GvHD)

## Acute GvHD

Ruxolitinib Viatris is indicated for the treatment of adults and paediatric patients aged 28 days and older with acute graft versus host disease who have inadequate response to corticosteroids or other systemic therapies.

## Chronic GvHD

Ruxolitinib Viatris is indicated for the treatment of adults and paediatric patients aged 6 months and older with chronic graft versus host disease who have inadequate response to corticosteroids or other systemic therapies.

## 2.2.2. Active substance

## 2.2.2.1. General Information

The chemical name of ruxolitinib is (R)-3-( 4-(7H-pyrrolo[2,3-d] pyrimidin-4-yl)-1H-pyrazol 1-yl)-3cyclopentylpropanenitrile corresponding to the molecular formula C17H18N6. ½ (C4H4O4). It has a relative molecular mass of 364.49 (free base) and the following structure:

Figure 1: active substance structure

<!-- image -->

The chemical structure of ruxolitinib hemifumarate was elucidated by a combination of FT-IR spectroscopy, 1 H and 13 C NMR spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, UV spectroscopy and elemental analysis. The solid-state properties of the active substance were measured by TGA, DSC and XRPD.

The active substance is a non-hygroscopic white to light pink crystalline powder which is highly soluble across the physiological pH range.

Ruxolitinib has a single chiral centre which is produced as the (R) enantiomer. The (S) enantiomer is controlled by chiral HPLC in the active substance specification.

Polymorphism has been observed for ruxolitinib hemifumarate. Information on the various polymorphic forms was presented, and it was demonstrated the that commercial manufacturing process routinely generates the form proposed for commercialisation. It was confirmed that polymorphic form is preserved after micronisation.

## 2.2.2.2. Manufacture, characterisation and process controls

Detailed information on the manufacturing of the active substance has been provided in the restricted part of the ASMF, and it was considered satisfactory.

Evidence of GMP compliance of the active substance manufacturing site has been provided in the QP declaration.

The active substance is synthesized in 3 main steps using well defined starting materials with acceptable specifications.

The chiral centre is introduced during the process in racemic fashion, and the desired enantiomer is isolated by resolution. Particle size is reduced to the required level by micronisation. Adequate in-process controls are applied during the synthesis. The specifications and control methods for intermediate products, starting materials and reagents have been presented.

The characterisation of the active substance and its impurities are in accordance with the EU guideline on chemistry of new active substances.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Potential and actual impurities were well discussed with regards to their origin and characterised.

The active substance is packaged in double low-density polyethylene (LDPE) bags (inner clear, outer black), each tied separately with a strip. The double polyethylene bags are placed inside a triple laminated bag which is sealed. The bags are kept inside a sealed HDPE container. The primary packaging material complies with EC 10/2011 as amended.

## 2.2.2.3. Specification

The active substance specification includes tests for description, solubility, identity water content, residue on ignition, fumaric acid content, related substances, assay, chiral purity, residual solvents, and particle size distribution.

Specifications have been set in line with ICH Q6A. Impurity limits are set according to ICH Q3A. The omission of a test for polymorphic form is acceptable based on the data and justification provided.

The analytical methods used have been adequately described and non-compendial methods appropriately validated in accordance with the ICH guidelines. Satisfactory information regarding the reference standards has been presented.

Batch analysis data from 3 production scale batches of the active substance were provided. The results are within the specifications and consistent from batch to batch.

## 2.2.2.4. Stability

Stability data from 3 production scale batches of active substance from the proposed manufacturer stored in the intended commercial package for up to 24 months under long term conditions (25 ºC / 60% RH) and for up to 6 months under accelerated conditions (40 ºC / 75% RH) according to the ICH guidelines were provided. In addition, stability data covering 24 months' long-term data and 6 months' accelerated data was provided for a small batch of micronised active substance.

The following parameters were tested: description, identity, water content, assay, impurities, chiral purity, and particle size. Forced degradation studies were used to demonstrate the stability indication nature of relevant analytical methods.

No significant trends were observed under long term or accelerated conditions, and all parameters remained within specification.

Photostability testing following the ICH guideline Q1B was performed indicating that the active substance is not sensitive to light.

The stability results indicate that the active substance manufactured by the proposed supplier is sufficiently stable. The stability results justify the proposed retest period of 36 months at controlled room temperature (20-25 ºC) in the proposed container for both micronised and unmicronised active substance.

## 2.2.3. Finished medicinal product

## 2.2.3.1. Description of the product and Pharmaceutical development

The finished product is an immediate release tablet containing 5, 10, 15, or 20 mg of ruxolitinib. The tablets are distinguished by size, shape and debossing as indicated in the descriptions below:

- 5 mg tablets: white to off white, round, beveled, biconvex, debossed with V5 on one side and R on other side. Dimension: 7.5 mm ± 0.3 mm
- 10 mg tablets: white to off white, round, beveled, biconvex, debossed with V10 on one side and R on other side. Dimension: 9.3 mm ± 0.3 mm
- 15 mg tablets: white to off white, oval, beveled, biconvex, debossed with V15 on one side and R on other side. Dimension: 15.00 mm ± 0.30 mm x 7.00 mm ± 0.30 mm
- 20 mg tablets: white to off white, oval, beveled, biconvex, debossed with V20 on one side and R on other side. Dimension: 16.50 mm ± 0.30 mm x 7.40 mm ± 0.30 mm

The finished product was developed as a generic equivalent to the reference medicinal product, Jakavi 5, 10, 15 and 20 mg tablets. The development target was established in the Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP), which was used to define critical quality attributes (CQAs).

The reference product contains the phosphate salt whereas the generic product contains the hemifumarate salt, although the active substance is freely soluble in aqueous media. Particle size has no impact on dissolution rate but could affect manufacturability so particle size is controlled in the active substance specification to ensure content uniformity.

The excipients used in the test product are qualitatively the same as those in the reference product and were found to be compatible with the hemifumarate salt in binary mixture studies. All excipients are well known pharmaceutical ingredients, and their quality is compliant with Ph. Eur. standards. There are no novel excipients used in the finished product formulation. The list of excipients is included in section 6.1 of the SmPC.

The applicant has only applied for the tablet formulation from the reference product, which has some therapeutic indications in the paediatric population from 28 days and older. It is stated in the SmPC that Ruxolitinib Viatris tablets are not suitable for patients who cannot swallow tablets whole and children less than 6 years old. Other formulations, for example an oral solution, are available for these patients under different brand names.

The development of the dissolution test method, including justification for the selected medium, apparatus and rotation speed, has been adequately discussed. The selected dissolution method is considered acceptable. The discriminatory power of the method has been appropriately demonstrated through variations in both formulation and manufacturing process parameters. Furthermore, it has been shown that differences in the particle size distribution and tablet hardness do not influence the release profile.

The 20 mg tablet was shown to be bioequivalent to the reference product in a clinical bioequivalence study. A biowaiver of strengths was proposed to bridge to the 5, 10 and 15 mg tablets. Comparative dissolution profiles were generated vs the 20 mg tablet at pH 1.2, pH 4.5 and pH 6.8. All tablets exhibited &gt;85% dissolution in all media and can thus be considered similar. The product therefore meets all requirements for a biowaiver according to the Guideline on Investigation on Bioequivalence (CHMP/EWP/QWP/1401/98 Rev 01).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

A discussion on the development of the manufacturing process was provided. Risk assessment was used to guide the experimental approach. The optimisation of parameters for each unit operation was described, and the control strategy justified.

The primary packaging is transparent PVC/PE/PVdC - Alu blister packs. The materials comply with Ph. Eur. and EC requirements. The choice of the container closure system has been validated by stability data and is adequate for the intended use of the product.

## 2.2.3.2. Manufacture of the product and process controls

For all sites involved in the manufacture, control and batch release of the finished product sufficient evidence of GMP compliance has been provided.

The manufacturing process consists of 6 main steps: sifting of intra-granular materials, wet granulation, drying, screening and blending with extra-granular excipients, compression and packaging. The process is considered to be a standard manufacturing process.

Major steps of the manufacturing process were validated on 3 consecutive production scale batches of each strength. It has been demonstrated that the manufacturing process is capable of producing the finished product of intended quality in a reproducible manner. Critical steps of the manufacturing process and their control are presented in the dossier. The in-process controls are adequate for this type of manufacturing process and pharmaceutical form.

## 2.2.3.3. Product specification

The finished product release specifications include appropriate tests for this kind of dosage form including appearance, identification, dissolution, uniformity of dosage units, assay, impurities, water content, microbiological examination, dimensions, resistance to crushing and friability.

The specifications are generally acceptable and cover all relevant product attributes including CQAs. Limits have been justified based on batch data.

The potential presence of elemental impurities in the finished product was assessed following a risk-based approach in line with the ICH Q3D Guideline for Elemental Impurities. The risk assessment demonstrated that each relevant elemental impurity was not detected above 30% of the respective PDE for an oral dosage form. The absence of elemental impurity controls in the finished product specification is justified.

A risk assessment concerning the potential presence of nitrosamine impurities in the finished product was performed considering all suspected and actual root causes in line with the 'Questions and answers for marketing authorisation holders/applicants on the CHMP Opinion for the Article 5(3) of Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 referral on nitrosamine impurities in human medicinal products' (EMA/409815/2020) and the 'Assessment report- Procedure under Article 5(3) of Regulation EC (No) 726/2004- Nitrosamine impurities in human medicinal products' (EMA/369136/2020). The applicant conducted analytical screening for small molecule nitrosamines using a suitably sensitive method and detected none. The applicant discussed the potential for Nitrosamine Drug Substance Related Impurities (NDSRIs) in the finished product due to the secondary amine in the pyrrolopyrimidine ring and explained that attempts to generate N-nitrosoruxolitinib had been unsuccessful. However, the CHMP considered that the risk assessment was not complete as the conditions used had not been discussed, and the potential for dealkylation and nitrosation of the pyrazole ring had not been considered, resulting in a major objection. In response, the applicant provided an overview of conditions used in attempts to generate N-nitrosoruxolitinib, Furthermore, attempts to nitrosate the dealkylated pyrazole ring were discussed. No nitrosated impurities were detected using suitable analytical methods. It can therefore be concluded that N-nitrosoruxolitinib cannot be formed under relevant conditions, or that it if does form, it is not sufficiently stable to be detected. Therefore, the risk of nitrosamine impurities in the ruxolitinib finished product is considered mitigated and the major objection was resolved.

## Methods

## · Study design

This was a randomized open-labelled, two-period, two-treatment, two-sequence, cross-over study investigating the bioequivalence of ruxolitinib 20 mg tablets with JAKAVI (ruxolitinib) tablets 20 mg, Novartis Europharm Limited, following a single oral dose administered in 28+1 healthy adult human volunteers under fasting conditions.

In each period, all subjects fasted overnight for at least 10.00 hours prior to dosing.

In each study period, a single oral dose of either Test product (T) Ruxolitinib Hemifumarate tablets 20 mg manufactured By: Mylan Laboratories Limited, India or Reference Product (R) Jakavi (Ruxolitinib Phosphate) 20 mg tablets was administered orally to the subjects in sitting posture, with at least 240 ml of water at room temperature, in each study period, as per the randomization schedule under fasting conditions.

During each study period, 23 blood samples were collected (1 X 5.0 ml) from each subject in prespecified K2EDTA vacutainers. Human plasma samples of study code RUXO-TBZ-1005 containing K2EDTA as anticoagulant were analysed for Ruxolitinib using high performance liquid chromatographic method with mass spectrometric detection. ## · Pharmacokinetic Variables

The maximum concentration (Cmax) and the time at which it occurred relative to the administered dose (Tmax) were determined from the observed plasma concentration-time profile over the sampling time interval. Area under the concentration-time curve from time zero to the last sample (with quantifiable concentration) calculated using the linear trapezoidal method (AUC0-t).

The primary pharmacokinetic variables for assessment of bioequivalence are Cmax and AUC0-t for Ruxolitinib.

## · Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were done on concentration values and pharmacokinetic parameters of Ruxolitinib using SAS® package (SAS® Institute Inc., USA, and Version 9.4). Statistical analyses would be performed on the pharmacokinetic parameters using the General Linear Models Procedure (PROC GLM) of SAS Software (SAS Institute, Version 9.4) with treatment, period, sequence and subject nested within sequence as fixed effect.

The parameters: Tmax, Kel, Thalf, Residual Area and AUCinf were analysed statistically using the nontransformed data. The natural log transformed parameters: lnAUC0-t and lnCmax were also analysed. The tests were performed to analyse statistically significant differences in the pharmacokinetic parameters and to determine the test to reference ratios of the pharmacokinetic parameters using Least Squares Means. Ninety (90%) percent confidence intervals were constructed using the two one-sided tests procedure.

## 2.4.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

This application concerns a generic version of ruxolitinib tablets. The reference product Jakavi is indicated for the treatment of myelofibrosis, polycythaemia vera and graft versus host disease.

From a clinical perspective, this application does not contain new data on the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics as well as the efficacy and safety of the active substance; the applicant's clinical overview on these clinical aspects based on information from published literature is considered sufficient.

To support bioequivalence, the Applicant submitted three studies with a randomized, two-period, twotreatment, two-sequence, single-dose, cross-over design (two in fasting conditions and one in fed state). The methodology of the studies is considered adequate to evaluate the bioequivalence of this formulation and was in line with the respective European requirements. Choice of dose, sampling points, overall sampling time as well as wash-out period were adequate. The bioanalytical method for study sample analysis was validated and acceptable. Pharmacokinetic and statistical methods applied are also adequate.

According to the SmPC of the reference product Jakavi, no specific recommendation regarding food intake is given for ruxolitinib posology since it can be taken with or without food. Therefore, a single dose study under fasting conditions, as more sensitive to detect a potential difference between formulations, would be considered sufficient. However, the Applicant also conducted a bioequivalence study in fed state. Since ruxolitinib phosphate (reference product Jakavi) and ruxolitinib hemifumarate (test product) exhibit pHdependent solubility, the fed study was designed to reflect the environment of elevated gastric pH. Nevertheless, in the quality dossier it was justified that that both salts are considered to be of high solubility, and given that ruxolitinib phosphate is classified as a BCS Class I and when compared to hemifumarate salt (test product) demonstrates either the same or slightly lower solubility across the pH range 1.2 - 6.8, the solubility and dissolution of the test product are not expected to be influenced by the modest pH changes due to co-administration of acid-reducing agents. Therefore, additional PPI study is not considered necessary according to the EMA Question &amp; Answer on the need for bioequivalence studies with acid-reducing agents (EMA/497207/2024).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The Applicant conducted two bioequivalence studies in fasting conditions, of which one failed (study RUXOTBZ-1005) and another (study RUXO-TBZ-1010) succeeded to prove bioequivalence. Since the Applicant failed to justify the irrelevance of the failed study, meta-analysis of two studies is logical and applicable solution in order to explore the whole body of existing evidence. This is because positive BE study does not necessarily contradict the earlier study but instead adds new data that allows one to be confident that the earlier failure was due to a lack of information (i.e. statistical power) rather than a lack of bioequivalence. According to the ICH M13A Q&amp;A, if multiple pivotal studies result in inconsistent BE conclusions, the totality of evidence should be considered and when relevant a combined analysis of all studies may be considered as a sensitivity analysis in addition to the individual study analyses.

Bioequivalence was confirmed in clinical study RUXO-TBZ-1010 which used a much higher number of healthy volunteers (84 versus 28) comparing to the study RUXO-TBZ-1005. Furthermore, RUXO-TBZ-1005 only just failed (lower bound of 79.83%, and point estimate within the normal acceptance range). It can therefore also be concluded without further calculations that pooling of these data (study 1010 &amp; 1005) would lead to a 90% CI within the range 80.00-125.00%. The Applicant provided the individual patient data (raw data) together with the statistical output for the pooled analysis of the two studies (RUXO-TBZ-1010 and RUXOTBZ-1005). Furthermore, no outliers have been found performing studentized outlier test. Therefore, pooled analysis of the two studies was considered acceptable to support the claim of bioequivalence between the test and reference products.

Moreover, a biowaiver for the lower (5 mg, 10 mg and 15 mg) strengths was considered acceptable. Similarity of dissolution profiles between three lower strengths and bio-batch was proven in all media without necessity for further calculation, since ruxolitinib rapidly dissolves (&gt;85% in 15 min).

Ruxolitinib Viatris will be available as coated tablets, while the originator Jakavi is available as tablets and oral solution. Although, the indication proposed in the SmPC includes 'paediatric patients aged 28 days and older with acute GvHD' and 'paediatric patients aged 6 months and older with chronic GvHD', Ruxolitinib Viatris tablets are not suitable for patients who cannot swallow tablets and children less than 6 years old. For these patients other formulations (oral solution) are available under different brand name. This is reflected in SmPC section 4.2.

No new studies were submitted, and none are required for generic applications.

No post-marketing data are available. The medicinal product has not been marketed in any country.

## 2.4.4. Conclusions on clinical aspects

Based on the presented bioequivalence studies, Ruxolitinib Viatris is considered bioequivalent with Jakavi.

The results of studies RUXO-TBZ-1005, RUXO-TBZ-1006 and RUXO-TBZ-1010 with 20 mg formulation can be extrapolated to other strengths (5 mg, 10 mg and 15 mg), according to conditions in the Guideline on the Investigation of Bioequivalence CPMP/EWP/QWP/1401/98 Rev.1, section 4.1.6

A summary of the literature regarding clinical data of ruxolitinib and justifications that the different salt of the active substance does not differ significantly in properties with regards to safety and efficacy of the reference product was provided and was accepted by the CHMP. This is in accordance with the relevant guideline and additional clinical studies were not considered necessary.

## 3. Benefit-risk balance

This application concerns a generic version of ruxolitinib tablets. The reference product Jakavi is indicated for the treatment of myelofibrosis, polycythaemia vera and graft versus host disease. No non-clinical studies have been provided for this application but an adequate summary of the available non-clinical information for the active substance was presented and considered sufficient. From a clinical perspective, this application does not contain new data on the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics as well as the efficacy and safety of the active substance; the Applicant's clinical overview on these clinical aspects based on information from published literature is considered sufficient.

The clinical programme consists of three bioequivalence studies, one of which is in fed conditions. The methodology of the studies is considered adequate to evaluate the bioequivalence of this formulation and was in line with the respective European requirements. Choice of dose, sampling points, overall sampling time as well as wash-out period were adequate. The bioanalytical method was validated. Pharmacokinetic and statistical methods applied are adequate.

The test formulation of Ruxolitinib Viatris met the protocol-defined criteria for bioequivalence when compared with Jakavi in one of the fasting studies and in the fed study. The point estimates and their 90% confidence intervals for the parameters AUC0-t and Cmax were all contained within the protocol-defined acceptance range of [range, e.g. 80.00 to 125.00%] in one fasting and fed study, i.e. bioequivalence of the two formulations was demonstrated in those studies. However, the first study in fasted state failed to show bioequivalence and data pooling was performed in order to support claim of bioequivalence for the two fasting studies.

Biowaiver for the lower 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg strengths is acceptable.

This application contains a different salt of the active substance. A summary of the literature with regard to non-clinical and clinical data of Jakavi and justifications that the different salt of the active substance does not differ significantly in properties with regards to safety and efficacy of the reference product was not provided and was accepted by the CHMP.
