## 2. Introduction

## Therapeutic Context

ATTR amyloidosis is a rare disease caused by protein misfolding, affecting various organs. Destabilisation of TTR tetramer leads to amyloid fibril formation and tissue deposition. When peripheral nerves are affected, the disease is termed ATTR-PN; when the heart is affected, it is called ATTR-CM. Both may occur separately or together. Without treatment, ATTR amyloidosis progresses to impaired function and death.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 2.1. Aspects of development

Vyndaqel (tafamidis meglumine) 20 mg soft capsules was approved in the EU under exceptional circumstances on 16 November 2011 for the 'treatment of ATTR in adult patients with stage 1 symptomatic polyneuropathy to delay peripheral neurologic impairment'.

A line extension, Vyndaqel (tafamidis free acid) 61 mg soft capsules, was approved in the EU on 17 February 2020 for the 'treatment of transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with wild-type or hereditary cardiomyopathy to reduce all-cause mortality and cardiovascular-related hospitalisation'.

With this submission, the MAH is applying for a new pharmaceutical form: a film-coated tablet containing 61 mg of tafamidis free acid.  To support this application, two phase 1 studies were conducted to evaluate the relative bioavailability (rBA) and bioequivalence (BE) of the novel tafamidis 61 mg film-coated tablet formulation versus the commercially available 61 mg soft capsule.

Compared with the 61 mg soft capsule, the therapeutic indication for the tablet includes the ATTRwtCM phenotype only, i.e. the hereditary phenotype has been deleted from the wording of the indication.

The MAH considers that the registration of this new pharmaceutical form will have minimal impact on medical practice. The MAH plans to provide additional educational materials directed to patients, healthcare professionals, and pharmacists to inform them about the new tablet pharmaceutical form.

## 2.2. Description of the product

The product is a new pharmaceutical form of Vyndaqel: a film-coated tablet containing 61 mg of tafamidis free acid.

Tafamidis stabilises the TTR tetramer, binding at the thyroxine site to inhibit dissociation, thus slowing the amyloidogenic process. By increasing the activation barrier for tetramer dissociation, tafamidis mimics protective effects seen in natural trans-suppressor variants. This mechanism supports its use in slowing progression of ATTR-PN and ATTR-CM.

The proposed indication of Vyndaqel 61 mg film-coated tablet is treatment of wild-type transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with cardiomyopathy (ATTRwt-CM).

The recommended dose is one tablet of orally once daily.

## 3.2.1.2. Pharmaceutical development

The finished product development was focused to develop a film-coated tablet. The formulation and process development of tafamidis film-coated tablets have focused on the quality attributes defined in a Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP).

The active substance used in the proposed film coated tablet is the same as that currently used for the commercial gelatine capsule. The active substance is micronised to promote dissolution as nonmicronised active substance demonstrates significantly slower dissolution.

Microcrystalline cellulose and lactose monohydrate are used as diluents for powder flow and compressibility. Crospovidone is used as a disintegrant and magnesium stearate as lubricant to minimize adhesion during manufacturing. The tablets are coated with opadry titanium dioxide free white without functional impact. Stability data confirm the compatibility of the active substances with these excipients.

All excipients are well known pharmaceutical ingredients, and their quality is compliant with Ph. Eur. standards except Opadry which conforms with in-house standard. There are no novel excipients used in the finished product formulation. The list of excipients is included in section 6.1 of the SmPC.

Clinical bioequivalence (BE) studies comparing the proposed 61 mg coated tablets to the commercial 61 mg soft gelatine capsule have been performed. The 61 mg film coated tablet met bioequivalence criteria and confirmed bioequivalence between the film coated tablet and the soft gelatine capsule, validating the commercial formulation.

The clinical trial formulations and the proposed commercial formulation are equivalent in composition and manufacturing process, with minor differences in debossing (\"VYN 61\" vs. \"61\") and batch scale.

The ease of swallowing the tablet in elderly patients was discussed. The proposed film-coated tablet is 11 mm (round) while the currently marketed capsule is 21 mm (oblong). Therefore, the smaller tablet size, combined with the coating, makes it easier to swallow.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The dissolution method was developed to enhance discriminatory power. This approach is justified by the low solubility of tafamidis and has been shown to have a discriminatory power for formulation differences, such as disintegrant levels and active substance particle size (micronised vs. nonmicronised).

Comparative dissolution data for the tablet and the commercial soft gelatine capsule in pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8 media has been provided. In pH 1.2 and 4.5, both products show negligible dissolution, consistent with the low solubility of tafamidis under acidic conditions. In pH 6.8, the unadjusted dissolution profiles are not similar; however, this is considered attributable to the different release mechanisms of the two dosage forms, in particular the lag time associated with rupture of the soft gelatine shell, together with the limited solubility of tafamidis under the test conditions. As bioequivalence between the tablet and the commercial capsule has been demonstrated in vivo, the discrepancy between in vitro and in vivo findings is considered adequately justified.

The commercial film-coated tablets are manufactured using a conventional dry granulation process. The development approach used for the manufacture includes a conventional dry granulation process followed by tablet compression and film coating defined in the QTTP. The dry granulation tableting process consists of blending, sieving, roller compaction, milling, tablet compression, and coating. A risk-based approach was used during the development of the proposed commercial manufacturing process to help define the experimental strategy needed to achieve a finished product of acceptable quality and chemical stability. A high-level risk assessment was performed to identify the potential relationships between process parameters and finished product quality attributes. Based on the risk assessment, statistically designed experiments were completed at the laboratory scale and incorporated into the clinical and registration batches manufacturing process. The results from these studies were analysed using statistical analyses and laboratory testing to establish acceptable operating ranges for the finished product manufacturing. The robustness of the proposed commercial manufacturing process was demonstrated via successful manufacture of clinical and registration batches.

## 3.2.3.1. Specifications

## 5.2.3. Pharmacodynamics

## 5.2.3.1. Mechanism of action

Tafamidis is a selective stabiliser of TTR.

Dissociation of the TTR tetramer to monomers is the rate limiting step in the pathogenesis of TTR amyloidosis. The folded monomers undergo partial denaturation to produce alternatively folded monomeric amyloidogenic intermediates. These intermediates then misassemble into soluble oligomers, profilaments, filaments, and ultimately, amyloid fibrils.

Tafamidis binds to the two thyroxine bindings sites of the TTR tetramer with negative cooperativity (dissociation constants of 2 nM [Kd1] and 154 nM [Kd2], respectively). Binding tafamidis to the TTR tetramer inhibits TTR dissociation into monomers and stabilizes TTR folding.

## 5.4.10. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical safety

## 5.4.10.1. Discussion

Safety data is provided from two studies B3461114 and B3461115 in which healthy participants aged ≥18 years were enrolled.

12 participants were randomized in study B3461114 and treated with 3 treatments of tafamidis 61 mg film-coated tablet (Test 1), tafamidis 70 mg film-coated tablet (Test 2), and tafamidis 61 mg soft capsule, with 2 participants each randomized to receive 1 of the 6 treatment sequences.

22 participants were randomised in study B3461115 into two groups receiving a single dose of tafamidis film-coated tablets on day 1 of period 1 and a single dose of tafamidis soft capsules on day 1 of period 2, and vice versa (11 and 10 patients respectively)

The number of related TEAEs related in patients receiving tafamidis 61 mg film-coated tablet and tafamidis 61 mg soft capsule was similar in both studies. The related TEAEs in patients taking tafamidis soft capsule generally correspond to the product safety profile (diarrhoea in 3 patients). The related TEAEs reported in patients taking tafamidis film-coated tablet are not known side effects (increased urine output/pollakiuria/thirst or decreased concentration/hyperacusis or headache in respectively 3 patients). However, it is agreed that the informative value of data from single-dose administrations in healthy patients is limited.

In any case, the reported TEAEs were mild and/or moderate in both studies. No SAEs and no deaths were reported.

The tafamidis 61mg film-coated tablet was generally safe and well-tolerated in healthy participants under fasted conditions, with a similar and acceptable safety profile as compared to tafamidis 61mg soft capsule.

No clinically significant abnormalities in vital signs, physical examinations, or ECG parameters were reported during the studies.

The safety profile of tafamidis 61mg film-coated tablet is considered to be in line to that of the currently available tafamidis 61mg soft capsule. No new safety concerns have been identified.

## 5.4.10.1.1. Overall assessment of available safety data

The studies submitted and reviewed in this line extension application involve healthy volunteers. The reported TEAEs were mild and/or moderate in both studies. The number of related TEAEs were similar between treatment groups. The safety profile of tafamidis 61mg film-coated tablet is considered to be in line to that of the currently available tafamidis 61mg soft capsule. No new safety concerns have been identified.

## 5.4.10.1.2. Adverse drug reactions in the SmPC

The SmPC section 4.8 for tafamidis 61 mg film-coated tablet is the same as for tafamidis 61 mg soft capsule.

Section 4.4 has been updated in order to add a warning regarding lactose monohydrate as an excipient.

## 5.4.10.2. Conclusions on clinical safety

No new safety concerns have been identified in this line extension. The TEAEs observed in healthy volunteers were mild to moderate and were mostly considered unrelated to the product. The safety profile of tafamidis 61mg film-coated tablets is acceptable.

The film-coated tablet is smaller than the existing soft capsule: the tablet measures 11 mm in diameter, whereas the soft capsule is 20 mm long. The tablet is therefore expected to be easier to swallow.

It should be noted that the development of the film-coated tablet was mentioned in the latest PSUSA (EMA/PRAC/537343/2024), where tafamidis capsules appeared 'difficult to swallow,' with cases of dysphagia, difficulty swallowing, and odynophagia observed, mainly in elderly patients. The tablet was therefore presented by the MAH as a measure to facilitate treatment administration in this population. The tablet formulation can thus be considered a positive step forward.

It is noted that the MAH is developing educational material for healthcare professionals and patients to support the appropriate use of both Vyndaqel pharmaceutical forms. The material will highlight the key differences between the two forms (differences in presentation, formulation, and therapeutic indication). Additionally, prescribers will be informed about the possibility of switching patients currently treated with the soft capsule to the film-coated tablet pharmaceutical form if they are eligible based on the SmPC recommendations (see also SmPC section 4.2).

## 9. Benefit-risk assessment

The currently approved presentations of Vyndaqel in the EU are:

- 20 mg (tafamidis meglumine) soft capsules, indicated for the 'treatment of ATTR in adult patients with stage 1 symptomatic polyneuropathy to delay peripheral neurologic impairment'.

-  61  mg  (tafamidis  free  acid)  soft  capsules,  indicated  for  the  'treatment  of  wild-type  or  hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM).'

The MAH submitted this line extension application to add a new Vyndaqel 61 mg (tafamidis free acid) film-coated tablet pharmaceutical form, which is specific to the ATTRwt-CM indication.

The objective of introducing the 61 mg film-coated tablet with a therapeutic indication limited to the treatment of ATTRwt-CM is to allow for the differentiation of the tablet in the orphan protected ATTRwtCM indication, whereas the orphan market exclusivity for the ATTRh-CM expired on 18 November 2021.

The CHMP agrees that, from a pharmacokinetic point of view, the line extension application is acceptable.

The 61 mg film-coated tablet has been demonstrated bioequivalent to the 61 mg soft capsule.

The balance of benefits and risks of tafamidis 61 mg soft capsule in the treatment of wild-type or hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) was assessed in procedure No. EMEA/H/C/002294/X/0049/G (positive CHMP opinion in December 2019) and the conclusions of this procedure also apply to the new film-coated tablet with the indication limited for wild-type ATTR-CM (ATTRwt-CM).

The benefit-risk for Vyndaqel 61 mg film-coated tablet is positive.
