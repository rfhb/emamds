## 1.2. Eligibility to the centralised procedure

The applicant Qilu Pharma Spain S.L. submitted on 9 September 2025 an application for marketing authorisation to the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for Nylaspeg (pegfilgrastim), through the centralised procedure falling within the Article 3(1) and point 1 of Annex of Regulation (EC) No 726/2004.

The applicant applied for the following indication:

Reduction in the duration of neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia in adult patients treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy for malignancy (with the exception of chronic myeloid leukaemia and myelodysplastic syndromes).

## 1.7.3. Opinion

Based on the CHMP review of data on quality, safety and efficacy, the CHMP considers by consensus that the benefit-risk balance of Nylaspeg is favourable in the following indication(s):

## 2. Introduction

## 2.1. Therapeutic context

QL0605 has been developed by Qilu Pharma as a biosimilar candidate to Neulasta with the active substance pegfilgrastim. In this application, the applicant seeks approval for QL0605 for the same indication as approved for Neulasta.

Neulasta is currently authorised in the EU with the following indication:

Reduction in the duration of neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia in adult patients treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy for malignancy (with the exception of chronic myeloid leukaemia and myelodysplastic syndromes).

## 2.2. Aspects of development

The biosimilar candidate QL0605 has been developed as a single-use pre-filled syringe containing 6 mg of pegfilgrastim in 0.6 ml solution for injection.

The aim of the clinical development was to demonstrate biosimilarity between QL0605 and the reference product in terms of clinical pharmacology, safety and immunogenicity, and not to establish patient benefit per se. A positive benefit/risk balance has already been established for the reference product, Neulasta.

The clinical development program consists of two clinical trials which have been completed: a phase 1 PK/PD clinical study in healthy male subjects (QL0605-OVE-001) and a phase 1 immunogenicity clinical study in healthy subjects (QL0605-OVE-002).

Trial QL0605-OVE-001 was a single center, randomized, double-blind, 2-period cross-over study to compare the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics and safety of a single subcutaneous administration of pegylated filgrastim QL0605 (6 mg) with US Neulasta (6 mg) in healthy male subjects.

Trial QL0605-OVE-002 was a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, 2-dose, 2-period parallel-arm study designed to assess the immunogenicity of QL0605 and US-Neulasta in healthy subjects.

## Elucidation of Structure and other Characteristics

The active substance of QL0605 has been comprehensively characterised by orthogonal methods.

Nylaspeg (QL0605) finished product (FP) is a single dose sterile solution for subcutaneous injection containing 10 mg/mL pegfilgrastim (based on protein) active substance in sorbitol (E420) as stabiliser, glacial acetic acid as buffering agent and polysorbate 20 (E432) as surfactant.

The aim of the pharmaceutical development was to develop a single-dose sterile liquid for subcutaneous administration as a pegfilgrastim biosimilar to the reference product Neulasta (Amgen). ## 4.1. Introduction

Qilu Pharmaceutical developed pegfilgrastim (Nylaspeg) as a similar biological medicinal product (biosimilar) to Neulasta (ATC code L03AA13). The Applicant provided an adequate non-clinical developmental program applicable for pegfilgrastim.

The non-clinical programme of pegfilgrastim included a series of in vitro comparative studies including a cell proliferation assay on the M-NFS-60 murine myeloblastic cell line and a comparison of binding to granulocyte colony-stimulating factor receptor (G-CSFR) by Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR). Invitro studies from comparability exercise between QL0605 and Neulasta were satisfactory, and no factors of concern were identified. Thus, no residual uncertainties were observed.

However, a non-clinical comparative study in non-neutropenic and neutropenic rats, which evaluated pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) following a single subcutaneous (SC) administration at doses of 100, 300, or 1000 μg/kg QL0605, or 300 μg/kg Neulasta was also conducted to further support the similarity demonstration between Nylaspeg and Neulasta.

## 4.2. Analytical methods

The in-vitro testing for QL0605 included cell proliferation assays and G-CSF receptor binding and bioactivity assessments covering the relative potency of pegfilgrastim and analysis of the binding affinity of pegfilgrastim to the G-CSF receptor.

An enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was validated for the quantitation of QL0605 and Neulasta in rat plasma . The validation endpoints were calibration curve, precision, accuracy, total error, specificity, selectivity, stability, dilution linearity, hook effect, parallelism, relevance and incurred sample reanalysis. Validation results met the specified criteria for bioanalysis and the method was validated for QL0605 quantitation (Study BAV-QL0605-Rats-PK-20190226).

## 4.3. Pharmacology

Biosimilarity on the non-clinical level was addressed by in vitro bioassays and an in vivo PD/PK study.

Several complementary functional assays were utilised to assess pegfilgrastim biological activity as part of the QL0605 analytical similarity assessment. Comparative in vitro assessment of the binding affinity to the G-CSF receptor as well as of the potency to stimulate proliferation of myeloblastic cells revealed that QL0605 and Neulasta are comparable with respect to their in vitro bio-functional activity. Details concerning biosimilarity are provided in the Quality section. The assays used included an in vitro cell-based proliferation assay. The specific binding of pegylated recombinant human granulocyte stimulating factor (PEG-G-CSF) to the surface receptor of mouse myeloid leukaemia lymphocytes (M-NFS-60) had a dose-dependent proliferative effect on their growth. QL0605 can specifically bind to the surface receptor of M-NFS-60 cells and induce their proliferation. This method is based on the specific binding of QL0605 to G-CSF receptors on the surface of mouse myeloid leukaemia lymphocytic cells (M-NFS-60), which induces a dose-dependent proliferative response. The extent of cell proliferation varies with the biological activity of PEG-G-CSF. The potency of the test sample is determined by comparing its ability to stimulate M-NFS-60 cell proliferation with that of a reference standard. The G-CSF receptor binding affinity of both QL0605 and Neulasta was assessed by surface plasmon resonance technique on Biacore. QL0605 and Neulasta samples, both US and EU-licensed, demonstrated similar affinity to the G-CSF receptor, as reflected in the values of apparent dissociation rate constant (kd), apparent association rate constant (ka) and apparent dissociation equilibrium constant (KD). For further comparability, a comparative in vivo PD/PK study has been performed in non-neutropenic and neutropenic Sprague-Dawley rats to assess the absolute neutrophil counts, area under the curve for the absolute neutrophil counts as well as systemic exposure. The experimental design is, apart from the relatively small experimental groups, considered suitable for the detection of PD/PK effects of QL0605 and the reference product Neulasta. Dose levels relevant for the clinical situation as well as the clinical route of administration have been taken into account. The investigated endpoints included Cmax and AUCall. From the results provided, an increase and dose-response in overall leukocyte levels and in neutrophil granulocyte levels could be observed after administration of QL0605 as well as of Neulasta in nonneutropenic and neutropenic animals. However, administration of QL0605 at a single SC dose of 100, 300 or 1000 μg/kg led to the increased plasma concentrations of QL0605 with increasing dose, and absolute neutrophil counts (ANC) increased dose-dependently and peak values were observed at 24 h, 48 h or 72 h, with a subsequent decline towards the baseline in healthy animals. ## 4.3.1. Pharmacodynamics

## 4.3.1.1. Primary pharmacodynamics

Pegfilgrastim is a positive regulator of granulopoiesis acting on different stages of myeloid cell development (Crawford J 2003, Curran MP and Goa KL 2002). It enhances the effector functions of normal mature neutrophils, including chemotaxis, phagocytosis and oxidative metabolism, via a high affinity filgrastim specific receptor mechanism. This accounts for its selective action as compared to many other cytokines (Lyman G 2005, Molineaux G 2004).

The molecular mode of action of QL0605 is directly related to G-CSF stimulating the proliferation and differentiation of neutrophil committed-progenitor cells by selectively and specifically binding to G-CSF receptor (G-CSFR) on the surface of myeloid progenitor cells and mature neutrophils, increasing the mature neutrophils in the blood and enhancing the functions of neutrophils. In view of this, the nonclinical program for QL0605 is consisted of in vitro comparative pharmacodynamics studies to support its biosimilarity with the reference medicinal product by assessing its G-CSFR binding and bioactivity. Furthermore, in vivo pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies were conducted in neutropenic and non-neutropenic rats to assess the absolute neutrophil counts, area under the curve for the absolute neutrophil counts as well as systemic exposure.

The pharmacodynamics of pegfilgrastim is directly related to the amount of pegfilgrastim available in the body. Estimation of pegfilgrastim binding to G-CSF receptor through SPR and estimation of relative potency of pegfilgrastim using in vitro cell proliferation assay provided a comprehensive in vitro pharmacodynamic profile of pegfilgrastim.

A comparative evaluation of PK parameters has been performed after subcutaneous administration of a single dose (100, 300, or 1000 μg/kg) of QL0605 or (300 μg/kg) of Neulasta in non-neutropenic and neutropenic male rats in combination with PD evaluation.

The method used for the quantification of human G-CSF in formulation samples as well as in rat plasma was validated. Relevant study endpoints were represented by systemic exposure determined by maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and area under the curve from the time of dosing to the last sampling point (AUCall).

In both PK studies, AUCall showed more than dose proportional increases following single subcutaneous (SC) administration of QL0605. Additionally, at the same dose level, mean PK parameters appeared comparable for both QL0605 and Neulasta.

Following a single SC administration in non-neutropenic and neutropenic SD rats, QL0605 demonstrated more than dose proportional increase in systemic exposures (20-fold) over a 10-fold dose range. ## 4.5.1. Discussion

## Pharmacology

## In vitro studies

A comprehensive panel of in vitro structural and functional assays was conducted to demonstrate biosimilarity of QL0605 (Nylaspeg) to its RMP EU-Neulasta. In addition, batches of US-Neulasta were also evaluated to demonstrate similarity of EU- and US-Neulasta as US-Neulasta was used as the RMP in two clinical studies (QL0605-OVE-001, batch no. 1099977; QL0605-OVE-002, batch no. 1103175). The three-way comparative analytical similarity assessment encompassed relevant state-of-the-art functional assays characterising the biological activity of QL0605 and the RMP. The main in vitro study determined potency of QL0605 and EU/US-Neulasta in a murine myeloid leukaemia lymphocyte proliferation bioassay. Granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) receptor binding affinity was determined by surface plasmon resonance (SPR). All data on in vitro pharmacology were included in Module 3 of the dossier and evaluation of the submitted biosimilarity data is a part of the quality assessment report. Thus, the discussion is not restated here in detail and reference is made to the Quality/Biosimilarity assessment. The analytical similarity of QL0605 to the EU-approved reference product Neulasta was extensively demonstrated in terms of biological activity with no significant differences in functional and binding attributes. The results indicate that QL0605 stimulates lymphocyte growth and binds to the G-CSF receptor in a manner highly similar to the RMP. For US-Neulasta, one batch (no. 1129054) was outside the upper quality range of EU-Neulasta, however it was still within its min-max, therefore no significant difference in potency is noted. Overall, the design of the provided studies appears to be adequate and sensitive enough to reliably detect any potential clinically relevant differences between the biosimilar candidate and reference product. From a non-clinical point of view, QL0605 had the expected in vitro biological activity and was similar to comparator Neulasta, which is acceptable. In vivo studies In accordance with 'Guidance on similar medicinal products containing recombinant granulocyte-colony stimulating factor' (EMEA/CHMP/BMWP/31329/2005), studies in in vivo rodent models (neutropenic and non-neutropenic Sprague-Dawley rats) were conducted to compare the pharmacodynamics of Nylaspeg and EU-Neulasta. Both test items were once subcutaneously (SC) administered to neutropenic and non-neutropenic rats (5/sex/group); Nylaspeg at three dose levels (100, 300 or 1000 μg/kg), Neulasta only at the mid-dose level (300 μg/kg). Doses were selected via a bracketing approach including the human therapeutic dose based on FDA Guidance for Industry. In normal rats, at 300 μg/kg, the absolute neutrophil counts (ANC) and neutrophil response magnitude values (AUCANC) did not show any statistical difference between QL0605 and Neulasta (p&gt;0.05). Moreover, ANC increased in a dose-proportional manner after QL0605 administration as expected. In neutropenic rats, after inducement of neutropenia and SC administration of the test or reference item, ANC increased 24-hours post-dose, decreased 48-hours post-dose and again increased between 72120 hours post-dose. These cycles, similar for both QL0605 and Neulasta, were explained as reflective of granulocytopoiesis kinetics and mature neutrophil trafficking, which is considered plausible. AUCANC values were also comparable at 300 μg/kg. Interestingly, AUCANC of QL0605 at 100 μg/kg seems to be higher than at the mid-dose level. In general, administration of the test or reference item to non-neutropenic or neutropenic rats resulted in comparable changes in ANC and AUCANC, thus demonstrating their similar pharmacologic effects in vivo.

## 5.2.1.1. Pharmacokinetics

## Method SG002/2020: Determination of Pegfilgrastim in Human Serum

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The quantitative determination of pegfilgrastim (QL0605 and US-sourced Neulasta) in human serum was performed using a validated electrochemiluminescence (ECL) sandwich immunoassay method (Synexa Method Number: SG002/2020). The assay employs the Meso Scale Discovery (MSD) platform. Microplate wells (Streptavidin-coated) are blocked, followed by the addition of a biotin-conjugated monoclonal anti-G-CSF capture antibody. Calibrators, Quality Controls (QCs), and samples are added, followed by a SULFO-Tag conjugated polyclonal anti-G-CSF detection antibody. The ECL signal is measured using an MSD Sector Imager.The method (SG002/2020) was validated in accordance with GCLP standards and applicable FDA/EMA guidelines. The validation assessed selectivity, accuracy, precision (intra- and inter-assay), dilution linearity, hook effect, robustness, and stability.

Linearity, sensitivity and quantification limit: The calibration curve consisted of seven standards ranging from 36.450 ng/mL to 0.050 ng/mL (in 10% matrix). The Lower Limit of Quantification (LLOQ) was validated at 0.500 ng/mL in 100% matrix (0.050 ng/mL in-well). The Upper Limit of Quantification (ULOQ) was 364.500 ng/mL in 100% matrix.

Selectivity and Specificity: Selectivity was evaluated in normal human serum as well as lipaemic and haemolysed matrices. The average recovery for the High QC (24.300 ng/mL) was 95.7%, and for the LLOQ (0.050 ng/mL) was 99.5%. Cross-reactivity assessments confirmed that the assay could suitably quantify both the biosimilar QL0605 and the comparator US-Neulasta with comparable recovery (Mean %RE range -4.2% to 15.0%).

Intra-/inter-assay precision and accuracy: Precision and accuracy were evaluated using five levels of Quality Controls (LLOQ, LQC, MQC, HQC, ULOQ).

Inter-assay Accuracy (%RE): Ranged from -1.7% to 7.9% for fresh QCs.

Inter-assay Precision (%CV): Ranged from 2.2% to 13.8% for fresh QCs and 3.5% to 9.1% for frozen QCs.

Dilution Linearity and Hook Effect: Dilution linearity was demonstrated up to a 2048-fold dilution using Assay Matrix and up to 1024-fold using Assay Buffer, allowing for the quantification of samples exceeding the ULOQ. No hook effect was observed up to a concentration of 145.800 ng/mL (1.458 mg/mL in 100% matrix).

Stability: Stability of pegfilgrastim in human serum was demonstrated for: Short-term (Bench-top): Stable at room temperature and 2-8°C for 24 hours. Freeze/Thaw: Stable for up to 3 freeze-thaw cycles.

Long-term Stability: Samples were confirmed stable for up to 12 months under Ultra-Low Temperature (ULT) conditions.

Incurred Sample Reanalysis (ISR) was successfully performed for the pivotal clinical studies. In Study QL0605-OVE-002, 83.0% of the 451 selected samples met the acceptance criteria. In Study QL0605OVE-001, 86.5% of the 454 selected samples met the acceptance criteria.

## 5.2.2. Pharmacokinetics

## 5.2.2.1. Introduction

After a single subcutaneous dose of pegfilgrastim, the peak serum concentration of pegfilgrastim occurs at 16 to 120 hours after dosing and serum concentrations of pegfilgrastim are maintained during the period of neutropenia after myelosuppressive chemotherapy. The elimination of pegfilgrastim is non-linear with respect to dose; serum clearance of pegfilgrastim decreases with increasing dose. Pegfilgrastim appears to be mainly eliminated by neutrophil-mediated clearance, which becomes saturated at higher doses. Consistent with a self-regulating clearance mechanism, the serum concentration of pegfilgrastim declines rapidly at the onset of neutrophil recovery

## 5.2.2.4. Bioequivalence

## Study QL0605-OVE-001

A randomized, double-blind, 2-period cross-over study to compare the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics and safety of a single subcutaneous administration of pegylated filgrastim QL0605 (6 mg) with US Neulasta (6 mg) in healthy male subjects

## Study design

This was a single center, randomized, double-blind, single dose, 2-period cross-over study designed to demonstrate the PK and PD BE and to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of QL0605 and US-Neulasta. A total of 174 healthy male subjects aged 18-55 years (inclusive) were planned to be included and randomized to 1 of the 2 treatment sequences, Sequence AB or BA in a ratio of 1:1, i.e., 87 subjects per sequence to receive the 2 treatments in the specified order.

As QL0605 was administered to humans for the first time in this study, a sentinel dosing approach was implemented in order to comply with the respective EMA guideline: In Treatment Period 1, the first 2 subjects were randomized to receive either QL0605 or US-Neulasta (1:1).

The study consisted of a Screening Visit between Day -28 and Day -2, 2 in-house Treatment Periods with admission to the clinical unit on Day -1 and discharge on Day 6 of each Treatment Period, followed by ambulatory visits on Days 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15 and 22 at the clinical unit for scheduled PK, PD, safety, and immunogenicity assessments and an outpatient Follow-up (FUP) Visit 28 days after dosing in both Treatment Periods. Dosing was performed on Day 1 of each Treatment Period.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The total individual study duration is up to 14 weeks.

## Primary objective

To demonstrate the PK bioequivalence (BE) of QL0605 compared to the reference drug US-Neulasta following a single 6 mg s.c. injection in healthy male subjects.

## Dose and mode of administration

Each enrolled subject received 2 single doses of 6 mg pegfilgrastim as the test formulation QL0605 and as the reference formulation US-Neulasta according to the randomization list.

The IMP as solution for injection in PFS was administered under the responsibility of the Investigator via deep s.c. injections in the periumbilical area of the left or right lower quadrant of the abdomen while subjects were supine, and the same procedure were used for all formulations.

## Sampling schedule

PK samples were collected on Day 1 at pre-dose and 0.5, 4, 8, 12 and 16 hours post-dose, and on Day 2 at 20, 24, 32 and 36 hours post-dose and 48, 60 (Day 3), 72, 84 (Day 4), 96, 108 (Day 5), 120 (Day 6), 144 (Day 7), 168 (Day 8) 192 (Day 9), 216 (Day 10), 264 (Day 12), and 336 (Day 15) hours post-dose.

## Washout period

At least 6 weeks

## Protocol deviations

All protocol deviations were considered minor, majority of which were procedural deviations or measurements outside of allowed time window.

## Prior and concomitant medications

Only one subject in sequence B/A has been reported to have received prior medication, i.e. vaccines. The majority of concomitant medications were anti-inflammatory and antirheumatic products (44 subjects (49.4% in sequence A/B, respective 43 subjects (48.3%) in sequence B/A)) and analgesics - paracetamol (15 subjects (16.9% in sequence A/B, respective 9 subjects (10.1%) in sequence B/A) and vaccines (10 subjects (11.2%) in sequence A/B, respective 4 subjects (4.5%) in sequence B/A).

## Pharmacokinetic variables

The primary PK endpoints were:

- AUC0-t: area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) from time zero to last quantifiable concentration
- Cmax: maximum serum concentration

The secondary PK endpoints were:

- Tmax: time corresponding to the occurrence of Cmax
- t½: apparent terminal half-life
- CL/F: apparent total body clearance
- Vz/F: apparent volume of distribution based on the terminal phase
- Λz: terminal rate constant

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- %AUCex: percentage of AUCinf obtained by extrapolation

## Statistical methods

Bioequivalence between the test product QL0605 and the reference product US-Neulasta was assessed using ANOVA models fitted on natural logarithm transformed AUCinf, AUClast and Cmax values. The mixed effects model included treatment, period and sequence as fixed and subject within sequence as random effects.

PK equivalence determination was made using the Pharmacokinetic Analysis Population.

PK equivalence was assessed by constructing the 90% CIs for the GMR (test/reference) for AUC0-inf, AUClast and Cmax. PK equivalence was concluded if the 90% CIs for both AUC0-inf and Cmax were completely contained within the acceptance limits of 80%-125%. The same step-down testing for PD variables was performed.

## Study QL0605-OVE-002

A randomized, double-blind, 2-period parallel-arm study to assess the immunogenicity of 2 subcutaneous doses of QL0605 (6 mg) compared to 2 subcutaneous doses of US-Neulasta (6 mg) in healthy male and female subjects

## Study design

This was a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, single-dose, 2 period parallel-arm study designed to assess the immunogenicity of QL0605 and US-Neulasta. A total of 300 healthy male and female subjects aged 18 to 55 years (inclusive) were to be included and randomized in a ratio of 1:1 to receive 1 s.c. injection of 6 mg QL0605 or 6 mg US-Neulasta in Treatment Period 1 and 1 s.c. injection of 6 mg QL0605 or 6 mg US-Neulasta in Treatment Period 2. The interval between doses was at least 42 days.

The study consisted of a Screening Visit between Day -28 to Day -2, 2 in-house Treatment periods with admission to the clinical unit on Day -1, administration of the study medication on Day 1 of each period and discharge on Day 6 of each period. In each Treatment Period, ambulatory visits at the clinical unit for scheduled immunogenicity, PK, PD and safety assessments were planned on Day 13, Day 20, Day 27, and Day 41 followed by an outpatient Follow-up Visit 49 days after the second administration of the study medication. Subjects who were ADA-positive at the Day 50 Follow-up Visit after the second dose were monitored every 3 months for 12 months or until levels return to baseline (BL). The main analysis was to be performed after all subjects had completed their last visit.

The total individual study duration was up to 17 weeks (including the 28-day Screening window).

## Primary objective

- To assess the immunogenicity of QL0605 compared to the reference drug US-Neulasta based on the percent difference in anti-drug antibody (ADA) response following 2 s.c. injections of 6 mg in healthy male and female subjects.
- To assess the development of neutralizing ADA in all subjects who test positive for ADA.

## Secondary objectives

- To assess any potential impact of ADA on the pharmacokinetic (PK) profile of QL0605 compared to US-Neulasta following 2 s.c. injections in healthy male and female subjects.
- To assess any potential impact of ADA on the PD response to QL0605 compared to USNeulasta as measured by absolute neutrophil count (ANC) following 2 s.c. injections in healthy male and female subjects.

## Dose and mode of administration

Each enrolled subject received 2 single doses of 6 mg pegfilgrastim as the test formulation QL0605 or as the reference formulation US-Neulasta according to the randomization list.

The study medication was administered under the responsibility of the Investigator via deep s.c. injections in the periumbilical area of the left or right lower quadrant of the abdomen while subjects were supine and the same procedure should be used for all formulations (test- and reference formulations).

## Sampling schedule

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Samples for immunogenicity assessment were collected at screening (only if no historical data were available), pre-dose in both Periods (≤2 hours before dosing) and on Day 13, Day 20, Day 27, Day 41 of both periods and on Day 50 in Period 2.

Subjects who were ADA-positive at the Follow-up Visit after the second dose was monitored every 3 months for 12 months or until levels returned to baseline.

## Protocol deviations

There were 2161 protocol deviations reported with 2060 PDs related to study procedures and test, from which 83 protocol deviations were classified as major and were related to either inclusion/exclusion criteria or study procedures and tests.

A detailed discussion was provided regarding the impact of the major protocol deviations on the study results. From a total of 83 major protocol deviations, majority (71) were immunogenicity specimen assessments not collected, one (1) consisted of ADA sample collected but not processed per the laboratory manual and 11 were related to violation of exclusion criteria.

The Applicant's as that deviations related to exclusion criteria are unlikely to impact the study results are agreed.

The 71 sample-related major protocol deviations affected 57 subjects (18.9%), with some subjects experiencing more than one missed ADA time point. These missed samples represented approximately 2% of the total expected samples, and the number of subjects was comparable between treatment arms, i.e., 30 subjects (19.6%) in QL0605 arm and 27 subjects (17.9%) in US Neulasta arm. Within these, three subjects experienced multiple missed samples: one randomized to US-Neulasta but not dosed and two subjects dosed only in Period 1, with missing Period 2 samples, one in US-Neulasta arm and one in QL0605 arm.

The Applicant performed three prespecified sensitivity analyses, original analysis, worst-case imputation, modified complete case, using both ADA assay methods (Method 1 and Method 2) to assess the effect of missing ADA samples.

Taking into account that all confidence intervals remained within the prespecified non-inferiority margin and non-inferiority was met for all sensitivity analyses performed, the small proportion of missed samples relative to total samples (2%), the balanced distribution across study arms of major protocol deviations, the Applicant's conclusion that the major protocol deviations did not impact the primary endpoint results are agreed.

## Sample size determination

This study utilized blinded sample size reassessment (BSSR) in the interim of the study as an adaptation to uncertainties in the initial sample size calculation. Based on literature data in healthy subjects, the proportion of subjects with any positive ADA after treatment with US-Neulasta is assumed to be an initial 12%. The study size was to include 300 as a preliminary estimate. Assuming 10% drop out, the planned 300 healthy subjects for enrolment would ensure to have 270 evaluable subjects.

Under this condition, the chosen number of evaluable subjects per arm (n=135) at one-sided significance level 0.05 would provide 80% power to reject inferiority at 10% noninferiority margin.

Pooled ADA incidence rate was monitored in a blinded manner during the course of the study. To obtain the final sample size a blinded sample size re-estimation (BSSR) was conducted in midcourse of the study. The original sample size was recalculated by updating the reference incidence rate in the original sample size calculation with the observed, pooled incidence rate.

Thus, the BSSR was performed when 50% of the subjects had completed the study. Overall, 33 ADA+ subjects were identified resulting in a rate of 0.220 ADA+ subjects. Subsequently, the updated sample size for the study was determined to be 472, but it was adjusted to a final sample size of 400 according to the predefined calculation method. As BSSR is regarded as having little or negligible effect on Type-I error rate, no alpha correction was applied (𝛼𝛼 = 0.05, 𝛽𝛽 = 0.2).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

However, considering the issues of the time and cost, the sponsor decided not to increase the sample size. Therefore, the final sample size for this study remains at the initially determined 300.

With the responses provided the Applicant confirmed that with the initial sample size of 300 subjects the study provided approximately 76% power to reject inferiority at the 10% margin, considering the pooled ADA incidence rate of 15.9% observed with the newly validated detection method (Method 2, SG043/2023) implemented for re-analysis of the study data.

Even if a blinded sample size reassessment for this study has been included and a new sample size has been recalculated by updating the reference incidence rate assumed at 12% based on the historical data in the original sample size calculation with the observed, pooled incidence rate of 22%, the Applicant decided to maintain the initial sample size of 300 subjects. The selected approach has been justified based on the fact that the enrolment of an additional required sample size of 172 healthy subjects would be considered ethically unacceptable and an improved validated detection method (Method 2, SG043/2023) has been implemented for re-analysis given the substantial increase from the assumed 12% to the observed 22% ADA incidence, that raised concerns about potential limitations of the initial detection method.

## Changes in the conduct of the study

In this study, the immunogenicity samples were analyzed twice. The reasons for re-analyzing the immunogenicity samples was that the sponsor conducted an audit during Q4 2022 of the immunogenicity assay (Method 1) and identified several technical deficiencies. As these deficiencies may interact and potentially magnify the impact on the assay performance, the reliability of the ADA results generated with Method 1 could be questioned.

Based on these findings, the sponsor planned to re-analyze the immunogenicity samples and requested FDA scientific advice.

FDA considered the proposal to re-analyze the samples from Study QL0605-OVE-002 for the ADA and neutralizing antibody (NAb) was reasonable and the proposed revisions to ADA and NAb assay methodologies (Method 2) were acceptable, and the results from Method 2 can be served as the primary immunogenicity data.

## 5.2.6. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical pharmacology

## 5.2.6.1. Discussion

The submitted clinical pharmacology programme adequately demonstrates biosimilarity between QL0605 and pegfilgrastim reference product Neulasta.

To demonstrate biosimilarity in the PK and PD of the candidate biosimilar, QL0605 was compared with US-Neulasta (pegfilgrastim) following single dose administration in healthy subjects in study QL0605OVE-001, which is the pivotal study for this application. The possibility to use a non-EU authorised comparator in part of the comparability exercise is reflected in EMA guidance (CHMP/437/04 Rev 1). The use of the US-Neulasta instead of the EU-Neulasta is considered acceptable since the CMC quality bridge between US-Neulasta and EU-Neulasta has been demonstrated.

Validated analytical methods were used for the determination of pegfilgrastim (QL0605 and USNeulasta), anti-drug antibodies (ADA), neutralizing antibodies (NAb), and pharmacodynamic (PD)

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

biomarkers.

## Immunogenicity assay

As per current guidance from the EMA and the FDA (FDA, 2016; EMA, 2017) a stepwise approach for the immunogenicity testing for the clinical development of QL0605 was implemented by the Applicant. This stepwise immunogenicity assessment designed to provide a comprehensive comparison of QL0605 and US-Neulasta included following:

Sensitive screening assay followed by a confirmatory assay to assess ADA positivity

Semi-quantification of detected ADA using a titer assay

Specificity assays to distinguish between PEG and filgrastim binding ADA

Cell-based neutralizing antibody (NAb) assay to evaluate neutralizing activity of the detected ADA.

The transition from first-generation assays (SG003/2020 and SG004/2020) to second-generation assays (SG042/2023 and SG043/2023) acknowledges the critical challenge of drug tolerance in pegfilgrastim bioanalysis.

Method validation included cut-point determination, sensitivity, precision, selectivity and stability. Validation of the method No. SG043/2023 (Synexa Study Number 130803) was conducted prior to the sampling testing. The implementation of acid dissociation in the pivotal ADA method (SG042/2023) is appropriate and aligns with current standards for bridging assays involving targets with long half-lives. After adaptation of the method (during the development programme), partial re-validation of the assay occurred. This re-validation included change of critical reagents, i.e. QL0605 batch due to stability testing. See OC raised regarding the partial re-validation. There were two amendments identified: 1.0 (Update of general run design, antigenic equivalence acceptance criteria and data analysis sections, removal of positive control stability section) and 2.0 (Update of low positive control section).

A qualitative CellTiterGlo Luminescent Cell Viability Assay (Promega) was used to detect, confirm, and titrate anti-QL0605 and anti-Neulasta (US) antibodies in normal human serum. Both description and validation of the assay were provided by the Applicant. Samples confirmed positive for the presence of anti-QL0605 and Neulasta antibodies were subject to the cell-based assay for detection of neutralising antibodies. This assay was a qualitative neutralising cell-based assay with an evaluation of M-NFS-cell proliferation (using mouse myelogenous leukaemia (M-NFS-60) cell line (ATCC, cat. no. CRL-1838) as the functional endpoint. The presence of ADAs was evaluated by using polyethylene glycol granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (PEG G-CSF) assay, PEG assay and G-CSF assay. Description and validation of this cell-based assay were provided by the Applicant as well. Presented data indicated that the screening assay method from pre-dose serum could be suitable for the detection of neutralising antibodies against QL0605 and Neulasta in normal human serum.

## Pharmacokinetics and PD Biomarkers

Pegfilgrastim was quantified using a validated electrochemiluminescence (ECL) immunoassay (Method SG002/2020) on the MSD platform. The assay range was 0.050 to 36.450 ng/mL (in 10% matrix) with an LLOQ of 0.500 ng/mL in neat serum. The method demonstrated robust performance during the analysis of clinical samples for both studies. The cumulative precision (%CV) and accuracy (%RE) for Quality Control samples (LQC, MQC, HQC) were well within the acceptance limits. Calibration curves met acceptance criteria. Incurred Sample Reanalysis (ISR) was successfully performed for pivotal study with pass rates &gt;80%.

The bioanalytical data indicates that the method SG002/2020 is reproducible and suitable for the determination of pegfilgrastim concentrations in the clinical studies Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) was measured using the ADVIA 2120i analyzer, and CD34+ cell counts were quantified via flow cytometry (BD FACSCanto II). Both methods were validated and suitable for use.

The bioanalytical method for PK (SG002/2020) demonstrated robust performance. Calibration curves, precision, and accuracy met acceptance criteria, and ISR pass rates were acceptable for both pivotal studies. The methods used for PD biomarkers (ANC and CD34+) are standard, validated clinical assays suitable for their intended use.

## Study QL0605-OVE-001

The pivotal PK/PD comparability study QL0605-OVE-001 was appropriately designed, conducted and analysed in line with the EMA biosimilar guidance and scientific advice.

The pivotal study was a single center, randomized, double-blind, single dose, 2-period cross-over study designed to demonstrate the PK and PD BE and to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of QL0605 as compared to US-Neulasta in healthy volunteers. The study design is considered acceptable and in line with the EMA/CHMP scientific advice. The cross-over concept is supported, considering the high inter-subject variability of pegfilgrastim. The wash-out period of at least 6 weeks (42 days) between the Period 1 and Period 2 of the study is considered adequate to avoid carry-over effects. The choice of healthy volunteers as study population is acceptable, since variability can be minimised and the mode of action is the same in healthy subjects and patients. In order to decrease further the PK variability, the Applicant performed the study in male subjects only. The demographics and baseline characteristics of the two treatment groups were balanced for age, height, weight, race and BMI.

The applied 6 mg dose of pegfilgrastim represents the therapeutical dose of the reference product Neulasta. The dose administered is considered adequate to establish PK similarity and is appropriate from safety point of view.

The sampling times were chosen based on the tmax and t1/2 of pegfilgrastim. In healthy volunteers, maximal pegfilgrastim concentrations are observed between 8-48 hours post-dosing, depending on the dose. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 46-62 hours. The time-points for PK sampling are considered adequate to reflect the absorption period, as well as the elimination period. The duration of treatment periods of 28 days (app. 10 half-lives) is considered sufficient to reliably estimate PK parameters. Therefore, the proposed sampling schedule seems adequate to characterize the pharmacokinetic profile and estimate PK parameters of pegfilgrastim.

No concerns are identified regarding the comparator suitability.

The majority of the concomitant medications used were anti-inflammatory and antirheumatic products and paracetamol. Near half of the subjects in each sequence received ibuprofen (49.4% in sequence A/B, respective 48.3% in sequence B/A).

All protocol deviations, majority of which were procedural deviations or measurements outside of allowed time window, were considered minor and with no impact on the study results.

The pharmacokinetic endpoints are considered adequate and in line with the scientific advice received and Guidance on similar medicinal products containing recombinant granulocyte-colony stimulating factor EMEA/CHMP/BMWP/31329/2005.

Bioequivalence between the test product QL0605 and the reference product US-Neulasta was assessed using ANOVA models fitted on natural logarithm transformed AUCinf, AUClast and Cmax values. The mixed effects model included treatment, period and sequence as fixed and subject within sequence as random effects. This is considered acceptable.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Based on the PK Analysis Population, bioequivalence was demonstrated for all pharmacokinetic parameters Cmax, AUC0-t and AUCinf as corresponding 90% confidence interval (CI) for treatment ratio QL0605/US-Neulasta was fully within equivalence range 80 - 125%. The 90% CI for Cmax was 98.6 - 115.0 and the point estimate 106.5, the 90% CI for AUCinf was 102.7 - 119.7 and the point estimate 110.9 and the 90% CI AUC0-t was 102.5 - 119.5 and the point estimate 110.6.

Similar results to those of the primary analysis were obtained in both sensitivity analyses performed. Pharmacokinetic equivalence was demonstrated in the study.

The impact of ADA on the PK parameters was studied. In ADA positive subjects, the serum PK parameters of QL0605 were slightly higher as compared to those obtained with US-Neulasta. In ADA negative subjects, the serum PK parameters were comparable for all subjects following SC administration of QL0605 and US-Neulasta.

The time-points for PD samples until day 22 are considered adequate to reflect the response and the duration of response.

The primary PD endpoints AUEC(0-504h) and Emax of the absolute neutrophil count (ANC) and secondary PD endpoints are adequate being relevant pharmacodynamic markers for the activity of pegfilgrastim. They are in line with the Guidance on similar medicinal products containing r-GCSF (EMEA/CHMP/BMWP/31329/2005).

Assessment of BE with respect to PD parameters is based on analysis of variance model.

Regarding the primary PD endpoints, AUEC(0-504h) and Emax of the absolute neutrophil count equivalence was demonstrated as the % CIs [99.9; 103.2] and [98.3; 102.8] are fully contained within the pre-defined equivalence margin (90% - 111%).

The demonstrated equivalence in key pharmacokinetic parameters (Cmax, AUC0-t and AUCinf) and the primary pharmacodynamic endpoints based on ANC (AUEC(0-504h) and Emax) at the therapeutic dose of 6 mg is considered robust and clinically meaningful. ANC represents the clinically most relevant pharmacodynamic marker of pegfilgrastim activity.

Supportive statistical analyses have been performed for PD Secondary endpoints.

Results of the sensitivity analysis of the BE for primary PD endpoints, which included those subjects that had data for both treatments, support the results of the primary PD analysis.

The GMR and 95% CIs for ANC ECmax and AOBEC(0-504h) were 100.7 [98.2; 103.2] and 102.0 [99.6; 104.4], respectively, which support the results of the primary PD analysis as the 95% CIs are fully contained within the pre-defined equivalence margin (90% - 111%).

PD equivalence was consistently demonstrated across primary, supportive and sensitivity analyses.

Also, regarding PD endpoints CD34+ AUEC(0-504h) and CD34+ AOBEC(0-504h) bioequivalence was concluded as the corresponding 95% CIs were [100.7; 108.4] and [100.0; 109.2], respectively.

Regarding PD endpoints CD34+ Emax and CD34+ ECmax bioequivalence was not concluded as the corresponding 95% CIs were [102.2; 113.1] and [102.2; 113.7], respectively. ISCV was 63.1% for CD34+ Emax and 65.0% for CD34+ AOBEC(0-504h). However, CD34+ PD parameters were secondary and supportive with inherent variability and limited direct clinical relevance for efficacy determination in the biosimilarity context of pegfilgrastim. The failure to meet equivalence margins for these secondary parameters does not outweigh the clear and consistent equivalence demonstrated for PK

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

exposure and ANC response. Nevertheless, this issue was addressed during the first assessment round through an OC, to which the Applicant provided an adequate response. In line with the co-rapporteur's assessment, the justification regarding the CD34+ findings is considered sufficient, and the matter is therefore regarded as resolved. No clinically relevant implications for efficacy, safety, or SmPC wording were identified, and the biosimilarity conclusion remains unaffected.

The immunogenicity assessment in the study QL0605-OVE-001 was limited as the study was designed primarily for PK/PD assessment.

## Study QL0605-OVE-002

The applicant performed a second study QL0605-OVE-002 in order to assess more sensitively the relative immunogenicity between QL0605 and Neulasta after repeated administration. Thus, immunogenicity results were primarily based on the results of the study QL0605-OVE-002. This was a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, 2-dose, 2-period parallel-arm study designed to assess the immunogenicity of 2 subcutaneous doses of QL0605 (6 mg) compared to 2 subcutaneous doses of USNeulasta (6 mg) in healthy male and female subjects.

Immunogenicity (ADA): A tiered approach was utilized. The first-generation bridging assay (SG003/2020) had limited drug tolerance. It was replaced in pivotal studies by Method SG042/2023, which incorporates an acid dissociation step to improve drug tolerance while maintaining a sensitivity of 2.950 ng/mL.

Immunogenicity (NAb): The first-generation cell-based assay (SG004/2020) had good sensitivity (287.720 ng/mL) but poor drug tolerance. It was replaced by Method SG043/2023, a competitive cellbased assay optimized for drug tolerance, though with reduced sensitivity (1430.595 ng/mL).

The transition from Method 1 to Method 2 (SG042/2023) was appropriate. The inclusion of an acid dissociation step aligns with current regulatory standards for bridging assays involving targets with long half-lives.

The pivotal nAb assay (Method 2, SG043/2023) demonstrates significantly lower sensitivity (1430.595 ng/mL) compared to the first-generation method (Method 1, SG004/2020; 287.720 ng/mL). This reduction in assay performance correlates with a reduced detection rate observed in study QL0605OVE-002, where Method 1 identified 15 positive subjects (8 in the QL0605 group and 7 in the Neulasta group), while Method 2 identified only 2 positive subjects (both in the Neulasta group). Regarding the Neutralising Antibody (NAb) assessment, this issue was addressed during the first assessment round through an OC, to which the Applicant provided an adequate response. In line with the co-rapporteur's assessment, the transition to Method SG043/2023 was sufficiently justified by improved assay specificity and the identified validation limitations of the previous method. Although some residual uncertainty regarding reduced assay sensitivity and the potential for false-negative results in low-titre antibodies cannot be fully excluded, the overall immunogenicity assessment remains acceptable. The absence of any clinically meaningful impact of ADA/nAb status on PK/PD, together with comparable immunogenicity between treatments, supports the conclusion that this issue is resolved and does not affect the biosimilarity conclusion.

The design of the parallel group study QL0605-OVE-002 to assess the comparative immunogenic response after repeated administration is considered adequate and in line with the EMA/CHMP scientific advice.

As pointed out in the scientific advice, as the safety profile of pegfilgrastim is well-known it is acceptable to conduct the study in healthy subjects. The dose as well as the interval between doses is agreed.

## 5.2.6.2. Conclusions

Pharmacokinetic equivalence between QL0605 and the reference product US-Neulasta was demonstrated based on the data submitted, thus PK biosimilarity with reference product Neulasta can be concluded, given that the CMC quality bridge is established between the EU- and US-Neulasta.

QL0605 and the reference product US-Neulasta are PD equivalent with respect to primary parameters ANC AUEC(0-504h) and ANC Emax; these are regarded as robust and clinically meaningful. The selection of ANC, and the use of Emax and AUEC(0-504h) as primary PD endpoints are considered scientifically justified and aligned with EMA guidance for G-CSF biosimilars. In addition, bioequivalence was concluded with respect to secondary parameters ANC ECmax, ANC AOBEC(0-504h), CD34+ AUEC(0-504h) and CD34+ AOBEC(0-504h).

Bioequivalence has not been demonstrated between QL0605 and the reference product US-Neulasta with respect to secondary parameters CD34+ Emax and ECmax as these parameters were not in the prespecified equivalence range (90%, 111%). The corresponding 95% CIs were [102.2; 113.1] and [102.2; 113.7], respectively. The failure to meet equivalence margins for these secondary parameters does not outweigh the clear and consistent equivalence demonstrated for PK exposure and ANC

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

response. No clinically relevant implications for efficacy, safety, or SmPC wording were identified, and the biosimilarity conclusion remains unaffected.

Additional supportive analyses evaluating PD parameters by ADA status further contextualise these findings. In ADA-positive subjects, PD responses for ANC were overall similar between QL0605 and USNeulasta, while CD34+ parameters showed slightly higher exposure estimates for QL0605. In ADAnegative subjects, PD parameters for both ANC and CD34+ cells were comparable between treatments. These observations do not indicate a consistent or clinically meaningful difference in pharmacodynamic behaviour between the two products.

Overall, the immunogenicity appears comparable between the QL0605 and the US-Neulasta group following the 2 s.c. injections. In addition, there were no clinically meaningful differences regarding the impact of ADA on the PK or PD across the 2 study drugs.

In summary, the totality of PK, PD, and immunogenicity data supports biosimilarity between QL0605 and Neulasta.

Please refer to the table of studies in section 5.1.2.

For the purpose of this document, the following definitions apply:

'Adverse event - AE' means any untoward medical occurrence in a subject to whom a medicinal product is administered and which does not necessarily have a causal relationship with this treatment.

'Serious adverse event - SAE' means any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose requires inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity, results in a congenital anomaly or birth defect, is life-threatening, or results in death. The definition (in line with ICH E2A) includes important medical events that may not be immediately life-threatening or result in death or hospitalisation but may jeopardise the patient or may require intervention to prevent one of the other outcomes listed in the definition above.

'Adverse drug reaction - ADR' means any untoward and unintended response to a medicinal product related to any dose administered, for which, after a thorough assessment, a causal relationship between the medicinal product and the adverse event is at least a reasonable possibility, based for example, on their comparative incidence in clinical trials, or findings from epidemiological studies and/or on an evaluation of causality from individual case reports.

## 5.4.1. Safety data collection

The safety assessment of QL0605 is based on two comparative Phase 1 studies QL0605-OVE-001 and QL0605-OVE-002 including healthy subjects, which is in accordance with requirements given in the Guideline on similar biological medicinal products containing biotechnology-derived protein as active substance: non-clinical and clinical issues (EMA/CHMP/BMWP/42832/2005 Rev1; 18-December-2014). Both studies were completed at the time of the submission. A secondary objective of these clinical studies was to compare safety between the candidate biosimilar QL0605 and US-Neulasta. The safety analysis population (SAF) included all randomized subjects who received at least 1 dose of the investigational medicinal product. The clinical studies were designed to rule out clinically relevant differences in safety between the candidate biosimilar and the reference product, and to confirm biosimilarity. The safety results from each study were presented separately and the data from two comparative studies was also presented combined. However, as the two studies do not have the same design, this assessment report will present the results per each study.

## Study QL0605-OVE-001

Overall, 178 subjects were included in the study and received at least one dose of study treatment. ## 5.4.11. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical safety

## 5.4.11.1. Discussion

## 5.4.11.1.1. Overall assessment of available safety data

The safety assessment of QL0605 is based on two comparative Phase 1 studies QL0605-OVE-001 and QL0605-OVE-002 performed in healthy subjects, which is in accordance with requirements given in the Guideline on similar biological medicinal products containing biotechnology-derived protein as active substance: non-clinical and clinical issues (EMA/CHMP/BMWP/42832/2005 Rev1; 18-December-2014) and the scientific advice provided by EMA in 2019.

This application concerns an application in accordance with Article 10(4) of EC Directive 2001/83/EC (similarity to a reference biological product) claiming Nylaspeg to be \"bio-similar\" to EU-sourced Neulasta (EU/1/02/227/001-002+004); however, the applicant used in both studies (QL0605-OVE001, QL0605-OVE-002) as a control US sourced Neulasta, claiming that the medicinal products are essentially similar. There was a comparability assessment done at quality level to prove the similarity of the US and EU sourced reference medicinal products Neulasta. No direct comparison between the Nylaspeg and EU sourced reference medicinal product Neulasta with regards to safety profile was done.

However as outlined in the EMA Guideline on Similar Biological Medicinal Products (CHMP/437/04 rev.1), it is possible to use non-EU sourced reference product batches to demonstrate clinical comparability, as long as the analytical comparability between the EU and non-EU reference product is confirmed. The Applicant conducted the bridging at CMC level which was in line with the SA and thus acceptable (for further details see the quality part).

Both studies were completed at the time of the submission. A secondary objective of these clinical studies was to compare safety between the candidate biosimilar QL0605 and US-Neulasta. The safety analysis population (SAF) included all randomized subjects who received at least 1 dose of the investigational medicinal product. The clinical studies were designed to rule out clinically relevant differences in safety between the candidate biosimilar and the reference product, and to confirm biosimilarity. The safety results from each study were presented separately and the data from two comparative studies was also assessed in combination.

Overall, the provided safety database was considered sufficient for the establishment of safety for this product taking into account the well-known safety profile of this active substance and its nature, i.e. biosimilar.

The safety analyses consisted of the assessment of AEs, clinical laboratory testing (haematology, clinical chemistry, urinalysis and coagulation), vital signs, 12. lead electrocardiogram, physical examinations, injection site tolerance (measured via VAS and by the investigator using the injection site reaction score), concomitant medication assessment, spleen ultrasound examination and immunogenicity assessment.

A total of 480 healthy subjects received at least 1 dose of Nylaspeg in both clinical trials.

In study QL0605-OVE-001, 178 subjects (89 per each Sequence) were randomized and received at least one dose of study treatment and 173 subjects (86 (98.9%) in Sequence A/B and 86 (98.9%) in Sequence B/A completed the study. Overall, 170 subjects received doses of both products over the two treatment periods and 8 subjects (5 for QL0605, 3 for US-Neulasta) received only one dose of the study treatment during Treatment Period 1 and did not receive a second dose in Treatment Period 2.

The demographic characteristics are balanced between the treatment groups. Only male subjects participated in this study, which is considered acceptable because of menstrual cycle dependent - increased variability observed in females. The mean age and mean BMI were comparable between the treatment groups.

There were no clinically significant findings with regard to medical history or previous medications for the subjects in study QL0605-OVE-001.

The majority of the concomitant medications used were anti-inflammatory and antirheumatic products and paracetamol. Near half of the subjects in each sequence received ibuprofen (49.4% in sequence A/B, respective 43 subjects (48.3%) in sequence B/A)). The use of concomitant medication was similar between sequence A/B and B/A.

In study QL0605-OVE-002, 304 subjects (153 in QL0605 arm and 151 in US-Neulasta arm) were randomized and 136 (88.9%) in QL0605 arm and 135 (89.4%) in US-Neulasta arm completed he study. Overall, 302 subjects included in the SAF (QL0605: 152 [100%]; US-Neulasta: 150 [100%]) received first dose of study medication and 272 (90.1%) subjects (QL0605: 137 [90.1%]; USNeulasta: 135 [90%]) received the second dose of the study medication.

## 9. Biosimilarity assessment

## 9.1. Comparability exercise and indications claimed

Claimed indication is identical to the reference product Neulasta:

\"Reduction in the duration of neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia in adult patients treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy for malignancy (with the exception of chronic myeloid leukaemia and myelodysplastic syndromes)\".

The provided SmPC is in line with Neulasta. The clinical programme was conducted in healthy subjects only, and thus no new information was acquired concerning drug effects on patients treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy for malignancy.

## Quality

The biosimilarity exercise for QL0605 followed the recommendations as laid down in EMA/CHMP/BWP/247713/2012.

The main analytical comparability (biosimilarity) study is described in sufficient detail. 26 batches of the RMP EU-Neulasta were used to establish the biosimilarity, which is considered sufficient. Additionally, 27 batches of the comparator US-Neulasta were included into the 3-way comparability study, as US-Neulasta was used in the clinical trial, which is considered adequate.

The risk ranking of the QA for the analytical comparability study was done by an adequate risk assessment including calculation of a criticality score.

For the quality range (QR) definition for quantitative methods a standard deviation approach (QR= Mean of reference product ± X standard deviation of reference product) was employed. The multiplier X is set to 1.5 for attributes with very high criticality relevant to the mode of action or high attributes with high (%RSD: = 15%) assay variability. The multiplier X is set to 3 for high attributes with low (%RSD: &lt;15%) assay variability and moderate attributes. High similarity was considered to have been demonstrated if the data points for the test product were within the QR of the reference product.

For quality attributes where data is qualitative and therefore not amenable to quantitative analysis, a direct comparison of raw data was made between the products. The statistical approach is considered adequate.

## Non-clinical

The non-clinical development programme for this application consisted of in vitro and in vivo studies to assess the biosimilarity between QL0605 (Nylaspeg) and Neulasta sourced from Europe and the US.

The non-clinical programme followed the Guideline on similar biological medicinal products (CHMP/437/04 Rev 1) and the Guideline on similar biological medicinal products containing biotechnology-derived proteins as active substance: non-clinical and clinical issues (EMA/CHMP/BMWP/42832/2005 Rev 1.). The applicant also sought scientific advice from the European Medicines Agency in November 2021 and a meeting request (Written Response Only) was submitted to BfArM on 21st May 2025 - on the test panel, lot selection, and statistical analysis methodology appropriate for the demonstration of analytical biosimilarity between QL0605 and EU-approved Neulasta and the proposed specifications appropriate for ensuring the quality of QL0605 drug substance, drug product, G-CSF intermediate and mPEG intermediate.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The assays used included an in vitro cell-based proliferation assay. The specific binding of pegylated recombinant human granulocyte stimulating factor (PEG-G-CSF) to the surface receptor of mouse myeloid leukaemia lymphocytes (M-NFS-60) had a dose-dependent proliferative effect on their growth. Surface plasmon resonance (Biacore) assay was used for determination of the apparent dissociation rate constant (kd), apparent association rate constant (ka) and apparent dissociation equilibrium constant (KD). These functional assays are directly related to the pegfilgrastim therapeutic mechanism of action that involves pegfilgrastim binding to receptors on the surface of hematopoietic cells in the bone marrow followed by concentration-dependent cell proliferation and differentiation. The methods used are appropriate and the in vitro assays have been validated or qualified.

The pharmacological activity of QL0605 was also assessed in a comparative in vivo PD/PK study which has been performed in healthy and neutropenic Sprague Dawley rats. This animal study is only considered as supportive for the biosimilarity assessment.

## Clinical

Two clinical studies have been conducted to demonstrate the pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, safety and immunogenicity biosimilarity between QL0605 and US-Neulasta: QL0605-OVE-001 and QL0605-OVE-002.

Study QL0605-OVE-001: a randomized, double-blind, 2-period cross-over study to compare the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics and safety of a single subcutaneous administration of pegylated filgrastim QL0605 (6 mg) with US Neulasta (6 mg) in healthy male subjects

The primary PK endpoints of the study were: AUCinf, AUC0-t and Cmax. The co-primary PD endpoints were ANC AUEC(0-504h) and ANC Emax.

Study QL0605-OVE-002: a randomized, double-blind, 2-period parallel-arm study to assess the immunogenicity of 2 subcutaneous doses of QL0605 (6 mg) compared to 2 subcutaneous doses of USNeulasta (6 mg) in healthy male and female subjects

The primary immunogenicity endpoints were Incidence (absolute and percentage) of neutralizing ADA by treatment, incidence (absolute and percentage) of ADA by treatment, categorized as overall, treatment-induced, and treatment-boosted and kinetics of ADA development (onset; duration).

Both clinical studies were completed at the time of submission of the MA application.

## 9.2. Results supporting biosimilarity

Quality

The analytical comparability supports the biosimilarity of QL0605 to its RMP EU-Neulasta.

## Non-clinical

Biosimilarity of QL0605 and EU-Neulasta was demonstrated by comparative assessment of the binding affinity to the G-CSF receptor as well as the potency to stimulate proliferation of myeloblastic cells.

The relative potency of QL0605 and the binding affinity to the G-CSF receptor were compared between QL0605 and EU- US-pegfilgrastim sourced products by cell proliferation assay and surface plasmon resonance (SPR).

The results provided show that potency was similar for QL0605 and EU/US-Neulasta in a murine myeloid leukaemia lymphocyte proliferation assay as well as binding affinity to the G-CSF receptor.

Consequently, biosimilarity of QL0605 (Nylaspeg) and Neulasta is established through this in vitro comparative assessment.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The results of a comparative PD/PK study in non-neutropenic and neutropenic rats provided an increase and dose-response in overall leukocyte levels and in neutrophil granulocyte levels after treatment with QL0605 and Neulasta.

The results obtained from the in vivo study in rats also indicate similarity, however the in vivo studies have some limitations such as the great variability and, thus, the in vivo results are regarded to have only a supportive role compared to the in vitro results.

From a non-clinical point of view, biosimilarity was shown to the reference product through in vitro and in vivo studies. No significant differences were found in these pharmacology and pharmacokinetic studies.

## Clinical Pharmacology

## 9.4. Discussion on biosimilarity

## Quality

The analytical comparability exercise for QL0605 versus the reference medicinal product EU-Neulasta is considered well designed, and the provided data support the claim of biosimilarity.

## Non-clinical

The non-clinical part of the comparability exercise was adequate to demonstrate biosimilarity.

The non-clinical biosimilarity programme comprised of comparative assessment of in vitro PD effects as well as an in vivo PD/PK study. The results of the vitro assays support biosimilarity. From a nonclinical perspective the results of the in vitro assays show similarity between QL0605 and the reference product (based on data from Neulasta sourced from either Europe or the US). The assays were performed on a sufficient number of batches, with appropriate methods and have been validated and qualified. They are therefore deemed suitable to claim biosimilarity between QL0605 and the reference product Neulasta.

Biological activity of QL0605 has been extensively demonstrated in the comparative in vitro biosimilarity assessment with EU/US-Neulasta. Potency was similar for QL0605 and EU/US-Neulasta in a murine myeloid leukaemia lymphocyte proliferation assay as well as binding affinity to the G-CSF receptor.

The in vivo study had some limitations and is regarded to be only of supportive nature.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In general, results obtained from the submitted in vivo PK/PD studies in non-neutropenic and neutropenic rats did not demonstrate statistically significant differences between QL0605 and EUNeulasta administered at the same dose level in terms of ANC, AUCANC and pharmacokinetics. From a non-clinical point of view, QL0605 had the expected in vitro and in vivo activity and was similar to comparator Neulasta.

In conclusion, from a non-clinical perspective the requirements for biosimilarity assessment have been met and sufficient evidence for the demonstration of biosimilarity has been provided.

## Clinical

## PK/PD biosimilarity

## PK/PD Study QL0605-OVE-001

Study QL0605-OVE-001 is an appropriate sensitive clinical model for biosimilarity assessment of pegfilgrastim, being a randomised, double-blind, cross-over study in healthy volunteers using the therapeutic 6 mg s.c. dose with intensive PK sampling and PD characterisation (ANC as primary PD, CD34+ as secondary PD).

## Strength of evidence for efficacy similarity (via PK/PD):

The study provides robust evidence for PK similarity between QL0605 and US-Neulasta, with Cmax, AUC0-t and AUCinf meeting standard bioequivalence criteria (90% CI within 80-125%). Given the well-established exposure-response relationship for pegfilgrastim and the sensitivity of the cross-over design, these PK results are supportive of similar clinical performance.

For PD similarity, the choice of ANC as the primary PD endpoint is scientifically and regulatorily appropriate, as ANC is a direct marker of G-CSF pharmacology and an accepted surrogate for pegfilgrastim effect. The ANC profiles were comparable between products, supporting similarity in pharmacological activity and, by extension, efficacy.

## Uncertainties and their likely impact on efficacy/safety:

The previously identified uncertainty related to the supportive CD34+ PD endpoint has been adequately addressed by the Applicant during the procedure. Although the upper bounds of the 95% CIs for CD34+ Emax slightly exceeded the predefined margins in supportive/sensitivity analyses, the Applicant provided a scientifically justified explanation demonstrating that CD34+ is an inherently more variable upstream biomarker with higher intra- and inter-subject variability. Importantly, the primary and clinically relevant ANC PD parameters (AUEC₀-t and Emax) met the predefined equivalence criteria, supporting PD comparability. In addition, CD34+ AUEC remained within the acceptance range, and point estimates for CD34+ Emax were close to unity, without evidence of systematic bias. The observed deviation is therefore considered minor, restricted to the upper CI bound, and without clinical relevance for efficacy, safety, or SmPC wording.

Overall, no residual uncertainty remains that would impact the biosimilarity conclusion or suggest clinical meaningful differences in efficacy or safety, in line with the EMA guideline for G-CSF biosimilars.

## Safety/immunogenicity interpretability within OVE-001:

Safety and immunogenicity conclusions from OVE-001 are inherently limited because the study is primarily designed for PK/PD; it can only provide supportive reassurance rather than a definitive comparative safety/immunogenicity estimate. Nonetheless, the PD profiles were reported as similar irrespective of ADA status, suggesting no obvious ADA-driven divergence in PD behaviour in this setting.

## Overall conclusion for OVE-001:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Within the scope of study QL0605-OVE-001, the comparability exercise is considered adequate and provides robust evidence supporting pharmacokinetic similarity and pharmacodynamic comparability, particularly for the clinically relevant primary ANC endpoints, which form the principal basis for the expectation of similar efficacy.

Overall, the PD profiles of QL0605 and US-Neulasta were similar in both ADA+ and ADA populations, with comparable mean values across most parameters, indicating that the presence or absence of ADAs did not significantly impact the PD responses of either treatment.

Safety profile and pooled analysis of the TEAEs were reported by the Applicant for both studies (QL0605-OVE-001 and QL0605-OVE-002). Overall, the discontinuation rate was low. Higher discontinuation rate was documented in the Immunogenicity Study QL0605-OVE-002 than in the PK/PD study. More subjects experienced TEAEs which led to discontinuation in the US-Neulasta arm than the QL0605 and only few were recorded as related to the study product (numerically more in the US-Neulasta arm/group).

In conclusion, safety data (based on two studies QL0605-OVE-001 and QL0605-OVE-002) are considered adequate for the assessment of biosimilarity between QL0605 and Neulasta. This conclusion relies on the bridge between the EU- and US-Neulasta at quality level. This decision was supported by the SA provided by the EMA in 2019. Furthermore, as outlined in the EMA Guideline on Similar Biological Medicinal Products (CHMP/437/04 rev.1) it is possible to use non-EU sourced reference product batches to demonstrate clinical comparability.

The safety profile of QL0605 is considered comparable to the reference medicinal product.

## Immunogenicity

The immunogenicity assessment in the Study QL0605-OVE-001 was limited as the study was designed primarily for PK/PD assessment.

Analysis of Immunogenicity was a primary objective in the Study QL0605-OVE-002. The Applicant presented the results as risk difference between the two groups: -0.02 (90% CI: -0.08; 0.05) indicating non-inferiority in immunogenicity status.

Overall, the immunogenicity is comparable between QL0605 and the Neulasta.
