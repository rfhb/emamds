## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## 2.3.1. Introduction

The MAH submitted a final report for the randomized, double-blind, phase 3 study that evaluated the efficacy and safety of vedolizumab IV in paediatric patients with UC (study MLN0002-3024).

## 2.3.2. Clinical study

## Clinical study MLN0002-3024:

This is Randomized, Double-Blind, Phase 3 Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Vedolizumab Intravenous as Maintenance Therapy in Paediatric Subjects with Moderately to Severely Active Ulcerative Colitis Who Achieved Clinical Response Following Open-label Vedolizumab Intravenous Therapy

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Description

In this study, vedolizumab was administered IV during a 14-week, open-label induction period followed by a 40-week, 2-dose arm, randomized, double-blind maintenance period. The study was designed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, PK, and immunogenicity of high and low doses of vedolizumab IV in 3 different weight groups for maintenance of remission in children aged 2 to 17 years, inclusive, who weighed ≥10 kg with moderately to severely active UC.

Data from this study will be used for extrapolation of efficacy from adults to pediatric subjects based on the efficacy and PK data from completed studies in adult subjects.

## Methods

The study included a DSMB to review the safety, PK, and other relevant data, and a program-level IAC was in scope to review and adjudicate potential PML events.

## Study participants

Subjects must have been diagnosed with moderately to severely active UC for at least 1 month before screening and failed response to, lost response to, or been intolerant to at least 1 of the current standard-of-care induction and maintenance therapies for UC, including immunomodulators (for example, AZA, 6-MP, MTX), and TNF-α antagonists (for example, infliximab, adalimumab).

Lack of response to corticosteroids at the time of the initial diagnosis of UC or inability to wean off corticosteroids without a recurrence of symptoms also qualified a subject for the study.

Subjects were evaluated during a 35-day screening period by using symptom diaries, colonoscopy, and determination of fecal calprotectin and CRP. Those with moderately to severely active UC, defined by a modified Mayo score of 5 to 9 (sum of the stool frequency, rectal bleeding, and endoscopic subscores with an endoscopic subscore of ≥2), were eligible to enter the open-label induction period. The PUCAI was also assessed.

All colonoscopies (at screening and all subsequent flexible sigmoidoscopies at Weeks 14 and 54 or the ET visit) were centrally read for Mayo endoscopic subscores. Historical colonoscopy (with recorded video), done up to 30 days before signing of the assent/ICF, could be submitted to central readers for assessment after assent/informed consent was obtained. If found to be adequate by central readers, the video colonoscopy was used for the assessment of the Mayo endoscopic subscore, which was used as the baseline assessment.

## Treatments

During the induction period, subjects who weighed ≥30 kg received open-label doses of vedolizumab 300 mg IV at Day 1, Week 2, and Week 6; subjects who weighed &gt;15 to &lt;30 kg received open-label doses of vedolizumab 200 mg IV at Day 1, Week 2, and Week 6; and subjects who weighed 10 to 15 kg received open-label doses of vedolizumab 150 mg IV at Day 1, Week 2, and Week 6.

Subjects who achieved a clinical response at Week 14 were stratified by TNF-α antagonist therapy status and by weight group. Subjects were then randomized 1:1 to one of 2 vedolizumab dose groups (low dose, high dose), within each of 3 weight groups (≥30 kg; &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg; 10 kg to 15 kg), for the double-blind maintenance period and initiated blinded IV doses of vedolizumab. Randomized subjects received 5 maintenance doses of vedolizumab IV Q8W at Week 14, Week 22, Week 30, Week 38, and Week 46. Corticosteroids (for example, prednisone or its equivalent) were used as AxMPs for rescue treatment during the maintenance period of the study. Lack of maintenance of clinical response and worsening of disease during the maintenance period resulted in a subject becoming eligible to receive rescue therapy with corticosteroids at the investigator's discretion in an attempt to re-establish response/remission.

If the investigator elected to continue the subject in the study and initiated corticosteroid rescue at a scheduled or unscheduled visit, it was to be based on evaluation of partial Mayo score with documentation of worsening. Prescribed doses of corticosteroids at the discretion of the investigator were not to exceed 40 mg or 1 mg/kg (whichever was lower) of oral prednisone per day or its equivalent. All subjects, including those initiated on corticosteroid rescue therapy who achieved no clinical response (as previously defined) in 2 subsequent consecutive visits were to be discontinued from the study. After initiation of corticosteroid rescue therapy, tapering of corticosteroids started if/when the subject had achieved clinical response based on partial Mayo score measured at the next scheduled or unscheduled follow-up visit. Corticosteroids were to be tapered on a controlled schedule, as defined in the protocol, designed to wean off corticosteroids within 12 weeks of initiation of tapering. Worsening of disease upon tapering of corticosteroids resulted in discontinuation from the study.

## Sample size

The primary statistical objective was the estimation of the primary endpoint, clinical remission at Week 54, by dose group (high dose, low dose). No formal hypothesis testing was planned. The sample size justification was based on ensuring adequate precision (using the half-width of the 95% CI) for the estimate of the true clinical remission rate.

Assuming the true clinical remission rate of 42% at Week 54, a sample size of 36 subjects in each maintenance arm will provide a 95% Clopper-Pearson exact CI with a lower bound of 25.5% and an upper bound of 59.2%. To ensure a randomized sample size of 72 subjects (36 per maintenance arm) during the maintenance phase, approximately 120 subjects will need to be enrolled in the induction phase of the study.

Approximately 90 subjects weighing ≥30 kg (HWC) and around 30 subjects weighing 10 to &lt;30 kg (LWC) were planned at the time of Vedolizumab IV (Pediatric) enrollment into the study.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Randomization took place after confirmation of eligibility for the maintenance phase. The randomization was stratified by previous exposure/failure to TNF-α antagonists therapy or naive to TNF-α antagonists therapy, and by weight group (≥30 kg; &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg; 10 kg to 15 kg).

## Statistical Methods

In general, the efficacy analysis for the maintenance period was performed based on the ITT-M population (consisting of subjects randomized for the maintenance period) by dose group (low dose, high dose). All analyses were descriptive in nature, and no formal hypothesis testing was planned or conducted.

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of 2 different dose regimens of vedolizumab IV in pediatric subjects with UC based on the primary endpoint of clinical remission at Week 54. The proportions of clinical remitters at Week 54 in the ITT-M population for both dose regimens (low and high vedolizumab IV maintenance doses) and overall, across both doses, were estimated along with corresponding 95% exact CIs. Additionally, the difference in proportions between the 2 doses, adjusted by prior TNF-α antagonist therapy status as reported on IRT and by weight group, with the corresponding 95% CIs, were determined using CMH methods. Results were also presented for the HWC subgroup (≥30 kg) and LWC subgroup (which included weight groups 10 to 15 kg and &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg). The primary efficacy endpoint was analyzed considering the subjects experiencing an ICE listed in the estimand framework as non-remitters. Likewise, subjects with missing data for the primary endpoint were considered as non-remitters.

The Mayo score and its variants described above will be derived using the different algorithms (approaches) for calculation of the clinical symptom subscores for stool frequency and rectal bleeding based on the patient reported diary data.

- Approach 1: Draft FDA UC Guidance 2016 approach (primary approach for analysis of Mayo-score-based efficacy endpoints)
- Approach 2: Gemini approach (Sensitivity 1)
- Approach 3: Draft FDA UC Guidance 2022 approach (Sensitivity 2)

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The most secondary efficacy endpoints were analyzed in a similar manner and with a similar estimand framework.

## Pharmacokinetics:

PK data were analyzed using the PKAS, PKAS-M, and PKAS-I analysis sets, depending on the specific analysis. PK analysis sets are defined as follow:

PKAS analysis set Included all subjects who received at least 1 dose of vedolizumab and had measurable drug concentration in serum (referred to as \"blood\" in the protocol, SAP, and tables) to allow for PK evaluation.

PKAS-I analysis set Included all subjects who received at least 1 induction dose with measurable drug concentration in serum to allow for PK evaluation but were not randomized into the maintenance period.

PKAS-M analysis set Included all subjects who received at least 1 maintenance dose with measurable drug concentration in serum to allow for PK evaluation and were randomized into the maintenance period.

Vedolizumab serum concentrations were determined using a validated sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. The method validation report is listed in Appendix 16.1.10.1. The bioanalytical report is provided in Appendix 16.1.10.2

## Immunogenicity

Immunogenicity of vedolizumab was summarized using the SAF, SAF-M, and SAF-I, depending on the specific analysis. AVA and nAVA status were evaluated during the study. In addition, the potential impact of AVA status on vedolizumab PK, efficacy, and safety was explored.

In the overall ITT population, 58 of 121 (47.9%) enrolled subjects reported prior medical conditions. The most frequently reported events (&gt;10%) occurred in the SOCs of infections and infestations (20.7%), gastrointestinal disorders (11.6%), and blood and lymphatic system disorders (10.7%).

The most frequently reported concurrent medical conditions (&gt;10%) occurred in the SOCs of blood and lymphatic system disorders (39.7%), gastrointestinal disorders (17.4%), skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders (17.4%), immune system disorders (10.7%), and metabolism and nutrition disorders (10.7%).

In the overall ITT population, extraintestinal manifestations of UC were uncommon and included the following: arthritis/arthralgia (6 of 121 subjects [5.0%]) and aphthous stomatitis (2 of 121 subjects [1.7%]).

## Prior and concomitant medications

Prior biologic history: overall, 49 of 121 subjects (40.5%) in the ITT population were treated with TNF-α inhibitors, with 47 subjects (38.8%) receiving infliximab. Reasons for discontinuation of biologic treatment included loss of response (24.0%) or inadequate response (16.5%). The primary reasons for intolerance were autoimmunity (n=3) or infusion-related reactions (n=3).

Of the standard medications used for UC, 5-ASAs were the most common concomitant medication and were used by 78.5% of subjects. A total of 46.3% of subjects received corticosteroids acting locally, and 22.3% of subjects received glucocorticoids. A total of 40.5% of subjects received other immunosuppressive medication (AZA [38.0%] and MTX [3.3%]).

- Approach 1: Draft FDA UC Guidance 2016 approach (primary approach for analysis of Mayo-score-based efficacy endpoints)
- Approach 2: Gemini approach (Sensitivity 1)
- Approach 3: Draft FDA UC Guidance 2022 approach (Sensitivity 2)

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

The MAH submitted a final report for the randomized, double-blind, phase 3 study that evaluated the efficacy and safety of vedolizumab IV in pediatric patients with UC (study MLN0002-3024). Data from this study will be used for partial extrapolation of efficacy from adults to pediatric subjects based on the efficacy and PK data from completed studies in adult subjects. The MAH plans to submit the request for paediatric indications for vedolizumab IV in UC and CD in the coming months.

In this study, vedolizumab was administered IV during a 14-week, open-label induction period followed by a 40-week, 2-dose arm, randomized, double-blind maintenance period. The study was designed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, PK, and immunogenicity of high and low doses of vedolizumab IV in 3 different weight groups (≥30 kg; &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg; 10 kg to 15 kg) for maintenance of remission in children aged 2 to 17 years, inclusive. The study design without placebo arm was agreed in the PIP and in line with other recent pediatric development programs in IBD. Subjects must have been diagnosed with moderately to severely active UC (defined by a modified Mayo score of 5 to 9) for at least 1 month before screening and failed response to, lost response to, or been intolerant to at least 1 of the current standard-of-care induction and maintenance therapies for UC. The randomization was stratified by previous exposure/failure to TNF-α antagonists therapy or naive to TNF-α antagonists therapy, and by weight group.

Efficacy: The primary statistical objective was the estimation of the clinical remission at Week 54, by dose group (high dose, low dose). No formal hypothesis testing was planned. The patients with discontinuation of study drug, dose escalation or rescue medication in the maintenance period were considered as non-remitters.

According to the current EMA Guideline for UC, the aim of UC treatment in children should be achieving remission without affecting growth and maturation. Remission should be defined as symptomatic remission accompanied by endoscopic MH. Therefore, the use of modified Mayo score (instead of PUCAI initially considered) which includes endoscopy for the primary endpoint of clinical remission (which includes stool frequency subscore 0 or 1 and decrease ≥1 from baseline, rectal bleeding subscore of 0 and endoscopy subscore 0 to 1) is agreed.

Subjects enrolled under previous protocol versions had not yet reached the Week 14 visit at the time that protocol amendment 2 came into effect. Therefore, the removal of PUCAI from the definition of clinical response at Week 14 did not have an impact on any subjects. There were 7 global protocol amendments in total; however, no relevant impact on study integrity can be expected.

Initially, a very high number of significant protocol deviations were reported in both ITT and ITT-M populations. Most protocol deviations that remained classified as significant after the post hoc analysis were related to concomitant medication. The MAH should discuss in the upcoming type II variation the impact of these concomitant medications on the efficacy results across dose and weight subgroups.

Mayo scores were derived using 3 different algorithms (approaches) to calculate the clinical symptom subscores for stool frequency and rectal bleeding based on data from subject-reported diary entries.

In the induction phase of the study 120 subjects were enrolled (90 subjects ≥30 kg, 27 subjects &gt;15 to &lt;30 kg and only 3 subjects 10-15 kg), as initially planned. Although the planned number of enrolled subjects &lt;30kg was reached, most of them were &gt;15 kg. Moreover, in the 10-15 kg group the subject with the lowest weight at the baseline had 14.5 kg weight. Therefore, no data is available regarding the subjects weighing 10-14 kg.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The subjects randomised in the maintenance phase to high/low (1:1) dose group by weight subgroups were 74, 18 and only 1 subject (high dose) in three weight subgroups, respectively. Although from the provided results seems that 2 out of 3 subjects enrolled in 10-15 kg subgroup had achieved clinical response at week 14, only 1 of them entered the maintenance phase. The MAH should clarify this point in the upcoming variation.

The age distribution within the study population (median of 13 years, range 2-17y) generally reflects the known epidemiology of pediatric UC. At baseline almost all patients had moderate (54.5%) or severe (41.3%, almost all in the highest weight group) UC based on complete Mayo score; one of 2 patients with mild UC (both in the ≥30 kg group) was included in the maintenance phase (low dose group). Majority (82%) had disease duration &lt;3 years at baseline. However, all patients with longer disease duration (≥3 years) were in the highest weight group, as attended. The low dose and high dose groups in the randomized phase were overall balanced for baseline disease severity, regardless of the score used. All three subjects of the 10-15 kg subgroup had pancolitis (the most extended form of UC), while it was present in about half of the patients in other two weight subgroups. About 60% of enrolled subjects were anti-TNF naïve and about 40% anti-TNF exposed/failed.

The primary efficacy endpoint of clinical remission at Week 54 based on the modified Mayo score for the ITT-M analysis set showed the clinical benefit of vedolizumab IV in this pediatric population. This benefit was observed in both the low and high dose groups: 51.1% (CI 36.1, 65.9) and 43.5% (CI 28.9, 58.9), respectively. In the overall PPS-M analysis set, the adjusted treatment difference between the high and low dose groups based on the CMH method was rather in favor of the high dose. Based on the results of Study C13006 (GEMINI 1), the priori assumption about efficacy in the pediatric population was approximately 42%. This assumption was met in Study 3024 based on the primary endpoint.

In the weight subgroup ≥30 kg the clinical remission at week 54 was achieved in 50% of the lowdose treated subjects and in the 41.7% of the high-dose group. In the subgroup &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg the clinical remission was achieved in 55.6% patients irrespectively of the dose subgroups (containing only 9 subjects each). The only patient of the 10 kg to 15 kg subgroup that entered the maintenance phase (high dose) did not achieve the clinical remission.

Clinical benefit was observed in both TNF-naive and TNF-exposed/failed subgroups following maintenance therapy with vedolizumab IV. The subgroup of TNF-naive subjects had numerically higher rates of clinical remission (50%) than the TNF-exposed/failed subgroup (43.2%), which is consistent with observations in adult studies. The clinical remission rate of the low dose group was numerically higher than that of the high dose group in TNF-naive subjects (62.1% and 36.4%, respectively), and the rate of the high dose group was numerically higher in TNF-exposed/failed subjects (33.3% low dose vs 52.6% high dose). These observations for the subgroups should be interpreted with caution, however, due to the small sample sizes in those subgroups.

Overall clinical remission at Week 54 was generally consistent across subgroups defined by other demographic and disease characteristics. However, the MAH should provide in the upcoming variation the results of primary and main secondary endpoints for the subgroups based on UC severity (moderate vs severe UC). Moreover, the impact of 2 patients with mild UC enrolled should be explored with a sensitivity analysis.

Throughout the maintenance period, both low and high dose groups showed clinical benefit across secondary endpoints, including endoscopic response at Week 54, clinical remission at Week 54 based on complete Mayo score, sustained clinical remission, sustained clinical response, sustained endoscopic remission, and corticosteroid-free clinical remission at Week 54. However, the results of low dose group across primary and most secondary endpoints tend to be better or at least comparable to the results for high dose group. In the overall ITT-M population with baseline corticosteroid use, 56.5% achieved corticosteroid-free clinical remission at Week 54 in the low dose group and only 27.8% in the high dose group. The endoscopic response at Week 54 in ITT-M was achieved in the 59.6% of subjects in the low dose group and 47.8% in the high dose group. The MAH should discuss and justify in the upcoming variation this apparent lack of dose response proportionality in pediatric patients with UC (see also PK discussion below).

Results for Week 14 secondary efficacy endpoints, including clinical remission and clinical response based on the modified Mayo score, show positive trends of early clinical benefit of vedolizumab IV in this population. In the three weight groups (≥30 kg; &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg; 10 kg to 15 kg) at Week 14 the clinical response was obtained in 74.7%, 74.1% and 66.7% respectively, while the clinical remission was achieved in 31.9%, 44.4% and 33.3%, respectively. These results could not be easily compared to the adults GEMINI study due to the different time point (Week 6) and endpoint definition (based on the complete Mayo score) applied in the adult study.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The analysis of the effects of vedolizumab on the endpoints of physical development showed positive and progressively increasing changes in the z-scores in both low and high dose groups. However, the MAH should provide and discuss in the upcoming variation the change from baseline in z scores for each weight subgroup (≥30 kg; &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg; 10 kg to 15 kg).

Positive changes from baseline in IMPACT-III total scores (health related QoL scores), as well as in individual domain scores, were observed through Week 54. These improvements were present across both low and high dose treatment groups and were similar across weight categories.

There was no apparent relationship between AVA status and clinical remission, although among subjects who did not achieve clinical remission at Week 54, 2 out of 49 (4.1%) tested positive for AVA (2 transiently positive), and 1 of them developed neutralizing antibodies.

Overall, the treatment with vedolizumab showed clinical benefit in the pediatric population 2-17 years of age with moderate to severe UC across different dose and weight subgroups. However, no or very limited data is available for the lowest weight subgroup (10-15 kg) included. Moreover, no clear conclusion could be drawn from the clinical data regarding the posology for each weight group studied. Both uncertainties should be thoroughly addressed in the upcoming variation.

Pharmacokinetics: Evaluation of Vedolizumab's PK in pediatric subjects with moderately to severely active UC after IV administration by measuring serum through concentrations of vedolizumab over time was one of the secondary endpoints for study MLN0002-3024. Moreover, the relationship between vedolizumab exposure and the clinical response in pediatric subjects treated with vedolizumab IV during both induction and maintenance therapy was explored as Exploratory Objectives.

Serum concentrations of vedolizumab in the PKAS-M during the maintenance period showed separation over time between low and high dose, starting from week 30 and up to the end of the study week 54, (Table 15.2.5.1.1), however this is clear when data are shown by All Weight Groups Combined and also where data represented for the &gt;30 kg weight group, while this separation between the two doses in less evident for the weight group &gt;15 &lt;30 kg and no data are available for the lowest weight group (1015 kg). This hampers a clear conclusion regarding a similar trend within each weight group. The MAH should present this data

In figure 4 of the CSR, Measured Serum Concentrations of Vedolizumab by Visit (All Weight Groups Overlaid) were reported in a Semilogarithmic Scale (PKAS-I and PKAS-M). Although it seems that Serum concentrations of vedolizumab between the weight groups were comparable overall, the figure is very difficult to read.

Compared with clinical non-responders, clinical responders had overall higher serum trough concentrations (approximately 1.7-fold higher based on geometric means) at Week 14. Analysis by weight group showed this consistent trend also in the &gt;15 kg to &lt;30 kg and ≥30 kg weight group, while limited and not interpretable data are available for the 10 to 15 kg weight group.

However, analyzing the same data within' the weight groups but looking at the results based on the dose level at which patients were randomized (high dose or low dose) the data do not show a unique trend, but different response rates within' the same exposure quartile depending on whether patients were treated with low or high dose level (Table 15.2.5.2.1). The MAH states that no clinically significant mean changes from baseline at any time point were observed for any chemistry, hematology and hepatic parameter. Markedly abnormal clinical laboratory results with regard to chemistry were overall observed in few subjects while reduced Hemoglobin and elevated leukocytes and platelets were reported respectively in 16.7%, 11.7% and 13.3% of subjects.

Laboratory abnormalities are currently not reported in the SmPC; therefore the MAH should present in the next Type II variation a critical discussion about the relatedness of such laboratory abnormalities with study drug and the appropriateness to be included in 4.8 section of the SmPC.

The MAH states that there was no relationship between AVA status and infusion-related reactions as well as hypersensitivity reactions. However, the very limited number of subjects with AVA positivity does not allow at present to draw firm conclusions on potential relationship of safety with AVA status.

In conclusion, the safety profile of vedolizumab IV in paediatric subjects from the age of 2 years with moderately to severely active UC, seems to be similar to that already known in adult authorized indications, with the most common reported AEs being upper respiratory tract infection, colitis ulcerative (disease worsening) and pyrexia. However, in the upcoming type II variation some insight regarding laboratory abnormalities as well as AEs, will be required to be addressed, in order to better understand if there are differences in type and frequency of ADRs, compared to those of adults, to be reflected in the SmPC. Moreover, a more in-depth discussion on differences between low and high dose groups should be provided.

No further action required, however further data are expected in the context of a variation prior any conclusion on product information amendments is made. The MAH committed to submit this variation application by 08/04/2026.

The main issues to be addressed in the type II variation related to safety are the following:

-The MAH should ensure that 4.8 section of the SmPC is updated with all ADRs observed in paediatric population which are currently not yet reflected in the SmPC, including those in the AESIs of hypersensitivity reactions (e.g. peripheral swelling, rash purpura and eosinophilia) and serious infections (e.g. appendicitis and peritonitis). Moreover, serious AEs such as peripheral swelling should be described under the table.

-The MAH should present in the next Type II variation a critical discussion about the relatedness of laboratory abnormalities with study drug and the appropriateness to be included in 4.8 section of the SmPC.

-Th MAH should provide a more in-depth discussion on differences in safety profile between low and high dose groups
