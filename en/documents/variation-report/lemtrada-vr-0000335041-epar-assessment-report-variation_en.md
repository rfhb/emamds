## 2. Overall conclusion and impact on the benefit/risk balance

A grouped application consisting of: C.4: Update of section 4.4 of the SmPC in order to add a new warning on vasculitis based on data from clinical studies and post-authorisation data sources and an Update of section 5.1 in order to update information on paediatrics based on final results from study EFC13429 (LemKids) listed as a category 3 study in the RMP. The RMP version 14.0 has also been submitted.

Pediatric Study EFC13429 (LemKids) was designed to assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of alemtuzumab in pediatric patients from 10 to less than 18 years of age with RRMS with disease activity on prior DMT. The evaluation of efficacy and safety in the paediatric population was initiated, but prematurely closed following safety issues identified in an adult study leading to an EMA Article-20 Pharmacovigilance review of alemtuzumab in adult RRMS. Eleven children and adolescents received treatment with alemtuzumab of whom seven participants received the full treatment.

Additional safety-data was provided from 11 children and adolescents who received treatment with alemtuzumab in the LemKids study. Additional information regarding paediatric data in the SPC is supported.

In addition, data was provided on safety issues regarding vasculitis in the general MS population, vasculitis cases from the clinical trials and vasculitis cases Sanofi PV database. Addition of vasculitis in the SPC section 4.4 is supported.

The proposed changes to the RMP are acceptable.

## 5. Introduction

While multiple sclerosis (MS) is typically considered to affect young adults, 2.7% to 10.5% of patients living with this disease are paediatric. Overall, 97% to 99% of pediatric patients living with MS have relapsing remitting phenotype (RRMS) versus 85% to 95% in adults. Early prevention of relapses in the initial years of clinical onset can significantly reduce worsening of disability.

Pediatric Study EFC13429 (LemKids) was aimed at assessing the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of alemtuzumab in pediatric patients from 10 to less than 18 years of age with RRMS with disease activity on prior disease-modifying treatment (DMT). Alemtuzumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting CD52, a cell surface antigen highly expressed on B and T lymphocytes, and at lower levels on natural killer cells, monocytes and macrophages. Alemtuzumab acts through antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity, and complement-dependent cytotoxicity following T and B lymphocyte cell surface binding. It induces rapid and sustained lymphocyte depletion and shows potent efficacy in both treatment-naïve and treatment-refractory adult patients with RRMS

## 6. Clinical Efficacy aspects

## 6.1. Methods

Pediatric Study EFC13429 (LemKids) was aimed at assessing the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of alemtuzumab in pediatric patients from 10 to less than 18 years of age with RRMS with disease activity on prior DMT. Study EFC13429 was an open-label, rater-blinded, single-arm, before and after switch study of efficacy, safety, and tolerability of alemtuzumab in pediatric participants with RRMS.

This study was conducted in Italy, the Russian Federation, the United Kingdom, France, Poland, and Türkiye from 23 October 2017 (first participant screened) to 08 September 2025 (LPLV). We report here the summary of efficacy for Study EFC13429.

The Sponsor terminated Study EFC13429 prematurely (LPLV on 08 September 2025) due to low recruitment and an EMA Article-20 Pharmacovigilance review of Alemtuzumab in adult RRMS. Recruiting new participants in accordance with the PIP ceased in April 2019.

Among the 16 participants enrolled, 12 participants completed the prior DMT phase (Period 1) and 11 participants were exposed to alemtuzumab. Of these 11 participants, 7 participants completed the alemtuzumab treatment phase (from alemtuzumab first dose at Month 0 to Month 24), 4 participants did not complete this treatment phase, including 1 participant discontinuing due to lack of efficacy and 3 participants for other reasons (1 withdrew consent due to poor treatment tolerance and 2 did not complete treatment due to health authority decision)11 patients were included in the efficacy analysis.

## Exposure:

It was planned to administer 2 courses of alemtuzumab: 5 infusions in 5 consecutive days during the first course and 3 infusions in 3 consecutive days during the second course, 12 months after the first course. Dose selection was based on body weight: 12 mg/day for participants ≥50 kg, and 0.24 mg/kg/day for participants &lt;50 kg.

## Endpoints:

The primary efficacy endpoint was the number of new or enlarging T2 lesions on brain MRI during Period 1 (continuation of prior DMT) compared to Period 2 (Month 4 through Month 8 after the first course of alemtuzumab treatment).

## Background

Vasculitis is a broad, non-specific medical term that encompasses wide conditions that involve wide range of pathologies in blood vessels. In fact, vasculitis is not used as a standalone medical diagnosis in everyday clinical practice but rather is used to describe pathology, defining inflammation of blood vessels. Vasculitis is more of a reaction pattern rather than a specific disease entity. Therefore, the clinical presentation of vasculitis (most commonly palpable purpura on the lower extremities) dictates a thorough history, review of systems, and a meticulous physical examination.

The clinical presentation varies according to the histologic type of inflammation, the size of the involved blood vessel segment, and the distribution of the involved vessels.

- 1) Large-vessel vasculitides characterized by claudication, absent peripheral pulses, blood pressure abnormalities, strokes, and features of internal organ ischemia.
- 2) Medium-vessel vasculitides presented by prolonged fever, mucocutaneous changes and lymphadenopathy.
- 3) Small-vessel vasculitides presented by palpable purpura, abdominal pain (which may be associated with gastrointestinal hemorrhage and/or intussusception), arthritis, and evidence of glomerulonephritis (hematuria, proteinuria). Cutaneous small vessel vasculitis (CSVV) is defined as a single-organ, skin isolated small vessel vasculitis or angiitis, often leukocytoclastic (LCV), without systemic vasculitis or glomerulonephritis. Other terms that have been used interchangeably with CSVV but should be considered separately are Leukocytoclastic vasculitis, Hypersensitivity vasculitis, Serum sickness and Immune complex small vessel vasculitis.

The MAH proposes an update of the safety specification to specifically add vasculitis to the important potential risk \"Other autoimmune disorders\". No changes to the additional PhV activities are proposed. While other autoimmune disorders are investigated in the ongoing category 3 PASS OBS13434, vasculitis is not a variable according to the latest study protocol. The PRAC Rapporteur does not consider any changes to the study protocol are warranted. Additional risk minimisation measures do not address \"Other autoimmune disorders\", and inclusion of vasculitis does not warrant any changes.
