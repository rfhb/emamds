## 1. Introduction

On 19 January 2026, the MAH submitted a completed study (Study 208505) for Nucala, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

These data are not submitted as part of specific EU obligations and Study 208505 is not part of any Paediatric Investigation Plan. No amendments to the Product Information are foreseen by the MAH as a result of the conclusions from the study.

Study 208505 was initiated as a post-marketing requirement in Japan, for Nucala solution for subcutaneous (s.c.) injection 100 mg (mepolizumab), following its approval on May 25, 2018. The approval condition for this study was lifted on May 14, 2024, and the study concluded (analysis complete) July 30, 2025.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

To note, as part of the EMA recommendation for approval of the additional indication granted in September 2021 ' as an add-on treatment for patients aged 6 years and older with relapsing-remitting or refractory EGPA ', the CHMP requested that the company perform a post approval safety study (PASS) to collect additional data on the use of Nucala (mepolizumab) in children aged 6-17 years with EGPA. Study 218065, a PASS to describe real-world safety and effectiveness of Nucala (mepolizumab) in paediatric EGPA patients in Europe, is ongoing at the time of this submission and therefore is not discussed.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that Study 208505: NUCALA Subcutaneous Injection Special Drug Use Investigation (EGPA, long-term) is a standalone study.

Mepolizumab (Nucala) is a humanized mAb (IgG1, Kappa) produced by recombinant DNA technology in Chinese hamster ovary cell, targeted against human IL-5, which binds to IL-5 receptors expressed on the eosinophil cell surface, and thus inhibits signalling, resulting in the reduction in the production and survival of eosinophils.

It is approved for severe asthma with an eosinophilic phenotype in the US, the EU and over 20 other countries, as well as for other eosinophilic indications, including eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA), hypereosinophilic syndrome, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Mepolizumab was initially developed and approved as a lyophilized drug product. A liquid drug product presented within an autoinjector (pre-filled pen) or a safety syringe was subsequently developed and approved for use in the registered indications.

Mepolizumab received centralised marketing authorisation in the European Union in September 2015 as an add-on treatment for severe refractory eosinophilic asthma in adult patients. Additional indications were added on the basis of clinical trial evidence, including EGPA - which was authorised in September 2021 as ' an add-on treatment for patients aged 6 years and older with relapsing- remitting or refractory EGPA' .

Study MEA115921 was the pivotal study submitted to the EMA in support of the indication in patients with EGPA. Study MEA115921 was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 52-week study which evaluated 136 adult patients with EGPA, who had a history of relapsing or refractory disease, and who were on stable oral corticosteroid therapy (OCS; ≥7.5 to ≤50 mg/day prednisolone/ prednisone), with or without stable immunosuppressant therapy (excluding cyclophosphamide).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

As part of its recommendation, the Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) requested that the company perform a post approval safety study (PASS) to collect additional data on the use of Nucala (mepolizumab) in children aged 6-17 years with EGPA (Study 218065).

In Europe, Nucala is administered as a subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks and is available as a 100 mg solution for injection in pre-filled pen or safety syringe, or as a lyophilised 100 mg powder for reconstitution.

The recommended dose in EGPA is dependent on age and weight:

- Adult and Adolescents aged ≥12 years: 300 mg (3 × 100 mg)
- Children aged 6 to 11 years ≥40 kg: 200 mg (2 × 100 mg)
- Children aged 6 to 11 years &lt;40 kg: 100 mg (1 × 100 mg)

## Context of Study 208505 :

This study, NUCALA Subcutaneous Injection Special Drug Use Investigation (EGPA, long-term) (Protocol No. 208505), was initiated as a post-marketing requirement in Japan for Nucala solution for subcutaneous (sc) injection 100 mg (mepolizumab), following its approval on May 25, 2018.

The rationale for this special drug use investigation DUI stemmed from an approval condition for the EGPA indication stipulated by the PMDA. Due to the limited number of Japanese participants in the clinical study, a DUI was mandated to collect and evaluate long-term safety and effectiveness data in all patients after market-launch. This was intended to accumulate sufficient data, ensure the proper use of the drug, and understand the characteristics of patients receiving Nucala for EGPA in real-world clinical practice.

Study 208505 is submitted in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended. Of the 1149 participants who were included in the safety analysis, the mean age was 58.9 ± 14.9 years (ranging from 14 to 92 years old) and there were 2 participants &lt; 15 years old.

## 2.3.2. Clinical study

## Study 208505: NUCALA Subcutaneous Injection Special Drug Use Investigation (EGPA, long-term)

## Description

Study 208505, was a post-marketing, all-case observational study in Japan. As such, it was conducted in daily clinical practice and did not involve blinding or comparator/control arms.

The observation period was defined as up to 96 weeks (2 years) from the start of Nucala administration for EGPA or until withdrawal/termination of Nucala treatment.

## Methods

## Study participants

The target population comprised all patients in Japan administered Nucala for the treatment of EGPA after this indication received approval, from May 25, 2018 to up to 3 months after termination of the observation period in participants eligible for case report form (CRF) collection (period of the follow-up investigation, if conducted) or the date of lifting of the approval condition, whichever comes later. This included patients who had already received Nucala for EGPA prior to conclusion of the institution contract and those who had been registered to a special DUI (Protocol No.204524) for bronchial asthma but had a change in treatment purpose from bronchial asthma to EGPA during the observation period.

## Treatments

- Nucala solution for s.c. injection 100 mg
- Nucala solution for s.c. injection 100 mg Syringe
- Nucala solution for s.c. injection 100 mg Pen

The study period commenced from May 25, 2018 at which point Nucala solution for s.c. injection 100 mg was authorised.

On May 27, 2020, Nucala solution for s.c. injection 100 mg Pen and Nucala solution for s.c. injection 100 mg Syringe were launched in addition to Nucala solution for s.c. injection 100 mg.

Thereafter, all patients who received any of these three Nucala brand products (hereinafter collectively referred to as 'Nucala') for the treatment of EGPA were included in this study.

## Observation period:

The observation period per participant is up to 96 weeks (2 years) from the start of Nucala administration for EGPA at a maximum. If a participant has withdrawn from/terminated administration of Nucala, it will be until the withdrawal/termination. Additionally, to consider the safety and effectiveness of Nucala administration in participants who had withdrawn from/terminated due to 'symptom improvement,' the 48-week (1-year) follow-up investigation should be conducted as much as possible.

## Objectives

The objective of this study is to collect and assess information on the safety and effectiveness of the long-term use of Nucala in daily clinical practice in patients with EGPA.

## Factors That Potentially Affect Effectiveness

In order to investigate factors that potentially affect effectiveness, a multivariate analysis was performed to detect factors in which odds ratios for effectiveness by participant characteristics met the criterion \"point estimates exceeding 2 or less than 0.5, and asymptotic 95% CIs not crossing 1.\" A univariate analysis was also performed. The analysis results revealed that 'age,' \"a comorbidity/past medical history (bronchial asthma or allergic rhinitis),\" \"concomitant therapy (surgery) for EGPA\" and 'baseline CRP (at the start of Nucala treatment' met the criterion.

The multivariate analysis was performed in consideration of correlations in each item of participant characteristics (to determine which factor was to be included in the model if the correlation was &gt;0.7) and in consideration of distribution biases within each factor (in the case of some categories being &lt;5%, the categories were combined; in the case of a category being binary, the factor was excluded from the model). The following items were selected as explanatory variables: 'gender,' 'age classification 2,' 'body weight,' 'the primary disease (disease duration),' 'severity classification 2,' 'hospitalization due to EGPA in the 48 weeks before the start of Nucala treatment,' 'a history of Nucala use before the start of Nucala treatment for EGPA,' 'comorbidity (bronchial asthma),' 'comorbidity (cardiovascular lesion),' 'the mean daily dose of OCS,' 'baseline blood eosinophil count (at the start of Nucala treatment),' 'baseline CRP (at the start of Nucala treatment),'and 'baseline clinical symptoms at the start of Nucala treatment.'

There were factors meeting the criterion in the variables of \"hospitalization due to EGPA in the 48 weeks before the start of Nucala treatment,\" \"comorbidity (cardiovascular lesion),\" \"baseline blood eosinophil count (at the start of Nucala treatment),\" and \"baseline CRP (at the start of Nucala treatment).\" These factors were investigated, and the results showed that the responder rates exceeded 90% in all subgroups, revealing no participant populations requiring special caution.

## Recruitment

A total of 4115 participants were enrolled across 863 sites. Case report forms (CRFs) were collected for 1169 participants. The safety analysis set comprised 1149 participants, and the effectiveness analysis set included 1098 participants after exclusions.

## Baseline data

The safety analysis set were predominantly female (59.2%, 680/1149 participants). The mean age was 58.9 ± 14.9 years (ranging from 14 to 92 years old) and there were 2 participants &lt; 15 years old. The mean duration of EGPA was 3.80 ± 4.49 years, with 61.2% (703/1149) classified as \"grade 3\" severity. A high proportion of participants had comorbidities (95.3%), with bronchial asthma being the most common (82.8%). Nucala was concomitantly used with oral corticosteroids (OCS) in 96.8% of participants and with immunosuppressants in 37.8%, including cyclophosphamide in 5.0%. The proportions of participants by participant characteristics among the 1098 participants included in the effectiveness analysis were generally similar to those in the safety analysis set.

## Number analysed

The safety analysis set comprised 1149 participants, and the effectiveness analysis set included 1098 participants after exclusions.

## Efficacy results

## Global Assessment of Effectiveness

Regarding the global assessment of effectiveness, the investigators assessed effectiveness by either 'effective,' 'not effective' or 'indeterminable' at Weeks 12, 48, and 96 after the start of Nucala administration, or at the time of withdrawal/termination, based on the course of subjective symptoms, course of clinical symptoms, etc. from the start date of administration to the end of the observation period.

Participants whose final assessment was \"effective\" were tabulated as responders, and participants whose final assessment was \"not effective\" were tabulated as nonresponders.

Among the 1098 participants in the effectiveness analysis set, the proportion of responders (the responder rate) was 94.9% (1042/1098 participants).

## Clinical Symptoms

In all 9 organ systems (general symptoms; cutaneous lesions; mucosal/eye lesions; ear, nose and throat (ENT) lesions; chest lesions; cardiovascular lesions; abdominal lesions; renal lesions; nervous system lesions), increases from baseline (at the start of Nucala treatment) were observed in the proportion of participants who had no clinical symptoms at Week 96 of treatment.

In the sections presented below, \"the absence of clinical symptoms,\" \"a state close to a Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score (BVAS) of 0,\" and \"participants in a state similar to remission\" are discussed.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

As per the protocol of this study, \"remission\" was defined as the absence of all clinical symptoms, which was different from the definition of remission (a BVAS of 0 and an OCS dose of ≤4 mg/day) in the pivotal Study MEA115921. Therefore, in this post-marketing study report, the absence of all clinical symptoms is described as \"the absence of symptoms,\" and \"a state close to a BVAS of 0\" and \"participants in a state similar to remission\" were separately defined and investigated. Of the effectiveness analysis population, 1065 participants were included in the assessment of clinical symptoms, excluding 33 participants whose data assessable on clinical symptoms were not available.

## EGPA Relapse

- (2) immunosuppressant administration newly started or an immunosuppressant dose increased,
- (3) hospitalization for the treatment of EGPA.

Of the 1098 participants in the effectiveness analysis set, 1065 participants were included in the assessment of EGPA relapse, excluding 33 participants whose data assessable on clinical symptoms were not available. ## Adverse Drug Reactions

The proportion of participants in whom adverse drug reactions (ADRs) occurred (the incidence rate of ADRs) among the 1149 participants in the safety analysis set was 6.5% (75/1149 participants).

The most common ADRs by System Organ Class (SOC) of MedDRA were 'General disorders and administration site conditions' with an incidence of 1.6% (18/1149 participants), followed by 'Infections and infestations' with an incidence of 1.4% (16/1149) and 'Skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders' with an incidence of 1.1% (13/1149).

The most frequently reported ADRs were as follows (in descending order of frequency): 'malaise' 0.6% (7/1149 participants), 'herpes zoster' 0.5% (6/1149), 'headache' 0.3% (4/1149), and 'EGPA' 0.3% (4/1149).

Serious ADRs were reported in 24 participants; the incidence rate was 2.1% (24/1149 participants). The reported serious ADRs were 'EGPA' in 4 participants, 'osteonecrosis' in 3 participants, and the other events in one participant each.

ADRs which resulted in the outcome 'fatal' were reported in a total of 2 participants: 'death' (PT) in one participant and \"cerebral haemorrhage\" (PT) in one participant. In the event reported as 'death'

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

(PT), a female patient had asthma, epilepsy, hydrocephalus, and hypertension as comorbidities. The attending physician reported that \"the only information reported to the physician regarding the participant's death was sudden death.\" Suspected factors other than Nucala, which might have been associated with the participant's death included heart disease due to hypertension and cerebrovascular disease, and any relationship with Nucala was determined to be unknown. Another event, \"cerebral haemorrhage,\" occurred as an ADR in a female patient with some pre-existing conditions including old cerebrovascular lesions prior to Nucala administration. According to the attending physician's report, blood tests showed no problems in blood coagulability, and platelet counts were within the normal range; however, the participant had been receiving long-term administration of anticoagulants, and it was impossible to determine whether EGPA-associated vasculitis and the administered drugs affected the pathology. Both events in these two participants were considered attributable to factors associated with baseline characteristics of the participants and did not suggest any positive relationship with Nucala.

No ADRs were reported in 20 participants excluded from the safety analysis.

In a global phase III study (MEA115921) conducted up to the time of approval, the incidence rate of ADRs was 51% (35/68 participants) in the mepolizumab group. The most common ADRs by SOC were 'General disorders and administration site conditions' 24% (16/68 participants), 'Infections and infestations' 13% (9/68), 'Nervous system disorders' 9% (6/68), and 'Skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders' 9% (6/68). The most frequently reported ADRs by event type (by PT) were \"injection site reaction\" 10% (7/68 participants), followed by \"headache\" 7% (5/68), \"asthenia\" 6% (4/68), and \"respiratory tract infection\" 6% (4/68). The incidence rate of serious ADRs was 4% (3/68 participants). The serious ADRs were classified as 'urinary tract infection' 1% (1/68 participants), 'abdominal pain' 1% (1/68), and 'hypersensitivity' 1% (1/68).

Although this post-marketing study cannot be compared directly with Study MEA115921 due to differences in participant characteristics and assessment methods, the incidence rate of ADRs was lower in this study than in Study MEA115921. There were no major differences in the most frequently reported ADRs by SOC, and the types of events, except for \"EGPA,\" were also observed in Study MEA115921. Since 'EGPA' is one of the indications for Nucala, this event is discussed in under Effectiveness.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

## Effectiveness

Among the 1098 participants in the effectiveness analysis set, the responder rate was 94.9% (1042/1098 participants). The responder rate was defined as the proportion of participants whose final global assessment of effectiveness was rated as 'effective.' Of the effectiveness analysis population, 1065 participants were included in the assessment of clinical symptoms, excluding 33 participants whose data assessable on clinical symptoms were not available. The proportion of participants with no clinical symptoms was 11.9% (122/1027 participants) at the start of Nucala treatment (at baseline), thereafter increasing to 29.1% (235/807 participants) at Week 48 and to 32.8% (224/682 participants) at Week 96. EGPA relapse was observed in 54 (5.1%) of the 1065 participants, and the time (mean ± SD) to the first relapse was 119.1 ± 145.0 days. EGPA relapse was observed in 5 of the 18 participants included in the follow-up analysis during the follow-up investigation. (follow-up period: 48.7 to 253.9 weeks). The median (mean) daily dose of OCS was 10.0 mg/day at baseline, thereafter, decreasing to 7.0 mg/day at Week 12, to 5.0 mg/day at Week 48, and to 3.0 mg/day at Week 96.

The MAH compared results to pivotal phase III, Study MEA115921. Differences in baseline participant conditions and assessment methods were noted between the studies, including differing definitions for endpoints (remission, BVAS of 0). In addition, the primary endpoint in Study 208505 of 'global assessment of effectiveness' was not clearly defined or similar to the pivotal primary endpoints. Although post-marketing Study 208505, cannot be compared directly with Study MEA115921, the results did not significantly differ from those of Study MEA115921, indicating a certain level of effectiveness.

Data on paediatric use were collected from 2 paediatric participants (under 15 years of age) in the effectiveness analysis set, and the responder rate among these paediatric participants was 100.0% (2/2 participants).

## Safety

Among the 1149 participants in the safety analysis set, 6.5% experienced adverse drug reactions (ADRs) (75/1149), the most frequent being malaise (0.6%), herpes zoster (0.5%), headache (0.3%) and EGPA (0.3%); serious ADRs occurred in 2.1% (24/1,149)-notably EGPA (4 participants) and osteonecrosis (3 participants), the remaining 17 events occurred with a frequency of 1 event per participant. ADRs which resulted in the outcome 'fatal' were reported in a total of 2 participants: 'death' preferred term (PT) in one participant and \"cerebral haemorrhage\" (PT) in one participant. The incidence rate of ADRs in this study was lower than the 51% (35/68 participants) observed in the global phase III pivotal study MEA115921, conducted up to the time of approval, and there were no major differences in the most common ADRs, indicating no new concerns.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In this study, hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis, which is an important identified risk, and infections and malignant tumours, which are important potential risks, were investigated as safety specifications. The incidence rate of ADRs related to hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis was 0.7% (8/1149 participants), and the incidence rate of serious ADRs related to hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis was 0.1% (1/1149 participants). The incidence rate of ADRs related to infections was 1.4% (16/1149 participants), and the incidence rate of serious ADRs related to infections was 0.6% (7/1149 participants). No malignant tumours-related ADRs were reported.

The MAH considered safety updates to the product information unnecessary as the incidence rates of hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis and of infections were lower in this postmarketing study than in Study MEA115921 conducted up to the time of approval. In addition, anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity are mentioned in section 4.8 of the Nucala SmPC, and precautions concerning anaphylaxis and other related symptoms and specific infections are provided in the package leaflet.

Sub-group analysis was carried out within the special populations of participants requiring high-dose OCS, participants co-administered with cyclophosphamide and by disease severity. The incidence rate of ADRs in participants who were co-administered with Nucala, and cyclophosphamide was higher than, but not significantly different from, 6.5% (75/1149 participants) in the overall population. The events observed did not show any characteristic trend compared with ADRs in participants not receiving cyclophosphamide concomitantly with Nucala. Thus, the MAH considered it unnecessary to provide further precautions in the product information. To note, in the pivotal Study MEA115921, subjects on cyclophosphamide were excluded, this detail is included in Section 5.1.

In the safety analysis set, 2 participants were reported to have been aged under 15 years at baseline, and no ADRs were reported in either paediatric participant.

Based on Study 208505 findings, no new concerns regarding the effectiveness or safety of Nucala in this indication arose.

The MAH has reviewed the results of mepolizumab obtained in Study 208505 and has concluded that they are in line with the approved product information for mepolizumab in the European Union (EU). Therefore, the MAH has recommended no changes to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for mepolizumab are considered necessary.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

Study 208505: NUCALA Subcutaneous Injection Special Drug Use Investigation (EGPA, long-term), was a post-marketing, all-case observational study in Japan. As such, it was conducted in daily clinical practice and did not involve blinding or comparator/control arms.

The rationale for this special drug use investigation (DUI) stemmed from an approval condition for the EGPA indication stipulated by the PMDA, due to the limited number of Japanese participants in the phase III clinical study.

Study 208505 has been submitted in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended. Of the 1149 participants who were included in the safety analysis, the mean age was 58.9 ± 14.9 years (ranging from 14 to 92 years old) and there were 2 participants &lt; 15 years old.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The MAH has proposed no product information (PI) updates as a result of Study 208505 efficacy and safety outcomes. This is agreed. The study has been carried out in the Japanese population to obtain further data in relation to the EGPA authorisation in this jurisdiction. Aside from the different population, the study is an observational design with no blinding or comparator/control arms. Efficacy endpoints cannot be compared directly with pivotal data due to differences in baseline participant conditions and assessment methods, however indirect comparison indicated a certain level of effectiveness in Study 208505. No major differences in the incidence of the most common ADRs and potential risks were identified and no new safety concerns were reported.

While the data is consistent with the known efficacy and safety profile of Nucala, it is not considered statistically compelling, to warrant further updates to the PI.

The number of paediatric participants is minimal and given that no additional signals were generated, it is agreed that no update to the PI is warranted in terms of paediatric data reported in Study 208505.
