## Overview

Abiraterone Mylan is a cancer medicine used to treat men with metastatic prostate cancer. This is a cancer that affects the prostate gland (a gland of the male reproductive system).

Abiraterone Mylan is used together with prednisone or prednisolone (anti-inflammatory medicines):

- when the cancer is newly diagnosed, metastatic (has spread to other parts of the body) and sensitive to hormones; Abiraterone Mylan is then used in combination with a type of hormone treatment called androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) with or without docetaxel;
- when the cancer is newly diagnosed, at high risk of getting worse, non-metastatic and sensitive to hormones; Abiraterone Mylan is then used in combination with ADT and radiotherapy (treatment with radiation to destroy cancer cells);
- when the cancer is metastatic and medical castration (using medicines to stop the production of male hormones) with ADT has not worked or no longer works in men who have either no symptoms or only mild symptoms of the disease, and who do not yet need chemotherapy (cancer medicines);
- when the cancer is metastatic and medical or surgical castration and chemotherapy containing docetaxel have not worked or no longer work.

Abiraterone Mylan contains the active substance abiraterone acetate and is a 'generic medicine'. This means that Abiraterone Mylan contains the same active substance and works in the same way as a 'reference medicine' already authorised in the EU called Zytiga.

### How is Abiraterone Mylan used?

Abiraterone Mylan can only be obtained with a prescription. It is available as tablets to be taken by mouth once a day.

Treatment is recommended until the cancer gets worse or unacceptable side effects occur, with a maximum duration of two years for patients with newly diagnosed, high-risk, non-metastatic prostate cancer that is sensitive to hormones.

If the patient develops liver problems, treatment should be stopped. Treatment may be resumed at a reduced dose if the liver function returns to normal.

For more information about using Abiraterone Mylan, see the package leaflet or contact your doctor or pharmacist.

### How does Abiraterone Mylan work?

The active substance in Abiraterone Mylan, abiraterone acetate, is changed in the body to abiraterone, which stops the body producing testosterone, a male hormone. Testosterone helps the cancer survive and grow. Abiraterone stops testosterone production by blocking an enzyme called CYP17 found in the testicles and elsewhere in the body. By reducing the testosterone levels, Abiraterone Mylan can slow the growth of the prostate cancer.

### How has Abiraterone Mylan been studied?

As for every medicine, the company provided data on the quality of Abiraterone Mylan. The company also carried out studies that showed that it is 'bioequivalent' to the reference medicine. Two medicines are bioequivalent when they produce the same levels of the active substance in the body and are therefore expected to have the same effect.

For some of the uses, studies on the benefits and risks of the active substance have already been carried out with the reference medicine, Zytiga, and do not need to be repeated for Abiraterone Mylan.

For newly diagnosed, metastatic cancer that is sensitive to hormones and for newly diagnosed, high-risk, non-metastatic cancer that is sensitive to hormones, which are uses for which Zytiga is not authorised, the company provided data from the scientific literature.

### What are the benefits and risks of Abiraterone Mylan?

For the uses for which Zytiga is authorised, because Abiraterone Mylan is a generic medicine and is bioequivalent to the reference medicine, its benefits and risks are taken as being the same as the reference medicine's.

For the other authorised uses of Abiraterone Mylan, literature data have shown that abiraterone improved disease control and prolonged the time patients lived.

In patients with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer, literature data have shown that abiraterone given with ADT increased the time people lived compared with ADT alone, irrespective of whether the cancer was at high risk of getting worse or not.

Further literature data in patients with newly diagnosed high-risk, non-metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer have shown that the addition of abiraterone to ADT and radiotherapy prolonged both the time people lived without their cancer becoming metastatic and how long they lived overall.

Data in patients with newly diagnosed, metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer have shown that abiraterone given with ADT and docetaxel increased both the time patients lived without their disease getting worse and how long they lived overall, compared with ADT and docetaxel alone.

No new safety concerns were identified in these additional uses.

Studies carried out with Abiraterone Mylan are described in more detail in the medicine's assessment reports.

### Why is Abiraterone Mylan authorised in the EU?

The European Medicines Agency concluded that, in accordance with EU requirements, Abiraterone Mylan has been shown to have comparable quality and to be bioequivalent to Zytiga. Therefore, the Agency's view was that as for Zytiga, the benefits of Abiraterone Mylan in Zytiga's authorised uses outweigh the identified risks.

For those uses that are not authorised with Zytiga, literature data have shown that treatment with abiraterone provides meaningful benefits to patients and its side effects are well-understood and manageable.

The Agency therefore concluded that the benefits of Abiraterone Mylan outweigh the identified risks and it can be authorised for use in the EU.

### What measures are being taken to ensure the safe and effective use of Abiraterone Mylan?

Recommendations and precautions to be followed by healthcare professionals and patients for the safe and effective use of Abiraterone Mylan have been included in the summary of product characteristics and the package leaflet.

As for all medicines, data on the use of Abiraterone Mylan are continuously monitored. Side effects reported with Abiraterone Mylan are carefully evaluated and any necessary action taken to protect patients.

### Therapeutic indication

Abiraterone Mylan is indicated with prednisone or prednisolone for:

- the treatment of newly diagnosed high risk metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in adult men in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT).
- the treatment of metastatic castration resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) in adult men who are asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic after failure of androgen deprivation therapy in whom chemotherapy is not yet clinically indicated.
- the treatment of mCRPC in adult men whose disease has progressed on or after a docetaxel based chemotherapy regimen.
