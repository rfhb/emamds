## 2.3.2. Clinical study

## mRNA-1345 P202

## Description

Study mRNA-1345 P202 (hereafter, referred to as 'study P202') was a Phase 2, randomized, observerblind,  study  to  evaluate  the  safety,  reactogenicity  and  immunogenicity  of  the  mRNA-1345  vaccine targeting RSV in children 2 to &lt;18 years of age with or without high risk factors for RSV disease. Study P202 is complete with LPLV having occurred on 27 Jun 2025 and database lock on 24 Jul 2025. The EOS date was 27 Aug 2025. This study consisted of 2 parts (Parts A and B).

## Part A

Part  A  was  a  phase  2  component  comprising  two  cohorts.  Cohort  1  included  approximately  160 participants aged 2 to &lt;5 years who were either healthy or had stable chronic conditions considered by the investigator to increase the risk of RSV disease. Assessments in Part A were conducted over a 6 month period following administration of study intervention on Day 1. In Cohort 1, the study schedule comprised up to four in person visits, namely a screening visit, visits on Days 1 and 29 and a Month 6/end of study visit, together with two safety telephone calls on Day 8 and at Month 3. In Cohort 2, the schedule comprised up to three in person visits, namely a screening visit and visits on Days 1 and 29, together with three safety telephone calls on Day 8, at Month 3 and at Month 6/end of study.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Part B

Part B was a safety follow up component offered to all participants in Part A Cohort 1 who had been enrolled and dosed, corresponding to approximately 160 participants. In this part of the study, no study intervention  was  administered.  Participants  were  re-enrolled  for  surveillance  of  RSV  disease  over  a further 6 month period, including the US RSV season from October 2024 to April 2025. The first visit in Part B, designated Day 1, corresponded to the day of re-enrolment into Cohort 1 Part B.

RSV surveillance in Part B was performed weekly during the RSV season and monthly during the off season. The study procedures included at least one in person visit, weekly text messages during the RSV season and three safety telephone calls at Months 2, 4 and 6, the latter representing the end of study visit. Additional unscheduled visits were to take place if participants developed symptoms considered relevant for RSV and convalescent visits could occur following confirmed RSV lower respiratory tract disease. Serious adverse events and adverse events of special interest occurring after the Part A end of study visit or discontinuation and before signing of the informed consent form for Part B were captured as interim medical history. Thereafter, serious adverse events and adverse events of special interest were collected from signing of the informed consent form for Part B through the Month 6/end of study visit. The rationale for implementing Part B was that children aged 2 to &lt;5 years may still be RSV naive and may not yet have passed through a full RSV season after receipt of mRNA-1345. This was considered of  particular  relevance  in  the  context  of  the  identified  potential  risk  of  vaccine  associated  enhanced respiratory disease, which had previously been observed in children aged 5 to 8 months who received mRNA-1345 or mRNA-1365 in study mRNA-1365 P101.

## Methods

## Study participants

The  study  population  comprised  paediatric  participants  in  two  age  defined  cohorts  in  Part  A,  namely children aged 2 to &lt;5 years who were healthy or had stable chronic conditions increasing the risk of RSV disease and children aged 5 to &lt;18 years with stable chronic conditions associated with increased RSV risk, followed by a safety follow up population in Part B. Relevant chronic conditions included medically treated asthma,  cystic  fibrosis,  other  chronic  respiratory  diseases  and  pulmonary  malformations,  trisomy  21, neuromuscular disease,  cerebral  palsy,  haemodynamically  significant  or  symptomatic  congenital  heart disease and sickle cell disease (provided that no blood transfusions had been required within the 3 months prior  to  enrolment).  For  women  of  childbearing  potential,  eligibility  required  compliance  with  local contraceptive requirements for clinical studies, including negative pregnancy tests at screening and on the day of injection, adequate contraception or abstinence for at least 28 days prior to Day 1, continuation of adequate contraception for 90 days after injection and no current breastfeeding. In Part B, eligibility was restricted  to  participants  from  Cohort  1  Part  A  who  had  received  study  intervention  and  had  either completed the Part A end of study visit or had been dosed and subsequently discontinued.

The exclusion criteria were intended to minimise potential confounding factors and to protect participant safety. Participants were excluded if they were acutely ill or febrile, defined as a temperature of at least 38.0°C,  within  72  hours  prior  to  or  at  screening  or  on  Day  1.  Further  exclusion  criteria  included  any diagnosis or condition which, in the opinion of the investigator, might interfere with study assessments or compromise safety, receipt or planned receipt of any licensed or authorised vaccine within 14 days before or after study vaccination, prior use of systemic immunosuppressive therapy, intravenous blood products within 3 months before enrolment, or RSV monoclonal antibodies within 6 months before enrolment. Short courses of oral corticosteroids of less than 7 days were permitted if completed at least 3 months before study entry. Additional exclusion criteria comprised recent participation in another interventional clinical study within 28 days prior to enrolment, a family or household relationship with personnel at the research center or others involved in study conduct and legal placement under the control or protection of a court, government, or comparable authority, for example in foster care.

## CHMP's comment:

The study population and the applied inclusion and exclusion criteria are considered acceptable for the objectives of the study.

## Treatments

mRNA-1345  is  an  mRNA  vaccine  comprising  a  single  mRNA  sequence  encoding  the  RSV  fusion glycoprotein stabilised in the prefusion conformation and formulated in lipid nanoparticles. In this phase 2 study, it was administered intramuscularly as a single injection at dose levels of 7.5 µg, 15 µg, 30 µg or 50 µg. Saline (0.9% NaCl) was used as placebo. The dose levels evaluated were selected on the basis of available nonclinical and clinical data for mRNA-1345, together with data from other sponsor mRNAbased vaccines formulated in the same SM-102 containing lipid nanoparticles. A placebo group was included in children aged 2 to &lt;5 years to account for the higher background frequency of apparent adverse  reactions  such  as  fever  in  this  age  group  due  to  intermittent  illnesses  and  to  support interpretation of reactogenicity. The placebo comparator was also intended to facilitate assessment of the effects of immunisation beyond those attributable to natural infection, since RSV-F binding antibody (bAb) and RSV neutralising antibody (nAb) concentrations may increase following natural exposure in children aged 2 to &lt;5 years.

## CHMP's comment:

The treatment scheme, including the limitation of the placebo comparator to  Cohort 1, is considered acceptable and adequately justified. The dose rationale is also considered acceptable overall.

## Objective(s)

The objectives of study P202 were specified separately for Cohort 1 Part A, Cohort 2 Part A and Part B. In both Part A cohorts, the primary objective was to evaluate the safety and reactogenicity of a single study injection  and  the  secondary  objective  was  to  assess  the  immunogenicity  of  the  injection.  The exploratory objective in Cohort 1 Part A was to further characterise the immune response, whereas in Cohort 2 Part A it was to evaluate immune response biomarkers as potential correlates of protection or risk of RSV disease. In Part B, the primary objective was to evaluate the incidence of RSV respiratory tract  disease (RSV-RTD) during the 6 months following re-enrolment. Additional objectives in Part B included evaluation of the safety of the single study injection administered in Part A during the same follow up period, as well as exploratory assessment of the persistence of immunogenicity and of immune response biomarkers; these latter assessments were optional for participants.

## Outcomes/endpoints

The endpoints of study P202 were defined separately for Cohort 1 Part A, Cohort 2 Part A and Part B in line with the respective study objectives. In both Part A cohorts, the primary endpoints related to safety and reactogenicity and included solicited local and systemic adverse reactions (ARs) through 7 days after injection, unsolicited adverse events (AEs) through 28 days after injection, medically attended adverse events (MAAEs), adverse events of special interest (AESIs), serious adverse events (SAEs) and AEs leading to discontinuation from Day 1 to the Month 6/end of study visit.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The secondary endpoints in Part A addressed humoral immunogenicity In Cohort 1, these comprised geometric mean titres (GMTs) of serum RSV nAbs and geometric mean concentrations (GMCs) of serum RSV prefusion F binding antibodies (bAbs) at Day 1, Day 29 and Month 6/EoS, together with geometric mean fold  rises  (GMFRs)  from  baseline  at  Day  29  and  Month  6/EoS.  In  addition,  the  proportion  of participants  with  a  seroresponse  (seroresponse  rate;  SRR)  was  assessed;  SR  was  defined  as  a  post injection titre showing a &gt;4-fold rise over baseline where the baseline value was above the lower limit of quantification, or a post injection titre &gt;4 times the lower limit of quantification (LLoQ) where the baseline value was below the LLoQ. In Cohort 2, corresponding immunogenicity endpoints were assessed at Day 1 and Day 29, with GMFR and SR likewise evaluated at Day 29. Exploratory endpoints in Part A included  further  characterisation  of  immune  responses,  such  as  transcriptome  analyses  in  Cohort 1, assessment of vaccine specific T cell and B cell responses and other immune response parameters to be determined.

In Part B, the primary endpoints were measures of RSV disease occurrence from Part B Day 1 to the EoS and included RSV respiratory tract disease (RSV-RTD), RSV lower respiratory tract disease (RSV-LRTD; defined as cough or difficulty breathing and SpO2 &lt;95% or RR increased and confirmed RSV infection), severe RSV-LRTD (defined as meeting the case definition of RSV-LRTD and SpO2  &lt;93% or lower chest wall in-drawing), very severe RSV-LRTD (defined as meeting the case definition of RSV-LRTD and SpO2 &lt;90% or failure to respond/unconscious) and RSV related hospitalisation. Secondary endpoints in Part B comprised adverse events of special interest and serious adverse events over the same follow up period. Exploratory  endpoints  included  GMTs  of  serum  RSV  nAbs  and  GMCs  of  serum  RSV  prefusion  F  and postfusion F bAbs at Part B Day 1, as well as further characterisation of immune responses, including cell mediated immunogenicity.

## CHMP's comment:

The study endpoints are considered acceptable and no issues are identified regarding their selection and definition.

## Sample size

The planned sample size was based on the probability of detecting at least one participant with an AE at a  true  underlying  incidence  rate  of  5%.  With  60  participants  per  study  group  this  probability  was approximately  95%  and  with  40  participants  approximately  87%,  which  was  intended  to  support  a preliminary assessment of reactogenicity at each dose level. A placebo group was included in Cohort 1 but not in Cohort 2, reflecting the expectation of a higher background rate of adverse reactions in the youngest age group, which was considered relevant for tolerability assessment.

## CHMP's comment:

The sample size is considered acceptable, as the study was designed to provide descriptive safety and immunogenicity  data  across  dose  groups  rather  than  to  support  formal  hypothesis  testing  or confirmatory comparisons.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

In Part A, randomisation was performed centrally in a blinded manner using an Interactive Response Technology  system  in  accordance  with  pre  generated  randomisation  schedules.  In  Cohort 1, approximately 160 participants aged 2 to &lt;5 years were randomised in a 1:1:1:1 ratio to receive a single injection  of  mRNA-1345  at  dose  levels  of  7.5  µg,  15  µg,  30  µg  or  placebo,  with  approximately  40 participants per study group. Enrolment and randomisation in this cohort were stratified by age, such that approximately 40% of participants were aged 2 to &lt;3 years and 60% were aged 3 to &lt;5 years, corresponding to around 60 participants in the younger subgroup.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In Cohort 2, approximately 180 participants aged 5 to &lt;18 years were randomised in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive a single injection of mRNA-1345 at dose levels of 15 µg, 30 µg or 50 µg, with approximately 60 participants  in  each  study  group.  Enrolment  and  randomisation  in  Cohort  2  were  stratified  equally between participants aged 5 to &lt;12 years and those aged 12 to &lt;18 years.

The study was conducted in an observer blind manner. The investigator, study site staff, participants, parents or legally authorised representatives, site monitors and sponsor personnel or their designees remained  blinded  to  the  assigned  trial  intervention  until  database  lock  and  unblinding,  except  in predefined  circumstances  or  emergencies.  For  participants  entering  Part  B,  the  original  treatment allocation from Part A remained applicable.

## CHMP's comment:

The randomisation procedures and the observer-blind design are considered acceptable for the conduct of this study.

## Statistical Methods

No formal statistical hypothesis testing was planned for study P202 and all analyses were descriptive. For Part A immunogenicity, the primary analysis set was the Per Protocol (PP) Set, with supportive analyses on the  Full  Analysis  Set  (FAS)  if  the  difference  in  participant  numbers  between  both  sets  exceeded  10%. Immunogenicity results were to be summarised by treatment group and cohort and included GMTs for RSV nAb, GMCs for RSV bAbs, GMFRs from baseline and SRRs with corresponding 95% CIs; the latter calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method.

Safety analyses were based on the Safety Set, except for solicited adverse reactions, which were analysed using  the  Solicited  Safety  Set.  In  Part  A,  safety  evaluations  included  solicited  local  and  systemic  ARs, unsolicited AEs, treatment related and severe AEs, serious AEs, medically attended AEs, AESI, AEs leading to discontinuation, as well as vital signs and physical examination findings. Adverse events were coded using MedDRA and toxicity grading followed the modified DHHS 2007 grading scale for participants aged 2 to &lt;18 years. In Part B, analyses focused on the incidence of RSV-RTD categories, including RSV-LRTD, severe and very severe RSV-LRTD, RSV hospitalisation and medically attended RSV disease, together with summaries of serious AEs and AESI.

The analysis populations comprised the Randomisation Set, Full Analysis Set, Per Protocol Set, Safety Set and Solicited Safety Set for Part A, with corresponding Full Analysis and Safety Sets for participants reenrolling in Part B. In general, immunogenicity analyses were based on participants analysed according to randomised treatment, whereas safety analyses were based on treatment actually received.

## CHMP's comment:

The statistical methods are considered acceptable for a study based on descriptive analyses without formal comparative hypothesis testing.

## Results

## Participant flow

In Cohort 1 Part A, 164 participants were randomised to receive mRNA1345 at dose levels of 7.5 μg, 15 μg, 30 μg or placebo. Overall, 162 participants received study intervention, corresponding to 98.8% of those randomised. One participant in the 7.5 μg group was found after randomisation not to meet the inclusion criteria (the participant was found to have been diagnosed with RSV &lt;3 months earlier) and was not dosed, and one participant with caregiver in the 15 μg group left the site prior to dosing. A total of 148 participants completed Part A, corresponding to 90.2% of those randomised, with completion rates of 90.0%, 88.1%, 97.6% and 85.4% in the 7.5 μg, 15 μg, 30 μg and placebo groups, respectively. Sixteen participants discontinued Part A prematurely, most commonly due to loss to follow-up, while smaller numbers discontinued because of withdrawal by parent or guardian, a protocol deviation related to recent RSV infection, or other reasons. No participant discontinued due to safety reasons. In Cohort 2 Part A, 185 participants were randomised across the 15 μg, 30 μg and 50 μg dose groups. Of these, 184 participants received study intervention and 178 completed the study, corresponding to 99.5% dosed and 96.2% completed overall. One participant was withdrawn by parent or guardian after randomisation and before dosing in the 15 μg group. Premature discontinuation occurred in 7 participants overall and was mainly attributable to loss to follow-up, with the remaining discontinuations due to withdrawal by parent or guardian. In Part B, 92 participants from Cohort 1 Part A were re-enrolled, including 24, 25, 24 and 19 participants from the 7.5 μg, 15 μg, 30 μg and placebo groups, respectively. Of these, 91 participants completed Part B and one participant in the 30 μg group was lost to follow -up.

Important protocol deviations were reported in 42 participants in Cohort 1 Part A, representing 25.6% overall, with the highest proportion observed in the 15 μg group and the lowest in the placebo group. The most frequent category of important protocol deviation across all treatment groups was related to study  procedures.  In  Cohort  2  Part  A,  51  participants,  corresponding  to  27.6%,  had  at  least  one important protocol deviation, again most commonly related to study procedures. One participant in the 50 μg group  did  not  meet the  eligibility criteria but was enrolled  and  received  study vaccination. In Part B, important protocol deviations were reported for 6 participants overall (corresponding to 6.5%) and were likewise predominantly related to study procedures. Additional deviations in the 7.5 μg group comprised one case of an inadequate informed consent process due to incorrect date capture on the consent form and one case in which an AESI was not reported, or was reported late, according to protocol requirements. These participants were retained in the study analyses.

## CHMP's comment:

The overall participant disposition appears acceptable.

The  handling  and  reporting  of  protocol  deviations  related  to  antibody-mediated  immunogenicity assessments  are  considered  sufficiently  clarified  by  the  applicant  and  do  not  preclude  an  adequate interpretation of the per-protocol immunogenicity analyses. In Appendix 16.2.2 of the CSR, 'Antibodymediated  Immunogenicity  Assessment  not  done  per  protocol'  was  reported  repeatedly  in  Part A Cohort 1, including 3 cases in the placebo group, 5 in the 7.5 µg group, 5 in the 15 µg group and 6 in the 30 µg group, with all 3 samples affected for subjects and , respectively, who were retained in the PP Set as valid immunogenicity data were available at the key analysis timepoints and the deviations were not considered relevant to the evaluation of the immune response. Similar deviations were also reported in Cohort 2, albeit less frequently. The explanation provided is considered plausible and the issue is therefore considered resolved. PPD PPD

## Baseline data

In Cohort 1 Part A, the median age at screening was 3 years, with participants ranging from 2 to 4 years of age; 39.6% were aged 2 to &lt;3 years and 60.4% were aged 3 to &lt;5 years. Approximately 90% of participants were considered healthy and around 10% had high-risk conditions, including asthma or bronchial hyperactivity. Overall, 41.4% of participants in the Safety Set had at least one medical history, most commonly seasonal allergy, eczema and asthma. No selected medical history of  clinical  interest  was  reported.  Baseline  demographic  and  clinical  characteristics  were  comparable across treatment groups (cf. Table 1).

## CHMP's comment:

The  baseline  data  are  considered  generally  acceptable.  However,  the  applicant's  characterisation  of baseline characteristics as 'consistent across treatment groups' appears somewhat overstated in view of the observed numerical imbalances in sex, race and (in Cohort 2 Part A) body weight and BMI. While such differences are not unexpected in small groups and are not considered to invalidate the baseline comparability overall, they should be interpreted with appropriate caution. In addition, it remains unclear why, for Part B, Part A baseline values for height, weight and BMI were retained rather than updated values at re-enrolment.

Furthermore, the minimum height of 71.0 cm reported in the 15 µg arm of Cohort 1 for a participant aged 2 to 4 years appears exceptionally low and was clarified by the applicant as most likely reflecting a data entry error. Notably, this value lies below the WHO 0.1st percentile reference for a 24-month-old girl (75.7 cm) and its plausibility is further questioned by the corresponding minimum body weight of 11.2 kg in the same arm, which does not appear commensurate with such a height. The explanation provided is considered plausible and the issue is therefore considered resolved.

## CHMP's comment:

The RSV nAb data are considered acceptable overall. In Cohort 1 Part A, all mRNA-1345 dose levels elicited higher Day 29 RSV-A and RSV-B nAb GMTs than placebo. However, the dose-response pattern was not fully monotonic, as the 15 µg group was numerically lower than the 7.5 µg group across several parameters and the separation between 7.5 µg and 15 µg remained limited through Month 6 (particularly for RSV-B). The Day 29 GMFR findings should also be interpreted with caution, as the similar RSV-A fold rises observed in the 7.5 µg and 30 µg groups were influenced by lower baseline GMTs in the 7.5 µg group. Likewise, the seroresponse rates in Cohort 1 did not show a consistent linear dose-response pattern, with the 15 µg group performing numerically less favourably for both RSV subtypes. In the age subgroup analyses of Cohort 1, the highest Day 29 point estimates were observed in the 30 µg group in both strata, but the wide confidence intervals in participants aged 2 to &lt;3 years limit the robustness of any dose ranking. In Cohort 2 Part A, the Day 29 GMT, GMFR and seroresponse data showed a more consistent numerical trend in favour of 50 µg. This pattern was broadly supported in the age subgroup analyses, although for RSV-B in participants aged 12 to &lt;18 years the overlapping 95% CIs across dose groups indicate limited separation between dose levels.

## CHMP's comment:

The RSV bAb findings are considered acceptable as supportive descriptive immunogenicity data and are broadly consistent with the pattern observed for RSV nAb responses. In Cohort 1 Part A, Day 29 Pre-F and Post-F bAb responses were higher than placebo across all mRNA-1345 dose levels, with the highest GMC point estimates in the 30 µg group.

However, the dose-response pattern was not fully monotonic, as the 15 µg group was numerically lower than the 7.5 µg group across several parameters and Day 29 GMFR and seroresponse data likewise did not show a consistent linear dose relationship, noting also the evident baseline dependence of fold-rise estimates. By Month 6, bAb responses had declined across all groups (several GMFR confidence intervals included 1) and seroresponse rates were markedly lower, indicating limited persistence in Cohort 1. In Cohort 2 Part A, Day 29 GMC and GMFR data showed a clearer numerical trend in favour of 50 µg, while differentiation between 15 µg and 30 µg remained limited, particularly for Post-F responses.

## Exploratory post hoc analysis of baseline RSV serostatus

In  Part  B,  the  applicant  additionally  presented  an  exploratory  post  hoc  analysis  of  baseline  RSV serostatus. The applicant stated that RSV infection status at enrolment had been unknown and that classification of RSV-naive versus RSV-experienced participants was subsequently based on a Post-F IgG threshold of at least 200 AU/mL derived from a post hoc analysis in children aged 5 to 23 months from study mRNA-1365 P101. Applying this threshold, 10 of 157 participants, corresponding to 6.4%, were classified as RSV-naive; all were aged 2 to 3 years and all had baseline Post-F values below the assay LLoQ. Of the 7 RSV-naive participants in the mRNA-1345 groups, 4 were reported to show minimal increases in Post-F bAb concentrations after vaccination, whereas 3 showed more marked increases, which the applicant considered potentially compatible with prior exposure or intercurrent RSV infection. The applicant further stated that baseline and post-immunisation RSV-A and RSV-B nAb titres and PreF bAb titres were consistent with this differentiation. Among placebo recipients classified as RSV-naive, Post-F concentrations reportedly remained largely unchanged in 2 participants, whereas 1 participant showed an increase from below the LLoQ at baseline to 4,561 AU/mL on Day 29.

## CHMP's comment:

This exploratory post hoc analysis is considered acceptable as a hypothesis-generating exercise only, but  it  is  of  limited  interpretative  value  and  is  not  regarded  as  decision-making  for  the  overall immunogenicity assessment.

## Safety results

In Cohort 1 Part A, 162 participants received a single injection of study intervention. The median time on study after the Day 1 injection was 180 days, with a range of 25 to 244 days. Similar findings were reported for the 157 participants included in PP set. In Cohort 2 Part A, 184 participants received a single injection of study intervention, with a median time on study after vaccination of 176 days, ranging from 31 to 233 days. ## Solicited Adverse Reactions

In Cohort 1 Part A, solicited ARs within 30 minutes after study injection were reported in 7/39 participants (17.9%) in the 7.5 µg group, 5/40 participants (12.5%) in the 15 µg group, 9/41 participants (22.0%) in  the  30  µg  group  and  6/41  participants  (14.6%)  in  the  placebo  group. ## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

The clinical methodology of study P202 is considered acceptable overall. The study population and the inclusion and exclusion criteria are appropriate for the objectives of the study. The treatment scheme, including the restriction of placebo to Cohort 1, is considered adequately justified and the overall dose rationale  is  acceptable.  The  selected  endpoints  are  appropriate  and  no  concerns  are  identified  with respect to their definition. The sample size is acceptable for a study intended to provide descri ptive safety and immunogenicity data across dose groups rather than formal hypothesis testing. Likewise, the randomisation  procedures,  the  observer-blind  design  and  the  statistical  methods  are  considered acceptable for a descriptive Phase 2 study without confirmatory comparisons.

However, the statement that only limited protocol deviation documentation was available regarding the missing 30-minute assessments appears to  mean  that,  among  the  participants  without  available  30-minute  data,  no  corresponding  protocol deviation was documented for the single missing participant in Cohort 1 Part A 15 µg, one such deviation ('eDiary  not  completed  as  specified  per  protocol')  was  documented  for  one  of  the  four  missing participants in Cohort 2 Part A 15 µg, while in Cohort 2 Part A 30 µg and 50 µg the number of such documented deviations exceeded the number of participants with missing 30-minute assessments. In this context, no alternative explanation for the absence of follow-up data other than non-completion of the eDiary is readily apparent. It therefore remains unclear why, in Cohort 2 Part A 30 µg and 50 µg, certain Day 1 eDiary entries that were documented as not completed per protocol were nevertheless included,  although  the  30-minute  analysis  was  stated  by  the  MAH  to  rely  on  a  single  recorded assessment. This is further underscored by the protocol requirement that the eDiary was to be completed by the parent(s)/legal guardian(s) approximately 30 minutes after study injection while still at the study site and under site staff guidance.

Accordingly,  the  MAH's  conclusion  that  these  discrepancies  can  be  excluded  as  relevant  for  the interpretation of the solicited AR analyses cannot be fully substantiated on the basis of the information provided.  Nevertheless,  given  the  small  number  of  affected  observations  and  the  absence  of  any indication of a meaningful impact on the overall reactogenicity profile, the explanation is considered overall plausible.
