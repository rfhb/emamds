## 2. Introduction

## Therapeutic Context

The current application is intended to support registration of a dispersible DRV/COBI 600/90 mg FDC film-coated tablet for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in paediatric patients aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg, who are either ART-naïve or ART-experienced.

The following wording is proposed by the Applicant for the dispersible tablet formulation:

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

REZOLSTA is indicated, in combination with other antiretroviral medicinal products, for the treatment of human immunodeficiency virus 1 (HIV 1) infection in adults and paediatric patients (aged 3 years and older, weighing at least 15 kg).

Genotypic testing should guide the use of REZOLSTA (see sections 4.2, 4.4 and 5.1).

## 3.1.1 Epidemiology

HIV continues to be a major global public health issue, having claimed more than 40 million lives so far. In 2022, 630 000 people died from HIV-related causes globally. There were approximately 39 million people living with HIV at the end of 2022, of whom 1.5 million were children 0-14 years old. Since 2010, the number of people acquiring HIV has been reduced, but there were still 1.3 million people becoming newly infected in 2022 globally, 130.000 being children 0-14 years old. (WHO FACT sheet).

There is no cure for HIV infection. However, with access to effective HIV prevention, diagnosis, treatment and care, including for opportunistic infections, HIV infection has become a manageable chronic health condition, enabling people living with HIV to lead long and healthy lives.

## 3.1.2. Biologic features

HIV-1 infection results in chronic activation of the immune system and a subsequent gradual loss of CD4+ T cells, eventually leading to a state of acquired immunodeficiency (AIDS). One of the predictors for HIV-1 disease progression is the level of HIV-1 RNA in the blood (i.e. viral load). The aim of treatment of HIV-1 infection is therefore to suppress, and subsequently maintain, the HIV-1 viral load to levels that are at least below the limit of detection of most commonly used assays (50 copies/mL of blood).

HIV-1 is a rapidly replicating virus, with an error-prone Reverse Transcriptase (RT). Mutations in the viral genome occur randomly when the virus replicates, with an estimated mutation rate of approximately one nucleotide mutation per replicative cycle. Many HIV variants are simultaneously present in each infected individual, which is also described as 'quasispecies'. Resistance-associated mutations (RAMs) can rapidly be selected when there is selection pressure due to e.g. too low concentrations of antiviral drugs. Often, resistance to drugs in a certain ARV class results in crossresistance to other drugs in that same class.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 3.1.3. Clinical presentation, diagnosis

The symptoms of HIV vary depending on the stage of infection. Though people living with HIV tend to be most infectious in the first few months after being infected, many are unaware of their status until the later stages. In the first few weeks after initial infection people may experience no symptoms or an influenza-like illness including fever, headache, rash, or sore throat. As the infection progressively weakens the immune system, they can develop other signs and symptoms, such as swollen lymph nodes, weight loss, fever, diarrhoea, and cough. Without treatment, they could also develop severe illnesses such as tuberculosis, cryptococcal meningitis, severe bacterial infections, and cancers such as lymphomas and Kaposi's sarcoma.

Children may have more rapid disease progression and accelerated damage of the developing immune system compared to adults, with higher viral loads and less effective immunological responses to HIV infection than adults.

## 3.1.4. Management

Standard of care for the treatment of HIV-1 requires use of combination ART to suppress viral replication to below detectable limits, increase CD4 cell counts, and stop disease progression. The choice of the combination regimen depends on the status of the patient, particularly in terms of plasma HIV viral load, CD4 cell counts, any previous treatments and prior treatment failure/intolerance.

Commonly used guidelines are those developed by the WHO, the European AIDS Clinical Society (EACS), the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) in the USA and the Paediatric European Network for Treatment of AIDS (PENTA), for use in children and adolescents.

Treatment options in children are more limited compared to adults. The recommended (collaborative Penta/EACS HIV treatment guideline, 2022) initial treatment of HIV-1 infection for paediatric patients is therapy with two NRTIs in combination with a drug from a different class (third agent). DTG is the preferred third agent in all children over 4 weeks of age and 3 kg. Whilst 'preferred options' are recommended, 'alternative options' are acceptable and remain important choices in settings where ART availability is limited or in individuals at particular risk of specific toxicity or DDIs.

## 2.1. Aspects of development

The proposed EoI is based on Study GS-US-216-0128 (hereafter referred to as 216-0128), which is an ongoing, Phase 2/3, multicenter, open-label, multicohort study evaluating PK, safety, and efficacy of COBI-boosted ATV, COBI-boosted DRV, and F/TAF  in HIV-1 infected children and adolescents aged ≥4 weeks to &lt;18 years in 5 cohorts, which are defined as follows (per most recent Protocol Amendment 10 dated 23 February 2024):

- Cohort 2: children aged ≥ 6 to &lt;12 years and weighing ≥ 25 to &lt;40 kg.
- Cohort 3 : children aged ≥ 2 years and weighing ≥ 14 to &lt;25 kg.
- Cohort 4b: children aged ≥ 4 weeks and weighing ≥ 3 to &lt;25 kg.
- Cohort 5: children aged ≥ 4 weeks and weighing ≥ 3 to &lt;14 kg.

For this application, the PK, efficacy, and safety of the dispersible DRV/COBI 600/90-mg FDC tablet in children with HIV-1 aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg are supported by data on COBI-boosted DRV from Cohort 3 (N=11) of Study 216-0128, combining DRV (600 mg qd) and COBI (90 mg qd) as separate formulations with F/TAF FDC (120/15 mg qd) in ART-experienced, virologically suppressed children with HIV-1 aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg. Data from participants who received the combination ATV and COBI in Cohort 3 are not included in Interim Analysis 2 and are not relevant for this submission.

## Pharmaceutical development

In line with the agreed paediatric investigation plan for Rezolsta, the new strength and dosage form was developed as an age-appropriate solid oral formulation: a dispersible tablet for children from 3 years of age and older and weighing 15 kg to &lt; 25 kg. This pharmaceutical form is suitable for patients in this age group of children who might experience difficulties swallowing tablets.

The quality target product profile (QTPP) for the finished product was adequately defined as a once daily immediate-release filmcoated dispersible tablet, containing 600 mg of darunavir and 90 mg of cobicistat as active substances. Requirements for the finished product were an acceptably low level of impurities/degradation products and microbial burden, and a minimum shelf life of 24 months when packaged in the proposed container closure system.

A discussion on physicochemical characteristics (solubility, solid state properties, and particle size) of the active substances that may influence the finished product performance, or manufacturability was presented, building on previous studies conducted for the marketed presentations. It was notably concluded that bioavailability is not affected by the presence of darunavir hydrate and the particle size of darunavir, and that the adsorption of cobicistat onto a solid carrier (silicon dioxide) increase its physical stability and facilitates its processing. Given the adsorption process, the particle size of cobicistat is not relevant.

The compatibility of the two active substances with the excipients has been demonstrated through stability studies performed on the finished product.

During initial formulation development, small-scale batches were manufactured using both either wet or dry granulation, building on prior experience with commercially available darunavir/cobicistat tablets. Based on manufacturability and dissolution data, a dry granulation process was selected using the same. intragranular excipients as that of the marketed product, with croscarmellose sodium selected to avoid oxidative degradation of cobicistat and ensure rapid disintegration. To improve palatability of the dispersible tablet, sucralose and strawberry flavour are added to the extra granular phase, based on the outcome of a taste assessment study. All excipients are well known pharmaceutical ingredients, and their quality is compliant with Ph. Eur. standards except for the filmcoating powder and the strawberry flavour that comply with in-house specifications. The choice of excipients has been justified. There are no novel excipients used in the finished product formulation. The list of excipients is included in section 6.1 of the SmPC.

A human bioequivalence study evaluated the in vivo performance of the proposed commercial fixeddose combination product dispersed in water compared the products tested in pivotal clinical study, i.e. the co-administration of 2 single agent formulations, cobicistat 90 mg tablets and a darunavir 600 mg 100 mg/mL suspension under fed conditions. In the initial submission, information on the reference batches was missing which did not allow an assessment of their suitability. Furthermore, the origin of the 90 mg cobicistat tablet not marketed in the EU was unclear which led to a major objection. In response, the applicant provided certificates of analysis demonstrating that the difference in assay between test and reference batches was less than 5%. Furthermore, the cobicistat reference batch used in the bioequivalence study was stated to have qualitatively the same composition as the 150 mg cobicistat (Tybost) tablet marketed in the EU. Therefore, the MO was considered solved. Overall, the results of the in vivo bioequivalence study showed that the single agents and the proposed finished product can be considered bioequivalent.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The acceptability of the proposed commercial finished product was tested in a phase 1 study in the target patient population of children aged 3 years and older and weighing ≥ 15 kg to &lt;25 kg. The assessment consisted of rating the ability to swallow, ease of swallowing, the palatability, and the ease of dispersion of the finished product in water, and the acceptability of taking the medicine daily. The results confirmed that the tablet and the ease of dispersion of the tablet are acceptable for the children and their caregivers. The taste evaluation confirmed that the addition of sweetener sucralose and strawberry flavouring is acceptable for the intended paediatric population.

The robustness of the proposed commercial finished product formulation was verified by manufacturing several small-scale batches (1.5 kg) with minor differences in composition. Robustness was studied against variations in excipient content, and in coating weight gain. The results showed that variations in excipient content, and in coating weight gain from 2% to 4% did not affect the properties of the core tablets nor the quality of the finished product within the ranges studied.

The effect of mixing the product with common foods or drinks was evaluated in compatibility studies demonstrating that the dispersed mixture can be further mixed with orange juice, apple sauce or yogurt to facilitate administration. Appropriate instructions on the method of administration are included in paragraph 4.2 of the SmPC.

The development of the dissolution methods for both darunavir and cobicistat in the fixed dose combination were provided and are the same as used routinely for the approved immediate release film coated tablets.

The proposed dissolution method for darunavir was demonstrated to be sensitive to the amount of lubricant and active substance particle size in the finished product. Furthermore, it was demonstrated to be discriminating towards the amount of disintegrant and to be stability indicating as shown by the slower dissolution rate observed when the product was exposed to high temperature and humidity stress conditions (50 °C/75% RH).

The proposed dissolution method for cobicistat was demonstrated to be sensitive to the hardness of the tablets and the amounts of disintegrant and lubricant. Furthermore, it was demonstrated to be stability indicating as shown by the slower dissolution rate observed when the product was exposed to high temperature and humidity stress conditions (50 °C/75% RH).

The discriminatory power of both dissolution methods has been demonstrated.

Comparison of dissolution profiles of the 2 active substances in the single agent products and in the fixed dose combination in media of different pH were provided. Some differences in release profiles were observed, which were attributed to the different formulations. This is considered acceptable considering the positive in vivo bioequivalence study.

## 4.1. Overall discussion and conclusions on non-clinical aspects

## 4.1.1. Discussion

No new juvenile toxicity studies were conducted, since such a study was already conducted and assessed in the previous application. Severe toxicity and mortality were seen in juvenile animals dosed before Post Natal Day 23 (PND 23). However, these effects were due to high brain exposure which was the result of the ontogeny of the CYP450 enzymes involved in metabolism of darunavir, and the immature blood brain barrier of rats at this age range. Although the activity of CYP3A4 in children &gt; 3 years of age might still be less than that of older children and adults, since it appears that the AUC is higher in this age group, the blood brain barrier is considered to be mature. The safety margins are smaller for children aged &gt;3 years, however, this observation is based on mild or toxicologically non-relevant findings and is not considered clinically meaningful. Further, the exposure in these young children is similar to the exposure in adults taking only darunavir without cobicistat, and this was well-tolerated. It is therefore anticipated that the safety profile of darunavir in human children &gt; 3 years of age is similar to that of older children and adults.

There are no objections from a non-clinical point of view to the inclusion of children &gt;3 years of age to the indication of Rezolsta.

## 5.2.1. Methods

## 5.2.1.1. Bioanalytical methods

In 2015, an assay was developed and validated at PRA (ICON) for DRV and COBI in EDTA plasma. There was additional validation done for selectivity, matrix effect, comedications, hemolysis and lipemic plasma testing. For both DRV and COBI, a stable isotope labeled internal standard was used for quantification of plasma concentrations using LC-MS/MS with a LLOQ of 5.00 ng/mL for both DRV (ULOQ of 5000 or 10000 ng/mL) and COBI (ULOQ of 5000 ng/mL). This method developed at PRA allowed simultaneous measurement of DRV and COBI.

This method at PRA was also used for analysis of plasma samples originating from clinical study FD1HTX1004. The bioanalytical method for study FD1HTX1004 has been sufficiently validated.

For measurement of samples from study 216-0128 separate validated LC-MS/MS assays developed at QPS were used, with a LLOQ of 20 ng/mL for DRV and of 5 ng/mL for COBI. Thus, all samples from paediatrics were measured with a method validated at QPS versus a method developed at PRA for all other studies used in the paediatric vs adult comparison and popPK modelling. It is noted that the LLOQ is 4-fold different for the methods at PRA and QPS. However, comparison of PK exposure between paediatrics and adults is the primary objective of this paediatric extension.

Validated assays were used, which have been assessed in previous variations. The Applicant compared the bioanalytical methods used for DRV and COBI for Study 216-0128 and the other clinical studies used in the population PK modelling (and thus in the exposure comparison between paediatrics and adults) and showed that similar bioanalytical assays were used which are both properly validated.

No cross-validation between the QPS methods for the Gilead sponsored studies and the bioanalytical methods for the Janssen sponsored studies, which were validated at ICON (formerly PRA), were performed. There are no future studies or bioanalytical validations of DRV/COBI planned by either Janssen or Gilead at this time. The Applicant could still consider doing such cross-validation for future submissions. For now, it remains unclear whether potential differences in the bioanalytical assays may have contributed to the observed differences between studies, although this is considered to be not very likely.

- To compare DRV AUC0-24h,ss and C0h,ss for paediatric participants enrolled in Cohorts 2 and 3 of Study 216-0128 with reference exposures from (i) adult Studies 216-0130 (DRV/COBI), TMC114FD2HTX3001 (DRV/COBI/emtricitabine/tenofovir), and TMC114IFD3013 (DRV/COBI/ emtricitabine/tenofovir), (ii) Cohort 1 Part A of Study 216-0128, and (iii) paediatric Studies TMC114-C228 (DRV/RTV, 3-6 yrs old) and TMC-114C230 (DRV/RTV, 12-18 yrs old).

## 5.2.1.3. Population PK model for COBI

A PopPK analysis of 2,895 observed COBI concentrations was conducted based on pooled data from 4 Phase 2/3 studies (GS-US-292-0106, hereafter referred to as 292-0106; GS-US-292-1515, hereafter referred to as 292-1515; GS-US-236-0112, hereafter referred to as 236-0112; and 216--0128) in 302 pediatric participants ≥2 to &lt;18 years of age with HIV-1. The analysis was based on previously developed population PK models for COBI.

COBI PKs were characterized by a 1-compartment model with sequential zero-order release and first-order absorption, and first-order elimination. The effects of body weight were included using allometric scaling with fixed coefficients (0.75 for clearances, 1 for volumes). In the final model, the effects of DRV/COBI and DRV/COBI+F/TAF combination treatments (relative to E/C/F/TAF) and the effect of race were estimated on CL/F.

The final model provided a good description of the data, as indicated by model diagnostic plots and pcVPCs (see Figure 3). The model appears to slightly under-predict the absorption phase and overpredict the trough concentrations in some studies. The prediction-corrected VPCs indicated adequate performance of the model overall. Point estimates for the effects of sex, health status, and use of concomitant P-glycoprotein inhibitors in the full model fell within the region of practical equivalence (80% to 125% of the null value) for steady-state AUCtau and Cmax. These effects were considered not clinically important and, therefore, are not included in the final model.

## 5.2.2. Pharmacokinetics

## 5.2.2.1. Bioequivalence Study TMC114FD1HTX1004 in Healthy Volunteers

Objective : The primary objective was to evaluate the single-dose PK and bioequivalence of DRV 600 mg in the presence of COBI 90 mg when administered as an FDC tablet dispersed in water (DRV/COBI 600/90 mg) compared to the coadministration of the separate formulations (DRV 100mg/mL suspension at a dose of 600 mg and COBI 1×90-mg tablet) under fed conditions in healthy participants.

The secondary objectives were to evaluate the single-dose PK and relative bioavailability of COBI 90 mg in the presence of DRV 600 mg when administered as an FDC tablet dispersed in water (DRV/COBI 600/90 mg) compared to coadministration of the separate formulations (DRV 100-mg/mL suspension at a dose of 600 mg and COBI 1 × 90-mg tablet), and to evaluate the short-term safety and tolerability of coadministration of DRV 600 mg and COBI 90 mg, under fed conditions in healthy participants.

Participants and Methods: This was a Phase 1, randomized, open-label, 2-way crossover study conducted at a single site in Belgium (SGS Clinical Pharmacology Unit) that evaluated the bioequivalence of a single dose of DRV 600 mg in the presence of COBI 90 mg as an FDC tablet dispersed in water compared to the coadministration of the separate formulations. DRV suspension was used as reference as it is the approved paediatric formulation. As the 90 mg COBI tablet has not been registered in the EU, the US-registered 90 mg COBI tablet was used, which is dose proportional in its composition to the US-registered 150 mg COBI tablet. The US-registered 150 mg COBI tablet has the same quantitative composition as the EU-registered 150 mg COBI tablet.

Study Period: 08 June 2022 (date first participant signed informed consent) to 05 October 2022 (date of last contact in the study).

Of the 32 participants (16 per treatment sequence) who entered the study, 30 completed study participation. All 32 participants received at least one dose of study intervention and were included in the Safety Analysis Set and the PK Data Analysis Set. One participant (treatment sequence A-B) tested positive for COVID-19 on Study Day 10 (pre-dose Period 2) and terminated study participation prematurely. This participant received last study intervention administration on Study Day 1 and did not receive Treatment B in Period 2. Another participant (treatment sequence A-B) withdrew consent on Study Day 4 and terminated study participation prematurely. This participant received last study intervention administration on Study Day 1 and did not receive Treatment B in Period 2. The 30 participants who completed both treatment periods and, for whom a PK parameter was reliably determined in both treatment periods were included in the statistical analysis.

Overall, 18 participants were female, and 14 were male. The median age was 40.0 years (range 18 to 55 years), and the median BMI was 23.4 kg/m 2  (range 19 to 29 kg/m 2 ). All participants were White, except for 1 participant who was Asian.

In the open-label treatment phase, 32 participants were assigned to 1 of 2 possible treatment sequences (AB or BA) in a 1:1 ratio and received the following treatments, one in each period:

- Treatment A: a single oral dose of DRV 600 mg and COBI 90 mg as an FDC tablet dispersed in water, under fed conditions (Treatment A, test).
- Treatment B: a single dose of DRV 600 mg as 100mg/mL suspension and COBI as 1×90-mg tablet, under fed conditions (Treatment B, reference).

Each treatment was separated by a washout period of at least 7 days.

Results : The plasma concentrations of DRV and COBI were determined using a validated, specific, and sensitive LC-MS/MS assay with a LLOQ of 5.00 ng/mL.

## In conclusion:

Both NCA calculations and PopPK simulations show that for paediatric patients in the weight band ≥15 to &lt;25 kg, a DRV/COBI regimen of 600/90 mg qd is expected to result in similar DRV trough values (C0h) at steady state as compared to adults. Total exposure to DRV at steady state (AUC0-24h using PopPK) for children in the 15-25 kg range may be 59% higher as compared to in children in Cohort 2 (25-40 kg range), 67% higher as in adults (HTX3001 reference population) and 22% higher than in the same children category in the C228 substudy (DRV/rtv combination). Comparing Cohort 3 DRV PK to the PK substudy of C228, NCA-derived PK exposures AUCtau and Cmax were 1.4-fold higher.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Given the total exposure to DRV a somewhat lower DRV dose would be desirable, but then DRV trough value would become too low. Therefore a lower DRV dose is not an option. Due to the higher daily exposure to DRV, higher than in adults and any other paediatric groups, the Applicant was requested to discuss the safety consequences (see Section 5.2.6.1.)

Participants in cohort 3 also received F/TAF 120/15 mg orally once daily. It should be noted that in general F/TAF is not expected to influence the PK of DRV/COBI. For PK results on F, TAF and TFV, check Section 5.2.2.3.3.

The Applicant proposed weight cut-offs now of 15-25-40 kg for Rezolsta in contrast to the weight cutoffs of 15-30-40 kg for Prezista to facilitate treatment in this paediatric population with a DRV/COBI FDC. This weight change was supported by the PopPK model (see previous EoI variation). However, the extension of indication of Rezolsta will also influence the prescription of DRV in combination with COBI administered separately, thus for Prezista. The recommended posology may become unclear if different weight cut-offs for DRV combined with ritonavir vs DRV combined with COBI are agreed. The Applicant agreed to update the Prezista SmPC at the next opportunity.

## In conclusion:

The COBI peak exposure in children aged 3 to 6 years in Cohort 3 of study 216-0128 (mean Cmax,ss of 2077 ng/mL) is 1.4-fold higher compared to the same age (BW) group on Genvoya. Genvoya (EVG/C/F/TAF) has been approved in the paediatric age groups based on study GS-US-292-0106, with a mean AUC0-24h,ss of 14485 ng.h/mL and Cmax,ss of 1526 ng/mL for the 14-25 kg BW cohort.

The total daily exposure to COBI in children aged 3 to 6 years in Cohort 3 of study 216-0128 (mean AUC0-24h,ss of 16071 ng.h/mL) is 1.7-fold higher compared with that in adolescents and adults, and also still 1.4-fold higher than in other children's cohorts. For a discussion on the safety consequences see Section 5.2.6.1.

It should be noted that in the clinical studies both DRV and COBI were administered as separate agents. The bioequivalence study HTX1004 showed that COBI when administered in the presence of DRV as a dispersible FDC tablet had a 15% lower bioavailability compared to the coadministration of the separate formulations.

## 5.2.2.3.3. Pharmacokinetics of FTC, TAF and TFV

As  noted  before,  participants  in  cohort  3  all  received  F/TAF  120/15  mg  orally  once  daily  as  NRTI backbone. FTC, TAF and TFV levels were therefore also measured in Cohort 3 and for completeness these data are also presented and assessed here, as without knowing these essential data, safety cannot yet be extrapolated from adults and higher age groups to this 3-6 yr old cohort for DRV and COBI. Formally the FTC, TAF and TFV level data will be part of other submissions, e.g. for line extension of Symtuza. Note that emtricitabine is abbreviated both as F and/or FTC.

The PK parameters of FTC, TAF, and TFV for Cohort 3 in Study GS-US-216-0128 are compared to other paediatric cohorts (Cohort 2 in Study GS-US-216-0128 and similar paediatric cohorts in other studies) and adults. Cohort 1 of Study GS-US-216-0128 is not included as no individual PK data are available for FTC, TAF, and TFV (not part of the objectives of the study for Cohort 1). For this analysis, as it specifically pertains FTC, TAF, and TFV exposures, data from interim analysis 3 were used for Cohort 2, because it contains data for 9 participants on DRV/COBI with a backbone of F/TAF versus only 3 participants in interim analysis 2, and is therefore considered more relevant.

It should be noted that the use of background ARV medication with REZOLSTA in children aged ≥3 years and weighing ≥15 to &lt;25 kg will be based on the availability and posology of such ARVs for this paediatric weight group. Currently, the combination of F/TAF (DESCOVY) as well as F/TDF (TRUVADA) is approved in the European Union for adults and adolescents ≥12 years old and weighing ≥35 kg. FTC is available as a single agent (EMTRIVA), with a 10 mg/mL oral solution to be dosed at 6 mg/kg in children weighing ≥15 to &lt;25 kg (i .e. an FTC dose of 90 to 150 mg). TAF is not available as a single agent. TDF as a single agent (VIREAD) is available for children weighing ≥15 to &lt;25 kg as a 123 -mg tablet and for children weighing ≥22 to &lt;28 kg as a 163 -mg tablet.

## 5.2.2.4. Drug-drug interactions

Data on drug-drug interactions for DRV and COBI in adults can generally be extrapolated to the paediatric population ≥ 3 years of age. This is based on the observation that microsomal protein contents in the livers of paediatric participants older than 1 year of age are comparable to adult values (Lacroix 1997; de Wildt 1999) and that CYP3A is the most relevant drug-metabolizing enzyme system involved in DRV and COBI metabolism. The direction and magnitude of drug-drug interactions is thus expected to be the same in the paediatric compared with adolescent and adult populations.

No changes are needed to the recommendations for use of DRV/COBI with coadministered drugs in patients &lt;18 years of age.

## 5.2.3. Pharmacodynamics

## 5.2.3.1. Mechanism of action

Darunavir is an inhibitor of the dimerisation and of the catalytic activity of the HIV-1 protease (KD of 4.5 x 10 -12 M). It selectively inhibits the cleavage of HIV encoded Gag-Pol polyproteins in virus infected cells, thereby preventing the formation of mature infectious virus particles.

Cobicistat is a mechanism based inhibitor of cytochromes P450 of the CYP3A subfamily. Inhibition of CYP3A mediated metabolism by cobicistat enhances the systemic exposure of CYP3A substrates, such as darunavir, where bioavailability is limited and half-life is shortened due to CYP3A dependent metabolism.

## 5.2.5. Dose selection and therapeutic window

The DRV/COBI 600/90-mg FDC formulation was studied in children with HIV-1 aged ≥ 3 to &lt;6 years in the acceptability study FD1HTX1001, and both products were administered separately in this population in Study 216-0128 (Cohort 3).

The proposed DRV dose of 600 mg is aligned with the approved DRV dose in combination with 100 mg rtv for patients weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;30 kg, while COBI 90 mg qd showed to be generally safe and well tolerated throughout its development, and boosted DRV exposures to levels that were comparable to the boosting effect of co-administered rtv 100 mg.

As Study FD1HTX1004 demonstrated bioequivalence for DRV between the dispersible DRV/COBI 600/90-mg FDC tablet and DRV and COBI administered concomitantly as the separate available formulations (DRV 100-mg/mL suspension at a dose of 600 mg; and COBI 1x90-mg tablet), the results from Study 216-0128 (Cohort 3) conducted with the single agents can be extrapolated to the dispersible DRV/COBI 600/90-mg FDC tablet formulation.

PopPK simulations showed that a DRV/COBI 600/90-mg qd dosing regimen for the entire weight band ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg in paediatric patients would result in similar or slightly lower DRV C0h (ie, 15% on average) and higher DRV AUC0-24h (ie, 43% on average) compared to adults. PK data from Study 216-0128 Cohort 3 showed that DRV exposures were within the range observed in previous studies (DRV/rtv). In Study 216-0128 Cohort 3, COBI exposures were also higher in children compared to adults, but within the range of the exposures previously observed in other studies. Therefore, this increase was not considered clinically relevant by the Applicant. In conclusion, the DRV/COBI FDC combining DRV and COBI at doses of 600 mg and 90 mg qd is considered appropriate for use in children aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg.

For discussion around this conclusion drawn by the Applicant, see Section 5.2.2.

## 5.2.6. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical pharmacology

## 5.2.6.1. Discussion

In support of the current variation, data from 11 participants enrolled in Cohort 3 of Study 216-0128 was submitted. PK data from Cohort 3 in study 0128 showed that for paediatric patients in the weight band ≥15 to &lt;25 kg a DRV/COBI regimen of 600/90 mg qd results in similar DRV trough values (C0h) as compared to adults. Total exposure to DRV (AUC0-24h) for children in the 15-25 kg range may be at least 1.4- to 1.6-fold higher as compared to other treatment groups. Given the total exposure to DRV, a somewhat lower DRV dose would be desirable, but then DRV trough value would become too low. Therefore, a lower DRV dose is not an option.

Due to the higher daily exposure to DRV (and COBI), higher than in adults and any other paediatric groups, the Applicant was requested to discuss the safety consequences. The Applicant concluded that, based on all available data, the observed differences in DRV and COBI exposure for children weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg in Study GS-US-216-0128 are not considered clinically relevant. This is based on the following rational:

- Population PK derived DRV exposures in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were within the range of those previously observed in adult and paediatric patients, especially with bid DRV dosing, and which were found to be safe and efficacious (Figure 11 and Figure 12).
- COBI exposures in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were in line with those previously observed in the E/C/F/TAF paediatric Study GS-US-292-0106 (Cohort 2 and Cohort 3,Table 20).
- Based on this, COBI 90 mg qd (as part of the E/C/F/TAF FDC) was approved for the same paediatric population as is the basis of this submission, i.e. children weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg.
- Throughout the development of DRV (boosted with either COBI or rtv), there have been no indications for an exposure-response relationship for any AEs.
- Similarly, COBI Phase 2 (GS US-216-0105) and Phase 3 (GS-US-216-0114) studies using population PK model-derived COBI exposures versus safety parameters revealed no relevant exposure-AE trends.
- Finally, juvenile toxicity studies showed that the toxicological profile of DRV and COBI was comparable to that in adult rats.

The rational and conclusions of the Applicant can be followed. Although DRV AUC0-24h was higher in Cohort 3 vs other studies, the values were still in the same range as those included in the exposuresafety analysis which was submitted as part of the original MAA for Rezolsta. As there was no exposure-safety relation observed, it can be agreed that the increased exposures seen in paediatric patients &gt;=14 to &lt;25 kg are not likely to be clinically relevant. However, as it cannot be excluded that there are safety aspects associated with this higher exposure that are specific to the paediatric population, i.e. it is uncertain whether the exposure-safety relationship is the same for paediatrics and adults, the Applicant should further follow this post approval (via routine pharmacovigilance).

Similarly, COBI exposure (mainly Cmax and Ctrough) was higher in study 0128 Cohort 3 vs E/C/F/TAF Cohort 3. Although there is no direct concern based on the information provided by the Applicant and the absence of an exposure-safety correlation in adults, also here this may be different in paediatrics and close attention of any safety signals is warranted.

In support of the current variation, data from 11 participants enrolled in Cohort 3 of Study 216-0128 was submitted. ## Statistical methods

Analysis sets and their definitions are as follows:

All Enrolled Analysis Set included all participants who were enrolled into the study. This is the primary analysis set for by-participant listings.

The Full Analysis Set (FAS) included all participants who took at least 1 dose of study drug. This is the primary analysis set for efficacy analyses.

The Safety Analysis Set included all participants who took at least 1 dose of study drug. All the data collected up to 30 days after participants permanently discontinued their study drug was included in the safety summaries. This is the primary analysis set for safety analyses.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The Intensive PK (IPK) Analysis Set was defined for each analyte of interest and included all participants who received at least 1 dose of study drug and for whom steady-state PK profiles at IPK visits were evaluable for the analyte of interest. The IPK Analysis Set was used for IPK analyses.

The Pharmacokinetic (PK) Analysis Set included all participants who received at least 1 dose of study drug and for whom at least 1 observed concentration data of any analyte of interest was available. This is the primary analysis set for all PK analyses. The PK analysis was used for the trough blood concentration listing.

The Spine DXA Analysis Set included all participants who (1) were enrolled in the study, (2) had received at least 1 dose of study drug, and (3) had non-missing spine BMD value for the Day 1 visit.

The Total Body Less Head (TBLH) DXA Analysis Set included all participants who (1) were enrolled in the study, (2) had received at least 1 dose of study drug, and (3) had non-missing TBLH BMD value for the Day 1 visit.

The FAS is the primary analysis set for efficacy endpoints. No formal statistical testing was planned.

All HIV-1 RNA data collected on treatment (ie, data collected up to 1 day after the last dose date) was used in the US FDA-defined snapshot algorithm. For the snapshot algorithm, the numbers and percentages of participants with HIV-1 RNA &lt; 50 copies/mL, HIV1 RNA ≥ 50 copies/mL, and reasons for no virological data at Week 24/48 were summarized by cohort. The 95% CI for the percentage of participants with HIV-1 RNA &lt; 50 copies/mL was constructed using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. The Week 24 virologic outcomes for the US FDA-defined snapshot algorithm were listed.

The proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA &lt; 50 copies/mL was also analysed by cohort and by visit using the following 2 methods for imputing missing HIV-1 RNA values:

-  missing = failure (M = F)

In this approach, all missing data were treated as HIV1 RNA ≥ 50 copies/mL. The denominator for the percentage was the number of participants in the FAS, excluding ongoing participants who had both missing HIV-1 RNA at a visit and had not reached the upper limit of that analysis window for the corresponding visit.

-  missing = excluded (M = E)

In this approach, all missing data were excluded in the computation of the percentages (ie, missing data points were excluded from both the numerator and denominator in the computation). The denominator for percentages at a visit is the number of participants in the FAS with non-missing HIV-1 RNA value at that visit.

For both M = F and M = E analyses, the number and percentage of participants with HIV-1 RNA in the following categories were summarized: &lt; 50 copies/mL (&lt; 20 copies/mL [&lt; 20 copies/mL not detectable, &lt; 20 copies/mL detectable], 20 to &lt; 50 copies/mL), 50 to &lt; 200 copies/mL, 200 to &lt; 400 copies/mL, 400 to &lt; 1000 copies/mL, ≥ 1000 copies/mL, and missing (only applicable to M = F analysis).

The CD4 cell count and CD4% data were summarized using observed, on-treatment data (ie, data collected up to 1 day after permanent discontinuation of study drug or all available data for participants who were still on study drug). Absolute values and changes from baseline in CD4 cell count (cells/μL) and CD4% at each visit were summarized b y cohort and by visit descriptively (sample size, mean, SD, median, Q1, Q3, minimum, and maximum) and also included the 95% CI based on the t-distribution. ## 5.3.10. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical efficacy

## 5.3.10.1. Discussion

In support of the current variation, data from Interim Analysis 2 of Study 216-0128 (Cohort 3) was submitted. Study 216-0128 is an ongoing open-label, multicentre, multicohort, two-part study (Part A and B) evaluating the PK, safety, efficacy, and antiviral activity of ATV/co or DRV/co administered with a background regimen (BR) in HIV-1 infected treatment-experienced, virologically suppressed paediatric subjects. A total of 11 participants were enrolled and received at least 1 dose of study drug. Further, study TMC114FD1HTX1001 was submitted to support the acceptability/swallowability of the dispersible DRV/COBI 600/90-mg FDC tablet in children with HIV1 aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg.

The proposed DRV dose of 600 mg is aligned with the approved DRV dose in combination with 100 mg rtv for patients weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;30 kg. COBI 90 mg qd showed to be generally safe and well tolerated throughout its development, and boosted DRV exposures to levels that were comparable to the boosting effect of co-administered rtv 100 mg. However, the total daily exposure to COBI in children aged 3 to 6 years in Cohort 3 of study 216-0128 (mean AUC0-24h,ss of 20418 ng.h/mL) is more than 2-fold higher compared with that in adolescents and adults. Further, the COBI peak exposure in children aged 3 to 6 years in Cohort 3 of study 216-0128 (mean Cmax,ss of 2077 ng/mL) is 1.4-fold higher compared to the same age (BW) group on Genvoya. ## 5.4.12.1. Discussion

## 5.4.12.1.1. Overall assessment of available safety data

In support of the current variation, safety data from Interim Analysis 2 of Study 216-0128 (Cohort 3) was submitted. Study 216-0128 is an ongoing open-label, multicentre, multicohort, two-part study (Part A and B) evaluating the PK, safety, efficacy, and antiviral activity of ATV/co or DRV/co administered with a background regimen (BR) in HIV-1 infected treatment-experienced, virologically suppressed paediatric subjects. A total of 11 participants were enrolled in Cohort 3 and received at least 1 dose of study drug. At the data cutoff date (19 September 2023), 10 (90.9%) participants had completed the main (48-week treatment) phase and had entered the extension phase. One (9.1%) participant had discontinued the study drug in the main phase prior to the data cutoff date due to loss to follow-up during the Week-48 window. At the data cutoff date, all 10 (100.0%) participants who had completed the main phase were continuing to receive study drug in the extension phase. The median (Q1, Q3) duration of exposure to DRV and COBI in Cohort 3 was 140.0 (65.7; 149.3) weeks.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

As such, exposure is too limited to reliably assess the safety profile in paediatric patients ≥3 years of age. Rather, safety is inferred based on similar exposures as observed in adults and adolescents. Overall, both the DRV as well as the COBI exposure data, based on PopPK modelling and on NCA for the paediatric population, for the proposed regimen indicate higher exposure values in children aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg as compared to adults and other children cohorts with DRV combination treatment. Upon request, the Applicant also provided quantitative and graphical comparison of the PK parameters of F, TAF and TFV for Cohort 3 as compared to the other paediatric cohorts and adults. From these data it can be concluded that the FTC (dosed at 120 mg) and TAF (dosed at 15 mg) levels for the combination of F/TAF with DRV/COBI in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US216-0128 are overall similar to those observed for Cohort 2 and other paediatric cohorts across studies (but at least 2-fold higher than those in adults). Notably, the TFV exposures from Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were similar to those from Cohort 2 of Study GS-US-216-0128, but approximately 3-fold higher than those from the same age/body weight group receiving B/F/TAF or E/C/F/TAF. However, these TFV levels were still 4- to 8-fold lower than those from adults receiving TDF (via E/C/F/TDF). This should be kept in mind when interpreting the safety information.

The submitted clinical data did not raise concerns. The observed AEs were consistent with those expected in the study population as well as with those observed in the already approved population. No deaths, AEs leading to premature study drug discontinuation, SAEs, or Grade ≥3 AEs were reported. AEs considered related to study drug were reported in 3 participants (1 participant experienced Grade 1 vomiting, 1 participant experienced Grade 1 fungal skin infection and Grade 1 upper respiratory tract infection, and 1 participant experienced Grade 1 abdominal pain, Grade 1 vomiting, and Grade 1 upper respiratory tract infection). Vomiting and abdominal pain are already listed as adverse reactions in section 4.8 of the SmPC. Overall, no new ADRs were identified. The high exposure observed for both DRV and Cobi (see PK section) is not deemed clinically relevant as the values were still in the same range as those included in the exposure-safety analysis which was submitted as part of the original MAA for Rezolsta and there was no exposure-safety relation observed.

Four (4) participants were not included in the Spine DXA Analysis Sets as they did not have a baseline assessment of their Spine BMD on or before the first dose date. The Applicant provided upon request the individual demographic (including weight-for-age z scores and weight-for-age percentile) and disease characteristics (including renal function and BMD z scores) at all available timepoints for these 4 participants. The available information for these 4 participants was in line with the information derived from the 7 participants who were included in the Spine DXA Analysis Set.

Following review of the safety information, no update to the list of safety concerns in the EU-RMP was made by the applicant.

The safety profile of DRV/Cobi is well-established in adults and adolescents and extrapolation of efficacy and safety data from adults to paediatric patients is in accordance with regulatory practice in the field of HIV. Some potential concerns have been identified by CHMP Rapp as the potential effects on growth or (pubertal) development, being the target population growing children, moreover the long-term effects on the renal and hepatic functions as reported in the EMA decision on PIP P/0257/2023.

## 9. Benefit-risk assessment

## 9.1. Therapeutic context

## 9.1.1. Disease or condition, proposed therapeutic indication

The most recent UNAIDS report estimates that of the 39.9 million people globally living with HIV in 2023, 1.4 million (4.4%) were children aged 0 to 14 years (UNAIDS 2023).

The current application is intended to support registration of a dispersible DRV/COBI 600/90 mg FDC film-coated tablet for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in paediatric patients aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg, who are either ART-naïve or ART-experienced.

The following wording is proposed by the Applicant for the dispersible tablet formulation:

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

REZOLSTA is indicated, in combination with other antiretroviral medicinal products, for the treatment of human immunodeficiency virus 1 (HIV 1) infection in adults and paediatric patients (aged 3 years and older, weighing at least 15 kg).

Genotypic testing should guide the use of REZOLSTA (see sections 4.2, 4.4 and 5.1).

## 9.1.2. Available therapies and unmet medical need

As for adults, HIV infection is a life-threatening condition in the paediatric population. Lifelong treatment is needed, and although treatment options have markedly improved the last decades, there remains a need to expand the available treatment options for children by a.o. additional ageappropriate formulations.

The recommended (collaborative Penta/EACS HIV treatment guideline, 2022) initial treatment of HIV-1 infection for paediatric patients is therapy with two NRTIs in combination with a drug from a different class (third agent). DTG is the preferred third agent in all children over 4 weeks of age and 3 kg. Whilst 'preferred options' are recommended, 'alternative options' are acceptable and remain important choices in settings where ART availability is limited or in individuals at particular risk of specific toxicity or DDIs.

The choice of HIV-1 therapy depends on various factors including the availability of age-appropriate formulation, ease of use, age/developmental stage of the patient, prior exposure (maternal or prevention), adherence, and in adolescent girls, the risk of pregnancy.

The goal of combination ART in paediatric patients is to achieve and sustain HIV-1 virologic suppression, preserve/restore immune function, minimize drug toxicity, prevent drug resistance, and ultimately lead to normal growth and neurocognitive development.

People living with HIV-1 who are aware of their status, take ART as prescribed, and get and keep an undetectable viral load can live healthy lives.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 9.2. Main clinical studies

For a detailed description of the main clinical studies supporting this application, please refer to section 5.3.2. of this document.

The proposed EoI and new pharmaceutical formulation is supported by data from Cohort 3 from Study GS-US-216-0128 (also referred to as 216-0128), which is an ongoing, Phase 2/3, multicentre, openlabel, multicohort study evaluating PK, safety, and efficacy of COBI-boosted ATV, COBI-boosted DRV, and F/TAF in HIV1 infected children and adolescents aged ≥4 weeks to &lt;18 years . In Cohort 3, 11 ART-experienced, virologically suppressed children with HIV-1 aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg were enrolled who received DRV (600 mg qd) and COBI (90 mg qd) as separate formulations with F/TAF FDC (120/15 mg qd).

## 9.3. Favourable effects

The efficacy of Rezolsta has been established in adult and paediatric patients 6 years of age and older. The efficacy demonstration in children is based on a PK bridge, inferred through similar exposure as in adults.

PK data from Cohort 3 in study 0128 showed that for paediatric patients in the weight band ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg a DRV/COBI regimen of 600/90 mg qd results in similar DRV trough values (C0h) as compared to adults and other treatment groups with DRV combinations in children, which is the driver for efficacy.

Ten out of eleven (10/11, 90.9%) participants in Cohort 3 remained virologically suppressed and none experienced virologic failure.

The acceptability and swallowability of the dispersible tablet were considered adequate.

## 9.3.1. Uncertainties and limitations about favourable effects

All 11 participants enrolled in Cohort 3 received F/TAF next to DRV/Cobi. As such, no information is available for other treatment combinations.

## 9.4. Unfavourable effects

Total exposure to DRV (AUC0-24h using PopPK) for children in the 15-25 kg range may be 59% higher as compared to children in Cohort 2 (25-40 kg range), 67% higher as in adults (HTX3001 reference population) and 22% higher than same children category in the C228 substudy (DRV/rtv combination). Comparing Cohort 3 DRV PK to the PK substudy of C228, NCA-derived PK exposures (Ctrough, AUCtau, and Cmax) were 1.4- to 1.6-fold higher.

The COBI peak exposure in children aged 3 to 6 years in Cohort 3 of study 216-0128 (mean Cmax,ss of 2077 ng/mL) is 1.4-fold higher compared to the same age (BW) group on Genvoya. Genvoya (EVG/C/F/TAF) has been approved in the paediatric age groups based on study GS-US-292-0106, with a mean AUC0-24h,ss of 14485 ng.h/mL and Cmax,ss of 1526 ng/mL for the 14-25 kg BW cohort.

The total daily exposure to COBI in children aged 3 to 6 years in Cohort 3 of study 216-0128 (mean AUC0-24h,ss of 20418 ng.h/mL) is more than 2-fold higher compared with that in adolescents and adults, and also still 1.4-fold higher than in other children cohorts.

No deaths, AEs leading to premature study drug discontinuation, SAEs, or Grade ≥3 AEs were reported. AEs considered related to study drug were reported in 3 participants:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

-  1 participant experienced Grade 1 vomiting
-  1 participant experienced Grade 1 fungal skin infection and Grade 1 upper respiratory tract infection
-  1 participant experienced Grade 1 abdominal pain, Grade 1 vomiting, and Grade 1 upper respiratory tract infection

Vomiting and abdominal pain are already listed as adverse reactions in section 4.8 of the SmPC.

Overall, no new ADRs were identified.

## 9.4.1. Uncertainties and limitations about unfavourable effects

Exposure is too limited to reliably assess the safety profile in paediatric patients ≥3 years of age. Rather, safety is inferred based on similar exposures as observed in adults and adolescents.

The Applicant explained that, based on all available data, the observed differences in DRV/COBI exposure for children weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg in Study GS-US-216-0128 are not considered clinically relevant. This is based on the following rational:

- Population PK derived DRV exposures in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were within the range of those previously observed in adult and paediatric patients, especially with bid DRV dosing, and which were found to be safe and efficacious (Figure 11 and Figure 12).
- COBI exposures in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were in line with those previously observed in the E/C/F/TAF paediatric Study GS-US-292-0106 (Cohort 2 and Cohort 3, Table 20).
- Based on this, COBI 90 mg qd (as part of the E/C/F/TAF FDC) was approved for the same paediatric population as is the basis of this submission, i.e. children weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg.
- Throughout the development of DRV (boosted with either COBI or rtv), there have been no indications for an exposure-response relationship for any AEs.
- Similarly, COBI Phase 2 (GS US-216-0105) and Phase 3 (GS-US-216-0114) studies using population PK model-derived COBI exposures versus safety parameters revealed no relevant exposure-AE trends.
- Finally, juvenile toxicity studies showed that the toxicological profile of DRV and COBI was comparable to that in adult rats.

The rational and conclusions of the Applicant can be followed. Although DRV AUC0-24h was higher in Cohort 3 vs other studies, the values were still in the same range as those included in the exposuresafety analysis which was submitted as part of the original MAA for Rezolsta. As there was no exposure-safety relation observed, it can be agreed that the increased exposures seen in paediatric patients &gt;=14 to &lt;25 kg are not likely to be clinically relevant. However, as it cannot be excluded that there are safety aspects associated with this higher exposure that are specific to the paediatric population, i.e. it is uncertain whether the exposure-safety relationship is the same for paediatrics and adults, the Applicant should further follow this post approval (via routine pharmacovigilance activities).

Similarly, COBI exposure (mainly Cmax and Ctrough) was higher in study 0128 Cohort 3 vs E/C/F/TAF Cohort 3. Although there is no direct concern based on the information provided by the Applicant and the absence of an exposure-safety correlation in adults, also here this may be different in paediatrics and close attention of any safety signals is warranted.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Further, the Applicant provided quantitative and graphical comparison of the PK parameters of F, TAF and TFV for Cohort 3 as compared to the other paediatric cohorts and adults. From these data it can be concluded that the FTC (dosed at 120 mg) and TAF (dosed at 15 mg) levels for the combination of F/TAF with DRV/COBI in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 are overall similar to those observed for Cohort 2 and other paediatric cohorts across studies (but at least 2-fold higher than those in adults).

Notably, the TFV exposures from Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were similar to those from Cohort 2 of Study GS-US-216-0128, but approximately 3-fold higher than those from the same age/body weight group receiving B/F/TAF or E/C/F/TAF. However, these TFV levels were still 4- to 8fold lower than those from adults receiving E/C/F/TDF. This should be kept in mind when interpreting the safety information.

## 9.6. Benefit-risk assessment and discussion

## 9.6.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

The extrapolation of efficacy and safety from adults to paediatric patients is in accordance with regulatory practice in the field of HIV. The PD target is expected to respond in the same manner to similar plasma concentrations of the HIV antivirals in both adult and paediatric patients.

Overall, both the DRV as well as the COBI exposure data, based on PopPK modelling and on NCA for the paediatric population, for the proposed regimen indicate higher exposure values in children aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg as compared to adults and other children cohorts with DRV combination treatment. Although there is no direct concern based on the information provided by the Applicant, it cannot be excluded that there are safety aspects associated with this higher exposure that are specific to the paediatric population. This should be monitored post approval. Also because the exposures of TAF/TFV were higher in children aged ≥ 3 years and weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg than those from the same age/body weight group receiving B/F/TAF or E/C/F/TAF. The observation that TFV levels remained well below those obtained in the E/C/F/TDF studies in adults (approximately 4- to 8-fold lower) is however reassuring.

The acceptability and swallowability of the dispersible tablet were considered adequate.

The safety assessment of study 216-0128 Cohort 3 is too limited to reliably assess the safety profile in paediatric patients ≥3 years of age but safety is inferred based on similar exposures as observed in adults and adolescents and in itself does not give rise to new safety concerns. No cases of virologic failure have been seen. The development of resistance mutations is important, as these may also have an impact on future ARV treatment options. Moreover, as the target population are growing children, potential effects on growth or (pubertal) development are also of importance.

## 9.6.2. Balance of benefits and risks

The overall data presented is supportive of the extension of the therapeutic indication of Rezolsta.

The overall benefit /risk balance of Rezolsta in the sought indication is positive.
