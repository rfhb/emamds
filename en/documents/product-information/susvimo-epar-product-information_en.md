This medicinal product is subject to additional monitoring. This will allow quick identification of new safety information. Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions. See section 4.8 for how to report adverse reactions.

## 1. NAME OF THE MEDICINAL PRODUCT

Susvimo 100 mg/mL solution for injection

## 2. QUALITATIVE AND QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITION

One mL solution contains 100 mg ranibizumab*.

Each vial contains 39.5 mg of ranibizumab in 0.395 mL solution. This provides a usable fill volume of 0.1 mL, which is required to deliver a single dose of 0.02 mL solution containing 2 mg of ranibizumab into the Contivue ocular implant.

* Ranibizumab is a humanised monoclonal antibody fragment produced inE. coliexpression vector by standard recombinant DNA technology.

## Excipient with known effect

Each 0.02 mL solution contains 0.002 mg polysorbate 20.

For the full list of excipients, see section 6.1.

## 3. PHARMACEUTICAL FORM

Solution for injection (injection).

Clear to slightly opalescent, colourless to slightly brownish solution.

## 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

Susvimo is indicated in adult patients for the treatment of neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) who have achieved stable disease after previously responding to intravitreal treatment with a vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitor medicinal product (see section 4.2).

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration

Susvimo is intended for continuous release into the vitreous via the Contivue implant only. The initial fill and implant procedure and implant removal procedure must be performed in an operating room setting by an ophthalmologist experienced in vitreoretinal surgery and trained in the implant procedures. The implant refill-exchange procedure should be performed under strict aseptic conditions in an outpatient setting by an ophthalmologist trained in the implant refill-exchange procedure.

## Patient selection

Treatment with Susvimo is initiated when disease stability after intravitreal anti-VEGF medicinal product is achieved as determined by the treating physician based on anatomic and/or visual parameters.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Posology

The recommended dose of Susvimo is 2 mg (0.02 mL) delivered via the Contivue implant with refill-exchange procedures administered every 24 weeks (approximately 6 months).

Susvimo must not be substituted for or with other formulations of ranibizumab.

## Supplemental dosing with intravitreal ranibizumab injection

Supplemental treatment with 0.5 mg (10 mg/mL) intravitreal ranibizumab injection may be administered in the affected eye with the Contivue implant in place if clinically necessary, based on significant decrease or reduction in visual acuity and/or extensive worsening of anatomical parameters, to reduce the risk of vision loss (see section 5.1).

Refer to the full product information for 0.5 mg (10 mg/mL) intravitreal ranibizumab injection.

## Duration of treatment

Duration of treatment with Susvimo should be determined by the physician and should be based on disease activity, as assessed by visual acuity and/or anatomical parameters. If, in the physician´s judgement, visual and anatomical parameters indicate that the patient is no longer benefiting from continued treatment, Susvimo refill-exchange procedures may be discontinued. The Contivue implant may be removed from the eye if deemed clinically necessary by the physician.

## Implant removal and reimplantation

Implant removal may be performed at the physician's discretion, if the benefit of removal is considered to outweigh the risks of the procedure. The decision should follow a comprehensive discussion between the physician and patient about the ocular risks associated with undergoing an implant removal procedure. The benefits and risks of retaining, removing, or removing and replacing an implant are not well characterised. Refer to the device manufacturer's implant removal procedure instructions for use.

## Delayed or missed doses (refill-exchange)

If a planned dose (refill-exchange) of Susvimo is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible and the subsequent refill-exchange procedure should be performed 24 weeks (approximately 6 months) thereafter.

## Special populations

## Elderly

No dose adjustment is required in patients ≥ 65 years of age (see sections 5.1 and 5.2).

## Renal impairment

Dose adjustment is not required in patients with renal impairment (see section 5.2).

## Hepatic impairment

Dose adjustment is not required in patients with hepatic impairment (see section 5.2).

## Paediatric population

There is no relevant use of Susvimo in the paediatric population in the treatment of neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).

## Method of administration

Susvimo is a single-dose vial for intravitreal use with the Contivue implant only.

In order to prevent medication errors, it is important to check the labels to ensure that the medicinal product being prepared and administered is Susvimo.

Susvimo should be inspected visually for particulate matter and discolouration prior to administration (see section 6.6).

Do not administer Susvimo by any route other than via the Contivue implant (e.g. not as a bolus intravitreal injection).

The initial fill and implant, and refill-exchange procedures should be performed with adequate anaesthesia under aseptic conditions to minimise the risk of intraocular infection as per standard of care.

## Initial fill and implant procedure

During the initial fill and implant procedure, a 1 mL Luer Lock Tip syringe is used to withdraw all of the medicinal product from the vial through a transfer needle (not included). Air is then expelled from the syringe, and the transfer needle is removed. The initial fill needle (blue cap) is then attached to the syringe and used to fill the implant with 0.02 mL of Susvimo (ranibizumab) solution for injection immediately prior to implant insertion. Once the implant is filled, it is inserted into the eye through the pars plana.

## Implant refill-exchange procedure

The Contivue implant is refilled with 0.02 mL of replacement Susvimo (ranibizumab) solution for injection via administration through the implant septum, which is covered by the conjunctiva and Tenon's capsule. A 1 mL Luer Lock Tip syringe is used to withdraw all of the medicinal product from the vial through a transfer needle (not included). Air is then expelled from the syringe, and the transfer needle is removed. The refill needle (clear cap) is attached to the syringe, and the plunger rod is pushed until all air is expelled from the syringe and the dose is adjusted to 0.1 mL. The refill needle is then inserted into the implant to exchange its contents with replacement Susvimo (ranibizumab).

For instructions on preparation before administration and disposal, see section 6.6.

## 4.3 Contraindications

Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1.

Active or suspected ocular or periocular infections.

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

## Traceability

In order to improve the traceability of biological medicinal products, the name and the batch number of the administered product should be clearly recorded.

## Educational materials

All physicians who are expected to prescribe Susvimo must ensure they have received and are familiar with the physician guide. Physicians must explain and discuss the benefits and risks of Susvimo therapy with the patient and provide them with the patient educational materials.

## Bilateral treatment

In clinical trials, the majority of patients with neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) in the fellow eye received treatment with an anti-VEGF intravitreal injection and a limited number of patients received Susvimo in the fellow eye. Due to the small number of patients with bilateral treatment with Susvimo to date, the safety and efficacy of Susvimo administered in both eyes have not been established.

## Implant and implant-related procedure adverse reactions

The implant and/or implant-related procedures have been associated with vitreous haemorrhage, conjunctival bleb/bleb leak, conjunctival erosion, conjunctival retraction, endophthalmitis, retinal tear and retinal detachment, traumatic cataract, device (implant) dislocation, septum dislodgement and post-surgical intraocular inflammation. All of these adverse reactions were reported during the clinical trials (see section 4.8). Patients should be instructed to report signs or symptoms, if any, that could be associated with these events without delay. In some cases, these events can present asymptomatically. The implant and the tissue overlying the implant flange should be examined carefully following the initial fill and implant and refill-exchange procedures to permit early medical or surgical intervention as necessary.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use for the initial fill and implant procedure, refill-exchange procedure and the implant removal procedure is required to minimise procedure-related risks.

Patients should be instructed not to push on the eye, rub the eye or touch the area of the eye where the implant is located for 30 days after the implant is inserted and for 7 days following the refill.

Following an implant insertion or implant removal procedure, the patient must wear a protective eye shield, and should be instructed not to remove the eye shield until advised by their physician (see section 4.7). Patients should wear the eye shield at bedtime for at least 7 nights after surgery.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Vitreous haemorrhage

Vitreous haemorrhage may result in temporary vision loss. Vitrectomy may be needed in the case of a non-clearing vitreous haemorrhage (see section 4.2).

Patients on antithrombotic medicinal products (e.g. oral anticoagulants, acetylsalicylic acid, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medicinal products) may be at increased risk of vitreous haemorrhage. Antithrombotic medicinal products are recommended to be temporarily interrupted prior to the implant insertion procedure.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. appropriate pars plana laser ablation, scleral cauterisation) is required to minimise risks of vitreous haemorrhage.

## Conjunctival bleb/bleb leak

A conjunctival bleb/bleb leak is an encapsulated elevation of the conjunctiva above the implant flange, which may be secondary to subconjunctival thickening or fluid. Conjunctival blebs/bleb leaks may require additional surgical and/or medical management to avoid further complications, especially if the implant septum is no longer identifiable due to the conjunctival bleb/bleb leak.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. appropriate scleral incision length, appropriate incorporation of conjunctiva and Tenon's capsule in peritomy closure, appropriate seating of the refill needle during refill-exchange procedure) is required to minimise risks of conjunctival blebs.

## Conjunctival erosion

A conjunctival erosion is a full thickness degradation or breakdown of the conjunctiva in the area of the implant flange. Conjunctival erosions could be associated with an increased risk of endophthalmitis, especially if the implant becomes exposed. Surgical intervention (e.g. conjunctival/Tenon's capsule repair) should be performed in case of conjunctival erosion with or without exposure of the implant flange.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. appropriate incorporation of conjunctiva and Tenon's capsule in peritomy closure, appropriate placement of conjunctival sutures to avoid contact with the implant edge) is required to minimise risks of conjunctival erosion.

## Conjunctival retraction

A conjunctival retraction is a recession or opening of the limbal and/or radial peritomy. Conjunctival retractions could be associated with an increased risk of endophthalmitis, especially if the implant becomes exposed. Surgical intervention (e.g. conjunctival/Tenon's capsule repair) should be performed in case of conjunctival retraction with or without exposure of the implant flange.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. including appropriate suturing of the conjunctiva and Tenon's capsule to the limbus) is required to minimise risks of conjunctival retraction.

## Endophthalmitis

Endophthalmitis is a serious complication of ocular procedures and requires prompt treatment as per standard of care to reduce the risk of vision loss and to maximise recovery. The incidence of endophthalmitis following Contivue procedures was 1.6 %, compared to 0.6 % for intravitreal injections of ranibizumab in the ARCHWAY study (see section 4.8). The Susvimo dose (refill-exchange) should be withheld until resolution of endophthalmitis (see section 4.2).

Patients should not have an active or suspected ocular or periocular infection or severe systemic infection prior to any implant procedure. Proper aseptic technique should always be used when administering Susvimo.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. appropriate sterile controls during implant insertion or refill-exchange procedures) is required to minimise risks of endophthalmitis (see section 4.8).

## Retinal tear and retinal detachment

Retinal tear and retinal detachment may result in vision loss if not promptly treated with an intervention (e.g. pneumatic retinopexy, vitrectomy or laser photocoagulation). Delay the Susvimo dose (refill-exchange) until the retinal tear or detachment has been treated (see section 4.2).

Careful evaluation of the retinal periphery by scleral indentation should be performed and any suspected areas of abnormal vitreo-retinal adhesion or retinal tears should be treated before inserting the implant in the eye. After insertion, the retinal periphery should be examined as per standard of care.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. appropriate management of vitreous prolapse after pars plana incision) is required to minimise the risks of retinal tear or retinal detachment.

## Device (implant) dislocation

The device (implant) may dislocate or subluxate into the vitreous cavity or extend outside the vitreous cavity into or beyond the subconjunctival space. Device dislocation requires urgent surgical intervention if it is associated with a positive Seidel test, retinal detachment, appositional choroidal detachment, conjunctival opening with vitreous leak and implant expulsion (open globe), or the dislocated device is resting on or near the macula, to prevent vision loss. Patients should be instructed to avoid high risk activities including pushing, rubbing or touching their eye where the implant is located. The implant should be monitored per standard of care.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. appropriate scleral incision length, appropriate targeting of the pars plana during laser ablation) is required to minimise risks of implant dislocation.

## Septum dislodgement

In clinical trials, a type of implant damage where the septum has dislodged into the body of the implant has been reported. A dilated slit lamp exam and/or dilated indirect ophthalmoscopy should be performed, to inspect the implant in the vitreous cavity through the pupil prior to and after the refill-exchange procedure to identify if septum dislodgement has occurred. Discontinue treatment with Susvimo following septum dislodgement and consider implant removal should the benefit of the removal procedure outweigh the risk (see section 4.2). The benefits and risks of retaining, removing, or removing and replacing an implant with a dislodged septum are not well characterised.

Strict adherence to the device manufacturer's instructions for use (e.g. appropriate handling and insertion of the refill needle into the septum (avoid twisting and/or rotation)) is required to minimise the risk of septum dislodgement.

## Intraocular inflammation

There is a risk of intraocular inflammation following Contivue procedures (e.g. implantation, refillexchange), consistent with the risks inherent to intraocular procedures. The majority of events experienced during clinical trials occurred during the first week following Contivue procedures and resolved within one month. Use peri-operative anti-inflammatory medicinal products per standard of care and by physician discretion. Patients should be instructed to report any signs or symptoms of intraocular inflammation to their physician.

## Hypersensitivity

Patients should be instructed to report any signs or symptoms of potential ocular hypersensitivity to their physician.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Systemic effects

Systemic adverse events, including non-ocular haemorrhages and arterial thromboembolic events (ATEs), have been reported following intravitreal injection of VEGF inhibitors, or with Susvimo treatment (see section 4.8). Through week 96 of the ARCHWAY clinical trial, 12 patients (4.8 %) in the Susvimo arm and 4 patients (2.4 %) in the intravitreal arm experienced an APTC event, typically in the presence of pre-existing comorbidities.

## Other

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scans should be performed under specific conditions as the implant contains titanium. It is, therefore, important that patients are informed and are given an implant card which should be kept with them at all times and shown to healthcare professionals if they require an MRI scan. The device manufacturer's instructions for use for the initial fill and implant procedure provide the scan conditions to safely perform an MRI scan for a patient with this implanted device.

## Information about excipients

This medicinal product contains 0.002 mg polysorbate 20 per 0.02 mL dose. Polysorbates may cause allergic reactions. Patients need to be instructed to tell their doctor if they have any known allergies.

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

No interaction studies have been performed.

Adjunctive use of verteporfin photodynamic therapy (PDT) and Susvimo has not been studied.

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation

## Women of childbearing potential and contraception

Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 12 months following the last dose (refill-exchange) of Susvimo.

## Pregnancy

There are no data from the use of ranibizumab in pregnant women.

Studies in animals have shown reproductive toxicity. A study in pregnant cynomolgus monkeys given intravitreal ranibizumab resulted in skeletal abnormalities in foetuses (see section 5.3).

The systemic exposure to ranibizumab is low after administration via the Contivue implant, but due to its mechanism of action, Susvimo must be regarded as potentially teratogenic and embryo-foetotoxic. Therefore, Susvimo should not be used during pregnancy unless the expected benefit outweighs the potential risk to the foetus. For women who wish to become pregnant and have been treated with Susvimo, it is recommended to flush the implant with preservative-free sodium chloride solution and wait at least 3 months after the implant flush before conceiving a child.

## Breast-feeding

Based on very limited data, ranibizumab may be excreted in human milk at low levels. The effect of ranibizumab on a breast-fed newborn/infant is unknown. Breast-feeding is not recommended during the use of Susvimo.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Fertility

There are no data available on fertility. Based on the anti-VEGF mechanism of action for ranibizumab, treatment with Susvimo may pose a risk to reproductive capacity (see section 5.3).

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines

Susvimo, Contivue, implant-related procedures and associated examinations have a moderate influence on the ability to drive and use machines. Patients may experience temporarily reduced vision after implant insertion, refill-exchange and implant removal procedures and the associated eye examination (see section 4.8). Patients should not drive or use machines until the eye shield, used following an implant insertion or implant removal procedure, can be removed and visual function has recovered (see section 4.4).

## 4.8 Undesirable effects

## Summary of the safety profile

## The safety profile of Susvimo

This safety profile includes adverse reactions related to Susvimo, Contivue, the initial fill and implant, refill-exchange and implant removal procedures.

The most frequent adverse reactions overall were conjunctival haemorrhage (72.1 %), conjunctival hyperaemia (25.6 %), iritis (25 %), eye pain (20.6 %), cataract (19.1 %), conjunctival bleb/filtering bleb leak (14.9 %), vitreous floaters (13.5 %), and headache (11.6 %).

The most frequent adverse reactions during the post-operative period (within 37 days of initial implantation) were conjunctival haemorrhage (65.9 %), conjunctival hyperaemia (21.5 %), iritis (20.8 %) and eye pain (12 %).

The most frequent serious adverse reactions overall were septum dislodgement (10.1 %), conjunctival erosion (5.2 %), endophthalmitis (2.6 %), device dislocation (1.9 %), conjunctival retraction (1.9 %), conjunctival bleb/filtering bleb leak (1.7 %), cataract (1.4 %) and retinal detachment (1.2 %).

Adverse reactions that resulted in implant removal were septum dislodgement (5 %), conjunctival erosion (1.6 %), conjunctival retraction (1.2 %), endophthalmitis (1 %) and device dislocation (0.9 %).

## The safety profile of intravitreal ranibizumab injections

In the intravitreal injection arm of the ARCHWAY clinical trial (see section 5.1), the ocular adverse events in the study eye with the highest incidence were conjunctival haemorrhage (13.8 %), vitreous detachment (8.4 %), eye pain (7.8 %), dry eye (5.4 %), vision blurred (5.4 %) and eye irritation (5.4 %).

Non-ocular adverse events with the highest incidence in the intravitreal injection arm of the ARCHWAY clinical trial were nasopharyngitis (15 %), fall (9 %), urinary tract infection (8.4 %), arthralgia (6.6 %) and bronchitis (6 %); other non-ocular adverse events observed included allergic reactions * (rash (1.8 %), pruritus (0.6 %)) (2.4 %), anaemia (1.8 %), hypersensitivity (1.8 %), cough (1.8 %), and anxiety (1.2 %).

* No events for urticaria or erythema were retrieved from the data analysed for allergic reactions.

## Description of selected adverse reactions

## Vitreous haemorrhage

Overall, 7.5 % (43/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced vitreous haemorrhage in the study eye. The majority of affected patients (31/43) experienced Grade 1 (≤ 15 letter BCVA loss from previous visit)or Grade 2 (16-30 letter BCVA loss from previous visit) events. 10 patients and 2 patients experienced a Grade 3 (&gt;30 letters of BCVA loss from previous visit) and Grade 4 (light perception or worse) event, respectively. Of the 43 affected patients, 40 patients experienced an event that resolved, 1 patient experienced an event that resolved with sequelae, 1 patient experienced an event that was resolving, and 2 patients experienced an event that was unresolved. 34/43 patients experienced events that resolved spontaneously without medical or surgical intervention, 4 patients required a vitrectomy.

During the post-operative period, 4.5 % (26/577) of patients experienced vitreous haemorrhage. These events were attributed to the implant procedure in all 26 patients, with 7 patients having additional contributing factors. Of the affected 26 patients, 14 experienced Grade 1 events, 5 experienced Grade 2 events, 6 experienced Grade 3 events, and 1 experienced a Grade 4 event. All these events resolved.

No patients discontinued Susvimo treatment or had their implant removed due to vitreous haemorrhage.

## Conjunctival bleb/Conjunctival filtering bleb leak

Overall, 14.9 % (86/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced conjunctival bleb/conjunctival filtering bleb leak in the study eye, with 5 % (29/577) of patients experiencing these events during the post-operative period. The majority of affected patients experienced mild (72/86) or moderate (10/86) events, and 4 patients experienced severe events. Of the 86 affected patients, 78 patients experienced an event that resolved, 1 patient experienced an event that resolved with sequelae, and 13 patients experienced events that were unresolved. Overall, 8/86 patients experienced an event that required surgical intervention.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Conjunctival erosion

Overall, 8.7 % (50/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced conjunctival erosion in the study eye, with 0.3 % (2/577) of patients experiencing these events during the post-operative period. The majority of affected patients experienced mild (16/50) or moderate (23/50) events, and 11 patients experienced a severe event. Surgical intervention was required for a conjunctival erosion event in 47 patients. Of the 50 affected patients, 49 patients experienced an event that resolved, while 2 patients experienced events that were unresolved. 7/50 patients discontinued Susvimo treatment due to conjunctival erosion. Implant removal was necessary in 8/50 patients due to conjunctival erosion, with 2 patients having additional contributing factors.

## Conjunctival retraction

Overall, 3.5 % (20/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced conjunctival retraction in the study eye, with 0.7 % (4/577) of patients experiencing these events during the postoperative period. The majority of affected patients experienced mild (9/20) or moderate (9/20) events, and 2 patients experienced a severe event. Surgical intervention was required for a conjunctival retraction event in 14 patients. Of the 20 affected patients, all patients experienced events that resolved. 6/20 patients discontinued Susvimo treatment due to conjunctival retraction. Implant removal was necessary in 6/20 patients due to conjunctival retraction, with 1 patient having additional contributing factors.

## Endophthalmitis

Overall, 2.8 % (16/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced endophthalmitis in the study eye. Of the affected patients, 4 patients experienced moderate events, and 12 patients experienced a severe event. Of these patients, 8/16 experienced preceding or concurrent conjunctival retraction or conjunctival erosion events. Surgical intervention was required for an endophthalmitis event in 12 patients. Of the 16 affected patients, 14 patients experienced an event that resolved, 2 patients experienced an event that resolved with sequelae, and 1 patient experienced an event that was unresolved. 8/16 patients discontinued Susvimo treatment due to endophthalmitis. Implant removal was necessary in 8/16 patients due to endophthalmitis, with 1 patient having additional contributing factors.

During the comparator period of the ARCHWAY clinical trial, the incidence of endophthalmitis was 1.6 % (4/248) in the Susvimo arm compared to 0.6 % (1/167) in the intravitreal ranibizumab arm. In clinical trials the management of endophthalmitis included a flush of implant contents with 100 µL of vancomycin (1.0 mg/0.1 mL), with or without previous flush with preservative-free sodium chloride solution, in addition to standard of care.

## Retinal tear and retinal detachment

Overall, 0.7 % (4/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced retinal tear in the study eye, with 0.2 % (1/577) of patients experiencing this event (mild) during the post-operative period. In the 4 affected patients, the events were mild (2 patients) and moderate (2 patients), and 2 patients experienced events that resolved, while 2 patients experienced events that were unresolved.

Overall, 1.6 % (9/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced retinal detachment in the study eye, with 0.3 % (2/577) of patients experiencing these events during the post-operative period. All 9 patients with retinal detachment experienced severe events, which were managed with surgical intervention. Of the 9 affected patients, 7 patients experienced an event that resolved, 1 patient experienced an event that resolved with sequelae, 1 patient experienced an event that was unresolved, and 1 patient experienced an event that was resolving. Implant removal was necessary in 1/9 patients due to retinal detachment, this patient discontinued Susvimo treatment.

## Device (implant) dislocation

Overall, 2.4 % (14/577) of patients in the Susvimo arm experienced device dislocation in the study eye. The majority of affected patients experienced mild (3/14) or moderate (6/14) events, and 5 patients experienced severe events. Surgical intervention was required for a device location event in

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

13 patients, including all severe cases. Of the 14 affected patients, 13 patients experienced an event that resolved and 1 patient experienced an event that was unresolved. 8/14 patients discontinued Susvimo treatment, and implant removal was necessary in 9/14 patients, due to device dislocation.

## Arterial thromboembolic events

Arterial thromboembolic events (ATEs) are adverse events potentially related to systemic VEGF inhibition. Through week 96 of the ARCHWAY clinical trial, 12 patients (4.8 %) in the Susvimo arm and 4 patients (2.4 %) in the intravitreal arm experienced an APTC event, typically in the presence of pre-existing comorbidities.

## 5. PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Ophthalmologicals, antineovascularisation agents, ATC code: S01LA04

## Mechanism of action

Ranibizumab is a humanised recombinant monoclonal antibody fragment targeted against human vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A). It binds with high affinity to all biologically active VEGF-A isoforms (e.g. VEGF110, VEGF121 and VEGF165), thereby preventing binding of VEGF-A to its receptors VEGFR-1 and VEGFR-2.

Binding of VEGF-A to its receptors leads to endothelial cell proliferation and neovascularisation, as well as vascular leakage, which contribute to the progression of the neovascular form of age-related macular degeneration.

## Immunogenicity

As with all therapeutic proteins, there is the potential for an immune response to Susvimo. Prior to treatment with Susvimo, 2.1 % (5/243) of patients with nAMD tested positive for anti-ranibizumab antibodies. After treatment with Susvimo, 13.4 % (33/247) of patients tested positive for antiranibizumab antibodies.

No clinically meaningful differences in the pharmacokinetics, efficacy or safety were observed in patients testing positive for anti-ranibizumab antibodies in clinical trials.

## Clinical efficacy and safety

The clinical efficacy and safety of Susvimo were assessed in a randomised, visual assessor-masked, open-label active treatment-controlled study (ARCHWAY) in patients with nAMD. A total of

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

415 patients (248 in the Susvimo arm and 167 in the intravitreal ranibizumab arm) were enrolled and treated in this study.

Patients were diagnosed with any type of macular choroidal neovascularisation (CNV) related to nAMD within the 9 months prior to screening and received ≥ 3 doses of anti-VEGF intravitreal agents in the study eye within the last 6 months prior to screening. At the screening visit, patients received an additional intravitreal injection of 0.5 mg ranibizumab if they received only 3 doses of anti-VEGF intravitreal agents within the last 6 months and/or if the last treatment received prior to screening was not 0.5 mg ranibizumab. Each patient had a demonstrated response to anti-VEGF intravitreal agents defined as: overall decrease in nAMD disease activity detected on spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) and stable or improved vision.

Patients were randomised in a 3:2 ratio to receive continuous delivery of Susvimo via the implant every 24 weeks or intravitreal injections of 0.5 mg ranibizumab every 4 weeks. For patients randomised to the Susvimo arm, supplemental treatment with 0.5 mg intravitreal ranibizumab injections was available at weeks 16, 20, 40, 44, 64, 68, 88, and 92 if one of the following criteria was met and due to nAMD disease activity: a loss of 15 early treatment diabetic retinopathy study (ETDRS) letters from the best recorded BCVA value on study, an increase of 150 microns from the lowest central subfield thickness (CST) value on study, or a loss of 10 letters from the highest BCVA value associated with an increase of 100 microns from the lowest CST value.

The baseline demographics were generally balanced across treatment groups; 43/415 (10.4%) patients were younger than 65 years of age and the mean [SD] age was 75 [7.91] years. The majority of patients were female (59 %). The mean [SD] BCVA score at baseline was 74.8 [10.4] letters.

Prior to study treatment, a median of 4 doses of anti-VEGF intravitreal agents were administered in the study eye of the patients in the Susvimo and intravitreal ranibizumab arms. The mean [SD] change in visual acuity in the study eye from diagnosis of nAMD to baseline was 11.3 [13.8] letters. Of the patients assessed for supplemental treatment in the Susvimo arm, 98.4 %, 94.6 %, 94.8 % and 94.7 % did not receive supplemental treatment in the first, second, third and fourth treatment intervals, respectively. Across all four treatment intervals, 31/246 patients (12.6%) received supplemental treatment, and 215/246 patients (87.4%) did not receive supplemental treatment. Of the 31 patients, 18 patients (58.1%) received only one supplemental treatment.

## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

## Absorption

Susvimo delivered via the Contivue implant provides continuous delivery of ranibizumab into the vitreous, with the release of ranibizumab decreasing over time as the concentration in the implant decreases. The observed serum and observed aqueous humour ranibizumab concentrations with Susvimo are maintained below the maximum and above the minimum concentrations achieved with monthly 0.5 mg intravitreal ranibizumab.

In the ARCHWAY clinical trial, the mean (± SD) maximum ranibizumab serum concentration (Cmax) was 0.48 (± 0.17) ng/mL and median (range) time to maximum serum concentration (Tmax) was 26 (1 - 89) days following implant insertion. Following the initial fill and refill-exchange procedures with Susvimo in patients with nAMD, maximum serum concentrations of ranibizumab were below the ranibizumab concentration necessary to inhibit the biological activity of VEGF by 50 %.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Distribution

Ranibizumab is distributed from the vitreous to the aqueous humour and serum. Based on population pharmacokinetics analysis, the ranibizumab concentrations in aqueous humour decrease with a half-life of approximately 15 weeks and the ranibizumab serum concentrations decrease with a half-life of approximately 29 weeks. Ranibizumab did not accumulate in serum when administered with refill-exchange procedures every 24 weeks.

## Biotransformation and elimination

The metabolism of Susvimo has not been directly studied. Susvimo is a monoclonal antibody fragment. Antibodies are cleared principally by catabolism.

## Special populations

## Elderly

In the population pharmacokinetic analysis of nAMD patients with Susvimo, the age of patients ranged from 50 to 92 years old. Age did not have a significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of Susvimo.

## Renal impairment

No formal studies have been conducted to examine the pharmacokinetics of Susvimo in patients with renal impairment. In a population pharmacokinetic analysis of nAMD patients with Susvimo, 73 % (266 of 364) had renal impairment (44 % mild [CrCL 60 to &lt; 90 mL/min], 27 % moderate [CrCL 30 to &lt; 60 mL/min] and 2 % severe [CrCL &lt; 30 mL/min]). This analysis showed that systemic clearance of ranibizumab was slightly lower in renally impaired patients but was not clinically significant.

## Hepatic impairment

No formal studies have been conducted to examine the pharmacokinetics of Susvimo in patients with hepatic impairment. However, systemic exposure of Susvimo is low, with concentrations remaining below the ranibizumab concentration necessary to inhibit the biological activity of VEGF by 50 % (11-27 ng/mL). Therefore, no dose adjustment is considered necessary in this population (see section 4.2).

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data

Non-clinical data reveal no special hazards for humans based on findings via intravitreal injection in cynomolgus monkeys or continuous delivery via the ocular implant in minipigs. Observed ocular effects (inflammation, cataracts, fibrosis, retinal detachment, optic nerve traction, swelling and photoreceptor nuclei drop down) were attributed to species-specific immune responses to the heterologous protein (evidenced by systemic anti-ranibizumab antibodies) and are not considered relevant to clinical use.

## Carcinogenicity and genotoxicity

No studies have been performed to establish the carcinogenic or mutagenic potential of Susvimo.

## Impairment of fertility

No fertility studies in animals have been performed to evaluate the effect of ranibizumab.

Although no effects on reproductive tissues were noted in repeat-dose monkey studies with intravitreal ranibizumab, inhibition of VEGF in human female reproductive tissues could potentially impair fertility.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Reproductive toxicity

No reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have been conducted with Susvimo (ranibizumab delivered via the Contivue implant).

In pregnant monkeys treated with intravitreal ranibizumab throughout the period of organogenesis, skeletal abnormalities including incomplete and/or irregular ossification of bones in the skull, vertebral column, and hindlimbs and shortened supernumerary ribs were seen at a low incidence in foetuses from animals treated with doses resulting in trough serum ranibizumab levels up to 18-times higher than observed human Cmax levels after treatment of a single eye with Susvimo. However, due to its mechanism of action, Susvimo must be regarded as potentially teratogenic and embryo-foetotoxic (see section 4.6).

## 1. What Susvimo is and what it is used for

## What Susvimo is

Susvimo is a medicine that contains the active substance ranibizumab. This belongs to a group of medicines called 'anti-neovascularisation agents'.

Susvimo is released into the eye using an eye (ocular) implant.

## What Susvimo is used for

Susvimo is used to treat adults with neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (nAMD), who have responded to medicine that attaches to and blocks 'vascular endothelial growth factor' (VEGF). nAMD happens when:

-  tiny, abnormal blood vessels grow in the back of the eye (retina).
-  these blood vessels leak blood or fluid in the middle part of the retina (the macula) that gives you sharp detailed vision.
-  these blood vessels and their bleeding can eventually form scars that can lead to permanent blind spots in the centre of your vision.

## How Susvimo works

Susvimo works by attaching to and blocking 'VEGF', a protein that can cause the growth of abnormal blood vessels in your eye. This medicine stops or slows the growth and leaking of these blood vessels.

Susvimo can help maintain and in some cases, improve your vision.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The medicine is gradually released from the implant into the back of your eye. The implant is inserted in the eye with surgery, and is about the size of a grain of rice. It is filled and refilled with Susvimo by your doctor.

## 2. What you need to know before you are given Susvimo

Before you start treatment with Susvimo, it is important that you and your doctor have discussed the benefits you could expect to receive from this treatment and the risks that are associated with it.

## You must not be given Susvimo

-  if you are allergic (hypersensitive) to ranibizumab or any of the other ingredients of this medicine (listed in section 6).
-  if you have or think you have an infection in or around your eye.
-  if you have an inflamed eye - indicated by pain or redness.

Tell your doctor immediately if you have any of these signs during treatment with Susvimo. You may need urgent medical or surgical treatment, or a delay before your next refill:

-  more moving spots or 'spider webs' in your vision (vitreous haemorrhage).
-  a sudden feeling that something is in your eye, eye discharge or watering (conjunctival bleb / conjunctival bleb leak / conjunctival erosion / conjunctival retraction).
-  worsening eye pain or vision loss, sensitivity to light or redness in the white of the eye (endophthalmitis).
-  flashing lights, a change or loss of vision (retinal tear or detachment).
-  clouding of the lens due to injury (traumatic cataract).
-  the implant has moved out of place (device dislocation).

## After the surgery to insert the implant

-  Keep your head above shoulder level for the rest of the day.
-  Sleep with your head raised on 3 or more pillows when lying down, that day and night.
-  Do not remove the eye shield from the eye, until you are told to by your doctor. At bedtime, continue to wear the eye shield for at least 7 nights after the surgery.
-  Take all eye medicines your doctor tells you to.
-  Do not push on the eye, rub the eye or touch the area of the eye where the implant is located (for example underneath the eyelid in the upper and outer part of your eye) for 30 days after the implant is inserted.
-  Do not participate in strenuous activities, such as running or lifting weights, until 1 month after the implant is inserted, or until your doctor says you can.

## Patient implant card and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scan

-  Get the patient implant card from your doctor and keep this card in a safe place for future reference - it has important information about the implant.
-  If you need to have an MRI scan, show your healthcare providers the implant card that you have been given.
-  You can only have an MRI scan under very specific conditions.

## After the refill procedure

-  Do not push on the eye, rub the eye or touch the area of the eye where the implant is located (for example underneath the eyelid in the upper and outer part of your eye) for 7 days following the refill.
-  Take all eye medicines your doctor tells you to.

## After the implant removal procedure (if removal is deemed medically necessary)

-  Keep your head above shoulder level for the rest of the day.
-  Sleep with your head raised on 3 or more pillows when lying down that day and night.
-  Wear an eye shield for at least 7 nights following the removal.
-  Do not participate in strenuous activities, such as running or lifting weights, until 14 days after the implant removal procedure.
-  Take all eye medicines your doctor tells you to.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Throughout treatment with Susvimo

-  Do not rub your eye, or touch the area of your eye where the implant is, as much as possible. If you need to, make sure you clean your hands before touching your eye.

## Implant damage

During your treatment with Susvimo, the implant may become damaged. If this damage prevents the proper filling of the implant, your nAMD may be inadequately treated and your physician may remove the implant from your eye and/or change your treatment.

## Children and adolescents

Susvimo has not been studied in children and adolescents, because nAMD only happens in adults.

## Other medicines and Susvimo

Before you receive Susvimo, tell your doctor if you are taking, have recently taken or might take any other medicines.

In particular tell your doctor if you are taking any of the following medicines, as their use would be an important consideration for surgery:

-  medicines that reduce the formation of blood clots, such as warfarin or low dose acetyl salicylic acid.
-  nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medicines, such as ibuprofen or naproxen.

If any of the above apply to you (or you are not sure), talk to your doctor before you are given Susvimo.

## Contraception

Women who could become pregnant should use effective contraception:

-  when being given this medicine, and
-  for at least 12 months after the last time the implant was refilled.

## Pregnancy and breast-feeding

-  If you are pregnant, think you may be pregnant or are planning to have a baby, ask your doctor for advice before you are given this medicine. There is no information currently available about using this medicine when you are pregnant. This medicine could harm an unborn baby. If you become pregnant during treatment, your doctor may replace the medicine with preservative-free sodium chloride solution in the implant. Your doctor will discuss this with you. They will only continue your treatment with this medicine if the potential benefits to you outweighs the potential risks to your unborn baby.
-  Do not use this medicine while you are breast-feeding because it is not known whether Susvimo passes into human milk. Ask your doctor for advice before treatment.

## Driving and using machines

You may have worse vision for a while after the implant is inserted, removed, refilled with medicine, or after eye examinations.

Do not drive or use machines until your doctor tells you to remove the eye shield - and the reduced vision is better.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Susvimo contains polysorbate 20

This medicine contains 0.002 mg polysorbate 20 per 0.02 mL dose. Polysorbates may cause allergic reactions. Tell your doctor if you have any known allergies.

## 3. How Susvimo is given

Before you are given Susvimo your doctor will check that you respond to medicine that attaches to and blocks 'VEGF'.

## How much and how often Susvimo is given

-  The recommended dose is 2 mg given gradually by the implant.
-  The implant is refilled every 24 weeks - roughly 6 months - by your doctor.

Based on how you respond to treatment with Susvimo, your doctor may give additional injections into your eye in the 24-week refill period.

## How you will be given Susvimo

An eye surgeon trained in the implant procedures will fill the implant with Susvimo before inserting the implant in your eye. After, the implant will be refilled by your eye doctor (trained in the refill procedure) at regular times.

-  Your doctor(s) will inject medicine, to numb the eye for both of these procedures.
-  Your doctor will perform an aseptic or sterile procedure to reduce the risk of infection. See section 2 'Monitoring and care during your treatment with Susvimo'.

Susvimo must only be given using the implant meant for this medicine.

## How long you will keep the implant

The implant is intended to remain in the eye, unless removed for medical reasons. Your doctor will keep track of your illness to check the treatment is working properly. They may need to stop the refills of Susvimo or safely remove the implant using surgery (if medically necessary).

## If you miss a dose (refill) of Susvimo

If you miss a refill, book a new appointment with your doctor to get the refill as soon as possible. Do not wait until the next planned refill.

## Before stopping treatment with Susvimo

If you are considering stopping Susvimo, make sure you discuss this with your doctor, as they will need to discuss with you about monitoring your eye and the need to retain or remove the implant. Your doctor will discuss other treatments to determine the best one for you. Stopping all treatment may increase your risk of vision loss and your vision may get worse.

If you have any further questions on the use of this medicine, ask your doctor.

## 4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, this medicine can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them.

The list of possible side effects includes those related to Susvimo, the implant in the eye and the procedures to place, refill and remove the implant.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Some side effects may be serious

Tell your doctor immediately if you notice any of the following serious side effects - you may need urgent medical or surgical treatment, or a delay before your next refill:

Very common: may affect more than 1 in 10 people. Common: may affect up to 1 in 10 people.

Uncommon: may affect up to 1 in 100 people.

-  more moving spots or 'spider webs' in your vision. You may have bleeding in the gel-like substance at the back of the eye (vitreous haemorrhage) - common
-  a sudden feeling that something is in your eye, eye discharge or watering. This feeling may mean:
- a small bulge in the layer that covers the white part of the eye (conjunctival bleb) - very common
- fluid leaking creating a blister-like bulge on the surface of the eye (conjunctival bleb leak) - very common
- the layer that covers the white part of your eye is wearing away (conjunctival erosion), which can expose the implant - common
- stitches are opening to make a gap in the layer that covers the white of the eye (conjunctival retraction). This could expose the implant - common
-  worsening eye pain with vision loss, sensitivity to light, or redness in the white of the eye. This might be an infection of the eyeball with inflammation inside of the eye (endophthalmitis) - common
-  flashing lights, a change or loss of vision. This may mean:
- You have a hole or tear in the layer (the retina) in the back of the eye that senses light (retinal tear) - uncommon
- You have separation of the layer (the retina) in the back of the eye where light is sensed (retinal detachment) - common
-  clouding of the lens of the eye due to injury (traumatic cataract) - uncommon
-  the implant has moved out of place (device dislocation) - common

If you notice any of the serious side effects above, tell your doctor immediately.

## Other side effects

Most of the side effects are mild to moderate.

Tell your doctor if any of the following side effects become severe:

Very common: may affect more than 1 in 10 people

-  blood in the layer that covers the white of the eye (conjunctival haemorrhage)
-  redness in the layer that covers the white of the eye (conjunctival hyperaemia)
-  inflammation of the coloured part of the eye (iritis)
-  eye pain
-  clouding of the lens of the eye (cataract)
-  moving spots in the vision (vitreous floaters)
-  headache
-  implant damage

Common: may affect up to 1 in 10 people

-  bleeding at the back of the eye
-  sensation that something is in the eye
-  eye irritation or discomfort - burning, stinging, itching, tearing, redness
-  nausea, vomiting
-  lowered pressure inside the eye

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

-  when the gel-like substance at the back of the eye (vitreous) shrinks and separates from the layer (retina) at the back of the eye that senses light (vitreous detachment)
-  vision blurred or less sharp
-  swelling of the eyelid, clear part at the front of the eye or the layer that covers the white part of the eye
-  inflammation of the eyelid - redness, pain, swelling and itching of the eyelids or eyelid scales
-  scratched eye surface (corneal abrasion)
-  drooping of the upper eyelid
-  itchy eyes
-  thinning of the white part of the eye
-  defect of the clear part (cornea) at the front of the eye
-  eye discharge, increased production of tears, or watering eyes
-  eyes feeling more sensitive to light
-  infection of the layer that covers the white of the eye (conjunctivitis)
-  pain during the procedure
-  inflammation of the gel-like substance at the back of the eye (vitritis)
-  separation of the layer of blood vessels from underneath the white part of the eye (choroidal detachment)
-  bruising around the eye following surgery
-  blood in front of the coloured part (the iris) of the eye
-  inflammation of the coloured part of the eye (the iris) and its nearby tissue
-  thinning of the layer that covers the white part of the eye
-  attachment of the coloured part of the eye (the iris) to other structures in the eye (iris adhesions)
-  defect of the layer that covers the white part of the eye

## Uncommon: may affect up to 1 in 100 people

-  cloudiness of the implant
-  redness in the white part of the eye
-  exposed stitches - or other problems after the implant procedure
-  discomfort after a refill at or near where the implant is, or discomfort after the procedure
-  scar tissue formation where the implant was inserted (fibrosis)
-  a reaction (for example swelling) at or near where the implant is
-  foreign object in the eye
-  swelling after the procedure

If any of these side effects become severe, please tell your doctor.

If you get any side effects, talk to your doctor.This includes any possible side effects not listed in this leaflet. You can also report side effects directly via the national reporting system listed inAppendix V. By reporting side effects, you can help provide more information on the safety of this medicine.

## The following information is intended for healthcare professionals only:

## How to prepare and administer Susvimo

Single-dose vial for intravitreal use with the Contivue implant (provided separately). Do not administer Susvimo by any route other than via the Contivue implant (e.g. not as a bolus intravitreal injection).

Do not substitute Susvimo for or with other formulations of ranibizumab.

The solution should be inspected visually for particulate matter and discolouration prior to administration. Susvimo is a clear to slightly opalescent, colourless to slightly brownish solution. Do not use the medicine if it has particulate matter, cloudiness or discolouration.

The initial fill and implant procedure and implant removal procedure must be performed by an ophthalmologist experienced in vitreoretinal surgery and trained in the implant procedures. The implant refill-exchange procedure should be performed by an ophthalmologist trained in the implant refill-exchange procedure.

Read and refer to the instructions for use provided by the implant device manufacturer prior to initially filling, implanting, refilling or removing the Contivue implant.

Do not prepare the medicine until you are ready to start the initial fill and implant procedure or refill-exchange procedure.

The initial fill and implant, and refill-exchange procedures should be performed with adequate anaesthesia under aseptic conditions to minimise the risk of intraocular infection as per standard of care.

## Initial fill and implant procedure

Devices necessary for administration:

-  Transfer needle (19-gauge, 1½ inch) (not included)
-  Luer lock syringe (1 mL, Luer Lock Tip) (not included)
-  Contivue initial fill needle
-  Contivue implant with insertion tool assembly

Using aseptic technique, the implant is filled with 0.02 mL of Susvimo (ranibizumab) solution for injection immediately prior to insertion into the patient´s eye. The following instructions should be followed to prepare the syringe:

## 1. Transfer dose from vial to syringe

Attach the transfer needle to the Luer Lock syringe, remove the needle cap, and use it to withdraw all of the contents of the Susvimo vial into the syringe.

## 2. Remove air from syringe

Holding the syringe with the needle pointing up, gently tap the syringe with your finger until any bubbles rise to the top. Slowly push the plunger rod until all air is expelled from the syringe and needle, preserving as much Susvimo solution as possible in order to completely fill the implant. Remove and properly dispose of the transfer needle.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 3. Attach initial fill needle (blue cap)

Attach the initial fill needle to the Luer Lock syringe and remove the needle cap. Remove air from the syringe, as described in step 2 above, and ensuring that when the plunger rod is pushed, a drop of Susvimo solution is seen at the needle tip.

## 4. Inspect the syringe for air bubbles

Inspect the syringe and the needle hub to ensure that no air bubbles are present. If air bubbles are present, continue to remove air from the syringe, as described above, and reinspect.

The syringe is now ready to load into the insertion tool assembly and fill the implant with Susvimo solution. Use the syringe within15 minutes of removing all air to avoid the Susvimo solution drying in the needle and impeding fluid flow.

Once the implant is filled, it is inserted into the eye through the pars plana. Do not recap the initial fill needle or detach it from the syringe. Dispose of the used syringe together with the initial fill needle in a sharps disposal container or in accordance with local requirements.

## Implant refill-exchange procedure

Devices necessary for administration:

-  Transfer needle (19-gauge, 1½ inch) (not included)
-  Luer lock syringe (1 mL, Luer Lock Tip) (not included)
-  Contivue refill needle

The implant contents will be extracted simultaneously as the implant is filled with 0.02 mL of replacement Susvimo (ranibizumab) solution for injection, using aseptic technique. The following instructions should be followed to prepare the syringe:

## 1. Transfer dose from vial to syringe

Attach the transfer needle to the Luer Lock syringe, remove the needle cap, and use it to withdraw all of the contents of the Susvimo vial into the syringe.

## 2. Remove air from syringe

Holding the syringe with the needle pointing up, gently tap the syringe with your finger until any bubbles rise to the top. Slowly push the plunger rod until all air is expelled from the syringe and needle, preserving as much Susvimo solution as possible in order to completely fill the implant. Remove and properly dispose of the transfer needle.

## 3. Attach refill needle (clear cap)

Attach the refill needle to the Luer Lock syringe and remove the needle cap.

## 4. Remove air from syringe and adjust dose

Remove air from the syringe, as described in step 2 above. Slowly push the plunger rod until all air is expelled from the syringe and needle and the dose of Susvimo solution in the syringe is adjusted to 0.1 mL.

## 5. Inspect the syringe for air bubbles

Inspect the syringe and the needle hub using magnification to ensure that no air bubbles are present.

The syringe is now ready for the refill-exchange procedure. Use the syringe within15 minutes of removing all air to avoid the Susvimo solution drying in the needle and impeding fluid flow.

Once the implant contents are exchanged, do not recap the refill needle or detach it from the syringe. Dispose of the used syringe together with the refill needle in a sharps disposal container or in accordance to local requirements.
