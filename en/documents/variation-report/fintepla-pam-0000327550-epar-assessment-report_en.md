## Description

Fenfluramine is currently licensed in the United States as well as the EU for the treatment of seizures associated with Dravet syndrome (DS) and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (LGS) in patients from 2 years of age onwards. Fenfluramine is a serotonin-releasing agent and thereby stimulates multiple 5hydroxytryptamine (5HT) receptor subtypes through the release of serotonin. Fenfluramine may reduce seizures by acting as an agonist at specific serotonin receptors in the brain, including the 5HT1A, 5-HT1D, 5 HT2A, and 5-HT2C receptors, and by acting on the sigma-1 receptor. Patients treated with fenfluramine without concomitant stiripentol (STP) are titrated from 0.2 mg/kg/d to effect up to the maximum maintenance dose of 0.7 mg/kg/d of fenfluramine (absolute maximum daily dose of 26 mg/d). With concomitant STP, patients are titrated from 0.2 mg/kg/d to the maximum dose of 0.4 mg/kg/d fenfluramine (absolute maximum daily dose of 17 mg/d).

Both DS and LGS are rare and severe treatment-resistant developmental and epileptic encephalopathies (DEEs) that are associated with high unmet need and an increased risk of mortality (Specchio et al, 2022; Zuberi et al, 2022). Lennox-Gastaut syndrome represents one of the most challenging forms of childhood epilepsy and is characterised by a triad of symptoms: refractory seizures, progressive cognitive impairment, and severe electroencephalogram (EEG) abnormalities. LGS places an enormous burden on families and healthcare systems, requiring intensive medical management, frequent hospitalisations, and lifelong comprehensive care.

Despite the established clinical efficacy of fenfluramine in LGS, real-world evidence of the impact of fenfluramine (FFA) use among patients with LGS on healthcare resource utilisation and broader patient outcomes is limited. Furthermore, real-world persistence data for fenfluramine in LGS populations remains inadequate.

RWE1608 is a non-interventional retrospective cohort analysis using U.S. Komodo claims data to address the afore-mentioned gaps. The study aimed to evaluate the impact of initiating fenfluramine treatment on clinical outcomes among patients diagnosed with LGS, assess fenfluramine persistence among these patients, and explore characteristics that may be associated with fenfluramine use and persistence.

## Baseline (pre-index) period

For pre/post analysis, the baseline period is defined as 12 months prior to first Rx claim of Fintepla, which provides sufficient duration to capture the major claims of interest and matches the follow-up period and enables pre/post comparison. For persistence analysis, the baseline period is defined as 3 months prior to the first Rx claim of Fintepla.

## Follow-up (post-index) period

For pre/post analysis, follow-up period is defined as 12 months following first Rx claim of Fintepla. For persistence analysis, follow-up period is defined as at least 6 months following first Rx claim of Fintepla, and there is no limit for maximum follow-up time other than end of data period (ie, Dec 31, 2024). The Fintepla discontinuation rule requires a 90-day period to determine if a patient is truly discontinued, hence a minimum of 3-month or 90-day follow-up period is required to apply this rule. Since 6-month and 12-month persistency are considered to be the most clinically meaningful measures the follow-up period was extended to 6 months.

## Data sources

The only data source that is used in this study is Komodo claims data (US, open and closed claims, Jan 2015 to Dec 2024). Komodo covers 75 to 80% of the US claims and 65 to 75% of the Pharmacy Claims that represent a large portion of US market. Komodo closed data source from 150+ payer complete datasets (a high number of diverse payers) and the data is well distributed by geography and patient demographics. It has near census-level coverage across geographies to minimize biases. Due to the sample size of this study, generalizability at the sub-national level is not recommended.

Komodo defines the data to be closed for a patient if they receive all of that patient's medical or pharmacy transactions in a given time period under that payer. The time-period is determined by the enrollment dates presented in eligibility tables.

- Calculate the value for each patient in each time-period based on definition.
- For each pre/post data pair, statistical analysis is conducted using paired t-test for continuous variables and McNemar's test for categorical or binary variables.

## Secondary outcome variables

- Analyze Fintepla patient persistence following first Fintepla Rx using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and probability estimates.
- For each patient, discontinuation is defined as a patient NOT having a Fintepla treatment claim within 90 days following the last day of days of supply of a previous Fintepla claim.
- The choice of 90 days assumes that the Fintepla patients need to use Fintepla on a daily basis to manage their symptoms, and not refilling Fintepla for 90 days indicates that the patient has discontinued Fintepla. Note: This is not the same definition that we used in primary outcome analysis, where 'persistent Fintepla use' is defined as no more than 14 days of gap between the last day of days of supply of a previous Fintepla claim and the next Fintepla claim.
- Persistence is defined as no discontinuation. Kaplan-Meier analysis has been conducted, and survival probabilities are calculated for every month. However, 6-month and 12-month probabilities are most important for interpretation as it is conventional to report persistence rates at these time points.

## Sensitivity analysis

## · Alternate analytic datasets

- o Adding non-LGS population

For this sensitivity analysis, additional 39 patients were added to the analysis who don't have an LGS or DS diagnosis code but have 1+ fenfluramine claims and have 12 months of coverage before and after the first fenfluramine claim (N=148 patients in the main analysis +39 additional patients=187 patients). The rationale for this is that there is a significant problem with under-use of the LGS-specific codes, so there are likely some patients with LGS using fenfluramine that are not coded under the LGSspecific code. It is highly unlikely that expanding to this additional set of patients will capture any off-label usage because fenfluramine is an expensive drug, and the Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) process can be cumbersome for any meaningful off-label usage as of now.

- o With only 6 months of pre and post period

This sensitivity analysis required a minimum of only 6 months of baseline period and minimum 6 months of persistent fenfluramine use in the post-period instead of 12 months. Due to the smaller number of time periods available in this sensitivity analysis, Interrupted Time Series analysis for this sensitivity analysis dataset were not conducted.

## · Alternate variable definitions

- o Alternative discontinuation definition for the secondary outcome (Persistency analysis)

For this sensitivity analysis the same persistence analysis and Kaplan-Meier analysis with one modification was performed: for discontinuation definition, instead of 90 days, shorten it to 60 days.

60 days has a shorter 'grace' period vs 90 days, but given that a Fintepla patient needs to use Fintepla on a daily basis to manage their symptoms, and not refilling Fintepla for 60 days still strongly indicates that the patient has discontinued Fintepla. The rationale of this sensitivity analysis is to understand the impact on outcome measures for the 90-day discontinuation definition.

## Safety results - outcome data

Outcomes included seizure related claims (including Generalized Tonic-Clonic Seizure (GTCS) and status epilepticus), inpatient hospitalisations and length of stay, ER visits, office visits, and ambulance use, each evaluated as all cause and seizure related, where applicable. Seizure-related classification was based on diagnosis codes on the associated medical claims, and utilisation outcomes were summarised as both event occurrence and event counts, including counts of all cause and seizurerelated hospitalisations.

Additional endpoints included non-pharmacologic treatment claims, office visits by provider type (epileptologist, neurology, paediatric neurology, and psychiatry), and medication based outcomes, including use of different classes of antiseizure medications, rescue medications, antipsychotics, and sleep medications. For outcomes where a patient did not have a specific claim in a given period, this was treated as zero events.

## Primary objective: Pre/post index comparison

## HCRU outcomes

Among 148 patients persistent on Fintepla for 12 months (cohort 1) acute care utilisation decreased significantly after index (see Table 4), with fewer ER visits overall (1.4 to 0.8, -42%, p&lt;0.01) and fewer seizure-related ER visits (1.3 to 0.7, -46%, p &lt;0.01). Ambulance use also declined (0.5 to 0.2, -62%, p&lt;0.01). Inpatient utilisation was similarly lower, including any inpatient hospitalisation claims (4.0 to 3.1, -23%, p=0.04) and seizure-related inpatient hospitalisation claims (4.0 to 3.0, -24%, p=0.04). The number of hospitalisations, number of seizure-related inpatient hospitalisations, length of stay, and seizure related length of stay were numerically lower post index but did not reach statistical significance. Other outcomes were generally stable across periods, including overall seizure related claims, GTCS claims, status epilepticus claims, overall office visits, seizure related office visits, and non-pharmacologic treatment claims. Specialty specific office visit claims increased modestly for epileptologists and paediatric neurology, while most specialty categories showed no statistically significant change.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

The aim of this real-world, claims-based study RWE1608 was to evaluate the impact of Fenfluramine (Fintepla®) initiation among patients diagnosed with Lennox Gastaut Syndrome (LGS) on several clinical and patient-related outcomes, and to assess long-term treatment persistence. Furthermore, the characteristics of persistent and non-persistent fenfluramine users, and of fenfluramine initiators and non-initiators, should be compared. It was observed that Health care resource utilisation (HCRU) among patients persistently using fenfluramine for 12 months decreased after initiation of fenfluramine, with consistent reductions in emergency room (ER) visits including seizure-related ER visits, inpatient hospitalisations including seizure-related hospitalisations, and ambulance use across the pre versus post analysis. Sensitivity analyses using interrupted time series models and matched difference-in-differences comparisons also revealed statistically significant declines in ER admissions and ambulance callouts. Furthermore, sensitivity analyses using shorter follow-up periods revealed that the reductions in seizure-related ER visits and the use of ambulance services remained significant, thus supporting the robustness of the main analysis findings. Compared with the pre-observation period, the burden of concomitant medication also generally decreased after the initiation of fenfluramine, with reductions observed for most of the evaluated ASMs studied. Statistically significant reductions on the patient level were observed for clobazam, levetiracetam, topiramate, and divalproex. Using a 90-day allowable gap definition, persistence among patients with LGS who initiated fenfluramine treatment was 73% at 6 months and 61% at 12 months, which is generally consistent with the findings of previous studies. Pringsheim et al. (2024) reported that, using a German national prescription database, persistence at 12 months was approximately 49% for patients with LGS. The recently finalised PASS EP0241 (currently under assessment via procedure EMA/PAM/0000323622) reported fenfluramine persistence among patients with LGS of 76.5% at six months, and 40.7% at 12 months. No major differences were observed when comparing the characteristics of patients who had taken fenfluramine for 12 months with those who had discontinued the drug before 12 months. In contrast fenfluramine initiators showed higher comorbidities, greater prior treatment burden, higher neighbourhood deprivation, and a greater likelihood of receiving care at Centers of Excellence compared with non-initiators. This suggests that fenfluramine use in routine practice may be more prevalent among patients with substantial clinical and social complexity. Overall, RWE1608 provided new real-world evidence on changes in clinical and HCRU outcomes among LGS patients who initiated fenfluramine treatment and helped determine the characteristics of these patients compared to those who did not initiate treatment.

The results of this study should be seen in light of the associated limitations, which prevent meaningful conclusions from being drawn. As the results are based on claims data from the US, they do not represent the full US population, nor are they directly representative of other regions outside the United States (such as Europe). The relatively small sample size of 148 patients in the main analysis and 187 patients in the sensitivity analysis may not be sufficient to achieve the desired statistical power for interrupted time series analysis and for propensity score matched difference-in-differences analyses. As the outcome measures are based on claims data, they can only act as an approximate indicator of patient experience and do not directly reflect the number and/or severity of actual seizure symptoms experienced by the patient. Other varying factors such as concomitant antiseizure medication changes, disease trajectory, health care provider management, and patient or caregiver level factors may have affected observed changes. ## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

This real-world claims-based study of patients with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome aimed to evaluate the impact of fenfluramine use on clinical outcomes, ASM use, and healthcare resource utilization, and to assess fenfluramine persistence among LGS patients who initiated treatment. Furthermore, differences in characteristics between persistent and non-persistent fenfluramine users as well as between fenfluramine-initiator and non-initiators were examined as exploratory objectives.

The persistence of LGS patients initiating fenfluramine treatment observed within this study is generally high and in line with findings from previous studies. The persistent use of fenfluramine (&gt;12 months) was associated with meaningful reductions in health care resource utilisation after initiation. These findings were consistent across multiple analytic approaches and remained robust in sensitivity analyses, which supports the reliability of the observed decreases in ER visits, seizure related ER visits, inpatient hospitalisations, seizure related hospitalisations, and ambulance use. Concomitant use of antiseizure medications also generally decreased after fenfluramine treatment was initiated, suggesting potential treatment simplification over time among persistent patients. Higher baseline levels of clinical complexity, social vulnerability and engagement with specialist care were observed among fenfluramine initiators compared with non-initiators, which indicates that fenfluramine use in routine care may be concentrated in patients with substantial unmet need.

The results of study RWE1608 can inform real world expectations regarding persistence and outcomes following treatment initiation with fenfluramine, and they support the clinical benefit of fenfluramine use in patients with LGS. Based on the results of RWE1608 no further action is deemed necessary. However, the results should be considered in the context of limitations associated with the study. As the results are based on claims data from the US database Komodo, they should only be interpreted as proxies for clinical experience, and they may not be applicable to other regions outside the United States, such as Europe. Due to factors not captured in claims data, such as adherence and caregiver management, residual confounding may remain. As data were retrieved only from long-term fenfluramine users, it is not appropriate to generalise these findings to the general LGS population of fenfluramine users.

No regulatory action required.
