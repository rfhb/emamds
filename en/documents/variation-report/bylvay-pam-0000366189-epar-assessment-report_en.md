## Introduction

The MAH submitted a final report for:

A4250-08 - An Open label Extension Study to Evaluate Long-term Efficacy and Safety of A4250 in Children with Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis Types 1 and 2 (PEDFIC 2).

## Clinical study

A4250-08: An Open label Extension Study to Evaluate Long-term Efficacy and Safety of A4250 in Children with Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis Types 1 and 2 (PEDFIC 2).

## Description

Study A4250-008 was a Phase 3, multicentre, open-label extension study to investigate the long-term efficacy and safety of the ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor odevixibat in patients with Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis (PFIC), following the Phase 3 pivotal Study A4250-005/ PEDFIC-1.

## Methods

## Study participants

## Inclusion Criteria

Patients who met all the following inclusion criteria were eligible for enrolment in this study. Patients who did not meet eligibility criteria may have been re-screened after consultation with the medical monitor. Patients who did not fulfil the inclusion/exclusion criteria after 3 attempts were not allowed to rescreen.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Cohort 1:

1. Completed the 24-week treatment period of Study A4250-005 or withdrew from Study A4250005 due to patient/caregiver judgment of intolerable symptoms after completing at least 12 weeks of treatment. Patients who withdrew from Study A4250-005 due to a study drug-related AE were not eligible. Note that the allowance for early rollover from Study A4250-005 (i.e. after 12 weeks of treatment) was removed with Study A4250-005 Protocol Amendment 6.

## Cohort 2:

1. Male or female patient of any age, with a clinical diagnosis of PFIC, including episodic forms (i.e. BRIC) and with a body weight ≥5 kg at Visit S-1.
2. Clinical genetic confirmation of PFIC.
3. Patients with PFIC, excluding BRIC, must have had elevated serum bile acid concentration, specifically measured to be ≥100 μmol/L, taken as the average of 2 samples at least 7 days apart (Visits S-1 and S-2) before the Screening/Inclusion Visit (Visit 1).
4. Patients with PFIC, excluding BRIC, must have had history of significant pruritus and a caregiver-reported observed scratching or patient-reported itching (for patients &gt;18 with no caregiver-reported observed scratching) in the eDiary average of ≥2 (on a scale of 0 to 4) in the 2 weeks before the Screening/Inclusion Visit (Visit 1).
5. Patients with episodic forms of PFIC must have had an emerging flare characterised by clinically significant pruritus and elevated serum bile acid levels/cholestasis as judged by the investigator.

## Exclusion Criteria

Patients with any of the following criteria at Visit 1 or Visit S-1 were not eligible for study participation:

## Cohort 1:

1. Decompensated liver disease: coagulopathy, history, or presence of clinically significant ascites, variceal haemorrhage, and/or encephalopathy.
2. Noncompliant with treatment in Study A4250-005.
3. Any other conditions or abnormalities which, in the opinion of the investigator or medical monitor, may have compromised the safety of the patient, or interfered with the patient's participation in or completion of the study.

## Cohort 2:

1. Known pathologic variations of the ABCB11 gene that have been demonstrated to result in complete absence of the BSEP protein.
2. Past medical history or ongoing presence of other types of liver disease including, but not limited to, the following:
3. Biliary atresia of any kind.
4. Suspected or proven liver cancer or metastasis to the liver on imaging studies.
5. Histopathology on liver biopsy suggestive of alternate non-PFIC related aetiology of cholestasis.

Note: Patients with clinically significant portal hypertension were allowed.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

6. Past medical history or ongoing presence of any other disease or condition known to interfere with the absorption, distribution, metabolism (specifically bile acid metabolism), or excretion of drugs in the intestine, including but not limited to, inflammatory bowel disease.
7. Past medical history or ongoing chronic (i.e. &gt;3 months) diarrhoea requiring intravenous fluid or nutritional intervention for treatment of the diarrhoea and/or its sequelae.
8. Confirmed past diagnosis of infection with human immunodeficiency virus or other present and active, clinically significant, acute, or chronic infection, or past medical history of any major episode of infection requiring hospitalisation or treatment with parenteral anti-infective treatment within 4 weeks of treatment start (Study Day 1) or completion of oral anti-infective treatment within 2 weeks before the start of screening period.
9. Suspected or confirmed cancers except for basal cell carcinoma, and non-liver cancers treated at least 5 years before Screening with no evidence of recurrence.
10. A liver transplant, or a liver transplant was planned within 6 months of the Screening/Inclusion Visit.
11. Decompensated liver disease, coagulopathy, history, or presence of clinically significant ascites, variceal haemorrhage, and/or encephalopathy.
12. International normalised ratio (INR) &gt;1.4 (the patient may have been treated with vitamin K intravenously, and if INR is ≤1.4 at resampling, the patient may have been included).
13. Serum ALT &gt;10× upper limit of normal (ULN) at Screening.
14. Serum ALT &gt;15× ULN at any time point during the last 6 months unless an alternate aetiology was confirmed for the elevation.
15. Total bilirubin &gt;10× ULN at Screening.
16. Suffering from uncontrolled, recalcitrant pruritic condition other than PFIC. Examples include, but are not limited to, refractory atopic dermatitis or other primary pruritic skin diseases.
17. Past medical history of alcohol or substance abuse. Patient must have agreed to refrain from illicit drug and alcohol use during the study.
18. Underwent administration of bile acid or lipid binding resins and medications that slowed gastrointestinal (GI) motility.
19. Have had investigational exposure to a drug, biologic agent, or medical device within 30 days before Screening, or 5 half-lives of the study agent, whichever was longer. Any other conditions or abnormalities which, in the opinion of the investigator or medical monitor, may have compromised the safety of the patient, or interfered with the patient participating in or completing the study.

## CHMP comments:

The study population included in A4250-005 was restricted to paediatric patients (6 months-18 years) with genetically confirmed PFIC Type 1 or 2, excluding those with prior biliary diversion surgery. A4250-008 retained this population in Cohort 1 (rollover patients from A4250-005) but broadened eligibility in Cohort 2 to include patients of any age, any PFIC subtype (including episodic BRIC), and those with a history of biliary diversion surgery.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

This inclusion strategy does sufficiently allow inclusion of a trial population of PFIC patients independent of their PFIC-subtype, gender, weight, age, or ethnicity. The only PFIC patients excluded from the study based on subtype are those with known pathologic variations of the ABCB11 gene that have been demonstrated to result in complete absence of the BSEP protein. Because odevixibat is an ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor that relies on a feedback loop of residual biliary bile acid secretion, it is ineffective in patients with zero functional BSEP expression.

Patients who withdrew early from A4250-005 could still enter A4250-008 (Cohort 1) if they left the study due to patient/caregiver judgment of intolerable symptoms and had completed at least 12 weeks of treatment before withdrawing. Patients who withdrew from Study A4250-005 due to a study drug-related AE were not eligible. Withdrawal due to intolerable symptoms doesn't necessarily indicate the drug caused harm. It could reflect insufficient efficacy, disease symptoms, or tolerability preferences. A drug-related AE, by contrast, means investigators formally assessed and attributed the event to the study drug.

However, despite the inclusion and exclusion criteria being sufficient to capture and reflect the \"middle band\" of disease severity, some factors should be considered while analysing the data:

1. Requiring s-BA ≥100 μmol/L excludes PFIC (type 3) patients with mild to moderate cholestasis. However, inclusion criteria might be needed to sufficiently detect treatment responses.
2. Exclusion of patients with decompensated liver disease, coagulopathy, history or presence of clinically significant ascites, variceal haemorrhage, encephalopathy, as well as a number of other exclusion criteria prevents the inclusion of progressive end-stage PFIC patients and patients with other underling diseases. However, exclusion is needed to protect patient safety, preserve the scientific integrity of the trial data, and ensure that any observed effects can be reasonably attributed to the drug being tested rather than the natural course of disease.
3. Minimum body weight (≥5 kg) excludes the smallest, most ill infants, who often represent the earliest and most severe presentations of PFIC1 and PFIC2.
4. A history of normal serum bile acids, previously used as an indicator of benign recurrent intrahepatic cholestasis (BRIC) (A4250-005), has been removed as an exclusion criterion for PFIC patients in Study A4250-008. As a result, PFIC patients with normal serum bile acids prior to the study were allowed to participate, provided their s-BA was ≥100 μmol/L, calculated as the average of two samples taken at least 7 days apart (Visits S-1 and S-2), before the Screening/Inclusion Visit (Visit 1).
5. Regional Cohorts from countries like Saudi Arabia and Pakistan identified PFIC3 as the predominant type, but PFIC3 patients only make up 11% of Cohort 2 or 6% (n=7) of overall odevixibat treated group. However, the trial population does reflect the European/ Dutch population.

Concluding remarks: The inclusion and exclusion criteria for A4250-008 allow inclusion of a trial population whose demographic characteristics sufficiently resemble the European/Dutch population, supporting possible extrapolation of the trial results to the real-world (European) population.

## Treatments

In A4250-008 (Cohort 1), all rollover patients from A4250-005 received open-label odevixibat, regardless of their original treatment assignment. Those previously on placebo switched to odevixibat at 40 μg/kg/day for 72 weeks, while those previously on odevixibat continued their odevixibat treatment for another 72 weeks. In total, 56 patients rolled over (19 from placebo, 37 from odevixibat), and all ended up on active treatment in the extension study.

Prior to Amendment 6 of the protocol, odevixibat therapy was initiated at a dose of 120 μg/kg/day; patients were to remain on this dose unless they required down-titration to the 40 μg/kg/day due tolerability issues. When Amendment 6 came into effect, patients entering Cohort 2 started treatment at 40 μg/kg/day with the possibility to dose escalate to 120 μg/kg/day after 12 weeks if there was no improvement in pruritus (based on investigator judgment).

The dose of 40 μg/kg/day is consistent with the current EU SmPC for Bylvay (odevixibat) in PFIC. The recommended starting dose of odevixibat for PFIC is 40 μg/kg administered orally once daily in the morning, with escalation to 120 mcg/kg/day permitted if an adequate clinical response has not been achieved after 3 months of continuous therapy.

All doses were to be taken orally, once daily. Odevixibat is supplied in 2 capsule sizes and 4 strengths: capsule size 0 (200 or 600 μg strength) that could be opened and sprinkled and mixed through ageappropriate food and liquids, capsule size 3 (400 or 1200 μg strength) to be swallowed intact but could be opened if unable to swallow the capsule. The specific dosing schedule can be found in SmPC section 4.2.

The number and type of capsules provided to each patient daily to achieve an approximate dose of 120 μg/kg was based on body weight thresholds, as identified in Table 1. In addition, if a patient's weight changed at any time during the study, adjustment was required in conjunction with the information in Table 1. The table also includes number and type of capsules for dosing at 40 μg/kg/day for patients who required dose reduction.

Treatment with odevixibat was to be interrupted if a patient developed diarrhea with at least 1 of the following concomitant signs or symptoms: grossly bloody stools, vomiting, dehydration requiring treatment with oral or intravenous rehydration and/or electrolyte imbalances, fever (≥38°C), and/or diarrhea persisting for 7 or more days. Once symptoms were resolved, rechallenge was allowed. If diarrhea re-occurred within 1 week with no alternate aetiology, dosing was to be permanently discontinued.

## Concomitant medication

Treatment with UDCA, rifampicin, and/or antihistamines was also allowed provided the patient was on stable dosage at least 4 weeks before enrolment, and no dosage changes were planned during the entire study period. Topical treatment was allowed without restriction. Other drugs/natural products with possible effects on GI motility (e.g. selective serotonin reuptake inhibiting drugs, tetracyclic antidepressants, fiber supplementation, yoghurt variants) were allowed provided there was stable usage of the product at least 4 weeks before enrolment until treatment discontinuation.

## CHMP comments:

Study A4250-008 was a Phase 3, multicentre, open-label extension study to investigate the longterm efficacy and safety of odevixibat in patients with PFIC, including episodic forms of the disease. Both placebo and odevixibat rollover patients (Cohort 1) started A4250-008 at 120 μg/kg/day, independent of their prior treatment or dose from A4250-005, where odevixibat treatment dose could have been either 40 or 120 μg/kg/day. Patients enrolled in Cohort 2 before amendment 6 received a starting dose of 120 μg/kg/day, whereas patients included after amendment 6 received a starting dose of 40 μg/kg/day.

It is unclear how many participants in Cohort 2 received the starting dose of 120 μg/kg/day before amendment 6 was approved and if participants (including those from Cohort 1) were switched to the 40 μg/kg/day dose after amendment 6 came into effect. However, results from Study A4250-005 showed that odevixibat 40 μg/kg/day had better and more sustained efficacy on the endpoints. Post-hoc analyses showed that there was no statistically significant difference between the two odevixibat dose groups in the proportion of positive pruritus assessments at the patient level over the 24-week treatment period (2-sided p-value = 0.5008) or in the proportion of serum bile acid responders (2-sided p-value = 0.1083). It is therefore assumed that the study outcomes are not unjustifiably positively influenced by the proportion of patients who received the 120 μg/kg/day starting dose.

The recommended dose of odevixibat in the SmPC is 40 mcg/kg/day, with the possibility to dose escalate to 120 mcg/kg/day if an adequate clinical response has not been achieved after 3 months. The treatments in clinical Study A4250-008 (after amendment 6) are in accordance with dose information and capsule recommendations mentioned in the odevixibat SmPC. All doses were to be taken orally, once daily.

## Objective(s)

The primary objectives of the study were:

1. To demonstrate a sustained effect of odevixibat on serum bile acids and pruritus in children with PFIC Types 1 and 2 (Cohort 1).
2. To evaluate the effect of odevixibat on serum bile acids and pruritus in patients with PFIC who either (1) did not meet eligibility criteria for Study A4250-005 or (2) who did meet the eligibility criteria for Study A4250-005 after recruitment of Study A4250-005 had been completed (Cohort 2).

The secondary objectives for Cohorts 1 and 2 of the study were:

1. To evaluate the long-term safety and tolerability of repeated daily doses of odevixibat.
2. To evaluate the effect of odevixibat on growth.
3. To evaluate the effect of odevixibat on the need for biliary diversion and/or liver transplantation.
4. To evaluate the effect of odevixibat on biochemical markers of cholestasis and liver disease.

## Outcomes/endpoints

Primary efficacy endpoint was the change from baseline in fasting serum bile acids after 72 weeks of treatment. Serum bile acids concentration at baseline was calculated as the average of the last 2 values prior to the first dose. The end value was calculated as the average of the values at week 70/72 after the start of treatment.

## Secondary efficacy endpoints

The efficacy of treatment with odevixibat was assessed by serial measurements of serum bile acids and evaluation of itching (Albireo PRO) and scratching (Albireo ObsRO) conducted twice daily in the morning (AM score, evaluating nighttime itching/scratching) and at bedtime (PM score, evaluating daytime itching/scratching) as recorded by the patient and caregiver in the eDiary.

## Exploratory Efficacy Endpoints

Additional efficacy assessments included serial evaluation of growth (height, weight and BMI z-scores), sleep parameters (including tiredness and number of awakenings) as assessed by items in the Albireo PRO and ObsRO, quality of life (QoL) based on the PedsQL, GIC/GIS, hepatic biochemical tests (ALT, AST, GGT, and total bilirubin), other parameters of hepatic health (PELD/MELD, APRI and FIB-4 scores), and number of patients undergoing biliary diversion surgery or liver transplantation.

## CHMP comment

Study A4250-005 was designed to investigate statistically significant efficacy of odevixibat relative to placebo, whereas A4250-008 was designed to characterize the durability and long-term maintenance of treatment effect.

A4250-005 was a placebo-controlled trial where primary endpoints were assessed over a 24-week treatment period. The EU primary endpoint was defined as the proportion of patients achieving at least a 70% reduction in fasting serum bile acids (s-BA) from baseline or reaching a level ≤70 µmol/L. The proportion of positive pruritus assessments, derived from caregiver-reported scratching via the Albireo observer-reported outcome (ObsRO) instrument was an important secondary endpoint. These endpoints are in line with the endpoints in the pivotal study 005.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In contrast, A4250-008 was an open-label extension study in which the mean change from baseline in fasting serum bile acids after 72 weeks of treatment was the primary efficacy endpoint. The mean change from baseline over time, for both serum bile acids and pruritus scores, were important secondary endpoints. The duration of the study, together with enrolment of odevixibat treated patients from Study A4250-005 and the possibility of an additional optional extension period, is able to provide important insights into long-term treatment effects of odevixibat and maintenance of effect.

## Sample size

No formal hypothesis testing was performed in this open-label study. The sample size for Cohort 1 was determined based on rollover of patients from Study A4250-005. The sample size of 60 patients for Cohort 2 was estimated based on the availability of target patient population to evaluate the therapeutic benefit for those patients.

## CHMP comments:

As this was a descriptive study, no formal sample size calculation was performed. The sample size of 60 patients is estimated based on the availability of target patient population. This approach is considered acceptable given the study's design and objectives.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

This is an open-label study. There was no randomization, stratification or blinding of treatments.

## CHMP comments:

No randomization or stratification took place in this open-label study.

## Statistical Methods

Efficacy analyses are presented across 3 study groups: Cohort 1 patients who received odevixibat in Study A4250-005, Cohort 1 patients who received placebo in that study, and Cohort 2 patients. In general, descriptive statistics were presented for all efficacy variables and endpoints, PK parameters, and safety variables, as appropriate.

## Endpoints

The primary efficacy endpoints were analysed as change from baseline in fasting serum bile acids is summarised descriptively at the end of the 72-week treatment period based on the average of the values at Weeks 70 and 72 compared to Baseline 2 (average of the last 2 values prior to the first dose in Study A4250-008) is used for analysis for all study groups. For Cohort 1 patients who received odevixibat in Study A4250-005, an additional analysis for change from Baseline 1 was conducted where Baseline 1 is the average of the last 2 serum bile acid values prior to the first dose in Study A4250-005.

For secondary endpoints the proportion of positive pruritus assessments at the patient level over the 72-week treatment period, where a positive pruritus assessment is defined as a scratching score of ≤ 1 or at least a 1-point drop from baseline on the Albireo ObsRO instrument. Both AM and PM pruritus assessments are included in the analysis of this endpoint. The proportion and the associated 95%

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

confidence interval are provided. Changes from Baseline 1 (Cohort 1 on odevixibat in Study A4250005) and Baseline 2 (all study groups) in monthly scratching severity score to end of the 72-week treatment in Study A4250-008 are presented. For other secondary efficacy parameters, including growth, APRI, FIB-4, and PELD/MELD scores, descriptive statistics are summarised for observed values and changes from baseline to each visit. Additional evaluations of pruritus include the proportion of individual assessments meeting the definition of a positive pruritus assessment at the patient level over cumulative weeks and the proportion of positive pruritus assessments at each 4-week interval. laboratory results as these laboratories use different normal ranges than the central laboratory.

## CHMP comments:

Descriptive statistics were presented for primary, secondary, and exploratory endpoints, and no significance tests were performed. However, the primary objective of this study was to demonstrate a sustained effect of odevixibat and to evaluate the long-term safety and tolerability of repeated daily doses, and not to test for superiority of odevixibat over another treatment or placebo control. The descriptive statistics are sufficient to answer the objectives, and the absence of significance testing is therefore not a concern.

## Results

## Participant flow

## Patient disposition

The study included an 8-week screening period (Cohort 2 only), a 72-week treatment period, and a 4week follow-up period. An optional extension period for continued treatment until commercial availability of odevixibat followed the 72-week treatment period. Participant disposition is presented in Table 2.

Approximately 120 patients total were planned, 60 patients per cohort. Out of 172 patients screened 2, 56 were screening failures. The primary reason for screen failure was attributed to study entry criteria that were not met. A significant number of failures were attributed to biochemical thresholds, most often for too high total bilirubin and ALT levels. Inclusion criteria failures were common regarding s-BA concentrations specifically failing to meet the requirement of ≥100 μmol/L. Additionally, several participants failed to demonstrate a history of significant pruritus. Other reasons for screen failure included the lack of clinical genetic confirmation of PFIC, known pathologic variations of the ABCB11 gene resulting in the complete absence of the BSEP protein, and the withdrawal of consent by caregivers. There were also two instances of screening restrictions due to COVID-19, one instance of the patient meeting genetic criteria for the A4250-005 study and one diagnosis of high-grade B cell lymphoma. Of the 172 screened patients, a total of 116 were enrolled and distributed across the placebo/odevixibat (n=19), odevixibat/odevixibat (n=37) (total of 56 in Cohort 1), and Cohort 2 (n=60) groups.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

During the 72-week treatment period, completion rates varied by group, with the placebo/odevixibat group being the highest at 78.9% (n=15) and Cohort 2 being the lowest at 66.7% (n=40), while the overall completion rate was 71.6% (n=83). Discontinuations during this period ranged from 21.1% (n=4) in the placebo/odevixibat group to 33.3% (n=20) in the Cohort 2 group.

Primary reasons for discontinuation included adverse events (ranging from 2.7% to 10.5%), withdrawal of consent/assent (6.7% to 10.8%), and \"Other\" reasons (5.3% to 15.0%), the latter of which included liver transplant referrals (5 patients), unsatisfactory itching results (4 patients), transition to commercial product or reimbursement programs (3 patients), and withdrawal of assent (1 patient).

Regarding the optional extension period, 63.8% (n=74) of the overall odevixibat group entered the period. However, 73.7% (n=14) of placebo/odevixibat, 73.0% (n=27) of odevixibat/odevixibat, and 55.0% (n=33) of Cohort 2 (63.8% [n=74] of the overall odevixibat) eventually discontinued it. The primary driver for extension discontinuation was the transition to commercial drug, occurring in 38.8% (n=45) of the overall odevixibat, 56.8% (n=21) of odevixibat/odevixibat, 26.7% (n=16) of Cohort 2, and 42.1% (n=8) of placebo/odevixibat patients, followed by \"Other\" reasons (20.7% in overall odevixibat) which encompassed liver transplant listings (6 patients), compassionate access or reimbursement programs (11 patients), lack of efficacy (1 patient), inability to travel (1 patient), and unreported reasons (5 patients).

## Protocol amendments and deviations

The initial protocol dates from 14 August 2017. Throughout the course of the study, a few global protocol amendments were implemented.

The study was amended (Amendment 2, 18 January 2019) to provide access to odevixibat to patients who were not eligible for Study A4250-005, including patients of any age, any PFIC type, and allowing for the inclusion of up to 40 patients with biliary diversion surgery into a second cohort (Cohort 2).

Significant changes to the study were introduced with the approval of Amendment 6 on 21 December 2021. Prior to Amendment 6 of the protocol, the starting dose for all patients was 120 μg/kg/day. As of Amendment 6, the starting dose of odevixibat for Cohort 2 patients was 40 μg/kg/day. Patients on this dose had the possibility to escalate to the 120 μg/kg/day dose after the first 12 weeks if there was no improvement in pruritus based on investigator judgement.

In addition, inclusion criteria for Cohort 2 were updated to include patients with BRIC. Patients with episodic forms of PFIC (i.e., BRIC) must have an emerging flare characterized by clinically significant pruritus and elevated serum bile acid levels/cholestasis as judged by the investigator. Furthermore, BRIC was removed as an exclusion criterium, and a note was added that patients with clinically significant portal hypertension are allowed.

## CHMP comments:

The original study protocol dates from August 2017 with the first patient enrolled in September 2018 and the study closed in February 2024. Amendment 6 was approved in 2021 meaning that participants included in the first 3 years of the study received another treatment regime compared to participants included in the last 3 years of the study. Patients that received a starting dose of 120 μg/kg/day were to remain on this dose unless experiencing tolerability issues that required down titration to the 40 μg/kg, whereas patients that received a 40 μg/kg/day starting dose had the possibility to escalate to the 120 μg/kg/day dose when needed.

## Baseline disease characteristics

Baseline disease characteristics of the study population are shown in Table 3. Across the placebo, odevixibat, and Cohort 2 groups, the mean years since PFIC diagnosis remained consistent at approximately 3.49 to 3.67 years. PFIC Type 2 was the most prevalent diagnosis overall (56.0%), with BSEP Subtype 2 being the dominant subtype (66.2%), while ABCB11 was the most frequently identified pathologic variant (58.6%). Two patients each had PFIC4 and PFIC6. Almost all patients (114, 98%) had a history of significant pruritus present per the investigator and most (76, 66%) had a history of serum bile acids &gt;100 μmol/L (40.85 μg/mL) within the past 6 months. Overall, 41 (35%) patients had serum bile acid levels ≥250 μmol/L (≥102.13 μg/mL) at baseline. Among patients in Cohort 1, a lower proportion of patients who had received 24 weeks of odevixibat in Study A4250-005 entered Study A4250-008 with serum bile acid levels ≥250 μmol/L (≥102.13 μg/mL) compared to those who had received placebo (22% vs 53%).

Summary of Baseline Hepatic and Renal Function of the study population are shown in Table 4. The study of 116 patients showed varying levels of hepatic impairment, with 66% classified as mild and 34% as moderate via Child-Pugh classification, though 28% were considered severe by NCI ODWG criteria. Median ALT (52.50 U/L) and AST (66.00 U/L) were similar across groups. Median total bilirubin level overall was 27.95 μmol/L (1.64 mg/dL) and was lower among patients who had received odevixibat in Study A4250-005 (14.00 μmol/L; 0.80 mg/dL) compared to patients who had received placebo (50.70 μmol/L; 2.96 mg/dL) and patients in Cohort 2 (30.60 μmol/L; 1.79 mg/dL). Renal function was normal, with a median eGFR of 161 mL/min/1.73 m2. Additionally, patients exhibited typical PFIC-related deficiencies in fat-soluble vitamins A, E (α tocopherol), and D, while vitamin K levels (measured by INR) remained within the normal range.

Summary of Medical and Surgical History of the study population is provided in Table 5. Overall, 87 patients (75%) had a medical and surgical history. The conditions reported were typical of patients with PFIC, e.g. pruritus, vitamin deficiencies, GI morbidities, jaundice and other hepatobiliary disorders, and poor growth. Twenty (17%) patients reported a history of pruritus, 18 (16%) patients reported prior biliary tract surgery or biliary catheter insertion (i.e. biliary diversion surgeries), 17 (15%) patients reported jaundice, and 16 (14%) patients reported hypovitaminosis.

## CHMP comments

Although this is not a comparative study, baseline characteristics, both anthropometric and disease, appear to be similar between cohorts. However, the high proportion of white participants make outcomes less generalizable to populations elsewhere in the world where PFIC type 3 might be more prevalent (e.g. Saudi-Arabia and Pakistan). This is reflective of the duration of treatment with Bylvay in in the ode/ode group compared to the other patients.

## CHMP comments

Given the debilitating intractable pruritus in PFIC patients and the progression to cirrhosis and ultimately liver failure requiring liver transplantation, the reduction of pruritus and improvement of liver-related parameters are considered crucial outcomes. Odevixibat is effective in lowering serum bile acids rapidly and sustainably. The decrease in serum bile acid is associated with a rapid reduction in pruritus, and consequently, better sleep, and an overall increase in the quality of life for the pediatric patient population studied.

In general, small improvements were observed in ALT, AST, and total bilirubin during long-term treatment with odevixibat, although the results were highly variable as were the results for GGT. While these secondary markers of liver pathology showed encouraging trends, it remains to be determined whether such minor improvements can translate into a delay in the need for surgical biliary diversion or liver transplantation. Data on clinical outcomes was collected in the study, however, the results of this analysis should be viewed with caution because the median time to event was not estimable. Furthermore, the long-term percentages suffer from high volatility because they are based on an extremely small number of patients at risk (n = 2 to 8), meaning a single event could disproportionately skew the results.

## Safety results

Safety was evaluated throughout the study, including monitoring for AEs and concomitant medications, physical examinations, vital signs assessments, laboratory tests (including chemistry, haematology, urinalysis, vitamins A and E, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, and INR), and abdominal ultrasound. Hepatic events underwent review and adjudication of aetiology by an independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)

## Adverse events

Overall, 112 (97%) of 116 patients experienced at least 1 Treatment-emergent AE (TEAE) during the study. Most TEAEs were mild to moderate in intensity and assessed as unrelated to study treatment. Events of severe intensity were reported in 22 (19%) patients overall, including 3 (8%) and 2 (11%) patients who had received odevixibat and placebo, respectively, in Study A4250-005 and 17 (28%) patients in Cohort 2.

Overall, the most reported TEAEs in Study A4250-008 were pyrexia, blood bilirubin increased and upper respiratory tract infection, diarrhea, cough, and nasopharyngitis.

TEAEs in the Infections and Infestations SOC were reported in 82 (71%) of the 116 patients, including 28 (76%) patients who had received odevixibat in Study A4250-005, 13 (68%) patients who had received placebo, and 41 (68%) patients in Cohort 2. Infections reported in ≥10% of patients overall were either seasonal or typical of a pediatric population.

Treatment-emergent AEs in the Investigations SOC were reported in 63 (54%) of the 116 patients, including 54%, 58%, and 53% of patients who had received odevixibat in Study A4250-005, in patients who had received placebo, and in patients in Cohort 2, respectively. The most common TEAEs in this SOC (≥10% of all patients) were blood bilirubin increased (27%), INR increased (17%), and ALT increased (12%). The majority of TEAEs in the Investigations SOC were mild to moderate in intensity. Five (4%) patients had Investigations TEAEs assessed as severe.

No patients experienced an event of clinical hepatitis. Treatment-emergent AEs in the GI Disorders SOC were reported in 58 (50%) of the 116 patients, including 49%, 53%, and 50% of patients who had received odevixibat in Study A4250-005, in patients who received placebo, and patients in Cohort 2. ## ADR's

Overall, 47 (41%) of the 116 patients experienced TEAEs assessed as drug related by the investigator, including 46% and 42% of patients who had received odevixibat and placebo in Study A4250-005, respectively, and 37% of patients in Cohort 2. ## Discussion on clinical aspects

Odevixibat is orally administered and acts locally in the gut where it binds reversibly to IBAT to decrease the reuptake of bile acids into the liver, increasing the clearance of bile acids through the colon and lowering hepatic bile acid load and serum bile acids. Odevixibat is indicated for the treatment of PFIC. PFIC is a life-threatening disease associated with significant morbidity.

## Study design and objectives

Study A4250-008 was conducted as a phase 3, multicentre, open-label extension trial designed to evaluate the long-term efficacy, safety, and durability of odevixibat in patients with PFIC, including episodic forms of PFIC. This study served as a follow-up to A4250-005, which was a placebo-controlled trial focused on establishing statistically significant efficacy over a 24-week period. While the primary endpoint of A4250-005 was the proportion of patients achieving a ≥70% reduction in fasting s-BA or reaching levels ≤70 μmol/L, A4250-008 focused on the mean change from baseline in fasting s-BA after 72 weeks of treatment.

The duration of Study A4250-008, together with enrolment of odevixibat treated patients from Study A4250-005, and the possibility of an additional optional extension period, provides important insights into long-term treatment effects of odevixibat and maintenance of effect.

## Patient population and eligibility

The study population was structured into two cohorts:

- Cohort 1: Comprised rollover patients from A4250-005 (paediatric patients, 6 months to 18 years, with genetically confirmed PFIC Type 1 or 2).
- Cohort 2: Expanded eligibility to include patients of any age and any PFIC subtype, including those that suffer from episodic PFIC and those with a history of biliary diversion surgery.

The inclusion strategy successfully captured a diverse PFIC population regardless of gender, weight, age, or ethnicity. However, patients with pathologic variations of the ABCB11 gene resulting in a complete absence of the BSEP protein were excluded. Because odevixibat is an ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor that relies on a feedback loop of residual biliary bile acid secretion, it is ineffective in patients with zero functional BSEP expression.

## Analysis of inclusion and exclusion criteria

While the criteria were sufficient to capture the \"middle band\" of disease severity, several limiting factors were noted:

- Biochemical Thresholds: The requirement for s-BA ≥100 μmol/L excluded PFIC Type 3 patients with mild to moderate cholestasis.
- Disease Severity: The exclusion of patients with decompensated liver disease, coagulopathy, and significant ascites prevented the inclusion of end-stage PFIC patients to ensure patient safety and data integrity.
- Weight Restrictions: A minimum body weight of ≥5 kg excluded the most severely ill infants.
- Subtype Distribution: While the population reflects European and Dutch demographics, it is less representative of regions where PFIC Type 3 is predominant, such as Saudi Arabia and Pakistan (PFIC3 patients constituted only 11% of Cohort 2).

Also, patients who withdrew early from A4250-005 could still enter A4250-008 (Cohort 1) if they left the study due to patient/caregiver judgment of intolerable symptoms and had completed at least 12

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

weeks of treatment before withdrawing. Withdrawal due to intolerable symptoms doesn't necessarily indicate the drug causes harm. It could reflect insufficient efficacy, disease symptoms, or tolerability preferences. A drug-related AE, by contrast, means investigators formally assessed and attributed the event to the study drug. Patients who withdrew from Study A4250-005 due to a study drug-related AE were therefore not eligible.

The inclusion and exclusion criteria for A4250-008 allow inclusion of a trial population whose demographic characteristics sufficiently resemble the European population, supporting possible extrapolation of the trial results to the real-world (European) population.

## Dosing regimen and protocol amendments

In A4250-008, dosing regimen changed for Cohort 2 inclusions via Amendment 6:

- Pre-Amendment 6: Patients received a starting dose of 120 μg/kg/day.
- Post-Amendment 6: Patients received a starting dose of 40 μg/kg/day.

The original study protocol dates from August 2017 with the first patient enrolled in September 2018 and the study closed in February 2024. Amendment 6 was approved in 2021 meaning that Cohort 2 participants included in the first 3 years of the study received another treatment regimen compared to participants included in the last 3 years of the study.

It is unclear how many participants in Cohort 2 received the starting dose of 120 μg/kg/day before amendment 6 was approved and if participants (including those from Cohort 1) were switched to the 40 μg/kg/day dose after amendment 6 came into effect. However, results from Study A4250-005 showed that odevixibat 40 μg/kg/day had better and more sustained efficacy on the endpoints. Posthoc analyses showed that there was no statistically significant difference between the two odevixibat dose groups in the proportion of positive pruritus assessments at the patient level over the 24-week treatment period (2-sided p-value = 0.5008) or in the proportion of serum bile acid responders (2sided p-value = 0.1083). It is therefore assumed that the study outcomes are not unjustifiably positively influenced by the proportion of patients who received the 120 μg/kg/day starting dose.

The recommended dose as mentioned in the SmPC is 40 μg/kg/day, with potential escalation to 120 μg/kg/day.

## Patient disposition

The study exhibited low discontinuation rates, with 71.6% of patients completing the 72-week period. Discontinuations were balanced across subgroups. Nine patients, from the 116 enrolled patients, discontinued treatment period early because of AEs. Withdrawals attributed to \"other\" reasons were primarily due to referrals for liver transplantation or unsatisfactory pruritus results.

## Baseline characteristics

Baseline characteristics, both anthropometric and disease, appear to be similar between treatment groups. A large proportion of the study participants were of the white race, providing good representation of both PFIC type 1 and type 2. This makes the findings well suited for realistic extrapolating treatment outcomes within Europe. Other baseline disease characteristics such as presence of s-BA &gt;100 µmol/L and severe pruritus, seem not to be notably different between subgroups. Interestingly, bilirubin levels, as well as Child-Pugh and hepatic impairment classification scores, were found to be lower in the odevixibat/ odevixibat group compared to the former treatment naïve placebo/odevixibat and Cohort 2 groups. This is reflective of the longer treatment duration of the odevixibat/odevixibat cohort.

## Treatment outcomes and efficacy

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Descriptive statistics were presented for primary, secondary, and exploratory endpoints, and no significance tests were performed. However, the primary objective of this study was to demonstrate a sustained effect of odevixibat and to evaluate the long-term safety and tolerability of repeated daily doses, and not to test for superiority of odevixibat over another treatment or placebo control. The descriptive statistics are sufficient to answer the objectives, and the absence of significance testing is therefore not a concern.

Odevixibat demonstrated efficacy in rapidly and sustainably lowering serum bile acids (primary efficacy endpoint), which correlated with a significant reduction in pruritus (secondary efficacy endpoint) and an improved quality of life for paediatric patients (secondary efficacy endpoint). Small improvements were observed in ALT, and AST, though results for GGT were highly variable. The lower baseline bilirubin in the long-term odevixibat group suggests that sustained treatment may improve bile flow and reduce hepatic stress.

## Safety

This study provides data from patients treated with odevixibat with a substantial follow-up duration. For the patients that switched from placebo in study 005 to odevixibat in 008, the median medication exposure was 162 weeks. Patients that were already on odevixibat in study 005 received odevixibat for an additional median 135 weeks in study 008. Cohort 2 patients received odevixibat for a median of 89 weeks in study 008.

While the incidence of TEAEs is very high (97%), this is common in long-term paediatric studies. The fact that the majority of these events were mild to moderate, unrelated to the drug with a low study withdrawal rates suggests that odevixibat is generally well-tolerated drug with an acceptable safety profile.

Reported ADRs mainly consisted of ADRs in the SOCs investigations (ALT/AST/bilirubin increased) and GI tract (diarrhoea, nausea and abdominal pain). These are known ADRs of odevixibat which are labelled in the SmPC section 4.8. No safety concerns arose from the submitted data.

## Conclusion

The inclusion and exclusion criteria of A4250-008 provided a trial population that sufficiently resembles the European PFIC population, supporting the extrapolation of these results to real-world clinical practice within Europe. Given the debilitating intractable pruritus in PFIC patients and the progression to cirrhosis and ultimately liver failure requiring liver transplantation, the reduction of pruritus and improvement of liver-related parameters are considered crucial outcomes. The study results indicate that the effect observed in the pivotal trial is maintained over many years of treatment and hence support long term efficacy. Data from patients switched to placebo and from cohort 2 is supportive of the short term efficacy of odevixibat. No new safety concerns were identified.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

The study results confirm earlier assessments. The B/R of odevixibat in the treatment of PFIC remains positive. No update to the SmPC is considered warranted.
