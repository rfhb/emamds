## 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

Povofortay is indicated for the treatment of active moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa (acne inversa) in adults who have responded inadequately to, or who are intolerant or contraindicated to an anti-TNF therapy.

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration

Treatment should be initiated and supervised by a physician experienced in the diagnosis and treatment of hidradenitis suppurativa.

## Posology

The dose is 45 mg or 75 mg once daily based on individual patient characteristics:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- A dose of 45 mg once daily is recommended for patients at higher risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE), major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and malignancy (see section 4.4).
- A dose of 75 mg once daily may be appropriate for patients with high disease burden who are not at higher risk of VTE, MACE and malignancy (see section 4.4)
- A dose of 75 mg once daily may also be appropriate for patients with an inadequate response to 45 mg once daily.
- The lowest effective dose to maintain response should be used.

Consideration should be given to discontinuing treatment in patients who have shown no evidence of therapeutic benefit after 12 weeks of treatment. Patients with an initial partial response at week 12 may subsequently improve with continued treatment.

If retreatment with povorcitinib is indicated, the above guidance on dose and treatment duration should be followed.

## Treatment initiation

Treatment with povorcitinib should not be initiated in patients with a platelet count &lt; 50 × 10 9 /L, an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) &lt; 1 × 10 3 /mm 3 , or who have a haemoglobin value &lt; 8 g/dL (see section 4.4).

If a patient develops a serious infection, treatment should be interrupted until the infection is controlled (see section 4.4).

## Missed doses

If a dose is missed, patients should be advised to take the dose as soon as possible unless it is less than 12 hours before the next dose, in which case the patient should not take the missed dose. Thereafter, dosing should be resumed at the regular scheduled time.

## Interactions

For patients receiving chronic treatment with strong systemic CYP3A4 inhibitors, the recommended dose is 45 mg once daily (see section 4.5).

Co-administration of povorcitinib with chronic use of strong systemic CYP3A4 inducers is not recommended (see section 4.5).

## Special populations

## Elderly

For patients 65 years of age and older, the recommended dose is 45 mg once daily (see section 4.4).

## Renal impairment

No dose adjustment is required in patients with renal impairment, including patients with end stage renal disease (ESRD) (see section 5.2).

## Hepatic impairment

No dose adjustment is required in patients with mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) hepatic impairment (see section 5.2). For patients with severe (Child-Pugh C) hepatic impairment, the maximum recommended dose is 45 mg once daily (see section 5.2).

## Paediatric population

There is no relevant use of povorcitinib in children under 12 years of age for the treatment of hidradenitis suppurativa.

The safety and efficacy of povorcitinib in children and adolescents between 12 and less than 18 years of age have not yet been established. No data are available.

## Method of administration

For oral use.

Tablets can be taken with or without food. Tablets should be swallowed whole and should not be split, crushed or chewed.

## 4.3 Contraindications

Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1. Active serious systemic infections, including active tuberculosis (TB) (see section 4.4). Pregnancy and breast-feeding (see section 4.6).

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

Povorcitinib should only be used if no suitable treatment alternatives are available in patients:

- 65 years of age and older;
- patients with history of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or other cardiovascular risk factors (such as current or past long-time smokers);
- patients with malignancy risk factors (e.g. current malignancy or history of malignancy).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Serious infections

Infections, including serious infections, have been reported in patients receiving povorcitinib (see section 4.8).

As there is a higher incidence of infections in elderly and in the diabetic population in general, caution should be used when treating the elderly and patients with diabetes. In patients 65 years of age and older, povorcitinib should only be used if no suitable treatment alternatives are available (see section 4.2).

Treatment with povorcitinib must not be initiated in patients with an active, serious infection (see section 4.3).

Risks and benefits of treatment prior to initiating povorcitinib should be considered for patients:

- with chronic or recurrent infections
- who have been exposed to tuberculosis (TB)
- with a history of a serious or an opportunistic infection
- who have resided or travelled in areas of endemic TB or endemic mycoses; or
- with underlying conditions that may predispose them to infection.

Patients should be closely monitored for the development of signs and symptoms of infection during and after treatment with povorcitinib. A patient who develops a new infection during treatment should undergo prompt and complete diagnostic testing and appropriate antimicrobial therapy should be initiated. The patient should be closely monitored, and povorcitinib therapy should be interrupted if the patient is not responding to standard therapy. Povorcitinib may be resumed once the infection is controlled.

## Tuberculosis

Patients should be screened for TB before starting povorcitinib therapy. Povorcitinib must not be given to patients with active TB (see section 4.3). Anti-TB therapy should be considered prior to initiation of povorcitinib in patients with previously untreated latent TB or in patients with risk factors for TB infection. Patients should be monitored for the development of signs and symptoms of TB.

## Viral reactivation

Viral reactivation, including cases of herpes virus reactivation (e.g. herpes zoster) was reported in clinical studies (see section 4.8). If a patient develops herpes zoster, interruption of treatment should be considered until the episode resolves.

Screening for viral hepatitis and monitoring for reactivation should be performed in accordance with clinical guidelines before starting and during treatment with povorcitinib. Patients with evidence of hepatitis B or C infection were excluded from studies with povorcitinib. If there is evidence of reactivation, a liver specialist should be consulted.

## Malignancy

Malignancies have been reported in patients receiving JAK inhibitors, including povorcitinib.

In a large randomised active-controlled study of tofacitinib (another oral JAK inhibitor) in rheumatoid arthritis patients 50 years and older with at least one additional cardiovascular risk factor, a higher rate of malignancies, particularly lung cancer, lymphoma and non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC), was observed with tofacitinib compared to tumour necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors.

In patients over 65 years of age, patients who are current or past long-time smokers, or with other malignancy risk factors (e.g. current malignancy or history of malignancy), povorcitinib should only be used if no suitable treatment alternatives are available.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC)

NMSCs have been reported in patients treated with povorcitinib. Periodic skin examination is recommended for all patients, particularly those who are at increased risk for skin cancer.

## Venous thromboembolism (VTE)

Events of deep venous thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) have been reported in patients receiving povorcitinib.

In a large randomised active-controlled study of tofacitinib (another JAK inhibitor) in rheumatoid arthritis patients 50 years and older with at least one additional cardiovascular risk factor, a dose-dependent higher rate of VTE including DVT and PE was observed with tofacitinib compared to TNF inhibitors.

In patients with cardiovascular or malignancy risk factors (see also section 4.4 \"Major adverse cardiovascular events\" and \"Malignancy\") povorcitinib should only be used if no suitable treatment alternatives are available.

In patients with known VTE risk factors other than cardiovascular or malignancy risk factors, povorcitinib should be used with caution. VTE risk factors other than cardiovascular or malignancy risk factors include previous VTE, patients undergoing major surgery, immobilisation, use of combined hormonal contraceptives or hormone replacement therapy, inherited coagulation disorder.

Patients should be re-evaluated periodically during povorcitinib treatment to assess for changes in VTE risk.

Promptly evaluate patients with signs and symptoms of VTE and discontinue povorcitinib in patients with suspected VTE, regardless of dose.

## Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE)

Events of MACE have been observed in patients taking povorcitinib.

In a large randomised active-controlled study of tofacitinib (another JAK inhibitor) in rheumatoid arthritis, patients 50 years and older with at least one additional cardiovascular risk factor, a higher rate of MACE, defined as cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction and non-fatal stroke was observed with tofacitinib compared to TNF inhibitors.

Therefore, in patients 65 years of age and older, patient who are current or past long-time smokers and patients with history of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or other cardiovascular risk factors, povorcitinib should only be used if no suitable treatment alternatives are available.

## Haematological abnormalities

Platelet counts of &lt; 50 × 10 9 /L, an ANC &lt; 1× 10 3 /mm 3 , and haemoglobin &lt; 8 g/dL were reported in the povorcitinib clinical studies. Treatment should not be initiated, or should be interrupted in patients with platelet counts &lt; 50 × 10 9 /L, an ANC &lt; 1 × 10 3 /mm 3 or haemoglobin &lt; 8 g/dL (see section 4.2). Complete blood count should be monitored before treatment initiation and thereafter according to individual patient management (see section 4.2).

## Lipids

Dose-dependent increases in blood lipid parameters were reported in patients treated with povorcitinib compared to placebo. Patients with abnormal lipid parameters should be further monitored and managed according to clinical guidelines (see section 4.2). The effect of these lipid parameter elevations on cardiovascular morbidity and mortality has not been determined.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Vaccination

No data are available on the response to vaccination in patients receiving povorcitinib. Use of live, attenuated vaccines should be avoided during or immediately prior to treatment. Prior to initiating treatment with this medicinal product, it is recommended that patients are brought up to date with all immunisations, including prophylactic herpes zoster vaccinations, in agreement with current immunisation guidelines.

## Use in patients 65 years of age and older

Considering the increased risk of MACE, malignancies, serious infections, and all-cause mortality in patients 65 years of age and older, as observed in a large, randomised study of tofacitinib (another JAK inhibitor), povorcitinib should only be used in these patients if no suitable treatment alternatives are available.

## Immunosuppressive medicinal products

Combination with biologic immunomodulators, potent immunosuppressants such as ciclosporin or other Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors has not been studied. Their concomitant use with povorcitinib is not recommended as a risk of additive immunosuppression cannot be excluded.

## Combination with strong CYP3A4 inducers

Co-administration of povorcitinib with chronic use of strong systemic CYP3A4 inducers is not recommended (see section 4.5).

## Excipients with known effect

## Sodium

This medicinal product contains less than 1 mmol sodium (23 mg) per tablet, that is to say essentially 'sodium-free'.

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

## Potential for other medicinal products to affect the pharmacokinetics of povorcitinib

Povorcitinib is metabolised primarily by CYP3A4. Therefore, povorcitinib plasma exposures can be affected by medicinal products that strongly inhibit or induce CYP3A4.

## Co-administration with strong systemic CYP3A4 inhibitors

Povorcitinib exposure is increased when co-administered with strong systemic CYP3A4 inhibitors (such as clarithromycin, itraconazole, ketoconazole, posaconazole, and voriconazole). In a clinical study, coadministration of povorcitinib with itraconazole resulted in an 83% increase in povorcitinib AUC0-∞, with no clinically meaningful impact on povorcitinib Cmax. For patients receiving chronic treatment with strong systemic CYP3A4 inhibitors, the recommended dose is 45 mg once daily (see section 4.2).

## Co-administration with moderate systemic CYP3A4 inhibitors

Povorcitinib exposure is increased when co-administered with moderate systemic CYP3A4 inhibitors (such as erythromycin and fluconazole). Using physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) modelling, these inhibitors are predicted to increase povorcitinib AUC0-∞, with no clinically meaningful impact on povorcitinib Cmax. No dose adjustment of povorcitinib is required in patients receiving moderate systemic CYP3A4 inhibitors.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Co-administration with strong systemic CYP3A4 inducers

Povorcitinib exposure is decreased when co-administered with strong systemic CYP3A4 inducers (such as rifampicin and phenytoin), which may lead to reduced therapeutic effect of povorcitinib. In a clinical study, co-administration of povorcitinib with rifampicin decreased povorcitinib Cmax and AUC0-∞ by approximately 46% and 80%, respectively. Co-administration of povorcitinib with chronic use of strong systemic CYP3A4 inducers is not recommended (see section 4.4).

## Co-administration with moderate systemic CYP3A4 inducers

Povorcitinib exposure may decrease when it is co-administered with moderate systemic CYP3A4 inducers (such as cenobamate, metamizole, phenobarbital, and primidone), which may lead to reduced therapeutic effect of povorcitinib. PBPK modelling predicted that co-administration of povorcitinib after multiple doses of efavirenz (a moderate CYP3A4 inducer) resulted in approximately 15% and 58% decreases in povorcitinib Cmax and AUC, respectively. Patients should be monitored for changes in disease activity if povorcitinib is co-administered with moderate systemic CYP3A4 inducers.

## Potential for povorcitinib to affect the pharmacokinetics of other medicinal products

Povorcitinib did not produce clinically significant changes in the exposures of oral contraceptives (e.g. levonorgestrel and ethinyl oestradiol) or substrates of the efflux transporter BCRP (e.g. rosuvastatin), or renal transporters OCT2/MATE1/MATE2-K (e.g. metformin).

In vitro, povorcitinib is an inhibitor of P-glycoprotein (P-gp). Co-administration of digoxin (a sensitive P-gp substrate), with a single dose of povorcitinib 75 mg increased digoxin AUC0-t and Cmax by approximately 26% and 38%, respectively, compared with administration alone. Caution should be exercised for concomitant use of povorcitinib with digoxin. The effect of povorcitinib on the pharmacokinetics of other P-gp substrates has not been evaluated. Caution should be exercised as the levels of P-gp substrates with a narrow therapeutic index, such as dabigatran, may increase.

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation

## Women of childbearing potential

Women of childbearing potential have to use effective contraception during treatment with povorcitinib and for at least 4 weeks after the last dose.

Povofortay is not recommended in women of childbearing potential not using contraception.

## Pregnancy

There are limited amount of data on the use of povorcitinib in pregnant women. Studies in animals have shown reproductive toxicity (see section 5.3). Povofortay is contraindicated during pregnancy (see section 4.3).

## Breast-feeding

It is unknown whether povorcitinib or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Available data in animals suggest that exposure to povorcitinib during lactation may occur (see section 5.3). A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded. Povofortay is contraindicated during breast-feeding (see section 4.3).

## Fertility

The effect of povorcitinib on human fertility has not been investigated. In rat studies, povorcitinib had no effect on fertility in males, but resulted in reversible effects on female fertility (see section 5.3).

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines

Povofortay has no or negligible influence on the ability to drive and use machines.

## Description of selected adverse reactions

## Infections

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), for up to 12 weeks, infections were reported in 28.2% of patients treated with povorcitinib 45 mg, 35% of patients treated with povorcitinib 75 mg, and 24.9% of patients treated with placebo. The majority of infections were nonserious, mild to moderate in severity, and did not lead to discontinuation of povorcitinib. The incidence of patients reporting infection-related adverse reactions in the 45 mg and 75 mg groups compared to placebo were: upper respiratory tract infections (13.3% and 14.7% versus 10.1%), folliculitis (1.2% and 3.7% versus 1%), urinary tract infection (3.4% and 2.2% versus 1.5%), herpes zoster (0.5% and 0.7% versus 0%). In the overall treatment period, for up to 54 weeks, infections were reported in 49.2% of patients (65.2 per 100 patient-years) treated with povorcitinib 45 mg and 55.1% of patients (71.9 per 100 patient-years) treated with povorcitinib 75 mg.

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), most of the herpes zoster events involved a single dermatome and were non-serious, mild to moderate in severity and did not lead to discontinuation of povorcitinib. For up to 12 weeks, the incidence rate of herpes zoster in patients treated with povorcitinib 45 mg was 2.2 per 100 patient-years, in patients treated with 75 mg was 3.3 per 100 patient-years, and in patients treated with placebo was 0 per 100 patient-years. In the overall treatment period, for up to 54 weeks, the incidence rate of herpes zoster in patients treated with povorcitinib 45 mg was 2.5 per 100 patient-years and in patients treated with 75 mg was 2.6 per 100 patient-years.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), for up to 12 weeks, the incidence rate of serious infections was 0 per 100 patient-years in patients treated with povorcitinib 45 mg, 2.2 per 100 patient-years in patients treated with 75 mg, and in patients treated with placebo was 6.6 per 100 patient-years. In the overall treatment period, for up to 54 weeks, the incidence rate of serious infections in patients treated with povorcitinib 45 mg was 1.1 per 100 patient-years and in patients treated with 75 mg was 2.6 per 100 patient-years.

## CPK elevation

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), for up to 12 weeks, blood creatine phosphokinase (CPK) increased were reported in 3.2% of patients treated with povorcitinib 45 mg, 2.7% of patients treated with povorcitinib 75 mg, and 0.7% of patients treated with placebo. Most elevations were transient and none led to discontinuation.

## Lipid elevations

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), for up to 12 weeks, there was a doserelated increase in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), total cholesterol, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c) relative to placebo. Elevations were observed within 6 weeks and remained stable thereafter, through week 54. There was no meaningful change in the HDL/LDL ratio or in the triglyceride levels in patients treated with povorcitinib relative to patients treated with placebo. Events related to hyperlipidaemia occurred in 2.7% of patients exposed to povorcitinib 45 mg, 2.0% of patients exposed to 75 mg and 2.2% of patients exposed to placebo and none led to discontinuation (see section 4.4).

## Neutropenia

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), for up to 12 weeks, neutropenia occurred in 0.2% of patients treated with 75 mg, and 0.0% of patients treated with 45 mg or placebo. Doserelated changes in neutrophil counts were transient, and observed within 3 weeks, after which the neutrophil count returned towards baseline despite continued therapy. There was no clear relationship between neutropenia and the occurrence of serious infections.

## Thrombocytopenia

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), for up to 12 weeks, thrombocytopenia occurred in 1.0% of patients treated with 75 mg, and 0.0% of patients treated with 45 mg or placebo. Dose-related changes in platelet counts were transient, and observed within 3 weeks, after which the platelet count returned towards baseline despite continued therapy.

## Increased transaminases

In placebo-controlled studies (STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2), for up to 12 weeks, events of increased ALT values (&gt; 3 × ULN) occurred in 0.7% of patients treated with 75 mg, and 0.2% of patients treated with 45 mg, and 0.0% of patients treated with placebo. Increased AST values (&gt; 3 × ULN) occurred in 0.5% of patients treated with 75 mg or 45 mg and 0.2% of patients treated with placebo. Most elevations were transient.

## Reporting of suspected adverse reactions

Reporting suspected adverse reactions after authorisation of the medicinal product is important. It allows continued monitoring of the benefit/risk balance of the medicinal product. Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions via the national reporting system listed in Appendix V.

## 4.9 Overdose

Povorcitinib was administered in clinical studies up to single oral doses of 450 mg once daily (6 times the recommended human dose of 75 mg once daily) and multiple oral doses up to 120 mg once daily for 10 days. Adverse reactions were comparable to those seen at lower doses and no specific toxicities were identified. Pharmacokinetics data in healthy adult participants indicate that more than 90% of the administrated dose up to 450 mg is expected to be eliminated after 5 days.

In the event of overdose, it is recommended that the patient be monitored for any signs and symptoms of adverse reactions (see section 4.8) and appropriate symptomatic treatment be instituted immediately.

## 5. PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Immunosuppressants, Janus-associated kinase (JAK) inhibitors, ATC code: not yet assigned

## Mechanism of action

Povorcitinib is a Janus kinase (JAK) 1 inhibitor. JAK1 is a member of the JAK family of intracellular enzymes, which also includes JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2). JAKs transmit signals arising from cytokine- or growth factor-receptor interactions on the cell membrane. Within the signalling pathway, JAKs phosphorylate and activate signal transducers and activators of transcription (STATs) which modulate gene expression. This influences cellular processes of immune cell function and haematopoiesis depending on the specific JAK isoforms involved. Povorcitinib modulates the JAK1 signalling pathway, preventing the phosphorylation and activation of associated STATs. By inhibiting immune cell activation by JAK1-dependent cytokines (such as IL-6, IL-2, IL-22) povorcitinib attenuates skin and systemic inflammation relevant to hidradenitis suppurativa.

## Pharmacodynamic effects

## Inhibition of IL-6 induced STAT3 phosphorylation

In healthy volunteers, oral administration of povorcitinib resulted in dose-dependent inhibition of IL-6-induced pSTAT3, a pharmacodynamic marker of JAK1 signalling. Maximal inhibition was observed between 1 and 6 hours after administration.

## High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP)

Decreases from baseline in serum hsCRP, an inflammatory marker primarily induced by IL-6 signaling through the JAK1 pathway were observed with povorcitinib as early as the first study visit (week 3).

## Cardiac electrophysiology

The QT interval prolongation potential of povorcitinib was assessed in healthy subjects who received a single dose of 450 mg povorcitinib in a placebo- and positive-controlled thorough QT study. No clinically relevant effect on the QTc interval was observed.

## Clinical efficacy and safety

The efficacy and safety of povorcitinib were evaluated in 1227 adult patients with moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) in two Phase 3 multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebocontrolled studies, STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2. Patients had a diagnosis of HS (Hurley Stage II or III) for at least 3 months and were required to have at least 5 inflammatory lesions (i.e. number of abscesses plus number of inflammatory nodules) affecting at least 2 anatomical areas at baseline.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Patients had a history of inadequate response to a 3-month or longer course of 1 or more conventional systemic therapy (oral antibiotic or biologic medicinal product) for HS or demonstrated intolerance to or had a contraindication to a conventional systemic therapy for HS. All patients received prior systemic treatment; of which 96% had received prior oral antibiotic therapy and 37% had received prior biologic therapy (predominantly adalimumab). A total of 330 patients comprised the pooled postanti-TNF population, defined as a history of inadequate response to a 3-month or longer course of 1 or more anti-TNF biologic drug for HS or demonstrated intolerance to or had a contraindication to an anti-TNF biologic drug for HS.

In STOP-HS1 and STOP-HS2, patients were randomised 1:1:1 to receive povorcitinib 45 mg once daily, povorcitinib 75 mg once daily for 54 weeks, or placebo once daily up to week 12 followed by povorcitinib 45 mg once daily or povorcitinib 75 mg once daily up to week 54. To evaluate the use of povorcitinib as monotherapy, concomitant systemic antibiotic use for HS was not permitted during the placebo-controlled period, except for rescue (imputed as non-responders).

The primary endpoint in both studies was the proportion of patients achieving Hidradenitis Suppurativa Clinical Response 50 (HiSCR50) at week 12, i.e., at least a 50% reduction in the total abscess and inflammatory nodule count with no increase in abscess or draining tunnel count relative to baseline. Key secondary endpoints included the proportion of patients who achieved HiSCR75 at week 12, the proportion of patients achieving Skin Pain NRS30 at week 12 among patients with baseline Skin Pain numerical rating scale (NRS) score ≥ 3, and the proportion of patients with a flare by week 12.

Pre-planned analyses provide data on the indicated post-anti-TNF population. STOP-HS1 evaluated 608 patients and STOP-HS2 evaluated 619 patients, of whom 154 patients (25.3%) and 176 patients (28.4%), respectively, represented the post-anti-TNF population. Baseline characteristics for this group were consistent across both studies and reflective of a population with moderate to severe HS. In the pooled post-anti-TNF population, the most commonly received prior biologic therapy was adalimumab and 87.6% of patients had received prior oral antibiotic therapy. Patients had a mean disease duration of 12.3 years. The proportions of Hurley Stage II and Stage III patients were 52% (48.7% in STOP-HS1 and 55.1% in STOP-HS2) and 48% (51.3% in STOP-HS1 and 44.9% in STOPHS2) respectively. The mean abscess and inflammatory nodule (AN) count was 14.4 and the mean number of draining tunnels was 3.7. ## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

## Absorption

Following oral single-dose or multiple-dose administration, povorcitinib is absorbed with a median Tmax of 2 to 4 hours. Steady state plasma concentrations are achieved within 9 days with an accumulation ratio of 2.2 to 2.6 -fold. Coadministration of povorcitinib with a high-fat meal had no clinically relevant effect on povorcitinib exposures at the 45 mg or 75 mg dose level (increased AUC0∞ by 8% to 14% and Cmax by 6% to 13%). In clinical trials, povorcitinib was administered without regard to meals. Based on population pharmacokinetic analysis, povorcitinib pharmacokinetics were linear across the evaluated dose range (6 mg to 225 mg).

## Medicinal product concentrations in skin

In healthy subjects, following oral administration of povorcitinib 75 mg once daily, concentrations of the medicinal product in skin tissue and plasma were similar for at least 7 days (168 hours) after the last dose.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Distribution

Povorcitinib is highly bound to plasma proteins, with a human ex vivo unbound fraction of 6.4%. Povorcitinib has limited distribution into blood cells, with a blood-to-plasma total radioactivity ratio of 0.656 to 0.887. Povorcitinib is highly bound to skin proteins, with an unbound fraction of 4.2% in dermatomed (epidermis plus dermis) human skin homogenate. Based on population pharmacokinetic analysis, the estimated apparent volume of distribution (Vd/F) was 285.9 L in HS patients.

## Biotransformation

The in vitro CYP phenotyping and metabolite profiling results showed that povorcitinib was mainly metabolised by CYP3A4, with lesser contributions from UGT1A4 and UGT1A3. In a human radiolabelled study, unchanged povorcitinib accounted for 69.3% of the total radioactivity in plasma while the only major metabolite (M12, product of N-glucuronidation of the dimethyl pyrazole moiety) accounted for 25.9% of the total circulating radioactivity. The pharmacologic activity of povorcitinib is attributed to the parent molecule. No active metabolites have been identified for povorcitinib.

## Elimination

Following single oral administration of [ 14 C]-povorcitinib, radioactivity was mainly excreted in faeces (59.2%) and lower amout in urine (30.6%). Intact povorcitinib was the most abundant analyte detected in human excreta (7.1% in urine and 29.1% in faeces), while the most abundant metabolite (M3, product of hydroxylation of the dimethyl bipyrazole moiety) comprised 12% of the administrated dose. Povorcitinib mean terminal elimination half-life ranged from 27 to 35 hours.

## Special populations

## Body weight, age, gender, and ethnicity

Body weight, age, gender, race, and ethnicity did not have a clinically meaningful effect on povorcitinib exposure.

## Renal impairment

Povorcitinib Cmax was 21% lower while AUC was 20% higher in subjects with severe renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate 15 to 29 ml/min/1.73 m 2 ) compared with subjects with normal renal function, where eGFR was calculated using Modification of Diet in Renal Disease (MDRD) formula. These changes in exposure are not considered clinically relevant. No dose adjustment is required in subjects with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate 15 to 89 ml/min/1.73 m 2 ).

In subjects with ESRD, povorcitinib Cmax was 36% and 31% lower, and AUC was 40% and 26% lower before and after haemodialysis, respectively, compared to subjects with normal renal function. Haemodialysis had no impact on povorcitinib pharmacokinetics. These changes in exposure are not considered clinically relevant and there is no safety concern based on the slightly lower plasma exposure.

No dose adjustment is required in subjects with ESRD, including those receiving haemodialysis.

## Hepatic impairment

Plasma povorcitinib (total or unbound) AUC and Cmax were similar for subjects with moderate hepatic impairment compared with healthy subjects with normal hepatic function. No dose adjustment is required for subjects with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Classes A and B).

In subjects with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C), unbound povorcitinib Cmax and AUC were approximately 40% and 76% higher, respectively, compared to subjects with normal hepatic function. Due to the increased unbound exposure, the maximum recommended dose in subjects with severe hepatic impairment is 45 mg per day (see section 4.2).

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data

Non-clinical data reveal no special hazard based on conventional studies of safety pharmacology, genotoxicity and carcinogenic potential.

## General toxicity

In non-clinical repeat-dose toxicology studies, effects were observed on the immune and haematopoietic systems that were attributed to the pharmacological properties (JAK inhibition) of povorcitinib.

Transient and reversible microscopic findings in the bone were observed in a 1-month toxicity study in Sprague-Dawley rats and in a 3-month study in Long-Evans rats [40 times the human AUC at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 75 mg] at an age comparable to adolescent humans ≥ 12 years; no bone findings were observed at similar doses in the 3- and 6-month rat toxicity studies (up to 42 times the human AUC at the MRHD of 75 mg). No bone findings were observed in repeatdose toxicity studies in dogs (comparable to adolescent humans ≥ 12 years; up to 9 times the human AUC at the MRHD of 75 mg).

## Reproductive and developmental toxicity

In fertility and early embryonic development studies, povorcitinib had no effects on fertility or spermatogenesis in male rats (exposures up to 37 times the human AUC at the MRHD of 75 mg). Povorcitinib resulted in adverse effects on female fertility (lower fertility index, corpora lutea, implantation sites and increased pre- and post-implantation loss). These effects reversed after cessation of treatment for 3 weeks. No fertility effects were noted at exposures equal to 0.9 times the human AUC at the MRHD of 75 mg.

In embryo-foetal development studies, oral administration of povorcitinib to pregnant rats and rabbits resulted in decreased embryofoetal survival (post-implantation loss) and decreased foetal weight in both species. In rats, an increased incidence of a common skeletal variation (rudimentary 14th ribs) was noted. No foetal malformations directly related to povorcitinib were observed in either rats or rabbits. Rabbits had an increased incidence of a common background malformation (omphalocele), which was considered secondary to effects on foetal weight. Povorcitinib showed no adverse effects on embryo-foetal development at exposures up to 4.1 and 4.9 times the human AUC at the MRHD of 75 mg in rats and rabbits, respectively.

In a pre- and postnatal development study, pregnant rats at the high dose (exposures equal to 16 times the human AUC at the MRHD of 75 mg) showed evidence of dystocia with prolonged parturition, while offspring at this dose had lower body weights and lower postnatal survival. Limited usage of forelimbs and hindlimbs consistent with necropsy findings of malrotated paws and skeletal variations of bent or misshapen limb bones resulted in early termination of offspring at postnatal day (PND) 21. No maternal or developmental toxicity was observed at exposures equal to 0.6 times the human AUC at the MRHD of 75 mg. Povorcitinib exposure was confirmed in offspring during the lactation period. The mean F1 pups to F0 females ratio of povorcitinib plasma concentration at 2 hours post-dose on PND 10 ranged from 0.08 to 0.2.

## 1. What Povofortay is and what it is used for

## What Povofortay is

Povofortay contains the active substance povorcitinib, which belongs to a group of medicines called Janus kinase inhibitors that help to reduce inflammation.

Povofortay is used to treat a condition called hidradenitis suppurativa, also sometimes called acne inversa. It is used in adults whose condition has not improved enough with other medicines known as anti-TNF therapy or who cannot receive treatment with these medicines.

Hidradenitis suppurativa is a long-term inflammatory skin condition that causes painful lesions including:

- nodules (lumps)
- abscesses (fluid-filled boils)
- tunnels (channels under the skin formed when lumps or boils connect) from which fluid may leak (discharge), causing an unpleasant smell or itching.

It most commonly affects specific areas of the skin such as under the breasts, armpits, inner thighs, groin and buttocks. Scarring may occur in the affected areas.

The active substance in Povofortay, povorcitinib, works by reducing the activity of an enzyme (protein) in the body called 'JAK1' (Janus kinase 1), which is involved in inflammation. By reducing the activity of this enzyme, povorcitinib helps to reduce lesions and pain caused by hidradenitis suppurativa.

## 2. What you need to know before you take Povofortay

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Do not take Povofortay

- if you are allergic to povorcitinib or any of the other ingredients of this medicine (listed in section 6).
- if you have an active serious infection, including tuberculosis.
- if you are pregnant or breast-feeding.

## Warnings and precautions

Talk to your doctor, nurse or pharmacist before taking Povofortay if you:

- are older than 65 years of age, Patients aged 65 years and older may be at increased risk of infections, heart problems including heart attack and some types of cancer. Your doctor will discuss with you if Povofortay is appropriate for you.
- have an infection or you often get infections. Tell your doctor if you get symptoms of an infection such as fever, wounds different from your usual hidradenitis suppurativa, feeling more tired than usual or dental problems. Povofortay can reduce your body's ability to fight infections, may make an existing infection worse, or may increase the chance of getting a new infection. If you have diabetes or are aged 65 years or older you may have an increased chance of getting infections.
- have, or have had tuberculosis or have been in close contact with someone with tuberculosis. Your doctor will test you for tuberculosis before starting Povofortay and may retest during treatment with Povofortay. Tell your doctor if you develop a persistent cough or fever, night sweats and weight loss during treatment with Povofortay as these can be signs of tuberculosis.
- have had a herpes zoster infection (shingles), because Povofortay may allow it to come back. Tell your doctor if you get a painful skin rash with blisters as these can be signs of shingles.
- have or have had hepatitis B or C.
- have recently had or plan to have a vaccination. This is because certain vaccines known as live vaccines are not recommended while using Povofortay.
- have, or have had in the past cancer, smoke or have smoked in the past. Your doctor will discuss with you if Povofortay is appropriate for you.
- Non-melanoma skin cancer has been seen in patients taking Povofortay. Your doctor may recommend that you have regular skin examinations while taking Povofortay. If new skin lesions appear during or after treatment or if existing lesions change appearance, tell your doctor.
- have or have had heart problems. Your doctor will discuss with you if Povofortay is appropriate for you.
- have had blood clots in the veins of your legs (deep vein thrombosis) or lungs (pulmonary embolism) or have an increased risk for developing this (for example: if you had recent major surgery, if you use hormonal contraception or hormonal replacement therapy, or if a blood clotting disorder is identified in you or your close relatives). Your doctor will discuss with you if Povofortay is appropriate for you. Tell your doctor if you get a swelling in the leg or arm, leg pain or tenderness, or redness or discolouration in the leg or arm, chest pain or pain in the upper back, shortness of breath or difficulty breathing as these can be signs of blood clots in the veins.

## Blood tests

Your doctor will carry out blood tests before or during Povofortay treatment and may adjust your treatment if necessary.

## Children and adolescents

Do not give Povofortay to children and adolescents under 18 years of age because it has not been studied in this age group.

## Other medicines and Povofortay

Tell your doctor or pharmacist if you are taking, have recently taken or might take any other medicines. This is because some medicines may reduce how well Povofortay works or may increase the risk of getting side effects. It is very important to talk to your doctor or pharmacist if you are taking any of the following medicines:

- clarithromycin, rifampicin, or erythromycin: medicines to treat bacterial infections

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- itraconazole, ketoconazole, posaconazole voriconazole, or fluconazole: medicines to treat fungal infections
- cenobamate, phenobarbital, phenytoin or primidone: medicines to treat seizures
- metamizole: medicine to treat pain
- ciclosporin, biologic immunomodulators, or other systemic Janus kinase inhibitors: medicines that affect your immune system

## Pregnancy, breast-feeding and fertility

If you are pregnant or breast-feeding, think you may be pregnant or are planning to have a baby, ask your doctor or pharmacist for advice before taking this medicine.

## Contraception (birth control) in women

If you can become pregnant, use effective contraception (birth control) during treatment with Povofortay and for at least 4 weeks after your last dose. Your doctor can advise you on suitable methods of contraception.

## Pregnancy

Do not use Povofortay if you are pregnant, as it can harm the unborn baby. Tell your doctor immediately if you become pregnant or think you have become pregnant during treatment.

## Breast-feeding

Do not use Povofortay while breast-feeding, as it is not known if this medicine passes into breast milk or if breast-fed babies are affected.

## Fertility

The effect of povorcitinib on human fertility has not been studied. Studies in animals suggest that Povofortay could cause temporary fertility problems in women who can become pregnant. This effect is reversed after stopping treatment.

## Driving and using machines

Povofortay is not expected to affect your ability to drive or use machines.

## Povofortay contains sodium

This medicine contains less than 1 mmol sodium (23 mg) per tablet, that is to say essentially 'sodiumfree'.

## 3. How to take Povofortay

Always take this medicine exactly as your doctor or pharmacist has told you. Check with your doctor or pharmacist if you are not sure.

## Recommended dose

The recommended dose of Povofortay is either 45 mg or 75 mg once daily. Your doctor will decide which dose is right for you, based on your condition and your medical history. Based on your response, further dose adjustments may be recommended by your doctor.

Swallow the tablet whole either with or without food. Do not crush, cut or chew the tablet.

Your doctor will decide for how long you need to take Povofortay.

If your condition has not improved after 12 weeks of treatment, talk to your doctor.

## If you take more Povofortay than you should

Talk to your doctor as soon as possible.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## If you forget to take Povofortay

- Take a missed dose as soon as you remember, if your next dose is due in 12 hours or more.
- If your next dose is due in less than 12 hours, skip the missed dose and take your next dose as usual when it is due.
- Do not take a double dose to make up for a forgotten tablet.

## If you stop taking Povofortay

Do not stop taking Povofortay without talking to your doctor first. If you stop treatment, symptoms of hidradenitis suppurativa may come back.

If you have any further questions on the use of this medicine, ask your doctor or pharmacist.

## 4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, this medicine can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them.

Talk to your doctor if you notice any of the following side effects:

Very common (may affect more than 1 in 10 people)

- upper respiratory tract (nose and throat) infections
- acne

Common (may affect up to 1 in 10 people)

- inflammation of hair follicles (folliculitis)
- urinary tract infection
- high blood fat (cholesterol) shown by blood test
- headache
- increased blood level of creatine phosphokinase (CPK) (an enzyme released into the blood when muscle is damaged)

Uncommon (may affect up to 1 in 100 people)

- shingles (herpes zoster; a painful, blistering rash in one part of the body)
- low white blood cell counts shown in blood tests
- low platelet count shown in blood test
- increased liver enzymes shown in blood tests (ALT and AST increased)

## Reporting of side effects

If you get any side effects, talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse. This includes any possible side effects not listed in this leaflet. You can also report side effects directly via the national reporting system listed in Appendix V. By reporting side effects you can help provide more information on the safety of this medicine.

## 5. How to store Povofortay

Keep this medicine out of the sight and reach of children.

Do not use this medicine after the expiry date which is stated on the blister and carton after \"EXP\". The expiry date refers to the last day of that month.

This medicine does not require any special storage conditions.

Do not throw away any medicines via wastewater or household waste. Ask your pharmacist how to throw away medicines you no longer use. These measures will help protect the environment.
