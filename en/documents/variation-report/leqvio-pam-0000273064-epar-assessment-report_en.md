## Description

Inclisiran was first approved in the EU on 09 December 2020 under the proprietary name of Leqvio. The currently approved EU indication is:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Leqvio is indicated in adults with primary hypercholesterolemia (heterozygous familial and nonfamilial) or mixed dyslipidemia, as an adjunct to diet:

- in combination with a statin or statin with other lipid-lowering therapies in patients unable to reach LDL-C goals with the maximum tolerated dose of a statin, or
- alone or in combination with other lipid-lowering therapies in patients who are statin-intolerant, or for whom a statin is contraindicated.

Study Orion 13 evaluated the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of inclisiran in adolescents from 12 to less than 18 years of age with homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) and elevated lowdensity lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C). It is part of the inclisiran paediatric program as agreed with the European Medicines Agency (EMA) in the paediatric investigation plan (PIP; EMEA-002214-PIP01-17M02, P/0322/2023) and intended to support the registration of inclisiran in adolescents with HoFH.

The study was conducted in two-parts, a double-blind inclisiran versus placebo part (Year 1), followed by an open-label inclisiran part (Year 2), as a randomised, multicentre study in adolescents with HoFH and LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L) on stable, individualised, optimal standard of care (SoC) background lipid-lowering therapy including maximally tolerated statin treatment, at the investigator's discretion.

To explore the pharmacokinetics (PK) of inclisiran in adolescents with HoFH, the study also included PK and pharmacogenetic assessments. PK assessment was based on sparse PK sampling (i.e. 3 samples at the Day 1 visit; pre-dose immediately before the first administration of study drug, 4-, and 24-hours after first dose). Optional pharmacogenetic analyses included analyses for potential mutations in the LDLR, ApoB, PCSK9, and LDLR adaptor protein 1 (LDLRAP1) genes.

The study design is summarised in the following table.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Table 1: Summary of study design

Twopart, randomised, multicentre, international Phase III study. The study had two sequential parts:

- Part 1/Year 1: 12 months double-blind, parallel group period, in which participants were randomised in a 2:1 ratio to receive either inclisiran sodium 300 mg s.c. or placebo. The primary endpoint was assessed at Day 330.
- Part 2 / Year 2: 12 months single arm, open-label follow-up period, with all participants receiving inclisiran sodium 300 mg s.c.

## Methods

## Study participants

The study population consisted of adolescents (male or female; ≥12 to &lt;18 years at screening) with a genetically confirmed diagnosis of HoFH (excluding LDLR null/null genotypes) and on a maximally tolerated dose of statin (at investigator's discretion) with or without other lipid lowering therapy (e.g., ezetimibe).

Key inclusion criteria were:

- HoFH diagnosed by genetic confirmation excluding LDLR null/null genotypes,
- fasting LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L) at screening,
- on maximally tolerated dose of statin (Investigator's discretion) with or without other lipidlowering therapy (e.g., ezetimibe),
- participants on lipid-lowering therapies (such as statin and/or e.g., ezetimibe) should have been on stable dose ≥30 days before screening with no planned medication or dose changes during study participation,
- participants on documented regimen of LDL-apheresis were allowed to continue apheresis during study if needed (schedule not allowed to change during double-blind period),
- estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) &gt;30 mL/min/1.73 m 2 at screening.

## Key exclusion criteria were:

- documented evidence of a null (negative) mutation in both LDLR alleles,
- previous treatment (≤90 days of screening) with monoclonal antibodies towards PCSK9,
- history of poor response to therapy with any monoclonal antibody directed towards PCSK9,
- treatment with mipomersen or lomitapide (≤5 months of screening),
- active liver disease or unexplained, confirmed ALT or AST elevation &gt;3 × ULN, or total bilirubin elevation &gt;2 × ULN (except Gilbert's syndrome) at screening,
- pregnant or nursing females,
- HeFH
- recent or planned use of other investigational medicinal products or devices,
- secondary hypercholesterolemia (e.g., hypothyroidism or nephrotic syndrome),
- major cardiovascular AEs ≤1 month prior to randomisation.

## Assessor's comment

The study included male and female adolescents from ≥12 to &lt;18 years of age with a genetic confirmation of HoFH, excluding LDLR null/null genotypes, and an LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL who were on a maximally tolerated dose of an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) with or without other lipid lowering therapy (excluding PCSK9 inhibitors, mipomersen, and lomitapide).

Patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) or secondary hypercholesterolemia were excluded.

For the primary efficacy assessment beta quantification LDL-C, i.e., LDL-C directly measured using ultracentrifugation, was used; if a beta quantification LDL-C was missing, calculated LDL-C could be used instead.

Participants were in a fasted state for all efficacy laboratory assessments. From randomisation (Day 1) until Day 360, the results were blinded to participants, investigator staff, persons performing the assessments, and the clinical trial team.

In addition, carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT) was measured locally at Day 1 (baseline), Day 330 (Year 1), and Day 720 (EoS) by ultrasonography. This assessment was exploratory / hypothesisgenerating in nature.

PK assessment was based on sparse PK sampling (i.e. 3 samples at the Day 1 visit; pre-dose immediately before the first administration of study drug, 4-, and 24-hours after first dose). Concentration-time data were summarised using descriptive statistics. Inclisiran plasma concentration data were listed by treatment, participant, and sampling time point. Descriptive summary statistics were provided by sampling time point, including the frequency (n, %) of concentrations below the LLOQ and reported as zero. The summary descriptive statistics included mean (arithmetic and geometric), standard deviation (SD), coefficient of variation (CV; arithmetic and geometric), median, minimum, and maximum. All participants were invited to optional pharmacogenetic analyses of the underlying causal mutation(s) of their HoFH. Samples were analysed for potential mutations in the LDLR, ApoB, PCSK9, and LDLRAP1 genes. The key safety assessments consisted of monitoring and recording of all adverse events (AEs), laboratory evaluations (haematology, biochemistry, urinalysis, pregnancy tests), anti-drug antibody (ADA) measurement, vital signs, growth (height, weight, body mass index [BMI]), and pubertal development (sex hormones, Tanner staging). Other safety evaluations were physical examinations, neurological evaluations, and electrocardiograms (ECGs).

## Assessor's comment

The primary endpoint was the percentage change in LDL-C from baseline to the end of the double-blind

treatment period at day 330 (year 1). Secondary endpoints included time-adjusted percent change in LDL-C from baseline after Day 90 and up to Day 330, absolute changes in LDL-C and other lipid parameters during Part 1, as well as assessments from baseline to the end of the open-label Part 2, i.e. day 720. The endpoints are considered to be in line with previous inclisiran studies in adults.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Part 1 of the study was double-blind and placebo-controlled. Participants, investigator staff, persons performing the assessments, and the clinical trial team were blinded to the identity of the treatment from the time of randomisation until at least database lock for all Part 1 (year 1) data. Exceptions were the independent statistician and programmer to prepare interim analysis reports for the DMC, and the PK analyst to avoid unnecessary analysis of placebo samples; in addition, a PK interim analysis (IA) team had access to unblinded individual-level PK/baseline data in order to update a PK extrapolation to paediatrics before the Part 1 database lock to support early planning of a dose selection strategy for paediatric studies in younger children. Part 2 of the study had a single-arm, open-label design.

After baseline visit (Day 1), participants were randomised by interactive response technology in a 2:1 ratio to receive double-blind s.c. injections of inclisiran sodium 300 mg or placebo in Part 1 (year 1) of the study (days 1, 90, 270). On day 360 (\"switch day\"), participants randomised to placebo in Part 1 received inclisiran and participants randomised to inclisiran in Part 1 received placebo. This dose remained blinded in order to maintain the blind for Part 1 of the study. Subsequently, all participants received open-label s.c. injections of inclisiran sodium 300 mg in Part 2 (year 2). Investigational and control drugs were supplied by the MAH as single-use pre-filled syringes for s.c. injection. Placebo was supplied as sterile normal saline (0.9% sodium chloride in water for injection) and administered as a 1.5 mL s.c. injection to match the dose in the inclisiran arm. The placebo prefilled syringes were blinded and were identical in appearance to their active counterparts.

## Statistical Methods

In cases where the dosing time at Day 360 did not allow to determine whether data belonged to Part 1 or 2, the data were considered to belong to Part 1. The following data sets were analysed. The Screened Set (SCR) included all participants who provided study informed consent. The Randomised Set (RAN) consisted of all participants who received a randomisation number, regardless of whether double-blind treatment was received or not. The Full Analysis Set (FAS) consisted of all randomised participants with the exception of those mis-randomised participants who did not receive study drug; mis-randomised participants were defined as those who were not qualified for randomization but were inadvertently randomised into the study. According to the intent to treat principle, participants were analysed according to the treatment they were assigned to during the randomisation procedure. The FAS was used in analyses for the primary, secondary, and exploratory efficacy objectives in Part 1 of the study. The Safety Set (SAF) included all participants who received at least 1 dose of study treatment. Participants were analysed according to the study treatment received. This was the primary population for the safety analyses in Part 1. The SAF allowed the inclusion of nonrandomised participants who received double-blind treatment in error. The PK analysis Set included all randomised participants who had at least 1 PK measurement. Participants were analysed according to the treatment they received. The Open-label Phase Analysis Set (OAS) included all participants who entered Part 2 of the study and received at least 1 dose of study treatment in Part 2. ## Assessor's comment

No new or unexpected safety signals have been reported, but interpretation of the provided data is limited by the very low number of participants included in study Orion 13 (13 participants overall, 9 inclisiran, 4 placebo) as well as by the open-label uncontrolled design of Part 2. No conclusions on the safety of inclisiran in adolescents can be drawn from these sparse data.

Study drug exposure was similar between groups in both periods of study ORION-13; over the entire study duration, all participants were administered all 6 scheduled study doses of study drug.

The sparse safety data of inclisiran in adolescents with HoFH gathered from both parts of the study are considered to be in line with the known general safety profile in adult patients but it has to be kept in mind that inclisiran has not been licensed in adults with HoFH. No participants died and no serious AEs and no AEs leading to study drug discontinuation were reported during either part of the study. Studydrug related AEs were injection site reactions, an identified ADR of inclisiran.

There were no clinically meaningful changes or between-group differences in safety laboratory parameters of haematology or biochemistry in either Part of the study and no participant experienced AEs of new onset or worsening of diabetes.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

No adverse effects regarding growth, pubertal development, or hormonal status were seen with inclisiran treatment in either period of the study.

Conclusions on immunogenicity are considered preliminary at present as relevant documentation has not been submitted. As mentioned in the method section, partial method validation report 3002412 (DMPK R2100159-ig-01) claimed to cover also the cross-validation of the ADA method used for Study Part 2 to the ADA method used for Study Part 1 could not be located and needs to be provided (RSI).

## 2.3.2. Discussion on clinical aspects

Inclisiran was first approved in the EU on 09 December 2020 under the proprietary name of Leqvio. Currently, inclisiran is only licensed in adults. Inclisiran is not licensed in patients with homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH) since in the phase III, two-part (double-blind placebo-controlled / open-label), multicentre study ORION-5 (MDCO-PCS-17-02) in adult patients with HoFH, inclisiran treatment failed to reduce LDL-C levels despite substantial lowering of PCSK9 levels (Raal F, Durst R, Bi R, Talloczy Z, Maheux P, Lesogor A, Kastelein JJP; ORION-5 Study Investigators. Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Inclisiran in Patients With Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia: Results From the ORION-5 Randomized Clinical Trial. Circulation. 2024 Jan 30;149(5):354-362. doi: 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.122.063460. Epub 2023 Oct 18. PMID: 37850379; PMCID: PMC10815002).

Study Orion-13 (CKJX839C12302) is part of post-authorisation measures for inclisiran and investigated its use in adolescents from ≥12 to &lt;18 years of age with HoFH and elevated LDL-cholesterol.

The randomised, multicentre Phase III study was conducted in two-parts; Part 1 (year 1) investigated inclisiran versus placebo in a double-blind setting, while in Part 2 (year2) all participants received open-label inclisiran. The study included male and female adolescents ≥12 to &lt;18 years of age with a genetic confirmation of HoFH, excluding LDLR null/null genotypes, and an LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL who were on a maximally tolerated dose of an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) with or without other lipid lowering therapy (excluding PCSK9 inhibitors, mipomersen, and lomitapide). Patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) or secondary hypercholesterolemia were excluded. During Part 1, participants received either inclisiran at the licensed standard dose and posology for adults or placebo. During Part 2, all participants received standard dose inclisiran.

The study included an evaluation of inclisiran pharmacokinetics (PK) based on sparse data and of changes in LDL-C by type of underlying causal mutation as exploratory endpoints.

ORION-13 included 13 participants, 9 in the inclisiran and 4 in the placebo group; all participants completed both parts of the study and were analysed. Approximately two thirds of the randomised participants were female (9/13; 69.2%) and the mean age was 14.8 years (range 12 to 17 years). One (1) participant in the placebo group was on LDL-apheresis. All participants had HoFH diagnosed genetically and there were no important differences in genotypes between groups.

For study ORION-13 no formal hypothesis testing was planned and none was performed. The mean percent change in LDL-C from baseline to Day 330 was -21.6% in the inclisiran and +11.7% in the placebo group (mean between-group difference [95% CI] -33.25% [-59.17, -7.34]). A supplementary analysis excluding the participant (n=1) on LDL-apheresis showed consistent results. A waterfall plot of individual percentage changes in LDL-C from baseline to Day 330 by participant showed a variable magnitude; all participants in the inclisiran group showed a reduction in LDL-C albeit with significant differences in magnitude, while response in the placebo group was less consistent with an increase in LDL-C of 54.9% as well as a decrease of -15.4%. Two thirds (66.7%) of the participants in the inclisiran group achieved a &gt;15% and more than half (55.6%) a ≥20% reduction in LDL-C from baseline to Day 330. In line with the primary endpoint, the mean time-adjusted percent change in LDL-C (after Day 90 up to Day 330) was -21.0% in the inclisiran and 13.0% in the placebo group (between-group difference [95% CI] -33.97% [-67.54, -0.39]); the supplementary analysis excluding the participant on apheresis showed consistent results. The percentage changes from baseline in LDL-C in Part 1were largely consistent across visits, but there were outliers. Reductions were seen for absolute change in LDL-C and percent change in ApoB, non-HDL-C, VLDL-C, and total cholesterol. No reduction was seen in Lp(a) and only small variable effects for triglycerides, HDL-C, and Apo A1. In Part 2, the extent of the overall mean change in LDL-C was lower compared to Part 1 (difference driven by 2 participants in inclisiran-inclisiran group with LDL-C of +27.3% and +47.4%, respectively). A waterfall plot of percentage change in LDL-C from baseline to Day 720 showed a variable magnitude; 10/13 participants had a decrease in LDL-C at Day 720, while in 3/13 participants, LDL-C increased at Day 720. As regards exploratory results, only 1 participant in the inclisiran group reached the pre-specified LDLC threshold of &lt;130 mg/dL and none an LDL-C &lt;100 mg/dL. In Part 2, 2 participants reached the prespecified LDL-C threshold of &lt;130 mg/dL, 1 participant an LDL-C &lt;100 mg/dL, and no participant an LDL-C below 70 mg/dL. Consistent LDL-C lowering was seen across the included genetic variants. No clinically relevant effect of inclisiran on carotid intima media thickness (cIMT) was seen. Since only 1 participant was treated with LDL-apheresis, no relevant analysis is feasible. The study included an evaluation of inclisiran pharmacokinetics (PK) based on sparse data. No final conclusions regarding plasma concentrations / PK are possible at present stage. This pertains to the fact that relevant documentation such as reports on method validation, cross validation as well as population PK were not submitted so far and are requested (see below). As regards safety, no new or unexpected signals have been reported, but interpretation of the provided data is limited by the very low number of participants included in study Orion 13 (13 participants overall, 9 inclisiran, 4 placebo) as well as by the open-label uncontrolled design of Part 2. No conclusions on the safety of inclisiran in adolescents can be drawn from these sparse data. Study drug exposure was similar between groups in both periods of study ORION-13; over the entire study duration, all participants were administered all 6 scheduled study doses of study drug. The sparse safety data of inclisiran in adolescents with HoFH gathered from both parts of the study are considered to be in line with the known general safety profile in adult patients but it has to be kept in mind that inclisiran has not been licensed in adults with HoFH. No participants died and no serious AEs and no AEs leading to study drug discontinuation were reported during either part of the study. Study-drug related AEs were injection site reactions, an identified ADR of inclisiran. There were no clinically meaningful changes or between-group differences in safety laboratory parameters of haematology or biochemistry in either Part of the study and no participant experienced AEs of new onset or worsening of diabetes. No adverse effects regarding growth, pubertal development, or hormonal status were seen with inclisiran treatment in either period of the study. Conclusions on immunogenicity (ADA measurements) are considered preliminary at present. As mentioned in the method section, partial method validation report 3002412 (DMPK R2100159-ig-01) claimed to cover also the cross-validation of the ADA method used for Study Part 2 to the ADA method used for Study Part 1 could not be located and needs to be provided (see below). In summary, although effects of inclisiran on reduction of LDL-C in adolescents with confirmed HoFH have been seen in study Orion 13 and no new or unexpected signals have been reported, no definite conclusions can be derived from the data presented.

According to the run summary provided in Table 5-1 of the bioanalytical report, in run 2, QCs for analyte A-120190 and for analyte A-122088 calibration standards did not meet acceptance criteria and the run was reinjected in run 3 for quantification of both analytes. According to ICH M10 (sections 3.3.2 Acceptance criteria for an analytical run and 3.3.5. The MAH submitted a completed paediatric study CKJX839C12302 for Leqvio, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.
