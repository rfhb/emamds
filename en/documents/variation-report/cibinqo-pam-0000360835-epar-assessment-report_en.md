## 1. Introduction

On 6 July 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Cibinqo, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

The MAH does not propose an update of the product information.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

Cibinqo which contains the JAK 1-inhibitor abrocitinib as active substance, was approved in EU on 09 December 2021 for the treatment of moderate-to-severe AD in adults who are candidates for systemic therapy. A type II Variation to expand the indication to include adolescents with moderate-to-severe AD who are candidates for systemic therapy was submitted to EMA in May 2023. The MAH received a positive CHMP Opinion on February 22, 2024, and a EC Decision on March 21, 2024.

According to the line listing of all the studies included in the development program submitted by the MAH, Study B7451129 \"Profile of US Adult and Adolescent Patients Treated With Cibinqo for Atopic Dermatitis: A Retrospective Observational Database Study\" was not part of the development program.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

The abrocitinib formulation currently marketed in the US is tablet formulation: 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg film-coated tablet. This is also the authorised formulation for treatment of AD inthe EU.

## Clinical study

## Study B7451129 - Profile of US Adult and Adolescent Patients Treated With Cibinqo for Atopic Dermatitis: A Retrospective Observational Database Study

## Description

Study B7451129 was a non-interventional, retrospective cohort study of adolescent and adult patients with AD treated with abrocitinib, any non-abrocitinib biologic in the US, utilising de-identified data from the Komodo Health claims database from January 1, 2021 to July 31, 2025. The study characterised patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib or comparison biologics in a real-world clinical setting and was primarily descriptive and exploratory in nature, without a pre-specified hypothesis (see Figure 1).

## Methods

## Study participants

The study population included adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (ages ≥18) patients with AD treated with abrocitinib, dupilumab, or other non-abrocitinib biologics in the US, utilising de-identified data from the Komodo Health claims database.

## Treatments

Three treatment cohorts were defined in this study for adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (ages ≥18) patients based on utilisation of abrocitinib, dupilumab, or other non-abrocitinib biologics on or after February 10, 2023. The other non-abrocitinib biologics included dupilumab, tralokinumab, lebrikizumab, or nemolizumab, which are indicated for moderate-to-severe AD. The date of the first fill was defined as the index date. Patients were excluded if they had ≥1 fill for the index treatment in the 12 months prior to index date.

## Objectives

The primary objectives of the study were to:

1. Describe the demographics and baseline clinical characteristics among adolescent (ages 12&lt;18 years) and adult (age ≥18 years) patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib.
2. Describe the place (1L, 2L, etc.) of abrocitinib in adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (age ≥18) AD patients' treatment journey.
3. Assess the early persistence (proportion of abrocitinib initiators who refill treatment within 60 days of first fill) of adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (age ≥18) AD patients treated with abrocitinib.

Secondary objectives included describing demographics and baseline clinical characteristics among adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (age ≥18) AD patients who initiate abrocitinib by subgroups: place of therapy (eg, abrocitinib in 1L, 2L, etc.), race/ethnicity, sex, provider type (Dermatologist, Allergist), and previous exposure to biologics vs. no previous exposure to biologics.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Outcomes/endpoints

This study was descriptive in nature, and no outcome data were collected or assessed.

## Sample size

As this study is descriptive without a pre-specified hypothesis, a sample size calculation was not applicable.

## Statistical Methods

This study conducted descriptive analyses, providing an overview of the data and summarising key characteristics of patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib, dupilumab, or comparison biologics in a real-world clinical setting. The analysis was conducted separately for adolescent and adult populations.

## Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

Among the 379 patients with AD treated with abrocitinib in the adolescent cohort, the mean (SD) age was 15.0 (1.6) years old. The adolescent cohort was predominately female (55.1%), of unknown (34.3%) or White (28.2%) race, and 16.1% were Hispanic or Latino. Geographically, most patients resided in the South (51.7%), followed by the Northeast (22.2%). Majority of the adolescent cohort (59.4%) were covered by commercial insurance. The median available baseline was approximately 7.9 years (2,900 days), and the median available follow-up time was about 13.3 months (404 days) from the index date.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Among the 3,673 patients with AD treated with abrocitinib in the adult cohort, the mean (SD) age was 43.2 (18.5) years old. The adult cohort was also predominately female (54.6%), of White (37.3%) or unknown (28.4%) race, and 12.7% were Black or African American. Geographically, most patients resided in the South (39.0%), followed by the Northeast (24.9%). The majority of the adult cohort (67.6%) were covered by commercial insurance. The median available baseline was approximately 8.1 years (2,949 days), and the median available follow-up time was about 13.8 months (485 days) from the index date.

## Baseline AD Treatment Characteristics

Among the 379 adolescent patients with AD treated with abrocitinib, 104 patients (27.4%) received abrocitinib as 1L advanced AD treatment, 216 patients (57.0%) received abrocitinib as 2L advanced AD treatment, and 59 patients (15.6%) received abrocitinib as 3L+ of advanced AD treatment. During the baseline period, 95.8% of 2L patients and all 3L+ patients received systemic biologics, primarily dupilumab. Oral systemic corticosteroid use before abrocitinib initiation was also common across lines of therapy, reported in 66.3% of 1L patients, 69.4% of 2L patients, and 61.0% of 3L+ patients.

Among the 3,673 adult patients with AD treated with abrocitinib, 1,214 patients (33.1%) received abrocitinib as 1L advanced AD treatment, 1,763 patients (48.0%) received abrocitinib as 2L advanced AD treatment, and 696 patients (18.9%) received abrocitinib as 3L+ advanced AD treatment. During the baseline period, 92.3% of 2L patients and all 3L+ patients received systemic biologics, primarily dupilumab. Oral systemic corticosteroid use before abrocitinib initiation was reported in 77.3% of 1L adult patients, 82.0% of 2L patients, and 88.5% of 3L+ patients.

## Treatment Characteristics and Early Persistence

The median starting dose of abrocitinib was 100 mg in both adolescents and adults. Most patients initiated abrocitinib at 100 mg, including 350 adolescents (92.3%) and 3,183 adults (86.7%). Smaller proportions initiated abrocitinib at 50 mg or 200 mg. Abrocitinib was most frequently prescribed by dermatologists and PA/NP. In adolescents, abrocitinib was prescribed by dermatologists in 46.4% of patients and by PA/NP in 38.8% of patients.

Among patients with at least 60 days of follow-up, early persistence, defined as a second fill of abrocitinib within 60 days, was observed in 243 adolescents (75.94%) and 2,388 adults (73.66%). The median time between the index date and second fill of abrocitinib was 28.0 days in both adolescents and adults. By line of therapy, early persistence among adolescents was 72.7% in 1L patients, 79.7% in 2L patients, and 78.2% in 3L+ patients. In adults, early persistence ranged from 68.0% in 1L patients to 78.8% in 3L+ patients.

## Safety results

Safety was not assessed in this study.

## The MAH´s conclusions

This was a non-interventional, retrospective cohort study of adolescent and adult patients with AD treated with abrocitinib, dupilumab, or any non-abrocitinib biologics in the US utilising de-identified data from the Komodo Health claims database.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

A total of 4,052 patients met the selection criteria; 379 (9.0%) adolescents aged 12- &lt;18 years and 3,673 (91.0%) adults aged 18+. Median age at treatment initiation was 15 years old for adolescents and 41 years old for adults. The proportion of female patients was 55.1% in adolescents and 54.6% in adults. The most common prescriber specialty of abrocitinib overall was dermatology (46.4% adolescents; 54.5% adults), followed by PA/NP (38.8% adolescents; 32.5% adults) and allergists (8.4% adolescents; 5.5% adults). Among patients with at least 30 days of follow-up, 44.5% of adolescents and 37.2% of adults had a co-prescription with topicals within 30 days of abrocitinib initiation. Abrocitinib was most used as 2L advanced AD treatment in both adolescents and adults; however, 27.4% of adolescents and 33.1% of adults received abrocitinib as their 1L advanced AD treatment. Among abrocitinib initiators with at least 60 days of follow-up, early persistence was 77.6% in adolescents and 73.5% in adults.

The investigation into the real-world treatment positioning of abrocitinib in the treatment journey of these patients revealed abrocitinib as the first advanced systemic treatment in around one-quarter of adolescents and one-third of adults. Given the recent approval of abrocitinib, and established treatment with other advanced systemic treatments, namely dupilumab, this result may indicate a shift in the management of patients with moderate-to-severe AD. The majority of abrocitinib patients had previous treatment with oral systemic corticosteroids, aligning with treatment recommendations for moderate-to-severe AD. Among patients with at least 60 days of follow-up after abrocitinib initiation, about three-fourths of patients had a second fill of abrocitinib. These early persistence data in both groups suggest that most patients who initiate abrocitinib refill their prescription on schedule. The interpretation of patients without a second fill of abrocitinib may be limited due to the short follow-up time post-abrocitinib approval, as an additional fill may have occurred at a timepoint later in their disease journey.

Adolescent initiators of abrocitinib had similar profiles to adult initiators. Early persistence in both groups suggests that the majority of patients who initiate abrocitinib refill their prescription on schedule. Future studies should further evaluate abrocitinib utilisation and outcomes in adolescent and adult patients with a longer post-approval follow-up.

## The MAH´s benefit/risks

This study was observational and descriptive in design. PK and PD were not examined, safety was not examined, and no outcome data were collected or assessed. Therefore, this study does not provide new efficacy or safety conclusions for abrocitinib in adolescent or adult patients with AD.

The therapeutic context for adolescent patients with AD includes a need for effective systemic treatment options for patients whose disease is not adequately controlled with topical or other systemic therapies. In this study, abrocitinib was used in US adolescent patients with AD in a realworld setting, including as 1L, 2L, and 3L+ advanced AD treatment. Early persistence data suggest that most adolescent patients who initiated abrocitinib refilled their prescription on schedule.

The interpretation of these findings is limited by the descriptive design, use of administrative claims data, lack of disease severity information, and absence of direct efficacy or safety assessment. However, no new risks were identified from this study, and the findings do not alter the established benefit/risk profile of abrocitinib for the paediatric population in the approved indication. No changes to the EU marketing authorisation, including the currently approved EU SmPC, are warranted.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

The MAH submitted the results for Study B7451129 in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Study B7451129 was a non-interventional, retrospective cohort study of adolescent and adult patients with AD treated with abrocitinib, dupilumab, or any non-abrocitinib biologics in the US utilising deidentified data from the Komodo Health claims database from January 1, 2021 to July 31, 2025. The primary objectives of the study were to describe and characterise patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib or comparison biologics in a real-world clinical setting, without a pre-specified hypothesis.

In the study results, key characteristics of patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib, dupilumab, or comparison biologics in a real-world clinical setting was reported. As already stated by the MAH, data interpretation from this study is constrained by the descriptive design, use of administrative claims data, lack of disease severity information, and absence of direct efficacy or safety assessment. In addition, safety was not examined in this study.

The benefit-risk of abrocitinib remains unchanged and no update to the product information has been proposed based on these data, which is endorsed by the CHMP.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

The final results of the non-interventional retrospective cohort Study B7451129, characterising patients initiating abrocitinib, dupilumab, and other biologics for the treatment of moderate-to-severe AD, have been reported. The benefit-risk profile of abrocitinib is not affected by this study. No update of the product information is warranted.
