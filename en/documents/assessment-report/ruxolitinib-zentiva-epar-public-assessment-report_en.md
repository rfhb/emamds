## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Introduction

This marketing authorisation application is submitted as a generic application under Article 10(1) of Directive 2001/83/EC for Ruxolitinib 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg, and 20 mg tablets.

The reference medicinal product is Jakavi 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets (Novartis Europharm Limited), authorised via the centralised procedure on 23 August 2012.

The active substance in the generic product is ruxolitinib hemifumarate, while the reference product Jakavi contains ruxolitinib phosphate. In accordance with Directive 2001/83/EC and its amendments, \"different salts, esters, ethers, isomers, mixtures of isomers, complexes or derivatives of an active substance shall be considered to be the same active substance.\"

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Ruxolitinib is a selective inhibitor of the Janus Associated Kinases (JAKs) JAK1 and JAK2 (IC50 values of 3.3 nM and 2.8 nM for JAK1 and JAK2 enzymes, respectively). These mediate the signalling of a number of cytokines and growth factors that are important for haematopoiesis and immune function.

Myelofibrosis and polycythaemia vera are myeloproliferative neoplasms known to be associated with dysregulated JAK1 and JAK2 signalling. The basis for the dysregulation is believed to include high levels of circulating cytokines that activate the JAK-STAT pathway, gain-of-function mutations such as JAK2V617F, and silencing of negative regulatory mechanisms. MF patients exhibit dysregulated JAK signalling regardless of JAK2V617F mutation status. Activating mutations in JAK2 (V617F or exon 12) are found in &gt;95% of PV patients.

Ruxolitinib inhibits JAK-STAT signalling and cell proliferation of cytokine-dependent cellular models of haematological malignancies, as well as of Ba/F3 cells rendered cytokine-independent by expressing the JAK2V617F mutated protein, with IC50 ranging from 80 to 320 nM.

JAK-STAT signalling pathways play a role in regulating the development, proliferation, and activation of several immune cell types important for GvHD pathogenesis.

In line with the reference product Jakavi 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets, the targeted indication for Ruxolitinib Zentiva 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets are:

## Myelofibrosis (MF)

Ruxolitinib Zentiva is indicated for the treatment of disease-related splenomegaly or symptoms in adult patients with primary myelofibrosis (also known as chronic idiopathic myelofibrosis), post polycythaemia vera myelofibrosis or post essential thrombocythaemia myelofibrosis.

## Polycythaemia vera (PV)

Ruxolitinib Zentiva is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with polycythaemia vera who are resistant to or intolerant of hydroxyurea.

## Graft versus host disease (GvHD)

## Acute GvHD

Ruxolitinib Zentiva is indicated for the treatment of adults and paediatric patients aged 28 days and older with acute graft versus host disease who have inadequate response to corticosteroids or other systemic therapies.

## Chronic GvHD

Ruxolitinib Zentiva is indicated for the treatment of adults and paediatric patients aged 6 months and older with chronic graft versus host disease who have inadequate response to corticosteroids or other systemic therapies.

Ruxolitinib exhibits stereoisomerism due to the presence of one chiral centre which is introduced selectively during the manufacturing process. The undesired (S)-isomer is controlled in the active substance specification by a chiral HPLC method.

## 2.2.2.2. Manufacture, characterisation and process controls

Evidence of GMP compliance has been provided in the QP declaration.

Detailed information on the manufacturing of the active substance has been provided in the restricted part of the ASMF and it was considered satisfactory.

The active substance is synthesized convergently from well-defined starting materials with acceptable specifications. The chiral centre is introduced racemically and the desired enantiomer is enriched via a resolution process.

Adequate in-process controls (IPCs) are applied during the synthesis. The specifications and control methods for intermediate products, starting materials and reagents have been presented.

The characterisation of the active substance and its impurities are in accordance with the EU guideline on chemistry of new active substances. Potential and actual impurities were well discussed with regards to their origin and characterised. However, the initial risk assessment for mutagenic impurities was inadequate as only the results for a subset of compounds were provided, resulting in a major objection. In response, the applicant provided a comprehensive risk assessment including hazard assessment and classified impurities according to ICH M7. It was demonstrated that impurities are either non-mutagenic or were not detected at relevant levels during development. The control strategy for mutagenic impurities was considered acceptable, and the major objection was resolved.

Solvents are controlled according to ICH Q3C. A risk assessment for elemental impurities was conducted according to ICH Q3D indicating that no specific controls are needed.

The active substance is packaged in a triple-layer system consisting of an inner low density polyethylene (LDPE) bag (twisted and tied), a middle high molecular weight high-density polyethylene (HMW/HDPE) bag and an outer QLUB bag (both nitrogen-purged and heat-sealed), placed within an HDPE container. The primary packaging material complies with EC 10/2011 as amended.

## 2.2.2.3. Specification

The active substance specification includes tests for description, solubility identification, polymorphic form, loss on drying, fumaric acid content, assay, related substances, residual solvents, and chiral purity.

The test parameters included in the specification are in line with ICH Q6A. Impurities limits are set according to ICH Q3A.

Batch analysis data from 3 production scale batches of the active substance were provided. The results were within the specifications and consistent from batch to batch.

## 2.2.2.4. Stability

Stability data from three commercial scale batches of ruxolitinib hemifumarate stored in the intended commercial package, for up to 24 months under long-term conditions (25±2 °C/60% RH) and for up to 6 months under accelerated conditions (40±2 °C/75% RH) according to the ICH guidelines were provided. Samples were tested for identity, appearance, loss on drying, related substances, assay, polymorphic form, and chiral purity. The analytical methods used were the same as for release and are stability indicating, as demonstrated by forced degradation studies.

All results remained within specifications at all testing time-points under both conditions. No significant changes or trends in the results for any of the parameters tested were observed.

Based on the current stability data, the proposed re-test period of 24 months for the active substance in the proposed container stored below 25 °C is considered acceptable.

## 2.2.3. Finished medicinal product

## 2.2.3.1. Description of the product and Pharmaceutical development

The finished product is presented as tablets containing ruxolitinib described as follows:

- 5 mg tablets: white to off-white coloured, round tablets with '5' debossed on one side and plain on the other side, with a diameter of approximately 7.5 mm.
- 10 mg tablets: white to off-white coloured, round tablets with '10' debossed on one side and plain on the other side, with a diameter of approximately 9.3 mm.
- 15 mg tablets: white to off-white coloured, oblong tablets with '15' debossed on one side and plain on the other side, with a width of approximately 7 mm and a length of approximately 15.2 mm.
- 20 mg tablets: white to off-white coloured, oblong tablets with '20' debossed on one side and plain on the other side, with a width of approximately 7.4 mm and a length of approximately 16.5 mm.

The finished product has been developed as a generic equivalent to the reference medicinal product, Jakavi® 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets which contain ruxolitinib phosphate as the active substance. The test product Ruxolitinib 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets contains Ruxolitinib hemifumarate as the active substance. The excipients used in the test product are qualitatively the same as those in the reference product. The finished product is indicated in the paediatric population. Adequate information regarding suitability for the paediatric population is included in the product information. All excipients are well known pharmaceutical ingredients and their quality is compliant with Ph. Eur. standards. There are no novel excipients used in the finished product formulation. The list of excipients is included in section 6.1 of the SmPC. Compatibility of the active substance with all excipients was demonstrated in studies on binary mixtures.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The quality target product profile (QTPP) was defined based on the physicochemical properties of the active substance, characterisation and labelling of the reference product, and the intended patient population. Formulation development followed a structured, knowledge-based DoE approach to achieve essential similarity to the reference product supported by comparative characterisation data including comparison of impurity profiles.

Although the solubility of ruxolitinib decreases with increasing pH, the active substance is highly soluble across the entire physiological pH range (pH 1.2 to 6.8) and it is considered a BCS Class 1 compound. It was demonstrated via stability studies that the manufacturing process and storage have no impact on chiral purity or polymorphic form.

The development of the dissolution test method including justification for the selected medium, apparatus and rotation speed were adequately discussed. The discriminatory power of the dissolution method was investigated on one batch manufactured with changes to excipient levels. While the changes made are seen as extensive, considering that the active substance is BCS class I with very high solubility across the physiological pH range, this was considered sufficient.

Bioequivalence with the reference product was demonstrated for the finished product in a clinical study under fasted conditions using the 20 mg tablet. Comparison of dissolution profiles between the test and reference bio-batches was conducted in media of pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8. Similar dissolution profiles were observed at pH 1.2 but Ruxolitinib Zentiva dissolves more rapidly in the other media. This is attributed to the higher solubility of ruxolitinib hemifumarate compared to ruxolitinib phosphate present in the reference product. Considering the in vivo demonstration of bioequivalence, the differences in in vitro dissolution rate are not considered an issue.

A biowaiver of strengths was requested for the 5, 10 and 15 mg tablets. All strengths are manufactured by the same manufacturer using the same manufacturing process, the qualitative composition is identical across strengths, and the proposed formulations of the 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg and 20 mg tablets are dose-proportional and manufactured from the same common blend. Dissolution profiles for one batch of each lower strength were compared with the 20 mg biobatch in media of pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8. In all cases, more than 85% dissolution was observed within 15 mins. As such, the biowaiver is considered acceptable.

Development of the manufacturing method was described. Wet granulation was selected and process parameters for each unit operation were optimised based on risk assessment and experiments.

The primary packaging is polyvinylchloride (PVC)/polychlorotrifluoroethylene (PCTFE)/PVC/Aluminium blisters or perforated unit dose blisters. The materials comply with Ph. Eur. and EC requirements. The choice of the container closure system has been validated by stability data and is adequate for the intended use of the product.

## 2.2.3.2. Manufacture of the product and process controls

For all sites involved in the manufacture, control and batch release of the finished product sufficient evidence of GMP compliance has been provided.

Major steps of the manufacturing process have been validated on 3 production scale batches of each strength. ## 2.4.2. Clinical pharmacology

## 2.4.2.1. Pharmacokinetics

Study BE/24/359: An open-label, balanced, randomized, two treatment, two sequence, two period, two way cross-over, single dose bioequivalence study of Ruxolitinib Hemifumarate Tablets 20 mg of Pharmacare Premium Ltd., Malta and JAKAVI ® (Ruxolitinib Tablets 20 mg of Novartis Europharma Limited, Vista Building, Elm Park, Merrion Road Dublin 4, Ireland in normal, healthy, adult, human subjects under fasting condition.

## Methods

## · Study design

Eligible subjects, confirmed through pre-study examinations conducted within 21 days prior to Period I, were admitted to the clinical pharmacology unit, provided with a standardized dinner, and fasted for at least 10 hours before dosing.

The study was designed as an open-label, randomized, two-period, two-sequence crossover study, with the randomization schedule generated using SAS software.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Subjects received a single oral dose of either the test or reference product with 240 mL of water under fasting conditions, in an upright position.

Samples were processed by centrifugation under controlled conditions, aliquoted in duplicate, stored at -20°C, and shipped in two consignments to the bioanalytical facility. Plasma ruxolitinib concentrations were determined using a validated bioanalytical method in accordance with applicable regulatory guidance and laboratory SOPs.

## · Test and reference products

Ruxolitinib hemifumarate 20 mg tablet manufactured by Pharmacare Premium Ltd, Malta (batch number 4SE01A, manufacturing date December 2024, expiry date June 2025) has been compared to JAKAVI 20 mg tablet manufactured by Novartis Europharm GmbH (batch SLRJ3, expiry date May 2027).

## · Population(s) studied

A total of 40 healthy adult subjects (36 males and 4 females), with two additional standby subjects, were enrolled in the study. Of these, 39 subjects completed the study, while one subject withdrew prior to Period II for personal reasons. Plasma samples from 40 subjects were analysed for pharmacokinetic evaluation, with 39 subjects included in the statistical analysis.

## · Analytical methods

Plasma ruxolitinib concentrations were determined using a validated LC-MS/MS method with solidphase extraction and HPLC separation, employing ruxolitinib-d9 as internal standard and K3EDTA as anticoagulants. The method, performed under light-protected conditions, covered a range of 1.07-1,498.91 ng/mL, used MRM mode, and calibration was by weighted (1/x²) linear regression.

## · Pharmacokinetic variables

The maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and the time to reach Cmax (Tmax) were determined from the observed plasma concentration-time profiles. The area under the concentration-time curve from time zero to the last quantifiable concentration (AUC0-t) was calculated using the linear trapezoidal method. The primary pharmacokinetic variables for the assessment of bioequivalence were Cmax and AUC0-t for ruxolitinib.

## · Statistical methods

Statistical analysis of pharmacokinetic parameters was performed using SAS Version 9.4.

Bioequivalence of ruxolitinib was assessed on the Ln-transformed primary PK parameters, Cmax and AUC0-t, using Schuirmann's two one-sided tests procedure with ANOVA at a 5% significance level. The ANOVA model included sequence, subjects nested within sequence, period, and treatment as fixed effects. Geometric least squares means, intra-subject CVs, and 90% confidence intervals for the ratio of test to reference were calculated. Outlier analysis using Lund's method found no outliers hence, all subjects were included. The test product was considered bioequivalent to the reference if the 90% confidence intervals for Cmax and AUC0-t fell within the predefined limits of 80.00-125.00%.

## 2.4.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

This application concerns a generic version of ruxolitinib tablets. The reference product, Jakavi, is indicated for the treatment of myelofibrosis, polycythaemia vera, and graft-versus-host disease.

From a clinical perspective, the application does not contain any new data on the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, efficacy or safety of the active substance. The applicant's literature-based clinical overview is considered adequate. Since the generic product uses a different salt form compared to the originator (hemifumarate rather than phosphate), the applicant has provided an adequate scientific justification demonstrating that the difference in salt form is not expected to have any significant impact on the safety or efficacy profile of the product.

To support bioequivalence with the reference product, the applicant has submitted one BE study with a randomised, single-dose, two-period, two-sequence, cross-over design in healthy adult subjects in fasting conditions, using the 20 mg strength. An additional study with proton pump inhibitors is not considered necessary as ruxolitinib phosphate (Jakavi) is a BCS Class I compound and the

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

hemifumarate salt in the test product demonstrates either the same or slightly higher solubility than ruxolitinib phosphate across the physiologically relevant pH range of 1.2 to 6.8. Therefore, the solubility and dissolution of the test product are not expected to be meaningfully affected by modest pH elevations due to co-administration with ARAs. This approach is in accordance with the EMA Question &amp; Answer on the need for bioequivalence studies with acid-reducing agents (EMA/497207/2024).

Concerns were raised following an FDA inspection outcome related to data manipulation in in vitro permeation tests conducted at a CRO. However, the applicant has adequately addressed these concerns by performing a study-specific assessment against the FDA findings, which did not identify any issues impacting the integrity of the study data or the bioequivalence conclusions. Additional information on subsequent regulatory inspections, including EMA and FDA, was also provided, with no evidence of data integrity deficiencies affecting the submitted study.

A total of 40 subjects were enrolled in the BE study, and 39 subjects completed the study. The study design, sampling scheme and analytical methods are in line with the relevant regulatory guidance, including ICH M13A, ICH M10 and the Guideline on the Investigation of Bioequivalence.

The pharmacokinetic parameters AUC0-t and Cmax were analysed using ANOVA. The 90% confidence intervals for the geometric mean ratios (test/reference) were fully contained within the conventional acceptance range of 80.00-125.00. Specifically, the geometric mean ratio was 100.89% (90% CI: 96.02-106.00) for AUC0-t and 99.96% (90% CI: 94.89-105.30) for Cmax. The intra-subject variability was low to moderate and did not necessitate scaled bioequivalence approaches. These results demonstrate bioequivalence between the test and reference products with respect to both the rate and extent of absorption.

No serious adverse events were reported during the conduct of the study. One mild adverse event (eosinophilia) was observed and considered unlikely related to the study medication, with full resolution upon follow-up.

A biowaiver has been requested for the lower strengths (5 mg, 10 mg, and 15 mg). The requirements for granting a biowaiver are considered fulfilled. Ruxolitinib exhibits linear pharmacokinetics over the therapeutic dose range, and appropriate in vitro dissolution data have been provided. Therefore, extrapolation of the bioequivalence results from the 20 mg strength to the lower strengths is scientifically justified in accordance with relevant guidelines.

Ruxolitinib Zentiva will be available as tablets, while the originator Jakavi is available as tablets and oral solution. Although, the indication proposed in the SmPC includes 'paediatric patients aged 28 days and older with acute GvHD' and 'paediatric patients aged 6 months and older with chronic GvHD', Ruxolitinib Zentiva tablets are not suitable for patients who cannot swallow tablets and children less than 6 years old. For these patients, other formulations (oral solution) are available under different brand name. This is reflected in SmPC section 4.2.

In conclusion, the available clinical data demonstrate bioequivalence between Ruxolitinib Zentiva 20 mg tablets and the reference product Jakavi 20 mg tablets, and support extrapolation to the lower strengths via a biowaiver. The overall clinical efficacy and safety profile is considered comparable to that of the reference product.
