## ANNEX I

## SUMMARY OF PRODUCT CHARACTERISTICS

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 1. NAME OF THE MEDICINAL PRODUCT

Colchicine AGEPHA Pharma 0.5 mg tablets

## 2. QUALITATIVE AND QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITION

Each tablet contains 0.5 mg of colchicine.

## Excipient(s) with known effect

Each tablet contains 88.87 mg lactose monohydrate. For the full list of excipients, see section 6.1.

## 3. PHARMACEUTICAL FORM

## Tablet.

White to slightly yellow, round, biconvex tablets, debossed with 'L1' on one side and plain on the other side (ca. 6.0 mm diameter, ca. 3.1 mm thickness).

## 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

Colchicine AGEPHA Pharma is indicated for secondary prevention of atherothrombotic events in adult patients with coronary disease stable for at least 6 months.

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration

## Posology

## Adults

The recommended dose is 0.5 mg of colchicine once daily. The maximum daily dose is 0.5 mg. The recommended  maximum  dose  must  not  be  exceeded.  The  initial  clinical  manifestations  of  such overdose include gastrointestinal disturbances such as diarrhoea, nausea, and vomiting (see section 4.4). If a dose of colchicine is missed, the missed dose should be taken as soon as possible, and the patient should then return to the normal dosing schedule. If a dose is skipped, the patient should not double the next dose.

## Duration of treatment

Colchicine can be safely used for a period of 28 months.

## Elderly

No dose adjustment is necessary. Clinical studies included elderly patients (≥ 65 years); however, very elderly patients (≥75 years) were under -represented. Available evidence does not indicate a clinically relevant age-related difference in safety or efficacy. As renal impairment, comorbid conditions and concomitant medicines are more common in elderly patients, colchicine should be used with caution in this population.

## Renal impairment

No dose adjustment is required in patients with mildly decreased (eGFR 60-89 mL/min) or normal renal function (eGFR ≥90 mL/min).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Patients  with  mildly  to  moderately  decreased  renal  function  with  eGFR ≥50 mL/min  should  be monitored closely for adverse reactions of colchicine. However, colchicine is contraindicated in patients with renal impairment (eGFR &lt;50 mL/min) (see section 4.3 ).

## Hepatic impairment

Patients  with  hepatic  impairment  should  be  monitored  closely  for  adverse  reactions  of  colchicine. Colchicine is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child Pugh C).

## Paediatric population

There is  no  relevant  use  of  colchicine  in  the  paediatric  population  for  the  indication  of  secondary prevention  of  atherothrombotic  events  in  adult  patients  with  coronary  disease  stable  for  at  least  6 months.

## Method of administration

## Oral use.

The tablet should be swallowed with a glass of water.

## 4.3 Contraindications

- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1.
- Coadministration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or P-glycoprotein inhibitors.
- Patients with pre-existing blood dyscrasias.
- Patients with renal impairment (eGFR &lt;50 mL/min).
- Patients with severe hepatic impairment.

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

## Important warning

Colchicine  has  a  narrow  therapeutic  index,  rendering  it  susceptible  to  significant  risks  of  severe overdose. The recommended maximum dose must not be exceeded. The initial clinical manifestations of such overdose include gastrointestinal disturbances such as diarrhoea, nausea, and vomiting. Depending on  the  dose  taken,  potential  multiple  organ  failure  may  ensue,  affecting  various  systems  including respiratory, cardiovascular, haematologic, and nervous system. Patients should be made aware of these signs of possible overdosage, which may also occur if interactions are ignored. In this case, a reduction in dose or discontinuation of treatment should be considered. Please refer to sections 4.4, 4.5, 4.8 and 4.9 of this SmPC.

The medicinal product must be kept out of reach of others before and after use.

## Cardiovascular

The use of colchicine in patients with New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class III or IV heart failure and a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of less than 35% is not supported by clinical efficacy and safety data. Therefore, caution is advised when considering colchicine treatment in this population. Healthcare professionals should carefully assess the potential benefits and risks on an individual basis. For information on other populations excluded from the LoDoCo2 study, refer to Section 5.1.

## Blood dyscrasias

Colchicine can cause myelosuppression, leukopenia, granulocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, and aplastic anaemia, which can be life-threatening or fatal (see sections 4.3 and 4.8). Gastrointestinal  symptoms  often  are  the  first  sign  of  colchicine  toxicity,  so  new  symptoms  should prompt an evaluation for toxicity. Concomitant use of medicinal products that reduce the metabolism of colchicine or the presence of hepatic or renal impairment increases the risk of developing blood dyscrasias.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Use of colchicine in patients with pre-existing blood dyscrasias, renal impairment (eGFR &lt;50 mL/min), or severe hepatic dysfunction, and concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or P-glycoprotein inhibitors is contraindicated (see section 4.3). Concomitant use of moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors with colchicine should be avoided in patients with risk factors for increased systemic exposure of colchicine (see  section  4.5).  Patients  with  any  degree  of  renal  impairment  and  hepatic  impairment  should  be monitored for colchicine toxicity.

## Neuromuscular toxicity

Colchicine  can  cause  neuromuscular  toxicity  and  rhabdomyolysis.  Concomitant  use  of  medicinal products  that  reduce  the  metabolism  of  colchicine  or  the  presence  of  hepatic  or  renal  impairment increases the risk of developing neuromuscular toxicity.

If a patient develops signs of neuromuscular toxicity, colchicine should be discontinued, other causes should  be  investigated  and  treated  appropriately.  Concomitant  use  of  colchicine  and  HMG  CoA reductase inhibitors, such as atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and other lipid-lowering medicinal products, gemfibrozil, fibrates and digoxin may potentiate the development of myopathy (see section 4.5).

Patients  with  any  degree  of  renal  impairment should be  monitored  closely  for  adverse  reactions  of colchicine. Colchicine is known to be excreted renally, and the presence of moderate to severe renal impairment  has  been  associated  with  colchicine  toxicity.  Urinary  clearance  of  colchicine  and  its metabolites may be decreased in patients with impaired renal function.

Patients with any degree of hepatic impairment should be monitored closely for adverse reactions of colchicine. Colchicine is metabolized primarily by the liver, and severe hepatic impairment has been associated with colchicine toxicity. Hepatic clearance may be significantly reduced, and plasma halflife prolonged in patients with chronic hepatic impairment.

## Women of childbearing potential / Contraception in males and females

Given the mechanism of action of colchicine (see section 5.3), women of childbearing potential should use a highly effective method of contraception while receiving colchicine and for up to one month after end of treatment. Male patients should not father a child during colchicine therapy, and up to 3 months after last dose of treatment.

Females  using  oral  contraceptives  should  be  prescribed  colchicine  with  caution  and  observed  for adverse reactions.

Based  on  non-clinical  reproductive  toxicity  findings  and  the  mechanism  of  action  of  colchicine, appropriate contraceptive measures are recommended during treatment (see section 4.6).

.

## Excipient (lactose)

Patients  with rare  hereditary  problems  of  galactose  intolerance, total  lactase  deficiency  or  glucosegalactose malabsorption should not take this medicine.

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

## Serious Drug Interactions

Co-administration of colchicine with strong P-gp inhibitors and/or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors will increase exposure to colchicine, which may lead to colchicine-induced toxicity, including fatalities. Such concomitant use is contraindicated (see section 4.3, and section 4.5, Table 1 ) .

Colchicine has not been studied for drug-drug interactions, however data and information available in the public domain related to the drug interaction potential of colchicine are presented.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Colchicine  is  a  substrate  of  the  efflux  transporter  P-glycoprotein  (P-gp).  Of  the  cytochrome  P450 enzymes  tested,  CYP3A4  was  mainly  involved  in  the  metabolism  of  colchicine.  If  colchicine  is administered with medicinal products that inhibit P-gp, most of which also inhibit CYP3A4, increased concentrations of colchicine are likely.

Physicians should ensure that patients are suitable candidates for treatment with colchicine and remain alert for signs and symptoms of toxicities related to increased colchicine exposure as a result of a drug interaction. Signs and symptoms of colchicine toxicity should be evaluated promptly and, if toxicity is suspected, consider interruption or discontinuation of colchicine.

## Interactions with food and drink

Patients are advised to avoid the use of grapefruit or grapefruit juice with colchicine, as it leads to increased exposure to colchicine.

## Oral contraceptives

Colchicine  can  interact  with  oral  contraceptives  like  norethindrone/ethinyl  estradiol  and  can  cause adverse reactions like diarrhoea, nausea, upper abdominal pain, cold sweat etc. Females using oral contraceptives should be prescribed colchicine with caution and observed for adverse reactions.

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation

## Women of childbearing potential / Contraception in males and females

Based on the mechanism of action of colchicine, which interferes with microtubule polymerisation and cell division, colchicine has the potential to adversely affect gametogenesis (see section 5.3).

Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment with colchicine and for at least one month after discontinuation of treatment.

Male patients should not father a child during treatment with colchicine and for at least three months after discontinuation of treatment.

## Pregnancy

Animal  studies  have  shown  that  colchicine  is  embryotoxic  and  teratogenic,  consistent  with  its pharmacological activity, including effects on chromosome segregation and cell division (see section 5.3).

Available human data from the literature, mainly derived from long-term use of colchicine in familial Mediterranean fever and other inflammatory conditions, have not identified an increased risk of major congenital  malformations,  miscarriage,  or  adverse  pregnancy  outcomes  when  colchicine  is  used  at therapeutic doses. However, data in pregnant women with ischaemic cardiovascular disease are limited. As  a  precautionary  measure,  colchicine should  not  be  used  during  pregnancy  unless  the  clinical condition of the woman requires treatment.

## Breast-feeding

Colchicine is excreted into human milk. The breastfed infant may receive up to approximately 10% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose.

Available published data have not reported adverse reactions in breastfed infants exposed to colchicine. However, given the pharmacological activity of colchicine, a risk to the breastfed infant cannot be excluded.

A decision must be made whether to discontinue breast-feeding or to discontinue/abstain from colchicine therapy, taking into account the benefit of breast-feeding for the child and the benefit of therapy for the woman.

## Fertility

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Animal  studies  have  demonstrated  that  colchicine  can  impair  fertility  in  both  males  and  females, including effects on spermatogenesis and oogenesis (see section 5.3).

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines

Colchicine has no influence on the ability to drive and use machines.

## 4.8 Undesirable effects

## Summary of the safety profile

Gastrointestinal disorders are the most common adverse reactions with colchicine. These are often the first signs of toxicity and may indicate that treatment needs to be interrupted. These adverse reactions include diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain or cramping.

Historical use of colchicine has been reported to cause neuromuscular toxicity, which may present as muscle pain or  weakness (see  section 4.4) . Serious  toxic  manifestations  associated  with  colchicine include myelosuppression, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and impairment of renal, hepatic, circulatory, and central nervous systems.

## Reporting of suspected adverse reactions

Reporting  suspected  adverse  reactions  after  authorisation  of  the  medicinal  product  is  important.  It allows  continued  monitoring  of  the  benefit/risk  balance  of  the  medicinal  product.  Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions via the national reporting system listed in Appendix V.

## 4.9 Overdose

Acute overdose exceeding 0.5 mg/kg (35 mg for a 70 kg average adult) is usually fatal. Fatalities have been reported with as little as 7 mg.

## Symptoms of overdose

Colchicine poisoning presents in three sequential and usually overlapping phases. The first stage of acute colchicine toxicity typically occurs within 24 hours of ingestion and includes gastrointestinal symptoms such as abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea, and significant fluid loss, leading to volume depletion. Peripheral leukocytosis may also be seen. The second stage develops 24 to 72 hours after ingestion and is characterized by multi-organ failure. Death is usually a result of respiratory depression and cardiovascular collapse. Recovery may be accompanied by rebound leukocytosis about one week after the initial ingestion.

## Treatment

No specific antidote is known. Elimination of toxins by gastric lavage followed by activated charcoal should  be  attempted  within  1-2  hours  of  ingestion.  Colchicine  is  not  effectively  removed  by haemodialysis.

## 5. PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Antigout preparations, preparations with no effect on uric acid metabolism, ATC code: M04AC01

## Mechanism of action

The mechanism of action of colchicine in the prevention of major cardiovascular (CV) events is not understood. Colchicine is considered to act against the inflammatory response by possibly inhibiting the migration of granulocytes to the inflamed area and by interaction with the microtubules, which together could contribute to the prevention of CV events.

Colchicine  is  an  anti-inflammatory  medicinal  product  that  inhibits  microtubule  polymerisation  and modulates inflammatory pathways implicated in atherosclerosis, including neutrophil activation and the NLRP3 inflammasome. Through these mechanisms, colchicine reduces vascular inflammation, which contributes to residual cardiovascular risk despite optimal standard therapy.

.

## Pharmacodynamic effects

The pharmacodynamics of colchicine in prevention of ischemic cardiovascular events is not completely understood; however, colchicine has been shown to exert cardioprotective, anti-inflammatory and antiatherosclerotic effects in several animal models in vivo and may stabilize atherosclerotic plaques by reducing inflammatory activity and plaque burden. Favorable plaque-modifying effects of daily 0.5 mg colchicine therapy, independent of substantial low-density lipoprotein reduction or high-dose statin intensification in patients post-acute coronary syndrome have been observed. Established pharmacodynamic effect (e.g. effect  on  uric  acid  metabolism)  does  not  occur  at  the  recommended posology of this product.

## Clinical efficacy and safety

LoDoCo2 (ACTRN12614000093684) was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentred  trial  in  patients  with  chronic,  stable  coronary  artery  disease  (CAD).  A  total  of  5 522 patients were enrolled, with 2 762 patients assigned to the Colchicine group and 2 760 to the placebo group who underwent median follow-up of 28.6 months.

Patients entered the randomized phase only after completing a 30-day open-label run-in period with low-dose  colchicine,  during  which  patients  with  intolerance  to  colchicine  were  excluded  from randomisation.  The  patients were  required  to  tolerate  colchicine  during  a  pre-randomisation  run-in period prior to study entry.

Eligible  patients  were  adults  with  documented  coronary  artery  disease  demonstrated  by  invasive coronary angiography, computed tomography coronary angiography, or a coronary-artery calcium score of  at  least  400  Agatston  units,  who  had  been  clinically  stable  for  at  least  6  months.  Patients  with moderate-to-severe  renal  impairment,  severe  heart  failure,  severe  valvular  heart  disease,  or  known intolerance to colchicine were excluded.

Baseline characteristics were generally balanced between treatment groups. The mean age of the study population was approximately 66 years, and the majority of patients were male. Most participants had established  cardiovascular  risk  factors  and  were  receiving  standard  secondary  prevention  therapy, including lipid-lowering agents and antiplatelet therapy.

The primary efficacy endpoint was the time to first occurrence of a composite of cardiovascular death, spontaneous myocardial infarction, ischaemic stroke, or ischaemia-driven coronary revascularisation.

Treatment  with  colchicine  significantly  reduced  the  risk  of  the  primary  composite  cardiovascular endpoint compared with placebo (hazard ratio [HR] 0.69; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.57-0.83; p&lt;0.001), corresponding to a relative risk reduction of approximately 31%.

The interpretation of the efficacy results is subject to several limitations. In LoDoCo2, patients entered the randomised phase only after completing a 30-day open-label run-in period with colchicine, during which  patients  with  early  intolerance  were  excluded.  The  study  population  excluded  patients  with moderate to severe renal impairment, severe heart failure, and other high-risk conditions, which may limit  the  applicability  of  results  to  these  populations.  Event  ascertainment  focused  on  prespecified cardiovascular outcomes, and the collection of non-cardiovascular safety data was limited.

Supportive  evidence  is  available  from  additional  randomised  studies  and  meta-analytic  data  in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. However, results across cardiovascular trials are heterogeneous, particularly between stable coronary disease and acute myocardial infarction populations. In CLEAR SYNERGY/OASIS-9,  a  large,  randomized  trial  in  patients  treated  shortly  after  acute  myocardial infarction, colchicine did not significantly reduce the primary composite cardiovascular endpoint. The interpretation of this study differs from stable CAD trials due to differences in population, timing of initiation after acute MI, background acute interventional therapy, adherence considerations and trial conduct during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The  clinical  benefit  of  colchicine  has  therefore  been  established  primarily  in  patients  with  stable coronary artery disease.

## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

Pharmacokinetic studies were conducted in healthy male subjects aged 18-45 years with BMI 18.5030.00 kg/m² (N=55 per study) under both fasting and fed conditions.

## Absorption

Oral  colchicine  intake  undergoes  an  entero-hepatic  recirculation.  In  healthy  adults,  colchicine  is absorbed when given orally, reaching a mean Cmax 2.1 ng/ml in approximately 1 hour after a single dose administered under fasting conditions.

The mean absolute bioavailability of colchicine is reported to be approximately 45%.

## Effect of food

When colchicine was administered with or following a high-fat, high-calorie meal in a bioavailability study, the results showed a lack of a significant food effect. Colchicine may be administered with or without food.

The mean peak plasma concentration of colchicine under fed conditions was found to be 1.8 ng/ml in approximately 1.75 hour.

## Distribution

The mean apparent volume of distribution in healthy volunteers is approximately 1300 L. Colchicine binding to serum protein is 39 ± 5%, primarily to albumin regardless of concentration. After reabsorption, is rapidly removed from the plasma and distributed to various tissues. Colchicine is found in high concentrations in leucocytes, kidneys, the liver, and spleen. Colchicine crosses the placenta. Colchicine  also  distributes  into  breast  milk.  Colchicine  can  also  cross  the  blood-brain  barrier  and accumulate within the brain, which contains large amount of tubulin.

## Biotransformation

Colchicine is demethylated to two primary metabolites, 2-O-demethylcolchicine and 3-Odemethylcolchicine (2- and 3-DMC, respectively) and one minor metabolite, 10-O-demethylcolchicine (also known as colchicine). In vitro studies using human liver microsomes have shown that CYP3A4 is involved in the metabolism of colchicine to 2- and 3-DMC. Plasma levels of these metabolites are (less than 5% of parent drug).

## Elimination

In  healthy  volunteers  (n=12),  40  to  65%  of  1  mg  orally  administered  colchicine  was  recovered unchanged in urine. Enterohepatic recirculation and biliary excretion are also postulated to play a role in colchicine elimination. Following single oral dose of 0.5 mg daily, the mean effective half-life in healthy volunteers is 19 hours. Colchicine is a substrate of P-gp. Colchicine is not removed by hemodialysis.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Renal impairment

No dedicated pharmacokinetic study has been conducted using Colchicine in patients with varying degrees of renal impairment. A published report described the disposition of colchicine (1 mg) in young adult men and women with familial Mediterranean fever (FMF) who had normal renal function or endstage renal disease requiring dialysis. Patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) had 75% lower colchicine clearance (0.17 vs. 0.73 L/hr/kg) and prolonged plasma elimination half-life (18.8 hours vs. 4.4 hours) as compared to subjects with FMF and normal renal function. In another study, 8 healthy subjects with normal renal function, 8 subjects each with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment, and 8 subjects with ESRD received a single 0.6 mg dose of colchicine prior to receiving, and again following, haemodialysis. The results showed that colchicine exposure was similar for subjects with normal  renal  function,  mild  impairment,  or  ESRD  prior  to  and  during  haemodialysis  (24.7-31.7 ng·h/mL), but was up to two-fold higher in subjects with moderate or severe renal impairment (48.9 and 48.0 ng·h/mL, respectively). A very small amount of the colchicine dose (mean of 5.2%) was recovered in dialysate.

## Hepatic impairment

No dedicated  pharmacokinetic  study  using  colchicine  has  been  conducted  in  patients  with  varying degrees of hepatic impairment. Published reports on the pharmacokinetics of IV colchicine in patients with severe chronic liver disease, as well as those with alcoholic or primary biliary cirrhosis and normal renal function suggest wide interpatient variability. In some subjects with mild to moderate cirrhosis, the clearance of colchicine is significantly reduced and plasma half-life prolonged compared to healthy subjects. In subjects with primary biliary cirrhosis, no consistent trends were noted. No pharmacokinetic data are available for patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).

## Paediatric population

No pharmacokinetics data are available in children.

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data

## General toxicology

No  general  toxicology  studies  have  been  conducted  with  colchicine,  however,  no  specific  safety pharmacology concerns have been identified beyond those attributable to the known pharmacodynamic properties of colchicine. Any overdose with colchicine is potentially lethal. In repeated-dose toxicity studies,  dose-dependent  adverse  reactions  were  observed  mainly  in  rapidly  proliferating  tissues, including  the  gastrointestinal  tract,  bone  marrow,  lymphoid  tissue,  and  reproductive  organs,  with species-specific sensitivity reported.

## Carcinogenicity

Based on the results of two-year studies in mice and rats, colchicine did not demonstrate any evidence of carcinogenic potential at oral doses up to 3 mg/kg/day in mice and 2 mg/kg/day in rats, corresponding to exposures approximately 29- and 38-times greater than the recommended human dose of 0.5 mg on a mg/m² basis.

## Genotoxicity

Colchicine was negative for mutagenicity in the bacterial reverse mutation assay. In a chromosomal aberration assay in cultured human white blood cells, colchicine treatment resulted in the formation of micronuclei. Since published studies demonstrated that colchicine induces aneuploidy from the process of mitotic nondisjunction without structural DNA changes, colchicine is not considered clastogenic, although micronuclei are formed.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Reproductive and developmental toxicity

Published nonclinical studies demonstrated that colchicine-induced disruption of microtubule formation affects meiosis and mitosis. Fertility studies also reported abnormal sperm morphology and reduced sperm counts in males, and interference with sperm penetration, second meiotic division and normal cleavage  in  females  when  exposed  to  colchicine.  Moreover,  published  animal  studies  indicate  that colchicine causes embryofetal toxicity and altered postnatal development at exposures within or above the clinical therapeutic range.

Colchicine interferes with microtubule formation and mitotic spindle function, resulting in inhibition of cell division. In animal studies, colchicine was embryotoxic and teratogenic, and induced aneuploidy, chromosomal abnormalities, and impaired embryonic development. Adverse effects on fertility were observed in both male and female animals, including impaired spermatogenesis, oocyte maturation, and implantation. These effects were dose-dependent and related to the timing of exposure. In humans, cases of reversible oligospermia and azoospermia have been reported during colchicine therapy. A causal relationship has not been definitively established.

Although  these  findings  raise  a  theoretical  concern  for  reproductive  toxicity  in  humans,  extensive clinical experience with colchicine at therapeutic doses has not demonstrated a consistent signal of increased risk of congenital malformations or permanent infertility. Nevertheless, due to the mechanism of action and non-clinical findings, precautionary contraceptive measures are recommended (see section 4.6) .

## 1. What Colchicine AGEPHA Pharma is and what it is used for

Colchicine AGEPHA Pharma contains colchicine which is used in adults with heart disease (stable for at  least  6  months)  caused  by  narrowing  or  blockage  of  blood  vessels  supplying  the  heart  muscle (coronary heart disease) that is stable to reduce the risk of:

- heart attack,
- the need for procedures to restore blood flow to the heart when reduced oxygen supply to the heart muscle is causing symptoms (ischaemia-driven coronary revascularisation),
- stroke caused by blocked blood supply to part of the brain (ischaemic stroke),
- and death caused by diseases of the heart or blood vessels.

It is used along with standard therapies.

The  way  that  the  active  substance  in  Colchicine  AGEPHA  Pharma,  colchicine,  works  is  not  fully understood. However, it is thought that it reduces inflammation by limiting the movement of certain white blood cells to areas of inflammation and by disrupting the release of inflammatory substances inside these cells. This is thought to prevent damage to the heart and blood vessels.

This medicine affects how cells divide and could have an effect on the unborn child in the event of conception during this treatment. Male patients should not father a child during and for at least 3 months following termination of colchicine therapy.

## Pregnancy, breastfeeding and fertility

If you are pregnant or breast-feeding, think you may be pregnant or are planning to have a baby, ask your doctor or pharmacist for advice before taking this medicine.

## Pregnancy

This medicine affects cell division and is known to cross the placenta in humans. The available data do not show any risk for the unborn child, but cases of genetic abnormalities, including Down syndrome, have been reported in association with colchicine, although this risk has not been confirmed in studies. Therefore,  Colchicine  AGEPHA  Pharma  should  not  be  used  during  pregnancy  unless  the  clinical condition of the woman requires treatment with colchicine.

## Breast-feeding

The active substance in Colchicine AGEPHA Pharma, colchicine, passes into breast milk. If you are breast-feeding ask your doctor for advice before taking Colchicine AGEPHA Pharma. Your doctor will decide whether you should stop breast-feeding.

## Fertility

If you are planning to have children, discuss this with your doctor before starting Colchicine AGEPHA Pharma.
