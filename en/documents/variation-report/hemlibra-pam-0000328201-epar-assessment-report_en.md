## Methods

## Study design

Figure 1. Phase III Study Design

<!-- image -->

Study was a multicentre, open-label, Phase III clinical study with randomized and non-randomized arms designed to investigate the efficacy, safety, and PK of emicizumab in patients with haemophilia A regardless of FVIII inhibitor status.

70 Chinese patients (from Mainland China and Hong Kong) aged &gt;=12 years who received episodic therapy with FVIII or bypassing agents prior to study entry and experienced at least 5 bleeds over the prior 24 weeks were randomized in a 2:2:1 ratio to the following regimens:

- Arm A: Emicizumab prophylaxis at 3 mg/kg every week (QW) subcutaneously for 4 weeks, followed by 1.5 mg/kg QW subcutaneously
- Arm B: Emicizumab prophylaxis at 3 mg/kg QW subcutaneously for 4 weeks, followed by 6 mg/kg Q4W subcutaneously
- Arm C: No prophylaxis (control arm).

Patients who were randomized to Arm C could switch to receive emicizumab prophylaxis at the Arm B regimen (6 mg/kg Q4W maintenance dose after 4 weeks of 3 mg/kg QW loading dose) after completing 24 weeks in the study to obtain additional safety and efficacy data on emicizumab. This yielded 2 study periods for Arm C: a \"no prophylaxis period\" for the first 24 weeks in the study (Arm control), followed by an \"emicizumab prophylaxis period\" (Arm Cemi). These 2 periods were analysed separately.

In addition, 15 paediatric patients with haemophilia A and inhibitors who received episodic therapy with bypassing agents prior to study entry were enrolled to Arm D (only from Mainland China):

- Arm D: Emicizumab prophylaxis at 3 mg/kg QW subcutaneously for 4 weeks, followed by 1.5 mg/kg QW subcutaneously

The primary efficacy analysis, defined as comparing the number of bleeds over time for patients randomized to receive prophylactic emicizumab versus no prophylaxis was conducted at the earliest timepoint when all randomized patients (Arms A, B, and C) had either completed 24 weeks in the study or discontinued from treatment. For Arm D, the primary efficacy analysis was conducted after the last enrolled patient had completed 24 weeks in the study or discontinued from treatment. Therefore, there was a range of observation periods from 6 to approximately 12 months, or longer. The endpoints were analysed separately for the two randomized emicizumab arms (Arms A and B): 1.5 mg/kg QW and 6 mg/kg Q4W, as well as for the additional arm in paediatric patients (Arm D): 1.5 mg/kg QW.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

After completing at least 24 weeks of treatment with prophylactic emicizumab, patients who received emicizumab prophylaxis (Arms A, B, or C after treatment switch and Arm D) and derived clinical benefit were allowed to continue emicizumab as part of this study as long as they continued to derive clinical benefit and emicizumab was still in clinical development. Those who were well controlled after 24 weeks on emicizumab (&lt;2 spontaneous and clinically significant bleeds) continued treatment on their assigned emicizumab regimen; whereas those who experience suboptimal control (&gt;=2 spontaneous and clinically significant bleeds) had the option to escalate to 3 mg/kg QW per Investigator's judgement after consulting with the Medical Monitor.

There was an extension phase in this study for all patients who completed at least 52 weeks of treatment with prophylactic emicizumab and still derived clinical benefit. The study was extended for 3 years after the last Arm D patient completed 1 year of treatment and patients still had clinical benefit were transferred to a post-trial continued access solution per Roche Global Policy on Continued Access to Investigational Medicinal Products.

## Study participants

This study enrolled patients with severe haemophilia A from China and other countries in the AsiaPacific region who previously received episodic therapy with either FVIII or bypassing agents in Arms A, B, and C and paediatric patients with haemophilia A with inhibitors from China mainland who previously received episodic therapy with bypassing agents in Arm D.

## Dosing Regimen

Each patient starting on prophylactic emicizumab received 3 mg/kg QW for 4 weeks as loading doses, followed by 1.5 mg/kg QW (Arm A) or 6 mg/kg Q4W (Arm B), for a total of at least 24 weeks or as long as they continue to derive sufficient clinical benefit. After completion of 24 weeks observation in the study, patients in Arm C switched to receive emicizumab prophylaxis at the Arm B dosing regimen (emicizumab prophylaxis at 3 mg/kg QW subcutaneously for 4 weeks, followed by 6 mg/kg Q4W).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Patients in Arm D received 3 mg/kg QW for 4 weeks as loading doses, followed by 1.5 mg/kg QW as maintenance during the 24-week treatment period. Individual patients experiencing suboptimal bleeding control on emicizumab also had their dose up-titrated if they met the same criteria as required for patients in Arm A, B and C and after consulting with the Medical Monitor.

## Sample size

The sample size calculation is based on the evaluation of the primary efficacy endpoint, defined as the number of bleeds over time (i.e., bleed rate) with emicizumab (treatment group) versus no prophylaxis (control group), which are said to follow a negative binomial (NB) distribution. With consideration of enrolment feasibility, a sample size of 70 patients, assuming an allocation ratio of 2:2:1 (28 patients in each randomized treatment group and 14 patients in control group), will achieve a power of more than 90% assuming a mean ABR of 4 and 18 bleeds (with variances =mean x 10) for the emicizumab treatment and control arms, respectively, representing an expected 78% reduction in the ABR compared with the control arm. Initial sample size calculations were performed with East®, Version 6 (Cytel, Cambridge, MA), assuming the patients from each treatment group are followed up to 0.5 units of time (i.e., 24 weeks).

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

This was an open-label study with randomized and non-randomized arms. Adolescents and adult patients who received episodic therapy with FVIII or bypassing agents prior to study entry were randomized in a 2:2:1 ratio to receive either prophylactic emicizumab at 3 mg/kg QW SC for 4 weeks, followed by 1.5 mg/kg QW (Arm A) or 6 mg/kg Q4W (Arm B) SC, or to the control arm (no prophylaxis; Arm C). A central randomization procedure was used for all patients who fulfilled the entry criteria at screening. A block-based randomization method was used, stratified by the number of bleeds in the last 24 weeks (&lt;9 or &gt;= 9). The proposed randomization method was designed to balance treatment group assignment within the prognostic stratification factor.

This study opened one additional non-randomized arm (Arm D) to investigate the 1.5 mg/kg QW regimen in paediatric patients aged ≤12 years with inhibitors. All patients were recruited to Arm D.

## Statistical Methods

Efficacy analyses followed the principle of intention-to-treat (i.e., based on randomized population). For Arm D, no formal hypothesis testing is planned. The clinical effect of prophylactic emicizumab treatment in paediatric patients with inhibitors was descriptive and performed separately.

The primary and secondary efficacy analyses to evaluate the clinical effect of prophylactic emicizumab compared with no prophylaxis included all randomized patients (Arms A, B, and C), with patients grouped according to the treatment assigned at randomization.

The safety analyses population will be based on all enrolled patients grouped according to the actual treatment received. For Arm C patients, all safety data reported up to the day prior to switching will be included in the \"control arm\" safety summaries, and all safety data reported on or after the date of switching to active treatment will be reported separately. Safety will be assessed for Arms A, B, and C and separately for Arm D through descriptive summaries of adverse events, laboratory test results, ECGs, vital signs, and antibodies to emicizumab and FVIII.

## Deaths

As of the final analysis cutoff (29 September 2025), one patient died due to AEs of Grade 4 cerebrovascular accident and Grade 4 cardio-respiratory arrest during the treatment period. The details of the case are provided below.

A male with severe haemophilia A without inhibitors was enrolled in Study YO39309. The patient had a medical history of hypertension, haemophilic arthropathy and gingival bleeding with concurrent medical conditions including fractures, osteoarthritis, emphysema, hypostatic pneumonia, bronchitis, fatty liver and mesenteric panniculitis.

The patient started treatment with emicizumab (3 mg/kg QW SC for 4 weeks, followed by maintenance at 1.5 mg/kg QW SC). After more than 4 years of treatment with emicizumab, on Study Day, the patient fell from a bicycle and sustained fractures in the right lateral clavicle and right distal femur. The patient was treated with recombinant human coagulation factor VIII (3000 units) for symptomatic prevention of bleeding in the emergency department. The patient was later diagnosed with Grade 1 peripheral vein thrombosis in the posterior tibial vein of the right leg, observed during an ultrasound on Study Day. On Study Day, the patient was hospitalized and underwent plaster fixation. The thrombosis was considered resolved by Study Day. The fracture and associated injuries remained unresolved at the time. On Study Day 1544, the patient experienced dizziness, headache, and nausea, followed by a sudden disturbance of consciousness during defecation. The patient was taken to the hospital, where he was given oxygen, chest compressions, assisted breathing, adrenaline and sodium bicarbonate. The patient died the same morning due to a cerebrovascular accident and cardiorespiratory arrest. No autopsy was performed.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The Investigator attributed the cerebrovascular accident, cardio-respiratory arrest, and peripheral vein thrombosis to the study treatment, while considering the fracture event unrelated to emicizumab.

It is worth noting that the patient experienced a fall from a bicycle, resulting in multiple fractures and subsequent immobilization with plaster fixation, which is a recognized risk factor for venous thromboembolism. The sudden disturbance of consciousness occurred immediately following defecation, possibly triggered by a Valsalva manoeuvre, posing a significant risk to the cardiovascular system in vulnerable individuals. The patient's age, ongoing hypertension, underlying respiratory conditions of emphysema and bilateral hypostatic pneumonia, combined with potential metabolic syndrome (fatty liver and mesenteric panniculitis), may also have been contributing factors.

In summary, while causality remains as assessed by the Investigator the patient's age, concurrent medical conditions, and recent history of trauma should be considered to the development of the reported events and fatal outcome. To date, no evidence from the emicizumab program, including clinical trials and post-marketing data, has suggested that emicizumab is associated with fatal cerebrovascular accidents, cardio-respiratory arrest, or a confirmed risk of thromboembolic events when administered alone.

## Overview of Pharmacokinetics

Emicizumab trough plasma concentrations in adolescents and adults receiving QW and Q4W dosing regimens were initially lower than in other Phase III studies but with time it approached levels seen in other studies. Trough concentrations in patients &lt;12 years old with QW were in-line with other studies.

No new samples were collected since the Update CSR. The pharmacokinetic results are provided in Section 6 of the Primary CSR (Report 1096131) and Section 5.3 of the Update CSR (Report 1115002).

## Overview of Pharmacodynamics and safety biomarkers

Pharmacodynamic biomarker levels (factor VIII-like activity and thrombin generation) increased during emicizumab loading doses and were sustained from Week 5 onwards. Activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) normalized after the first emicizumab dose and remained within or below normal range throughout the study. Treatment with emicizumab had no effect on concentrations of factor IX (FIX), factor X (FX), D-dimer, prothrombin fragments 1 and 2, and prothrombin time which, overall, remained stable throughout the study duration.

No new samples were collected since the Update CSR. The exploratory pharmacodynamic and safety biomarker results are provided in Section 5.4 of the Update CSR (Report 1115002).

## Immunogenicity

The development of anti-drug antibody (ADA) was not associated with any adverse events, such as systemic hypersensitivity reactions. With the exception of one patient in whom ADA were associated with a transient decrease of trough concentrations and a few bleeding events, the presence of ADA had no impact on the PK profiles. Furthermore, none of the non-inhibitor patients developed de novo FVIII inhibitors, while in inhibitor patients, FVIII inhibitor titers remained stable or tended to decline over time.

No new samples were collected since the Update CSR or Addendum to Update CSR. The immunogenicity analysis results are provided in the Section 7.14.3 of the Primary CSR (Report 1096131); Section 5.5 of the Update CSR (Report 1115002) and Section 3.1 of the Addendum CSR (1121207).

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

Emicizumab is a recombinant, humanised, bispecific, immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody that binds with moderate affinity to activated factor IX (FIXa) and factor X (FX), mimicking the cofactor function of activated FVIII (FVIIIa). Efficacy and safety of emicizumab was established through an extensive clinical development program in patients with Haemophilia A irrespective of the presence of FVIII inhibitors.

Study YO39309 was a multicentre, open-label, Phase III clinical study conducted at 13 Asia-Pacific investigational sites: China (8), Hong Kong (2), Malaysia (2) and Thailand (1); and designed to investigate the efficacy, safety, and PK of emicizumab.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

70 Asian patients &gt;=12 years old with severe haemophilia A regardless of FVIII inhibitor status who received episodic therapy with FVIII or bypassing agents prior to study entry and experienced at least 5 bleeds over the prior 24 weeks were randomized in a 2:2:1 ratio to the following regimens:

- Arm A: Emicizumab prophylaxis at 3 mg/kg QW SC for 4 weeks, followed by 1.5 mg/kg QW SC
- Arm B: Emicizumab prophylaxis at 3 mg/kg QW SC for 4 weeks, followed by 6 mg/kg Q4W SC

- Arm C: No prophylaxis (Ccontrol), possible switch to the Arm B after completing 24W in the study (Cemi).

In addition, 15 Chinese paediatric patients (&lt; 12 years old) with haemophilia A and high-titer FVIII inhibitor who received episodic therapy with bypassing agents were enrolled to:

- Arm D: Emicizumab prophylaxis at 3 mg/kg QW SC for 4 weeks, followed by 1.5 mg/kg QW SC

Overall, the primary and key secondary efficacy endpoints related to the bleed rates were met with high statistical significance and indicated that 1.5 mg/kg QW and 6 mg/kg Q4W emicizumab prophylaxis provide consistent and clinically meaningful bleed control in patients with severe haemophilia A without FVIII inhibitors and HA with inhibitors.

A total of 26 paediatric and adolescent and 59 adult patients received emicizumab prophylaxis across study arms. The proportion of patients treated with emicizumab who experienced any AE in the paediatric and adolescent population (100.0% [26/26 patients]) was comparable to the adult population (94.9% [56/59 patients]).

A lower percentage of Grade ≥3 AEs was observed among paediatric and adolescent patients treated with emicizumab (19.2% [5/26]) than among adult patients (28.8% [17/59]). There were no similarities/trends in Grade ≥ 3 AEs between the adults and paediatric populations.

A lower percentage of SAEs was observed among paediatric and adolescent patients treated with emicizumab (15.4% [4/26]) than among adult patients (28.8% [17/59]). Of these, none of the SAEs reported in the paediatric and adolescent population were considered related to the study drug; whereas 6 treatment-related SAEs and two thromboembolic events occurred in adults.

No AESIs were reported in the paediatric and adolescent population, and no patients across any age group experienced an AESI of thrombotic microangiopathy. Two patients in the adult population reported AESIs related to thromboembolic event and hypersensitivity reaction. There is higher frequency of AEs observed in Metabolism and Nutrition Disorders, Vascular Disorders, and Musculoskeletal Disorders System Organ Class in adult age group compared to paediatric age group.

Overall, safety data from the study HAVEN 5 show that emicizumab maintained a favourable and welltolerated safety profile. The safety profile was generally consistent across both paediatric and adolescent population and adult population. Age-related differences in baseline risk factors, comorbidities, and physiological profiles provide a plausible explanation for the differences observed between the two age groups.

In conclusion, the comparative efficacy and safety analyses in Study (HAVEN 5) confirm that emicizumab remains well tolerated and clinically effective in all age groups, with a safety profile consistent with previous HAVEN studies. These results do not impact the established benefit-risk profile of emicizumab.
