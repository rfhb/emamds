## 1. Introduction

On 21 July 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Ervebo, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that the long-term follow-up (LTFU) of study V920-007 \"A Phase 1, Randomized, Open-Label, Dose-Escalation Study to Evaluate the Safety and Immunogenicity of the V920 (VSVΔGZEBOV) Ebola Virus Vaccine Candidate in Healthy Adult and Children Volunteers in Lambaréné, Gabon\" is a stand-alone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Ervebo (V920) also known as Ebola Zaire vaccine (rVSVΔG-ZEBOV-GP, live) is a live, attenuated recombinant vesicular stomatitis virus (rVSV) vector, which has the gene encoding for the VSV glycoprotein (GP) deleted from its RNA and replaced with the gene encoding for the ZEBOV GP (rVSVΔG-ZEBOV-GP). The vaccine is a genetically engineered, replication-competent, attenuated live vaccine that induces immune responses after a single dose. One dose (1mL) contains ≥ 72 million plaque-forming units (pfu) of rVSVΔG-ZEBOV-GP.

The posology in children 1 to 17 years of age is the same as in adults. In study V920-007, different dose levels of rVSVΔG-ZEBOV-GP were administered.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

The MAH submitted a LTFU final report for:

- Study V920-007: \"A Phase 1, Randomized, Open-Label, Dose-Escalation Study to Evaluate the Safety and Immunogenicity of the V920 (VSVΔG-ZEBOV) Ebola Virus Vaccine Candidate in Healthy Adult and Children Volunteers in Lambaréné, Gabon\".

## Description

V920-007 study was originally planned in adults. Later, the study was amended to add enrolment of children 6 to 17 years of age. The study was designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of 5 dose levels of V920 from Vaccination Day through Month 12 (for the base study) and through Year 5 post-vaccination (for the LTFU).

First subject first visit was 17 November 2014, and 10 July 2020 was the last subject last visit for the LTFU. 23 January 2026 was considered the study completion date.

In study V920-007, paediatric participants were administered at a single dose level, while adult participants were exposed to different dose levels.

A total of 115 adult participants were enrolled into five V920 dose cohorts and vaccinated with a single dose of V920 (3×10E5 pfu, 3×10E6 pfu, 2×10E7 pfu, 3×10E3 pfu, or 3×10E4 pfu). Additionally, 40 paediatric participants (20 children 6 to 12 years of age and 20 children 13 to 17 years of age) were enrolled and vaccinated with a single dose of V920 2×10E7 pfu, which is the dose level that was selected for commercialization.

All doses were administered via the intramuscular (IM) route. Each participant received 1 injection of the V920 vaccine.

Safety results through Day 365 postvaccination, immunogenicity results through Day 180 postvaccination, and vaccine viremia and vaccine viral shedding through Day 7 post-vaccination were reported in the base study CSR.

After the conclusion of the base study follow-up (Vaccination Day to Day 365) the study was amended to add a 5-year extension (amendment #6 of 19-Sep-2016) in order to evaluate long-term immunogenicity, safety and tolerability of V920.

The CSR submitted within this procedure includes analyses performed based on the MAH's statistical analysis plan (SAP). This CSR summarizes the safety results from the LTFU study period (Day 366 to Year 5) for all adult and paediatric participants and immunogenicity results from the entire study period (Vaccination Day to Year 5), using the validated GP-ELISA, for paediatric participants. Immunogenicity results previously submitted were obtained with a qualified only GP-ELISA.

## Methods

## Study participants

V920-007 included healthy adults from 18 to 50 years of age and children 6 to 17 years of age, living in Lambaréné, Gabon, who were not pregnant or breastfeeding, with no moderate or severe illness and/or fever &gt;38°C within 1 week prior to vaccination, and no immunosuppressive conditions or medication or any condition which would limit the ability of the participant to meet the requirements of the study.

To be considered for the study, participants had to be willing to minimize blood and body fluid exposure for 5 days after vaccination.

Participation in the study for the first 6 months was required for entry into the LTFU period.

Main exclusion criteria for V920-007 participants at screening were the following:

- Had a history of severe local or systemic reactions to any vaccination or a history of severe allergic reactions;
- Were participating in another clinical study;

## Study assessment and procedures

## Immunogenicity assessments

In the base study, blood samples for serological analysis were obtained from participants at baseline (before the study intervention was administered) and at Days 7, 14, 28, 56, 84, 180, and 365 postvaccination. In the LTFU, blood samples were collected at Days 730 (Month 24), 1095 (Month 36), 1460 (Month 48), and 1825 (Month 60).

For paediatric participants, samples collected at all time points during the base study and the LTFU were (re)-analysed at the central laboratory using a validated GP ELISA.

Of note, the base study CSR (P007V920) described immunology results generated using a locally conducted non-validated GP-ELISA.

## Validated ZEBOV Anti- GP Immunoglobulin G (IgG) Human Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (GP ELISA)

To measure and quantify total IgG antibodies against V920, a validated indirect ELISA was developed which uses a purified rGP as the coating antigen and an enzyme-conjugated anti-human IgG secondary antibody as the reporter or signal system. Briefly, microtiter plates are coated with purified ZEBOV Kikwit rGP. Serum samples from participants and controls are then incubated with the rGP coated wells, allowing GP-specific antibodies to bind. Additionally, a serial-diluted reference standard, obtained from a pool of vaccinated donors, is included. Each well is then incubated with goat anti-human IgG horseradish peroxidase conjugate, which enzymatically reacts with the tetramethylbenzidine substrate. After incubation, the enzymatic reaction is stopped using a sulfuric acid solution. The optical density is measured on an ELISA plate reader and serum sample titre concentrations are calculated from the standard curve using a 4-parameter logistic curve fit.

Titres are reported as GP-ELISA units/mL.

## Statistical Methods

The immunogenicity and safety analyses in the submitted LTFU CSR were performed by the MSD BARDS department based on the MSD SAP.

V920-007 LTFU did not include any hypothesis testing.

The safety analyses include descriptive summary statistics for participants who reported SAEs and deaths during LTFU (Day 366 to Year 5). The safety population was the All Participants as Treated (APaT) population which included randomized participants who received at least 1 dose of study intervention and entered LTFU. Participants were included in the vaccination group corresponding to the study intervention they received for the analysis of safety data using the APaT population.

Immunogenicity analyses included the assessment of GMTs, GMFIs, and seroresponse as measured by the validated GP-ELISA (timepoints up to Year 5) for paediatric participants. For GMTs and GMFIs, data were first log transformed. The transformed data were then analyzed by ANOVA. The ANOVA statistics were then back-transformed into GMTs and GMFIs.

Seroresponse statistics were based on frequencies. Seroresponse by the 2 definitions (≥2-fold increase from baseline and ≥200 EU/mL, and 4-fold increase from baseline) was assessed.

The immunogenicity population was the GP-ELISA Per-Protocol (PP) population, consisted of all randomized participants who received the relevant study vaccination and had serology data collected within the acceptable day range. The PP population excluded participants who violated certain inclusion/exclusion criteria.

## Results

## Subjects disposition and demographic characteristics

All 155 participants randomized into the base study (20 children 6 to 12 years of age, 20 children 13 to 17 years of age, and 115 adults) received study vaccination with one of 5 doses of V920 vaccine.

A total of 135 participants entered LTFU, and of these, 84.4% of participants completed the study (Table 2). The main reason for study discontinuation during LTFU was being lost to follow-up (14.8%).

Of the 40 children 6 to 17 years of age vaccinated with a single dose of V920 2×10E7 pfu, 17 children 6 to 12 years of age and 16 children 13 to 17 years of age entered the LTFU study period. Of these, 94.1% and 68.8%, respectively, completed the 5-year post-vaccination follow-up. All 6 participants (1 participant 6 to 12 years of age and 5 participants 7 to 13 years of age) who discontinued from LTFU were lost to follow-up.

A total of 102/115 adult participants vaccinated across the 5 dose cohorts entered LTFU and of these 85.3% completed the study. Fourteen of the 15 adult participants who discontinued LTFU were lost to follow-up and 1 participant discontinued due to 'other' reasons.

## GP-ELISA GMFIs

GP-ELISA GMFIs were greater than 1 from baseline through Month 60 in children 6 to 12 years of age and children 13 to 17 years of age, except for Day 7. Sample sizes were small due to missing baseline values in many participants.

## GP-ELISA Seroresponse rates

All paediatric participants had a seroresponse defined as a ≥2-fold increase from baseline and ≥200 EU/mL at any time post-vaccination.

At Month 60 post-vaccination all participants with data achieved seroresponse defined as a ≥4-fold increase from baseline.

Sample sizes are small due to missing baseline values in many participants.

## Safety results

A total of 32 SAEs were reported during LTFU for 24 (17.8%) participants, and none of the SAEs were vaccine related as assessed by the investigator. The most common SAE was malaria, reported for 11 (8.1%) participants. No participants died during the LTFU.

Regarding SAEs in participating children 6 to 17 Years of Age, 2/33 (6.1%) paediatric participants (both in the 13 to 17 years of age cohort) experienced SAEs during from Day 366 through Year 5 post-vaccination. None of the SAEs were assessed as vaccine related. One child 13 to 17 years of age experienced dental caries and another child 13 to 17 years of age experienced malaria. One additional participant in the 13 to 17 years of age cohort had an SAE of malaria which occurred during LTFU but was assigned to the base study period as a result of date imputation and is not included in the SAE summaries for LTFU.

Regarding SAEs in adult participants, 22/102 (21.6%) adult participants experienced a total of 30 SAEs during LTFU with no apparent relationship between SAE frequency and dose potency across the V920 dose cohorts; none of the SAEs were assessed as vaccine related by the investigator.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

Within this procedure, the MAH submitted the CSR of the 5-year LTFU (Day 366 to Year 5) of V920-007 that includes safety results from the LTFU period (Day 366 to Year 5) for all participants and immunogenicity results using a validated GP-ELISA from the entire study period (Vaccination Day to Year 5) for paediatric participants (6 to 17 years of age, inclusive).

A total of 33 children/adolescents out of the 40 initial participants enrolled in the base study were included in the LTFU study. Reasons for not being included in the LTFU study were not described. Median age (range) was 11 years (7-17) and most of the participants were of male biological sex.

The descriptive GP-ELISA IgG humoral response data from the LTFU of V920-007 were obtained on a very limited number of paediatric subjects aged 6-17 years, with numbers variable across timepoints. Nevertheless, it can be overall concluded that these are aligned with LTFU immunogenicity data from another clinical study conducted in adults, which indicate that a single IM administration of Ervebo induces humoral responses that are still sustained and above baseline levels up to 5 years after vaccination. Consider in that respect the Month 60 data from study V920-009 (included in the SmPC section 5.1). According to the MAH, LTFU data of study V920-016 that enrolled paediatric participants will be available soon. Results of V920-016 study up to Month 12 post-vaccination are currently described in the product information.

Concerning clinical safety aspects, review of the SAEs data collected during LTFU of study V920-007 on a limited number of paediatric and adult participants did not identify new safety concerns.

It is agreed with the MAH that the results of this study do not change the benefit/risk profile of Ervebo, and no product information update is warranted.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

The limited descriptive immunogenicity and safety data generated in the long-term follow-up of study V920-007 are aligned with the Year 5 immunogenicity data already available and do not identify new safety concerns.

No product information update is warranted. The benefit/risk of Ervebo remains unchanged.
