DIVERGENT POSITION DATED 21 May 2026

VIJOICE (alpelisib) - EMEA/H/C/006539/0000

The undersigned members of the CHMP did not agree with the CHMP's majority positive opinion treatment of adult and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older with severe or life-threatening manifestations of PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum (PROS) who require systemic therapy.

recommending the granting of a Conditional Marketing Authorisation (CMA) for Vijoice (alpelisib) for the The unmet medical need for effective treatments in PROS is acknowledged. A sound mechanistic rationale

exists for the use of alpelisib in the treatment of PROS. Furthermore, data suggest that the tumour reduction observed in patients with PROS may be attributable to alpelisib. However, the reasons for the divergent opinion were the following:

- The clinical data package supporting the MAA is limited and the overall size of the efficacy and safety database is small. Considering that the PROS population is heterogeneous in terms of clinical presentation, lesion types and disease severity, the observed variable responses relevantly hamper interpretation of the results. This issue questions the external validity of the results.
- There are significant concerns regarding the reliability and robustness of the results from the pivotal EPIK-P1 study, a retrospective chart review that included a total of 57 patients from a single centre, treated in the context of a compassionate use program. Despite the implementation of mitigation measures, important limitations remain due to the retrospective, open - label design and the lack of an internal control, with potential bias affecting imaging - based endpoints and clinical decision - making. The supportive studies (EPIK-P2 and EPIK-P3) have also several limitations (failed study that included a broader population and/or methodological constraints/major design limitations).
- The durability and clinical relevance of the effect of alpelisib in the intended indication have not been adequately/robustly demonstrated.
- It is uncertain whether the Applicant may be able to provide comprehensive data postauthorisation in the context of a CMA and properly address the outstanding uncertainties. The ongoing EPIK-P4 study is a single-arm trial lacking a comparator that shares several of the same shortcomings and limitations already highlighted for the previous studies, including the same proposed primary endpoint, which may not adequately capture clinically meaningful benefit.
