## 1. Introduction

On 27 May 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Entyvio, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

Vedolizumab was approved in May 2014 in the European Union under the invented name Entyvio (EMEA/H/C/002782) for the treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active UC or CD who have had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to conventional therapies.

A PIP (EMEA-000645-PIP01-09) is in place for vedolizumab in UC and CD. A waiver was granted for the paediatric population from birth to age &lt;2 years, and the PIP applies to the paediatric population subsets including children aged 2 to &lt;18 years. Under Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No. 1901/2006, as amended, any study sponsored by the Marketing Authorisation Holder that involves the use of an authorized medicinal product, including any subset of the paediatric population, must be submitted to the relevant competent authority within 6 months of study completion, regardless of the study's inclusion in a PIP.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study&lt;ies&gt;

Entyvio is available as a powder concentration for solution for infusion in vials containing 300 mg of vedolizumab (induction and maintenance treatment) and as solution for injection in prefilled syringes and prefilled pens containing 108 mg of vedolizumab for subcutaneous administration (maintenance treatment). The solution for infusion was used in this study.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## Introduction

The MAH submitted the results of the retrospective analysis for:

Real-World Study of VDZ-Treated Pediatric Patients with CD or UC in ICN Registry

## Clinical study

ICN (ImproveCareNow) Study: Disease Characteristics, Treatment Patterns, and Clinical Outcomes in Pediatric Patients Receiving Vedolizumab for Crohn's Disease or Ulcerative Colitis: A Retrospective, Longitudinal Study of the ImproveCareNow (ICN)

## Description

ICN was a non-interventional study designed to evaluate vedolizumab IV use in paediatric patients aged 2 to &lt;18 years with Ulcerative Colitis (UC) or Crohn Disease (CD) using the ICN registry, a large, multicentre, longitudinal data source that captures data from more than 30,000 paediatric IBD patients across over 100 US centres, with the aim of generate evidence on VDZ IV use in pediatric IBD in a realworld setting, characterizing patient demographics, treatment patterns, clinical outcomes, and healthcare resource use (HRU) in the primary analysis population and by age and weight subgroups.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In particular, a retrospective, non-interventional cohort study design was used to address all study objectives using observational data from the ICN registry. Retrospective, individual-level data for paediatric patients with UC or CD treated with VDZ IV were analysed from the first recorded visit to the last recorded visit in the ICN registry during the most recent 10-year data period spanning from February 1, 2015 to February 1, 2025.

The definitions of the study design elements are reported in the table below.

## Methods

## Study participants

The study population was composed of paediatric patients (≥2 to &lt;18 years of age) diagnosed with moderate-to-severe UC or CD, who consented to participate in the ICN registry, and were treated with VDZ IV in the U.S. after February 1, 2015.

The criteria below were used to identify the primary analysis population:

- Patients with reported use of VDZ IV in the U.S. between February 1, 2015, and February 1, 2025, AND
- Patients with a diagnosis of UC or CD at enrolment in ICN registry, AND
- Patients with at least one visit prior to the index date without VDZ IV use and no VDZ IV use recorded in the ICN registry before February 1, 2015,1 AND
- Patients were included only if they had no diagnosis of the alternate condition (i.e., UC patients with no CD diagnosis and CD patients with no UC diagnosis) on or before the index date, AND
- Patients ≥2 to &lt;18 years of age as of the index date
- Patients with moderate-to-severe disease defined by the following criteria: o PUCAI score ≥35 or sPCDAI score ≥30 for UC and CD patients, respectively, at the index date or within 12 weeks prior to the index date; if multiple assessments were available during this time period, the maximum value was used.

No exclusion criteria were identified.

The end of the follow-up period was defined by the patient's last recorded visit in the ICN registry. Patients may no longer appear in the ICN registry for the following reasons:

- Transferred to an adult gastroenterologist

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Transferred to another pediatric gastroenterology practice
- Transferred to another physician who is not a gastroenterologist
- Colectomy for UC
- Moved to another city
- Lost to follow-up
- Death

Of note, while patients were required to be &lt;18 years of age as of the index date, patients were not censored as of their 18th birthday, meaning that patients may have contributed follow-up time to the observation period as adults.

## Treatments

Vedolizumab IV was administered as per clinical practice.

## Objective(s)

The aim of this study was to describe the clinical effectiveness (including remission and response), laboratory markers (albumin and C-reactive protein [CRP]), treatment patterns, and healthcare resource utilization (HRU), separately among UC and CD patients, with analyses stratified by age (≥2 to &lt;13 years versus ≥13 to &lt;18 years) and weight group (&lt;30 kg versus ≥30 kg). The study population was classified into two cohorts based on the disease, i.e., UC or CD and provide additional real-world evidence to inform understanding of VDZ IV use in paediatric patients, including those weighing less than 30 kg, as this group is historically underrepresented in clinical trials due to recruitment challenges and stringent eligibility criteria.

## Outcomes/endpoints

The primary study objectives were as follows:

· To describe the demographic and clinical characteristics of paediatric patients with UC or CD who received VDZ IV

· To assess the clinical effectiveness of VDZ IV, measured by clinical remission using Paediatric Ulcerative Colitis Activity Index (PUCAI) and Short Paediatric Crohn's Disease Activity Index (sPCDAI) scores, in paediatric patients with UC or CD

The secondary study objectives were as follows:

· To assess the clinical effectiveness of VDZ IV, measured by clinical response using PUCAI and sPCDAI scores, in paediatric patients with UC or CD

- To assess the clinical effectiveness of VDZ IV based on changes from baseline in biomarkers including albumin and CRP, in paediatric patients with UC or CD
- To describe HRU as measured by hospitalizations, intra-abdominal gastrointestinal (GI) surgeries, and serious infection in paediatric patients with UC or CD who received VDZ IV

The exploratory study objectives were as follows:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

· To describe VDZ IV treatment patterns of time to discontinuation, VDZ IV dosing, including dosage and frequency of administration, and timing of dosage administration (e.g., weeks since index date or weeks between dosages) in paediatric patients with UC or CD.

In particular, the clinical effectiveness was assessed as follow:

## Clinical Remission at Week 14 and Week 54 (Primary Objective)

- UC cohort: Clinical remission was defined as the proportion of patients who had a PUCAI &lt;10 at Week 14 (±6 weeks) and, separately, at Week 54 (±12 weeks). The PUCAI measure closest to Week 14 and Week 54 and within the specified window was used to assess remission status.
- CD cohort: Clinical remission was defined as the proportion of patients who had an sPCDAI ≤10 at Week 14 (±6 weeks) and, separately, at Week 54 (±12 weeks). The sPCDAI measure closest to Week 14 and Week 54 and within the specified window was used to assess remission status.

## Clinical Response at Week 14 and Week 54 (Secondary Objective)

- UC cohort: Clinical response was defined as the proportion of patients who had a reduction in the PUCAI ≥20 points from baseline to Week 14 (±6 weeks) and separately, at 54 (±12 weeks). The PUCAI measure closest to Week 14 and Week 54 within the specified window was used to assess response status.
- CD cohort: Clinical response was defined as the proportion of patients who had a reduction in the sPCDAI ≥15 points from baseline to Week 14 (±6 weeks) and separately, at 54 (±12 weeks). The sPCDAI measure closest to Week 14 and Week 54 within the specified window was used to assess response status.

## Change in Albumin and CRP at Week 14 and Week 54 (Secondary Objective)

- Change in albumin and CRP at Week 14 and Week 54 was defined as the difference between the follow-up lab values at specific timepoints and the baseline lab values for serum albumin and CRP. The baseline lab value was defined as the lab value on or within 12 weeks prior to the index date. If there were multiple lab values that fell in this period, the least favorable value was used to define baseline (e.g., lowest value for albumin and highest for CRP). Specifically, the change in lab values at Week 14 (±6 weeks) and Week 54 (±12 weeks) were evaluated.

The thresholds for clinical remission (PUCAI &lt;10, sPCDAI ≤10) and response (PUCAI ≥20, sPCDAI ≥15) were chosen based on the thresholds used in the clinical trials of VDZ IV inpediatric patients with UC or CD (NCT03138655, NCT04779307).19,20

## HRU: Hospitalization, Surgery, and Serious Infections (Secondary Objective)

The following HRU outcomes were available in the ICN data and were summarized during the 54-week post-index follow-up period:

- Hospitalizations

All-cause hospitalizations: included all hospitalizations recorded in the CRF, regardless of whether they were noted as being related to IBD, unrelated to IBD, or of unknown association with IBD

- IBD-related hospitalizations:

Hospitalizations identified by the physician in the CRF as being associated with IBD were classified as IBD-related

- Intra-abdominal GI surgeries

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

This was defined as any intra-abdominal GI surgery that occurred during a hospital admission

- Serious infection

ICN defined serious infection as infections that require hospitalization or intravenous (IV) therapy

## Treatment Patterns of VDZ IV (Exploratory Objective)

## Time to Treatment Discontinuation

- VDZ IV treatment discontinuation was assessed from the index date to the first documented visit without VDZ IV treatment.

## VDZ IV Dosing

- Dosage administered (in mg) of VDZ IV treatment
- Number of recorded VDZ IV doses received
- Dosing interval: defined as frequency of VDZ IV administration (in weeks) based on recorded data. This was calculated as the time between VDZ IV dose administrations based on what was recorded in the ICN data.

## Timing of recorded VDZ IV Doses

- The timing of recorded VDZ IV doses during the 54-week follow-up period were described for up to eight doses. Specifically, the intervals between consecutive doses were reported, starting from dose 1 (index date) to dose 2, dose 2 to dose 3, and continuing through dose 8.

## Sample size

Given the retrospective, descriptive nature of this study, which contemplates the use of secondary data from over 100 clinical centres, no patient recruitment was conducted as part of this study; moreover, for the same reasons and the absence of comparisons between cohorts, no formal power analysis was required as well as hypothesis testing, as the study required a descriptive analysis. However, a precision analysis was performed to assess the adequacy of the sample size in producing results with sufficient statistical precision.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Not applicable.

## Statistical Methods

Continuous variables were summarized using means, medians, and SDs, and categorical variables were summarized using frequency counts and proportions. Time to VDZ IV treatment discontinuation was described using Kaplan-Meier (KM) analysis, and patients who did not have evidence of treatment discontinuation were censored at the last recorded ICN visit. All analyses were descriptive, and no statistical testing was performed.

- Methods for evaluation of missing data: Analyses were conducted to assess the potential impact of missing data on clinical effectiveness outcomes. Baseline characteristics were summarized for patients with and without a Week 54 disease activity score to evaluate potential differences associated with missingness. In addition, post hoc analyses applied a non-responder imputation (NRI) approach, whereby patients with missing Week 54 outcome data were classified as not achieving clinical response or clinical remission.

## Discussion on clinical effectiveness aspects

ICN Study is a non-interventional study, designed to evaluate vedolizumab intravenous use in paediatric patients aged 2 to &lt;18 years with Ulcerative Colitis (UC) or Crohn Disease (CD) using the ICN registry, a large, multicentre, longitudinal data source that captures data from more than 30,000 paediatric IBD patients across over 100 US centres. The aim of this study is to generate evidence on intravenous Vedolizumab (VDZ IV) use in paediatric IBD in a real-world setting; the consequent objectives were focused on providing descriptive information about patient demographics, treatment patterns, clinical outcomes, and healthcare resource use (HRU) in the primary analysis population and by age and weight subgroups. In general, the information from a real world setting is considered diriment, because it provides useful data about subpopulation of subjects of lower age (&gt; 2 &lt; 13 years) and weight (&lt; 30 kg), usually underrepresented in clinical trials.

A total of N=1,473 patients for each cohort (UC and CD) were identified in the ICN registry with reported use of VDZ IV in the U.S. between February 1, 2015, and February 1, 2025, with the date of first recorded VDZ IV treatment defined as the index date. Overall, analyses were conducted on 174 patients with moderate-to-severe UC and 126 with moderate-to-severe CD, which presented all the inclusion criteria requested for protocol analyses (diagnosis of CD or UC at ICN enrolment, at least one visit prior to index, disease activity score available). The number of subjects aged between ≥2 to &lt;13 years was 73 and 53 in the UC and CD groups, respectively, while subjects &lt; 30 kg were 24.1% and 23%, respectively. Overall, the number of subjects ultimately included in the analysis exceeded the estimated number of 70- 89 patients who was initially required for the analysis, but this is welcome in view of an increase in the representativeness of the sample. In general, the study methodology can be considered acceptable, although studies like this are prone to left-truncation biases, especially if patients entered the ICN registry after having already initiated VDZ IV therapy, as their true treatment start might not have been observed. To mitigate this, the study required patients to have at least one visit prior to the index date without VDZ IV use and no VDZ IV use before February 1, 2015, which served as a \"washout\" period to ensure that all included patients had a documented registry visit before initiating therapy, reducing the risk of left-truncation bias.

At baseline, mean (SD) age at diagnosis was 11.3 (4.1) for UC and 9.4 (3.5) years for CD, respectively; male and female were well balanced. Most patients were White (75.3%) and non-Hispanic (86.8%). Nutritional status was satisfactory for most patients (71.8%) as well as for growth status (80.5%) for UC, while in CD cohort the percentage were lower (59.5% and 73.8% respectively). All subjects available for the analysis presented active and mild-moderate disease: most of subjects with UC have pancolitis (73.6%), with a mean (SD) baseline PUCAI score was 50.8 (13.8), consistent with moderate activity. For CD-specific disease characteristics, the large majority (88.9%) of patients exhibited an inflammatory, non-penetrating, non-stricturing phenotype; based on the baseline PGA, 57.9% of patients had moderate disease activity and 6.3% had severe disease. The mean (SD) sPCDAI score was 43.8 (12.0) at baseline. In both groups, laboratory markers reflected active inflammation and most of them presented a previous exposure to corticosteroids in the large majority of subjects; more than half of subjects was previously exposed to infliximab in both cohorts, while use of adalimumab was less frequent.

As expected, subjects in the lower-weight subgroup presented a more common impairment in the nutritional and growth.

In the UC group, the availability of the PUCAI score at baseline throughout the observation period guided the evaluation of clinical effectiveness: in general, although the percentage of subjects for which the PUCAI score progressively declines during observation (69.5% at week 14, 63.8% at Week 54), the global number of subjects available for the analysis are considered sufficient for a descriptive analysis. In the whole population, percentage of clinical response remained stable between week 14 and 54, while a progressive increase in the clinical remissions can be observed throughout the follow-up. To note, the percentages of clinical remissions and responses appeared to be numerically higher in subjects aged ≥2 to &lt;13 years (n = 73), although the small number of subjects included in this subgroup limits the strength of the evidence. No substantial difference can be noted between the two weight group (&lt; 30 kg and &gt; 30 kg), except for a nominally higher rate of clinical remission at week 14 in favour of subjects &lt; 30 kg. Post hoc analyses were conducted to better understand the potential impact of missing data on clinical effectiveness outcomes; subjects with missing data on response at week 14 and 54 were classified as non -responders. When the Non Responder Imputation (NRI) is applied, the percentages of clinical remission and response at week 14 were 25.3% (44/174) and 51.7% (90/174), respectively, and 35.1% (61/174) and 50.6% (88/174) achieved a clinical response.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Similarly to what stated for the UC cohort, in the CD group (126 subjects) the effectiveness evaluation is subjected to the availability of the sPCDAI score during the observation period: data were available for more than half of subjects, 66.7% (n =84) of patients had an available sPCDAI score at Week 14 (±6 weeks), and 52.4% (n = 66) patients had a score at Week 54 (±12 weeks). Also in this arm, a progressive increase in the percentages of clinical remissions can be noted at week 54 in respect to week 14 (48.5% and 33.3%, respectively), as well as for clinical responses (66.7% and 78.8%, respectively). The analysis per age subgroups showed similar remission and response rates by week 54 and numerically higher remission and response rates were observed among patients aged ≥13 to &lt;18 years relative to those aged ≥2 to &lt;13 years. At Week 14, remission and response rates were comparable across weight subgroups. By Week 54, both remission and response rates appeared numerically higher among patients weighing ≥30 kg relative to those weighing &lt;30 kg. In both cases, the small number of subjects does not allow definitive conclusions to be drawn. The post-hoc analysis using the NRI approach(all patients were included in the denominator, regardless of whether they had an available disease activity score within the relevant timeframe), showed, as expected, lower rates of responders (22.2% and 44.4% of clinical remission and clinical response at week 14 and 25,4% and 41,3% at week 54, respectively).

The progressive disease control during the observation period was confirmed also by the changes of the laboratory outcomes associated with disease activity (mean albumin and CRP value) in both cohorts.

The Hospital Resource Utilization represents a major issue in subjects with IBD, because it has an impact on the Quality of Life of subjects and on healthcare system utilization; in general, the rates of all causes hospitalization resulted higher in the CD cohort in respect to the UC. In both groups, IBD related complications were the main cause of hospitalization for both groups. Considering that the analysed population included subjects with moderate-to-severe disease, this finding is expected.

In this non-interventional study, more than half of subjects continued to receive intravenous vedolizumab (VDZ IV) at week 54 for both groups; at week 102, 54.8% of subjects with UC and 46.6% of subjects with CD were on treatment. Median time to VDZ IV discontinuation was 129.1 weeks in UC; similar results were obtained in both the age groups, while a numerical higher median time to discontinuation can be observed in the weight group &lt; 30 kg. Among the overall UC cohort (n = 174), the mean (SD) dosage of the index (first) VDZ IV was 275.9 (57.4) mg, with the most frequent dosage category being 300 mg, administered to 79.3% of patients. The median number of VDZ IV doses was 4.0, and was similar across all subgroups.

In the CD group, the median time to VDZ IV discontinuation time resulted more heterogeneous in the subgroup analysis; in the whole population it resulted to be of 76.6 weeks, with longer median time registered in the age group &gt; 13 to &lt; 18 years and in subjects weighting &gt; 30 kg, while in patients &lt; 30 kg it resulted shorter than the median one reported in the general analysis. In all subgroup but the one with weight &lt; 30 kg, more than half of subjects resulted in treatment at 54 weeks. The median number of VDZ IV doses was reduced in subjects aged &gt; 2 &lt; 13 years and weighting &lt; 30 kg. In general, the variability noted in the CD cohort could be a direct consequence of the heterogeneous clinical behaviour of the CD disease, although the descriptive nature of this study and the small numbers of subjects impaired the strength of these evidences.

Overall, information provided by this study is considered of relevance, given that the enrolled population came from the ICN registry, which encompasses a diverse network of institutions across the United States; moreover, the relatively long-term follow-up provides information about drug use during time. In addition to that, a relatively high number of subjects aged &gt;2 to &lt;18 years with IBD were included in this analysis: considering that this population is typically underrepresented in clinical trials, information obtained by this descriptive analysis is considered relevant for providing useful insights about treatment patterns in this subgroup of subjects; similar considerations can be applied to subjects weighting &lt; 30 kg.

As reported before, although this study is considered informative, some limitations should be outlined: first, the retrospective nature of Study ICN reduced the strength of evidence to an observational level, making it difficult to draw definitive conclusions; in addition to that, the relatively high rate of missing activity scores (47.6% in CD and 36.2% in UC) necessarily had an impact on the global strength of the results; this bias is further complicated by a two pre-specified windows for efficacy evaluation at 14 (+

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

6) and 54 (+ 12) weeks, which appears too far from each other, with the consequent risk of missing clinically important events. Lastly, a possible effect of missing data on the treatment administration and adherence cannot be excluded, considering that the median number of doses of vedolizumab resulted lower than expected, and this appear to be not in line with the moderate-severe grade of disease included in this analysis. In respect to the median time to vedolizumab discontinuation, a possible effect of the left-truncation cannot be entirely excluded for those subjects who entered the study after having already started vedolizumab before the enrolment, although some mitigation strategies were made in place. Finally, the data generalizability could be questioned too, because, despite the relative high number of clinically informative subjects, the study was entirely conducted in US; in particular, given the difference in terms of healthcare system organization between EU and US and its impact on treatment compliance and utilization of resources, the transferability of the results of this study cannot be considered immediate to the EU setting.

## 3. Rapporteur's CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

In conclusion, results from ICN study are considered clinically relevant, as they provide useful information about the real world practice of VDZ use in IBD pediatric subjects, especially for those categories of subjects usually underrepresented in clinical trials; however, the presence of several limitations intrinsic in a observational, long term study of real world practice, does not allow firm conclusions to be drawn. Therefore, no changes in SmPC are warranted.
