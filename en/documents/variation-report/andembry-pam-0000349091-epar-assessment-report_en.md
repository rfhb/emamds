These data are also submitted as part of the post-authorisation measures (PAM).

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that study CSL312\\_3003: Phase 3 Open-label Study to Evaluate the Safety, Pharmacokinetics, Pharmacodynamics, and Efficacy of CSL312 (Garadacimab) in the Prophylactic Treatment of Hereditary Angioedema in Pediatric Subjects 2 to 11 Years of Age is a standalone study.

The MAH stated that study CSL312\\_3003 is a part of pediatric clinical development program. The extension application consisting of the full relevant data package (i.e containing several studies) is expected to be submitted by November 2026. A line listing of all the concerned studies is annexed.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

The study product was supplied as a combination product that includes 2 separate products, biologic / device (ie, CSL312 [100 mg] / needle safety device), produced as a single entity. CSL312 at a concentration of 100 mg/mL was supplied in a 2.25 mL ready-to-use staked-in-needle prefillable glass syringe with a rigid needle shield and syringe plunger. The prefilled syringe is assembled with a plunger rod and embedded in a needle safety device and an extended finger flange, which are not in contact with the liquid.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## Introduction

## Clinical study CSL312\\_3003

The MAH submitted a final report(s) for Study CSL312\\_3003: Phase 3 Open-label Study to Evaluate the Safety, Pharmacokinetics, Pharmacodynamics, and Efficacy of CSL312 (Garadacimab) in the Prophylactic Treatment of Hereditary Angioedema in Pediatric Subjects 2 to 11 Years of Age

## Description

This was a multicenter, open-label, parallel-arm, phase 3b pediatric study designed to investigate the safety, PK / PD, and efficacy of SC administered CSL312 in the prophylactic treatment of HAE in CSL312-naïve pediatric subjects 2 to 11 years of age with C1-INH HAE type 1 or 2.

The study consisted of a Screening Period (up to 1 month), a Treatment Period (at least 12 months), and a Follow-up Period (ie, 3 months after the last investigational product administration). Subjects had the option to continue to receive CSL312 (if they met the criteria to participate in a post-study access program) or another prophylactic treatment without completing the Follow-up Visit. The study design is shown in Figure 1.

C1-INH HAE = hereditary angioedema with C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency.

The Follow-up Visit could have been conducted via telephone instead of at study site. Assessments / tests were omitted if the Follow-up Visit was conducted via telephone. The Follow-up Period in this study was not applicable for subjects who chose to continue to receive CSL312 (and met the criteria to participate in a post-study access program) or another prophylactic treatment. Subjects who did not choose the post-study access program or another prophylactic treatment completed the Follow-up Visit as mandated in this study.

The study included children with HAE with an attack rate of at least 2 attacks in the last 6 months, as reflected in their medical records. The HAE disease in children is similar to adults / adolescents in clinical presentation and pathophysiology; however, the disease in prepubertal children is typically milder with less frequent and severe symptoms (Aygören-Pürsün and Andarawewa, 2019). Therefore, the attack rate criteria for the patient population of children 2 to 11 years of age with HAE are lower than the attack rate required in previous adult and adolescent studies to align with the respective disease activity expected in this age group and to accurately capture the patient population.

## Methods

## Study participants

## Main Inclusion Criteria

To be enrolled into the study, subjects had to meet all the following inclusion criteria:

1. Aged 2 to 11 years, inclusive, with body weight ≥ 10th percentile based on age, at the time of providing written informed consent / assent.
2. Diagnosed with clinically confirmed C1-INH HAE:

a. Documented clinical history consistent with HAE (SC or mucosal, nonpruritic swelling episodes without accompanying urticaria), and

b. C1-INH antigen and / or functional activity ≤ 50% of normal as documented in the subject's medical records, and

c. C4 antigen concentration below the lower limit of the reference range as documented in the subject's medical records.

3. Experienced ≥ 2 HAE attacks during the 6 months before Screening

Note 1: For subjects taking any prophylactic HAE therapy during the 6 months before Screening, ≥ 2 HAE attacks may have been documented over 6 months before commencing the prophylactic therapy.

Note 2: If clinical confirmation of C1-INH HAE in the medical records was from 5 years or more ago (for subjects 6 to 11 years of age) or not available (for subjects 2 to 5 years of age), a blood sample to confirm the HAE disease was collected at Screening.

## Exclusion criteria:

1. Concomitant diagnosis of another form of angioedema, such as idiopathic or acquired angioedema, recurrent angioedema associated with urticaria, or HAE type 3.
2. Any preplanned major surgeries or procedures during the clinical study.
3. Used C1-INH products, androgens, antifibrinolytics, approved or future approved medications, or other small-molecule medications for routine prophylaxis against HAE attacks within a minimum of 2 weeks before the Treatment Period.
4. Participated in another interventional clinical study during the 30 days before the Treatment Period or within 5 half-lives of the final dose of the investigational product administered during the previous interventional study, whichever was longer.
5. Known or suspected hypersensitivity to mAb therapy or hypersensitivity to the investigational product or to any excipients of the investigational product.
6. Had laboratory clinical abnormalities assessed as clinically significant by the investigator

Note: Subjects with ALT or AST 2 × upper limit of normal were eligible for participation if the results were not clinically significant.

7. Subject had any condition that might have compromised their safety or compliance, impede successful conduct of the study, interfere with interpretation of the results, or would otherwise render the subject unsuitable for participation in the study, eg, significant illnesses or major comorbidities, such as a preexisting condition of coagulopathy or thrombotic disorders

## Treatments

Eligible subjects were assigned to open-label CSL312 administered SC for at least 12 months with a dosing regimen dependent on their age at the time of consent as follows:

- 2 to 5 years of age: 100 mg every 2 months (Q2M)
- 6 to 11 years of age: 100 mg once monthly (Q1M)

If a subject turned 6 years of age during the study, the dosing regimen remained unchanged and the subject was analyzed in the age group according to the subject's age at the time of consent.

Dose modifications were not permitted during the study.

Anatomical Location: Abdomen, upper arm, or thigh.

## Objective(s)

The primary objectives of the study were to evaluate the safety and pharmacokinetics (PK) of subcutaneous (SC) administration of CSL312 in the prophylactic treatment of pediatric subjects with C1-INH HAE.

The secondary objectives of this study were to evaluate the efficacy, pharmacodynamics (PD), and safety of CSL312 in the prophylactic treatment of pediatric subjects with C1-INH HAE.

The exploratory objective of the study was to further evaluate the PD effects of CSL312.

## Sample size

No formal sample size calculation is made, and the sample size is not based on formal statistical considerations. The study would enroll a prespecified number of subjects as agreed with health authorities. The target sample size for this study was approximately 20 subjects to ensure that at least 15 subjects complete 12 months of treatment.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

This was an open-label study, and all study personnel and study subjects were unblinded.

An Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC) was established to monitor the safe conduct of the study. The IDMC consisted of independent clinical specialists in HAE, who have experience in clinical studies. The IDMC reviewed the safety data at planned intervals and provided recommendations regarding study conduct matters that affect safety. No success or futility thresholds were set for the IDMC reviews. There were 2 pediatric IDMC meetings conducted during the study. No safety concerns were identified, and the IDMC recommended that the study continue as planned.

## Statistical Methods

## Analysis of the Primary Endpoints

Safety:

The study assessed safety by monitoring treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) during subcutaneous CSL312 prophylaxis in pediatric HAE patients from Day 1 through end of treatment (or last visit). Results were summarized using the Safety Analysis Set, including the number and percentage of patients with TEAEs, total events, and rates per exposure year and injection. Analyses covered overall TEAEs, treatment/device-related events, injection site reactions, events within 24 hours, discontinuations, severity, outcomes, serious TEAEs, and hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis). A sensitivity analysis excluded COVID-19-related TEAEs.

## PK (Pharmacokinetics):

PK parameters were estimated using noncompartmental methods and summarized descriptively by dosing regimen. Key parameters included Cmax, Ctrough, and Tmax. Statistics (e.g., mean, median, SD, CV%, min/max) were reported for all parameters (with limited metrics for Tmax). Ctrough was also summarized by visit, and individual subject data listings were provided.

## Analysis of the Secondary Endpoints

Efficacy:

Efficacy was assessed by summarizing the time-normalized number of HAE attacks (per month and year), focusing on median values (primary) and mean values (secondary) with 95% CIs. Analyses included attacks requiring on-demand treatment and moderate/severe attacks. Use of on-demand medications was summarized by frequency and proportion of subjects. Response rates were reported, with responders defined as ≥50% reduction in attack rate versus historical data; higher thresholds (≥70% and ≥90%) were also presented with 95% CIs.

## Safety:

TEAEs were summarized descriptively through the follow-up visit, including counts, percentages, total events, and rates per exposure year and injection. The presence of anti-CSL312 antibodies was also summarized by study visit.

## PD (Pharmacodynamics):

FXIIa-mediated kallikrein activity was analyzed per subject and summarized by time point and dosing regimen. Descriptive statistics included counts, proportions below quantification limits, central tendency and variability measures (mean, median, SD, CV%), ranges, quartiles, and geometric mean with 90% CI.

## Primary Safety Estimand

The primary safety interest was to assess whether TEAEs occurred at least once during SC administration of CSL312 in the prophylactic treatment of pediatric subjects with C1-INH HAE during treatment from the first investigational product administration at Day 1 Visit through the End of Treatment Visit or the latest available visit or event (whichever was later) for subjects without the End of Treatment Visit while subjects were allowed to treat HAE attacks with on-demand medications.

The primary safety estimand, in line with the primary safety interest of the study, followed the treatment policy strategy and is described as follows:

- Treatment condition: Q1M or Q2M treatment with 100 mg CSL312

## Analysis of Primary Pharmacokinetic Endpoint

PK parameters were derived using noncompartmental PK analyses and summarized descriptively by dosing regimen (ie, 100 mg Q2M, 100 mg Q1M). PK parameters included Cmax, Ctrough, and Tmax. The following descriptive statistics were presented for all PK parameters, except for Tmax: n, mean, SD, CV%, median, geometric mean, geometric CV%, minimum, and maximum. For Tmax, n, median, minimum, and maximum were provided. Ctrough values were summarized descriptively by study visit. By-subject listings were provided for the PK parameters.

## Handling of Missing Data

Missing efficacy data were not imputed, as no formal statistical analysis was done. All subjects who dropped out within the first 30 days of the Treatment Period were regarded as missing for all efficacy analysis.

There were no imputation of partial or complete missing dates.

Where AE start dates and / or times were missing or partially missing, AEs were assumed to be treatment-emergent, except if the partial start dates and / or times or the AE end date and / or time indicated that the AE started before the first administration of investigational product. If AE start dates or end dates were missing or partially missing for an AE, no duration was calculated. If for a TEAE the relationship to investigational product was missing, the worst case was assumed for summarizing analysis (ie, the relationship to investigational product was assumed to be \"Related\"). In case of missing relationship to investigational product for non-TEAEs, the relationship was set to \"Not related.\"

## Discussion on clinical aspects

Garadacimab is approved for routine prevention of recurrent attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE) in adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older. The approved posology is initial loading dose of 400 mg administered subcutaneously followed by a monthly dose of 200 mg.

The MAH presented a final report of a multicenter, open-label, parallel-arm, phase 3b pediatric study designed to investigate the safety, PK / PD, and efficacy of SC administered CSL312 in the prophylactic treatment of HAE in CSL312-naïve pediatric subjects 2 to 11 years of age with C1-INH HAE type 1 or 2. The study included a Treatment Period (at least 12 months), and a Follow-up Period (ie, 3 months after the last investigational product administration).

The primary objectives of the study were to evaluate the safety and PK of subcutaneous (SC) administration of CSL312 in the prophylactic treatment of pediatric subjects with C1-INH HAE, while the secondary objectives were to evaluate the efficacy, PD and safety. The target sample size for this study, agreed with health authorities, was approximately 20 subjects to ensure that at least 15 subjects complete 12 months of treatment.

The subjects enrolled needed to have a clinically confirmed C1-INH HAE (clinical history, C1-INH antigen and / or functional activity ≤ 50% of normal and low C4 antigen).

The subjects had to have experienced ≥ 2 HAE attacks during the 6 months before Screening. For subjects taking any prophylactic HAE therapy during the 6 months before Screening, ≥ 2 HAE attacks may have been documented over 6 months before commencing the prophylactic therapy. This is acceptable. Of note, the eligibility criteria in the pivotal study in adults and adolescents included ≥ 3 HAE attacks during the 3 months before Screening and at least an average of 1 HAE attack per month during the Run-in Period. The HAE disease in children is similar to adults / adolescents in clinical presentation and pathophysiology; however, the disease in prepubertal children is typically milder with less frequent and severe symptoms (Aygören-Pürsün and Andarawewa, 2019). Therefore, the attack rate criteria for the patient population of children 2 to 11 years of age with HAE are lower than the attack rate required in previous adult and adolescent studies to align with the respective disease activity expected in this age group and to accurately capture the patient population.

Eligible subjects were assigned to open-label CSL312 administered SC for at least 12 months with a dosing regimen dependent on their age at the time of consent as follows: 2 to 5 years of age: 100 mg every 2 months (Q2M); 6 to 11 years of age: 100 mg once monthly (Q1M). Of note, differently from adult/adolescent posology, no loading dose is foreseen. This approach should be discussed and justified in the incoming type II variation.

A total of 22 subjects were enrolled and received treatment, including 6 subjects in the CSL312 100 mg Q2M dosing regimen and 16 subjects in the CSL312 100 mg Q1M dosing regimen. All 22 subjects had 12 months of exposure, completed treatment, and completed the study. No protocol deviations led to exclusion of a subject or subject's data from any analysis set or were considered violations of inclusion and exclusion criteria.

The majority of subjects were male (17 [77.3%] subjects) and White (21 [95.5%] subjects). The mean (SD) overall age of all subjects was 7.7 (2.88) years, ranging from 2 to 11 years. All 22 subjects had a biochemically confirmed diagnosis of HAE; 19 (86.4%) subjects had a diagnosis of C1-INH HAE type I and 3 (13.6%) subjects had a diagnosis of C1-INH HAE type II. This is in line with epidemiological data and with respective adult population included in the pivotal studies. The majority of subjects had a family history of HAE (18 [81.8%] subjects), and 5 (22.7%) subjects had a history of laryngeal attack.

The treatment compliance was high, 100% in 20 subjects, 91.7% in 1 subject, and 83.3% in 1 subject.

The PK parameters following SC administration of CSL312 at steady state (Cmax, Ctrough, and Tmax) were among the two primary endpoints of this study.

Cmax and Tmax appear comparable between the 100 mg Q2M and 100 mg Q1M garadacimab regimens, although a high degree of variability was observed, with the geometric CV for Cmax reaching up to 76.6% with Q1M administration.

While Tmax is consistent with adult data, Cmax media values appear to be higher for both pediatric dosing regimens, to note that the approved dosage for adults is 400 mg garadacimab loading dose followed by 200 mg Q1M. Any differences should be investigated and clarified for a future EoI, especially if an exposure matching approach is intended to be used.

Ctrough values reached in the two pediatric groups' are very different, as consequence of the different dosing frequency, thus only the Q1M administration lead to a consistent trend with adult values; however it is not clear which minimum Ctrough values (including the high variability observed in the present pediatric study) should be maintained, or more in general which PK parameters could be used as parameter driven efficacy. In order to better elucidate the PK profile in pediatric patients, AUC data should be analysed too.

A more comprehensive discussion of dose proportionality is expected.

In the study CSL312\\_3003 report, it is stated that \"A population PK model was developed to characterize the PK of CSL312 based on pooled data from previous clinical studies. PK simulations following 100 mg Q1M and 100 mg Q2M in these age groups suggest overall comparable steady-state exposures to adults following 200 mg Q1M dosing, which is the phase 3 dose in adults and adolescents.\" However, results from popPK model have not be provided make not feasible draw any conclusion.

Overall, given the small sample size and substantial variability, firm conclusions on selected doses cannot be drawn, particularly considering data about Ctrough parameter and the lack of AUC parameter. Therefore, the rationale for dose selection based on exposure matching to adult plasma concentrations remains uncertain. Further analysis to support the selected dose is expected at the time of the EoI application.

The safety profile of garadacimab in pediatric subjects is based on Study CSL312\\_3003. The study enrolled a total of 22 patients (6 in 100 mg Q2M dosage and 16 in 100 mg Q1M dosage). The overall median (range) duration of exposure was 11.91 (11.8, 12.2).

The primary safety analysis included treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) until the End of Treatment Visit. No additional TEAEs were reported in the secondary safety analysis (until the Followup Visit).

72.7% of subjects experienced TEAEs, of which only 4 (18.2%) were related, with the majority (14 TEAEs in 3 subjects) being not serious ISRs. The frequency of TEAEs seems to be higher in CSL312 100 mg Q2M (83.3%) than in CSL312 100 mg Q1M (68.8%), mainly driven by infections and infestations, which is unexpected. However, the low number of patients preclude any firm conclusion.

One subject in the CSL312 100 mg Q1M dosing regimen had a TEAE of Activated partial thromboplastin time prolonged that was moderate in severity.

Most TEAEs were mild (59.1%) or moderate (36.4%) in severity. There were 3 severe TEAEs reported in 2 (9.1%) subjects in the CSL312 100 mg Q1M dosing regimen, which were assessed as not related to study treatment: one subject had a serious event of Skull fracture and one experienced a wrist fracture (recovered/resolved,) and an event of Henoch-Schonlein purpura (not recovered/not resolved).

There were no deaths or TEAEs leading to study treatment or study discontinuation.

The most frequently (≥ 10% of subjects overall) reported TEAE by PT was Upper respiratory tract infection (8 [36.4%]), with similar incidence between the 2 dosing regimens (33.3% vs 37.5% of subjects).

There were no AESIs (severe hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis) during the study as per protocol. However, 10 events in 4 (18.2%) subjects were identified by SMQ search (hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis) for further review (cought, conjunctivitis, asthma, Henoch-Schonlein purpura). The MAH states that none of the events were assessed as related to study treatment or hypersensitivity reactions. However, a further discussion on their potential causality with study drug, including time of onset of the events, is expected in the incoming type II variation.
