## 1. Introduction

On 17 March 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Sogroya, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

Study NN8640-4263 is not part of a paediatric investigation plan as Sogroya (somapacitan) has been granted a PIP waiver (EMEA-001469-PIP01-13). However, the trial is part of the development program for Sogroya in children with growth hormone deficiency (GHD).

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that study NN8640-4263, \"A trial comparing the effect and safety of once weekly dosing of somapacitan with daily Norditropin in children with growth hormone deficiency\", is a standalone study.

Study NN8640-4263 was comprised of two periods: a 52-week main trial period and followed by a 3year safety extension period. After completion of the safety extension (week 208), subjects in countries where somapacitan was not available for prescription for children with GHD were offered to continue the trial and receive treatment with somapacitan until somapacitan became available for prescription in the subject's respective country or until September 2025 at the latest. The total trial duration for a subject up to the end of the safety extension was 4 years.

The Marketing Authorisation Holder (MAH) submitted the data from the main trial period of study NN8640-4263 (CSR dated 18 March 2022) on 21 June 2022 as part of a grouped line extension procedure EMEA/H/C/005030/X/0006/G to extend the indication of Sogroya to children (aged 3 years and older) and adolescents with GHD. These data have therefore been assessed previously.

The 3-year extension trial period was a single-arm, open-label extension period with once weekly dosing of somapacitan to evaluate safety. The extension period until somapacitan is available for prescription was included to ensure that subjects finishing the trial before somapacitan is available for prescription can continue treatment. The extension part is the focus of the current procedure.

The current assessment performed focusses on the results of the extension period with a potential impact on the BR, SmPC and/or RMP of Sogroya. Information related to the 52-week main trial period will not be assessed or discussed.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Children with GHD were randomized (2:1) to receive either once-weekly somapacitan 0.16 mg/kg/week or daily Norditropin 0.034 mg/kg/day for the 52-week main trial period. After the 52week main period, all subjects are allocated to once-weekly somapacitan 0.16 mg/kg/week.

## Description

The Phase 3 study NN8640-4263, further abbreviated to study 4263, is a randomised open-labelled two arm study designed to compare the efficacy and safety of once-weekly dosing of somapacitan with daily dosing of somatropin after 52 weeks in children with GHD, followed by a 3-year single-arm extension period with once weekly dosing of somapacitan to evaluate safety (Figure 1).

Two hundred (200) study patients were randomised in a 2:1 manner to receive either somapacitan or somatropin medicinal product Norditropin during the main 52-week period. Fixed doses of somapacitan (0.16 mg/kg/week) and somatropin (0.034 mg/kg/day) were used throughout the first 52 weeks. After

## Study participants

Eligible were (all required):

- prepubertal children
- confirmed diagnosis of growth hormone deficiency determined by two different growth hormone stimulation tests performed within 12 months prior to randomisation, defined as a peak growth hormone level of ≤ 10.0 ng/ml using the WHO International Somatropin 98/574 standard,
- FOR JAPAN ONLY: Confirmed diagnosis of growth hormone deficiency within 12 months prior to screening as determined by one growth hormone stimulation test for patients with intracranial organic disease or symptomatic hypoglycaemia and two different growth hormone stimulation tests for other study patients defined as peak growth hormone level of ≤ 6 ng/ml by assay using recombinant growth hormone standard.

## Treatments

After the 52-week main period, all subjects are allocated to once-weekly somapacitan 0.16 mg/kg/week.

Dose reduction in consecutive steps of 25% of the current dose could be considered at the investigator's discretion for adverse events judged as probably related to study product by the investigator. If the adverse event persisted, the study patient could be discontinued and if the adverse event resolved, the dose could then be resumed to the original planned dose at the investigator's discretion. If measures of IGF-I SDS exceeded +2.5 SDS at two consecutive visits, the investigator was informed by the applicant. Dose reduction was then to be done by a 25% reduction of the current dose.

## CHMP comment

Dose reductions due to adverse events and/or IGF-I levels exceeding +2 SDS reflect the common treatment practice. This is acceptable.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Study patients were randomised in a 2:1 manner to receive either somapacitan or somatropin treatment, using a central interactive web response system.

To ensure equal distribution of important prognostic factors across treatments, the randomisation was stratified by:

- region (Japan versus rest-of-the-world)
- age group (&lt; 6 versus ≥ 6 years at randomisation)
- gender (boys versus girls)
- growth hormone peak level (&lt; 7.0 versus ≥ 7.0 ng/ml).

After the main study period of 52 weeks, all study patients initially randomised to somatropin were switched to treatment with somapacitan.

This study was open-label. The study was observer-blinded with regard to primary endpoint, i.e., the person performing the height measurements was blinded to treatment allocation. Novo Nordisk staff involved in the interpretation of all data was kept blinded during the study conduct until the database lock for the primary endpoint.

## CHMP comment

The fact that this study is a single-arm, open label study does hamper a thorough assessment of the safety profile.

## Statistical Methods

The following analysis sets were defined in the protocol, prior to unblinding, and in accordance with ICH E924:

- Full analysis set (FAS): all randomised subjects. Subjects will be analysed \"as randomised\".
- Safety analysis set (SAS): all randomised subjects exposed to at least one dose of randomised treatment. Subjects will be analysed \"as treated\".

The following two observation periods were defined in the trial for the extension period up to week 208:

- on-treatment: from first administration and up until last trial contact, visit 19 (week 208) or 14 days after last administration, whichever comes first.
- in-trial: from first administration and up until visit 19 (week 208) or last trial contact, whichever comes first.

Descriptive statistics for HV, HVSDS, HSDS, bone age up to week 208 (including change from baseline) are included in the current submission.

- IGFBP-3 SDS (-10 to +10)

## Results

## Participant flow

Main trial period

In total, 348 children with GHD were screened and 200 (100%) subjects were randomised and exposed to treatment. Of these, 132 subjects were exposed to somapacitan and 68 to Norditropin®. All 200 subjects completed the 52-week main trial period, and 199 (99.5%) subjects completed the 52-week main treatment period. 1 subject (somapacitan group) discontinued treatment due to a violation of inclusion/exclusion criteria. This subject was withdrawn from the trial after week 52.

Safety extension trial period (week 52 to 208)

A total of 199 subjects were exposed in the safety extension (Table 1). 11 subjects in the somapacitan/somapacitan and 6 subjects in the Norditropin®/somapacitan group discontinued trial product in the safety extension period (from week 52 to 208). Of the 11 subjects in the somapacitan/somapacitan group, 4 subjects were lost to follow up and 7 subjects discontinued due to reason reported as 'other'. None of the discontinuations were due to AEs. All discontinued subjects in the extension period were withdrawn from the trial.

Additional safety extension period (after week 208)

A total of 103 subjects (51.5%) continued in the additional safety extension period (Table 1). 93 subjects (46.5%) from both treatment groups discontinued the trial product as somapacitan was available for prescribing in their country. 1 subject in the somapacitan/somapacitan group discontinued due to AE and 1 subject discontinued due to physician's decision.

## Safety results

The safety evaluation is based on the safety analysis set including all subjects exposed (200 subjects, 100%).

Data for AEs with onset in the main trial period (week 0-52) or the safety extension period (week 52208) or the additional safety extension period (week 208-312) are presented. For the on-treatment population, the following AEs are included in the analyses with the following definitions:

- For weeks 0-52, AEs with onset after the first administration of trial product and up until 14 days after last trial drug administration or until visit 7 whichever comes first.
- For weeks 52-208, AEs with onset from visit 7 until 14 days after last trial drug administration or until visit 19 whichever comes first.
- For weeks 208-312, AEs with onset from visit 19 until 14 days after last trial drug administration or until visit 28 whichever comes first.

For subjects switching from Norditropin to somapacitan, AEs were assigned to the treatment group at onset of the AE, and if the onset was after the switch to somapacitan, the investigator should consider if the AE should be assigned to both Norditropin and somapacitan. ## Discussion on clinical aspects

This submission concerns the final study report of study NN8640-4263, a phase 3, randomised openlabelled two arm study. Study NN8640-4263 was comprised of two periods: a 52-week main trial period and followed by a 3-year safety extension period. The study was conducted to support an extension of indication to children (aged 3 years and older) and adolescents with GHD. The Marketing Authorisation Holder (MAH) submitted the data from the main trial period of study NN8640-4263 (CSR dated 18 March 2022) to support the extension of indication, which was assessed during procedure EMEA/H/C/005030/X/0006/G. The safety extension part of the study was still ongoing.

The current assessment performed focusses on the results of the safety extension period with a potential impact on the BR, SmPC and/or RMP of Sogroya. Information previously submitted related to the 52-week main trial period will not be re-assessed or discussed.

The population included in the study consists of growth hormone treatment-naïve pre-pubertal paediatric GHD patients aged 2.5 years up to 10 years (girls) or 11 years (boys). The additional data regarding growth, pharmacodynamic effects and safety data over 3 additional years is appreciated.

Although the fact that this study is a single-arm, open label study does hamper a thorough assessment of the safety profile. The observed clinical effects with respect to growth-related parameters, IGF-I SDS levels and IGFPB-3 SDS levels indicate that the growth promoting effects of somapacitan persist up to a follow-up time of 3 years, although at a lower rate which is expected after an initial catch up. After the additional 3 years, data became sparse. Although again no clear differences between the somapacitan/somapacitan group and the norditropin/somapacitan group were observed. In total 182 patients completed the safety extension period. Of these, 132 patients were included in the somapacitan/somapacitan group and received somapacitan for 4 years and 60 patients were included in the norditropin group during the 52-week main-trail period after which they were switched to somapacitan and treated for an additional 3 years. Exposure during the additional safety extension period was variable. Overall, 92% of patients in the somapacitan/somapacitan group experienced an adverse event, which were mostly mild to moderate in intensity and resolved without sequela. The most frequently reported AEs were consistent with data observed during the 52-week main trial period and are linked to common childhood events. The most frequently reported possibly or probably related AEs, are already included in the SmPC of Sogroya. However, next to the AEs reported in Table 8, the following AEs were considered possibly or probably related to Norditropin or somapacitan and were reported in &gt;2 subjects in the safety extension period: growing pains (n=2), abdominal pain (n=2), lipoatrophy (n=5). Based on the results presented in the summary of AEs in the safety focus area, the majority of AEs reported there are already included in the SmPC or are linked to common childhood events (e.g. vomiting). However, 4 AEs of lipodystrophy were experienced by 2 patients, 7 AEs of lipoatrophy in 5 patients and 1 AE of lipohypertrophy in 1 patient were observed. One AE of lipoatrophy was a SAE considered possibly related to the investigational drug by the investigator and the MAH. Finally, 1 SAE of lipoma was considered possibly related to the investigational drug. Based on the narrative, which included a CT, one lipoma had a poorly defined border and was present at the usual injection site. This calls into question the diagnosis of lipoma. It might be that the event was lipohypertrophy and could therefore be related. It is commonly known that repeated injections at the same location can lead to local reactions of lipoatrophy and lipohypertrophy. As these reactions take time to develop, these ADRs are expected to be observed in long-term studies. Therefore, based on the information provided, the MAH is requested to submit a type II variation to include lipoatrophy and/or lipohypertrophy as ADRs for children. In addition, it could be considered to combine the ADR tables in SmPC section 4.8 for adults and children in a single table for reasons of clarity and readability. However, this is to be discussed in the proposed type II variation. In total, 32 SAEs were reported in 22 patients. The SAEs were reported in various SOCs and only the SAEs of gastroenteritis (n=3) and adenoidal hypertrophy (n=3), which are common occurrences in childhood, were reported by &gt;1 patient. 4 SAEs in 3 patients were considered related by the investigator: pharyngotonsillitis, lipoma, lipoatrophy and abdominal pain. Only the SAE of lipoatrophy was considered possibly related to the investigational drug by the MAH. As stated above, we question the SAE of lipoma should be lipohypertrophy. No deaths occurred during the study. Based on the current data, the safety profile of Sogroya did not appear to be different during the 3year safety follow-up compared to the 52-week main trial period, except for the identification of lipoatrophy and/or lipohypertrophy. (NN8640-4787). The data are awaited.

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

Overall, the data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Sogroya. The timetable is a 30-day response timetable without clock stop.
