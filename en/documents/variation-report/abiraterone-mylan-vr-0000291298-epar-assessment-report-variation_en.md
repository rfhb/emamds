## Disease or condition

Prostate cancer is a malignant neoplasm originating from the epithelial cells of the prostate gland and is one of the most prevalent cancers among men globally. It represents a significant cause of

The prostate, a male accessory reproductive organ situated below the bladder and surrounding the urethra, is responsible for producing seminal fluid, which contributes to sperm motility and viability. Malignant transformation of prostatic cells leads to the development of adenocarcinoma, the predominant histological subtype of prostate cancer (Sandhu et al 2021).

The etiology of prostate cancer is multifactorial, involving a complex interplay of genetic, hormonal, environmental, and lifestyle factors. Advanced age remains the most significant risk determinant, while familial predisposition, racial background, and specific genetic mutations-such as BRCA1, BRCA2, and HOXB13-also contribute to increased susceptibility. Epidemiological data indicate considerable geographical variation in incidence and mortality rates, reflecting differences in screening practices, genetic predisposition, and access to healthcare services (Culp et al 2020; Giri et al 2020).

Despite notable progress in early detection and therapeutic modalities, prostate cancer continues to impose a considerable global health burden. Ongoing research is therefore essential to elucidate its molecular mechanisms, improve prognostic assessment, and optimize personalized treatment approaches aimed at enhancing patient survival and quality of life.

Abiraterone acetate is an androgen biosynthesis inhibitor, converted in vivo to abiraterone which selectively inhibits the enzyme 17α-hydroxylase/C17,20-lyase (CYP17). This enzyme is expressed in and is required for androgen biosynthesis in testicular, adrenal and prostatic tumour tissues. CYP17 catalyses the conversion of pregnenolone and progesterone into testosterone precursors, DHEA and androstenedione, respectively, by 17α-hydroxylation and cleavage of the C17,20 bond. CYP17 inhibition also results in increased mineralocorticoid production by the adrenals.

Androgen-sensitive prostatic carcinoma responds to treatment that decreases androgen levels. Androgen deprivation therapies, such as treatment with LHRH analogues or orchiectomy, decrease androgen production in the testes but do not affect androgen production by the adrenals or in the tumour. Treatment with abiraterone decreases serum testosterone to undetectable levels (using commercial assays) when given with LHRH analogues (or orchiectomy). Abiraterone has been on the EU market since 2011, under the brand name of Zytiga (EU/1/11/714/002-003).

## Epidemiology, risk factors and screening tools

Prostate cancer is among the most common cancers in men globally, and a major cause of death in many countries (Culp et al 2020). Large geographical variation suggests factors such as screening practices (e.g., PSA testing), health system access, lifestyle and genetics play a big role.

Prostate cancer is the second most commonly diagnosed cancer in the world, with 161,360 estimated new cases of prostate cancer in 2017 (Siegel et al 2017). Less than 5% of patients present with metastatic disease, up to 40% of detected cases will eventually develop metastasis (Beltran et al 2011). It is the most common type of cancer found in males and affects one in six men and is the second leading cause of cancer death, with approximately 0.3 million of estimated deaths per year (Torre et al 2015). Infections, corpora amylacea, hormonal changes, diet, and urine reflux are some of common etiological agents, which aids in the inflammation of prostate. The inflammation of prostate may further lead to obstruction of bladder outlet as compared to that in a healthy prostate, which may lead to difficulty in urination (Hickling et al 2015).

In 2019 there were ~1.41 million incident cases, ~487,000 deaths and ~8.64 million DALYs (disability-adjusted life years) globally from prostate cancer. In 2022, it is estimated that about 1.5 million new cases of prostate cancer occurred worldwide (Zhang et al 2023).

## Clinical presentation, diagnosis and stage/prognosis

Prostate cancer exhibits a heterogeneous course, ranging from indolent, asymptomatic forms to aggressive variants with metastatic potential. Diagnosis typically involves the measurement of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, digital rectal examination (DRE), and histopathological confirmation through biopsy. Advances in imaging and molecular profiling have further refined diagnostic accuracy and risk stratification. Current management strategies encompass active surveillance, radical prostatectomy, radiotherapy, androgen deprivation therapy, and novel systemic treatments, selected according to disease stage and biological behaviour (Parker et al 2020).

The clinical manifestations of prostate cancer are highly variable and depend on the stage and extent of the disease. In its early stages, prostate cancer is often asymptomatic and may be detected incidentally during routine screening with prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing or digital rectal examination (DRE). As the disease progresses, patients may present with lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) such as urinary frequency, urgency, hesitancy, weak stream, and nocturia. ## Management

The management of prostate cancer has evolved significantly with the advent of precision medicine, minimally invasive surgical techniques, and targeted systemic therapies. Optimal treatment requires an individualised, multidisciplinary approach balancing oncologic efficacy with functional outcomes and patient-centred care. Continued research into molecular mechanisms, biomarkers, and novel therapeutic targets holds promise for improving survival and reducing disease burden globally.

The management of prostate cancer is guided by the stage, histological grade, tumour aggressiveness, patient age, comorbidities, and personal preferences. Treatment aims to achieve optimal oncological control while minimizing adverse effects and preserving quality of life. Management strategies range from active surveillance for indolent tumours to multimodal therapy for advanced disease (Cornford et al 2024).

## 1. Active Surveillance

For patients with low-risk, localised prostate cancer, active surveillance is often the preferred approach. It involves regular monitoring through PSA testing, digital rectal examination (DRE), and repeat biopsies or MRI scans to detect disease progression. This strategy helps avoid overtreatment of slow-growing tumours that may never become clinically significant. Watchful waiting may be recommended for older patients or those with significant comorbidities, focusing on symptom management rather than curative intent.

## 2. Surgical Management

Radical prostatectomy remains a cornerstone in the treatment of localised prostate cancer, especially in younger or medically fit patients. The procedure involves complete removal of the prostate gland, seminal vesicles, and, in some cases, regional lymph nodes. Advances such as robot-assisted laparoscopic prostatectomy (RALP) have reduced postoperative morbidity, blood loss, and recovery time while maintaining oncological outcomes comparable to open surgery. Potential complications include urinary incontinence and erectile dysfunction, which can significantly affect quality of life, emphasizing the need for careful patient selection and counselling.

## 3. Radiation Therapy

External beam radiation therapy (EBRT) and brachytherapy are effective options for localised and locally advanced prostate cancer. Modern EBRT techniques, including intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) and image-guided radiation therapy (IGRT), allow precise dose delivery while sparing surrounding tissues such as the rectum and bladder. ## 4. Hormonal (Androgen Deprivation) Therapy

Prostate cancer growth is largely dependent on androgen stimulation. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), achieved through luteinising hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonists or antagonists, orchiectomy, or anti-androgens, aims to suppress testosterone levels and slow disease progression.

ADT is used as a primary treatment for metastatic or locally advanced disease and as an adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapy alongside radiation. While effective, long-term ADT is associated with metabolic side effects such as osteoporosis, cardiovascular risk, and sexual dysfunction.

## 5. Chemotherapy and Novel Systemic Therapies

For patients with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), chemotherapy remains a key therapeutic modality. Docetaxel and cabazitaxel are the standard chemotherapeutic agents shown to improve overall survival. Recent advances have introduced novel systemic treatments, including androgen receptor signalling inhibitors (e.g., abiraterone acetate, enzalutamide, apalutamide) and PARP inhibitors (e.g., olaparib) for patients with DNA repair gene mutations. Immunotherapy, such as the vaccine sipuleucel-T, and radioligand therapy using 177 Lu-PSMA, have also demonstrated efficacy in selected patient populations, marking a new era of personalized prostate cancer care.

## 6. Multimodal and Palliative Approaches

For high-risk or advanced cases, a multimodal approach combining surgery, radiation, and systemic therapy is often employed to maximise disease control. In patients with symptomatic metastases, palliative treatments, including targeted radiation for bone pain, bisphosphonates, and bone-targeting agents such as denosumab, play an important role in maintaining quality of life.

## 2.1.2. About the product

The drug substance in this product (Abiraterone Mylan) is abiraterone on the form of abiraterone acetate. Abiraterone acetate is converted in vivo to abiraterone, an androgen biosynthesis inhibitor. Specifically, abiraterone selectively inhibits the enzyme 17α-hydroxylase/C17,20-lyase (CYP17). This enzyme is expressed in and is required for androgen biosynthesis in testicular, adrenal and prostatic tumour tissues. The recommended dose is 1000 mg (two 500 mg tablets or one 1000 mg tablet) as a single daily dose that must not be taken with food.

Following oral administration of abiraterone acetate in the fasting state, the time to reach maximum plasma abiraterone concentration is approximately 2 hours. Upon administration of 1000 mg of AA once daily, area under curve (AUC) and mean steady state Cmax were found to be 1173 ng/ml and 226 ng/ml, respectively (Goldberg and Berrios-Colon 2013). At steady state, AA is accumulated, reported time to reach the steady state is not known, although two times increase in AUC is observed on a single dose of AA. 5630 L is the apparent volume of distribution of AA (Benoist et al 2016).

Food effect on abiraterone pharmacokinetics when co-administrated with low-fat or high-fat meals was examined in healthy subjects and metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) patients (Chi et al 2015). In mCRPC patients, abiraterone AUC was 2-fold higher with a high-fat meal and similar with a low-fat meal versus modified fasting state.

A population pharmacokinetic analyses was performed to estimate pharmacokinetic parameters after oral administration of 1,000 mg/day of abiraterone acetate in patients with mCRPC, with or without prior chemotherapy, and after a single 1,000 mg dose in healthy volunteers (Stuyckens et al 2014). ## Pharmacokinetic interaction studies

Bonnet et al performed a single centre retrospective study from electronic medical records of mCRPC patients treated with abiraterone from 2011 to 2015. Potential DDI with abiraterone were identified using Micromedex and were categorized by a 4-point scale severity. Seventy-two out of ninety-five mCRPC pts (median age: 77 years [68 82]) had comorbidities. The median number of drugs used per patient was 7 [5-9]. 66 potential DDI with abiraterone were detected in 49 patients (52%): 39 and 61% were classified as major and moderate DDI, respectively. The drug classes most commonly involved in drug interactions with abiraterone were opioid pain killers (29 DDI, 44%), beta-blockers (11 DDI, 17%), and anti-arythmics (6 DDI, 9%). The drugs most frequently having potential for interaction with abiraterone were two major CYP2D6 (14%). Interaction with ketoconazole and rifampicin

The impact of a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, ketoconazole, and a strong inducer, rifampicin, on the pharmacokinetic (PK) exposure of abiraterone was evaluated in two studies in healthy men. When given with ketoconazole, abiraterone exposure increased by 9% for maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and 15% for area under the plasma concentration-time curve from 0 to time of the last quantifiable concentration (AUClast) and AUC from time 0 to infinity (AUC1) compared to abiraterone acetate alone. When given with rifampicin, abiraterone exposure was reduced to 45% for Cmax and AUC1 and to 42% for AUClast compared to abiraterone acetate alone. Ketoconazole had no clinically meaningful impact on abiraterone exposure. Rifampicin decreased abiraterone exposure by half (Bernard et al 2014).

## 2.3.4. Discussion on clinical pharmacology

The absorption data reported in the literature are consistent with the established SmPC. The marked and variable increase in abiraterone exposure with food confirms the need for administration on an empty stomach. No new absorption-related concerns were identified and no change to the product information is warranted.

Use with products known to prolong QT interval

Since androgen deprivation treatment may prolong the QT interval, caution is advised when administering abiraterone with medicinal products known to prolong the QT interval or medicinal products able to induce torsades de pointes such as class IA (e.g. quinidine, disopyramide) or class III (e.g. amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide, ibutilide) antiarrhythmic medicinal products, methadone, moxifloxacin, antipsychotics, etc.

Second-line nonsteroidal antiandrogen (NSAA) is used in treating metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC). Whether prior treatment with a second-line NSAA would impact the efficacy of abiraterone acetate (Abi) remains uncertain.

The three literature studies identified no new clinically relevant interactions. Bonnet et al reported frequent potential interactions with concomitant medicines; Shameem et al found no evidence that prior docetaxel altered abiraterone exposure; and Bernard et al showed no meaningful effect of ketoconazole, while rifampicin reduced abiraterone exposure by approximately half. Hence, strong CYP3A4 inducers should be avoided or used with careful evaluation of clinical efficacy when administered with abiraterone acetate. These findings are consistent with the current SmPC and do not warrant changes to section 4.5.

-- Trial recruits from population; powered in M1

For each comparison of research arm against control, the trial was conducted in a number of stages: a Pilot/Safety Phase, Activity Stages and a final Efficacy Stage.

- The primary outcome measure of the Pilot/Safety Phase was safety. Research arms were only to continue to recruitment in the next stage if they had shown to be both safe and feasible, although patient data from all patients and all stages were to be included in the final analyses.
- In the Activity Stages the primary outcome measure was failure-free survival (FFS). Some evidence of activity was required for a research arm to continue past each stage.
- The Efficacy Stage took place when a pre-specified number of deaths were observed amongst the control arm patients for that relevant comparison. For the \"abiraterone comparison\" (Arm A vs Arm G), this was around 267 deaths reported in the control arm.

## Treatments

The trial had multiple arms.

Arm A (control A): standard therapy alone, which was androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) only, achieved through the use of luteinising hormone releasing hormone (LHRH) analogues or antagonist or bilateral orchidectomy according to local practice. ADT was given for at least 2 years; radiotherapy at 6 to 9 months after randomization was mandatory for patients with node-negative, nonmetastatic disease and optional for patients with node-positive, nonmetastatic disease.

Arm G (abiraterone acetate comparator): standard therapy (ADT) plus abiraterone acetate (1000 mg daily) and prednisolone (5 mg daily). For patients with nonmetastatic disease with no radiotherapy planned and for patients with metastatic disease, treatment continued until PSA, radiologic, or clinical progression or until another treatment was started; for patients with nonmetastatic disease with radiotherapy planned, treatment was to continue for 2 years or until any type of progression, whichever came first.

## Sample size

The sample size was calculated using Stata nstage and predecessor programs designed for multiarm, multistage trial designs. Assuming a median FFS of 2 years and a median OS between 4 and 5 years for ADT, a 25% relative difference between the combination group and the ADT-alone group was targeted for both FFS (hazard ratio for treatment failure, 0.75) and OS (hazard ratio for death, 0.75).

## Randomisation

The trial was sponsored by the Medical Research Council (MRC) and conducted by the MRC Clinical Trials Unit at University College London.

Minimisation with a random element of 80% was used, with stratification according to randomizing center, age at randomisation (&lt;70 vs. ≥70 years), the presence or absence of metastases, planned use of prostate radiotherapy (yes vs. no), nodal involvement (negative vs. indeterminate vs. positive), World Health Organization performance status (0 vs. 1 or 2 [on a scale of 0 to 4, with higher numbers indicating greater disability]), type of ADT, and regular, long-term use of aspirin or nonsteroidal antiinflammatory drugs (yes vs. no).

Randomisation was performed centrally, with patients allocated in a 1:1 ratio to standard of care ADT alone or ADT in combination with abiraterone acetate plus prednisolone.

## Blinding (masking)

Open label.

## Statistical methods

The main analysis for the comparison of combination therapy against control for survival could be performed after the occurrence of approximately 267 deaths in the control group for 90% power and a one-sided alpha level of 2.5%, after accounting for three intermediate lack-of-benefit analyses of FFS. Prior to analysis, the hypothesis was pre-specified that there would be no difference in the treatment effect from adding abiraterone acetate + prednisolone (AAP) across the subgroups. Data on patients without an event of interest were censored when they were last known to be event-free.

Kaplan-Meier estimates were used to generate survival curves, and Cox proportional hazards regression models were applied to estimate relative treatment effects.

All the patients were included in the efficacy analyses under their assigned treatment on an intention-to-treat (ITT) basis. Pre-specified subgroup analyses looked at the consistency of treatment effect according to stratification factors (including metastatic status).

## Ancillary analyses

## Ancillary analysis 1: Hoyle et al., 2019. Abiraterone in \"high-\" and \"low-risk\" metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer

In 2018, AAP was approved for the treatment of M1 patients with \"high-risk\" disease only. Uncertainty existed regarding the treatment benefit for patients with \"low-risk\" M1 disease.

Patients in the \"abiraterone comparison\" of STAMPEDE underwent image-based post hoc subset analysis, stratified retrospectively by baseline staging risk to assess whether ADT + AAP is effective in low- as well as high-risk M1 disease.

Hoyle et al (2019) support that the addition of abiraterone acetate and prednisolone (AAP) to ADT is beneficial for men with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC), improving overall survival and disease control across both \"high-risk\" and \"low-risk\" disease categories. This combination is established as an alternative standard of care to ADT alone.

In 2019, a post hoc subgroup analysis of the abiraterone comparison within the STAMPEDE trial was conducted by Hoyle et al., applying risk stratification criteria derived from the LATITUDE trial. While LATITUDE enrolled only patients with newly diagnosed high-risk mHSPC, STAMPEDE did not prospectively stratify patients according to metastatic risk or volume.

According to the LATITUDE trial definition, high-risk mHSPC was defined by the presence of at least two of the following criteria:

- three or more bone metastases on bone scan,
- Gleason score ≥8, and
- presence of visceral metastases.

The primary outcome measure for this analysis was overall survival (OS), with failure-free survival (FFS) as a key secondary endpoint. Additional exploratory analyses evaluated progression-free survival (PFS), clinical skeletal-related events, and prostate cancer-specific mortality. Time-toevent outcomes were analysed using Kaplan-Meier survival estimates and Cox proportional hazards regression models.

A total of 901 patients with mHSCP were included in the stratified analysis. Using the LATITUDE criteria, 428 patients (48%) were classified as low risk and 473 patients (52%) as high-risk. Almost all patients (95%) had newly diagnosed disease. Median follow-up was 42 months.

## 2.4.2. Abiraterone in combination with ADT and docetaxel in mHSPC (Variation #2)

## 2.4.2.1. Main study(ies)

## PEACE-1: A Phase III Study for Patients With Metastatic Hormone-naïve Prostate Cancer

Results from the PEACE-1 study were provided by the WSA as the basis for demonstrating clinical efficacy for Variation #2 (abiraterone in mHSPC in combination with ADT and docetaxel) of this grouping.

## Methods

The PEACE-1 study is an open-label, randomised, phase 3 study with a 2×2 factorial design.

Participants were randomly assigned (1:1:1:1) to:

- SOC (androgen deprivation therapy alone),
- SOC with intravenous docetaxel 75 mg/m² once every 3 weeks,
- SOC plus radiotherapy,
- SOC plus abiraterone (abiraterone plus prednisone), or
- SOC plus radiotherapy plus abiraterone.

## Study participants

## Key inclusion criteria

Male patients aged 18 years with histologically confirmed or cytologically confirmed prostate adenocarcinoma documented as de novo metastatic by bone scan, CT, or MRI (according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors [RECIST] version 1.1 criteria) and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1 (or 2 due to bone pain) were eligible for inclusion, provided they had received ADT for no more than 3 months before randomisation and had at least 6 weeks between the initiation of ADT and the first docetaxel dose.

## Key exclusion criteria

Patients with a pure small-cell carcinoma, or previous prostate cancer treated by a definitive local treatment were not eligible.

## Treatments

ADT was planned to be continuously maintained by either a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist or antagonist, or bilateral orchiectomy.

Patients assigned to receive docetaxel as part of SOC with continuous ADT were to receive six cycles of intravenous docetaxel (75 mg/m2 per cycle; maximum dose of 150 mg per cycle), to be administered every 3 weeks (plus or minus 3 days). Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) injections after each docetaxel cycle were recommended until the protocol amendment on Jan 22, 2018, made G-CSF prophylaxis mandatory for patients who received docetaxel. The first docetaxel cycle had to be administered within 14 days after randomisation and be at least 6 weeks after ADT initiation.

Patients assigned to receive abiraterone received 1000 mg of abiraterone (four 250 mg tablets, orally) once daily plus prednisone 5 mg orally twice daily, starting within 6 weeks after ADT

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

initiation. Abiraterone and prednisone (hereafter referred to as abiraterone) were administered until disease progression to castration resistance, withdrawal of consent, unacceptable toxicity, or death.

Patients assigned to receive radiotherapy (74 Gy in 37 fractions administered over 7-8 weeks) were planned to start radiotherapy at least 3 weeks after docetaxel completion (but no more than 8 weeks after completion).

Treatments given after disease progression followed common practice, and investigators were able to change therapy as they thought was most appropriate, on the basis of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) concentration variation or clinical progression, even in absence of radiographical evidence.

Every enrolled patient was to be followed-up for a duration of 10 years. Survival status data were gathered within 3 months of the cutoff date for 97% of patients.

## Objectives, outcomes/endpoints

The coprimary endpoints were:

- Radiographical progression of soft-tissue lesions was evaluated by either CT or MRI, on the basis of RECIST version 1.1. Progression of bone lesions was assessed by bone scan according to the adapted version of Prostate Cancer Working Group 2 criteria,21 with no secondary bone scan required to confirm progression. Radiographic progression-free survival was defined as the time between randomisation and the occurrence of radiographical progression or death from any cause.
- Overall survival was defined as the time between randomisation and death from any cause. Patients without events were censored at the date of last follow-up.

The secondary endpoints were:

Castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC)-free survival, serious-genitourinary-event-free survival, prostate-cancer-specific survival, time to next skeletal-related event, PSA response rate, prognostic study of serum PSA measured 6-8 months after initiation of systemic therapy, time to pain progression, time to chemotherapy for CRPC, quality of life, changes in bone mineral density, correlation of biomarkers with outcome, event rate per 100 person-years of treatment analysis, and toxicity.

## Sample size

The initial protocol in 2013 planned for a sample size of 916 patients. However, due to a change in the SOC and the knowledge gained from the LATITUDE and STAMPEDE trial results published in late 2017, the planned sample size was increased to 1173 and the coprimary study aims were revised.

Because PEACE-1 was the only phase 3 trial to date that could determine abiraterone efficacy in patients with mCSPC receiving ADT plus docetaxel as SOC, the timing of the final analyses was based on this population (population of interest). Sample size calculation was performed with East software (Cytel; Cambridge, MA, USA), on the basis of the assumption that no significant interaction would take place between radiotherapy and abiraterone, to allow for a 2 × 2 factorial analysis of abiraterone efficacy.

For abiraterone efficacy evaluation, the predetermined acceptable probability of a type I error was set at 0.05, divided between the two coprimary endpoints (0.049 for overall survival and 0.001 for radiographic progression-free survival).

\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_\\_

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

It was hypothesised that adding abiraterone to ADT plus docetaxel would improve overall survival by 30% over a median of 53 months and progression-free survival by 40% over 30 months. Hence, with 355 patients assigned to ADT with docetaxel plus abiraterone (with or without radiotherapy) and 355 patients assigned to ADT with docetaxel without abiraterone (with or without radiotherapy), 249 deaths would give an 80% power to detect an overall survival hazard ratio (HR) of 0.70 at a two-sided α level of 0.049. With the same group allocation, 262 radiographic progression events or deaths were predicted to have an 80% power to detect a radiographic progression-free survival HR of 0.60 at a two-sided α level of 0.001.

## Randomisation

Eligible patients were centrally randomly assigned in the Alea Clinical Portal), in a 1:1:1:1 ratio, to SOC, SOC plus radiotherapy, SOC plus abiraterone (abiraterone plus prednisone), or SOC plus radiotherapy plus abiraterone.

This randomisation process was performed via the Tenalea autonomous software, solely accessed by the trial data manager within the Epidemioloy and Biostatistics facility of the Gustave Roussy Center in Villejuif, France until 2015, then by each investigator.

Randomisation was done using a minimisation algorithm, stratified by study site, ECOG performance status (0 vs 1-2), type of ADT (gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist vs antagonist vs bilateral orchiectomy), planned administration of docetaxel (yes vs no), and disease extent or burden based on metastatic status (lymph node metastases only vs bone metastases [with or without lymph node metastases] vs visceral metastases).

A similar weight was given to each of the five stratification factors, with 80% probability to minimise imbalance in the number of patients assigned to each treatment group. As the case report form gathered information for patient stratification according to high-volume versus lowvolume metastatic burden classification (as defined in the CHAARTED study [high volume was defined as the presence of visceral metastases or at least four bone lesions with one beyond the vertebral bodies and pelvis]), this later classification was retained to enable inter-trial comparisons.

## Blinding (masking)

Open label.

## Statistical methods

PEACE-1 had a factorial design, based on the assumption that there would be no interaction between abiraterone and radiotherapy. Before analysing the various outcomes, the presence of such an interaction for both coprimary endpoints was tested by analyses of maximum likelihood estimates using a Cox model adjusted for stratification factors (ECOG performance status [0 vs 1-2], ADT type [gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist vs antagonist vs bilateral orchiectomy], metastatic burden [low vs high; as defined in the Randomisation and masking section],2 and docetaxel [no docetaxel before amendment vs no docetaxel after amendment vs with docetaxel after amendment]). In the absence of a qualitative interaction (p&gt;0.05) between abiraterone and radiotherapy, the groups were to be combined two by two on the basis of abiraterone administration, regardless of radiotherapy, before sequentially assessing abiraterone efficacy first in the overall population and then in the ADT with docetaxel population (assuming the significance level was reached in first instance).

As the preplanned number of progression-free survival and overall survival events had not been reached at the time of this analysis, the efficacy of radiotherapy remains to be analysed.

The coprimary endpoint results presented here correspond to the final planned analysis; no interim analysis was conducted.

All efficacy analyses were conducted in the intention-to-treat population, defined as all patients who were randomly assigned to a treatment group.

The median follow-up was estimated by the inverse Kaplan-Meier method. Time-to-event endpoints were estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method. The Cox proportional hazards model adjusted for radiotherapy and stratification factors provided significances and an estimate of the abiraterone effect (p value and HR with CIs adjusted to match adjustment made to significance levels in the corresponding test [ie, 99.9% for radiographic progression-free survival, 95.1% for overall survival, and 95% for secondary endpoints]). The assumption of proportional hazards was evaluated on the basis of the weighted Schoenfeld residuals. The heterogeneity of the effect of abiraterone in the predefined stratification subgroups was evaluated using Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for radiotherapy and stratification factors and including an interaction term between abiraterone and the studied stratification variable. These analyses are illustrated using forest plots.

Notably, the efficacy of abiraterone in the ADT with docetaxel population was analysed by disease burden. Non-parametric CIs for the median survival differences between groups were analysed by the bootkm function of the R Hmisc package (5000 repeats). Statistical analyses were performed with SAS (version 9.4) and R (version 4.0.2).

## Recruitment

For radiographic progression-free survival and overall survival, the median follow-up periods were longer in the overall population (3.5 years [IQR 2.8-4.6] and 4.4 years [3.5-5.4], respectively) than in the ADT with docetaxel population (3.0 years [2.1-3.8] and 3.8 years [2.9-4.5], respectively).

Regardless of the population and survival outcome considered, the median follow-up periods did not differ between the groups who did or did not receive abiraterone. For the radiographic progression-free survival analyses, the median follow-up periods from randomisation to the cutoff date of Sept 1, 2020, were 3.53 years for the overall population (3.52 years for the SOC without abiraterone groups and 3.56 years for the SOC plus abiraterone groups; log-rank p=0.86) and 2.99 years for the ADT with docetaxel population (3.00 years and 2.97 years; log-rank p=0.93).

For the overall survival analyses, the median follow-up periods from randomisation to the cutoff date of June 1, 2021, were 4.41 years for the overall population (4.44 years for the SOC without abiraterone groups and 4.39 years for the SOC plus abiraterone groups; log-rank p=0.98) and 3.81 years for the ADT with docetaxel population (3.75 years and 3.85 years; log-rank p=0.95).

The trial was initially designed to compare abiraterone and/or radiotherapy added to ADT alone in 916 patients, with overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) as key efficacy outcomes.

Amendment 14 (2015): introduced docetaxel as part of the standard of care for fit patients, after ADR with docetaxel had shown improvement in overall survival, and added docetaxel use as a stratification factor. 273 patients had already been accrued at the time of this protocol amendment. The MAH indicated that the expected control-arm survival was prolonged and acknowledged that this could reduce statistical power.

Amendment 20 (2017): following the LATITUDE and STAMPEDE data supporting abiraterone plus ADT, restricted further recruitment to patients eligible for ADT plus docetaxel because ADT alone was no longer considered an ethical comparator for newly enrolled eligible patients. The sample size was increased from 916 to 1,168 patients to account for a possible interaction between abiraterone and docetaxel.

Amendment 23 (2018): followed a temporary pause after two docetaxel-related deaths from neutropenic fever at one site. Recruitment resumed with mandatory granulocyte-colony stimulating factor prophylaxis and additional blood monitoring for all docetaxel-treated patients. The MAH stated that no further toxicity-related deaths occurred after this amendment.

Amendment 32 (2021): revised the study objectives and statistical analysis plan because external studies had already addressed some original research questions. The co-primary objectives were refocused on the efficacy of abiraterone added to ADT plus docetaxel and the efficacy of prostate radiotherapy in patients with low metastatic burden. PFS was replaced by radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) as a co-primary endpoint, PSA progression was downgraded to a secondary endpoint, interim analyses were removed, and the alpha allocation was shifted to prioritise OS, with a fixed-sequence testing strategy introduced to control multiplicity by testing first in the overall population and then in the ADT plus docetaxel subgroup.

## Outcomes and estimation

In the overall population (n=1172), no interaction between abiraterone and radiotherapy was found for radiographic progression-free survival (p=0.64), overall survival (p=0.86), CRPC-free survival (p=0.56), or prostate-cancer-specific survival (p=0.54), after adjusting for the four stratification factors. Likewise, in the ADT with docetaxel population (n=710), no interaction between abiraterone and radiotherapy was found for radiographic progression-free survival (p=0.94), overall survival (p=0.85), CRPC-free survival (p=0.75), or prostate-cancer-specific survival (p=0.98), after adjusting for ECOG performance status, ADT type, and disease burden. Consequently, the comparative evaluation of abiraterone efficacy on survival outcomes was conducted by pooling the groups two by two (SOC without abiraterone [with or without radiotherapy] groups vs SOC plus abiraterone [with or without radiotherapy] groups).

In the overall population, the addition of abiraterone decreased the number of radiographic progression or death events from 371 to 252, increased the median rPFS from 2.22 to 4.46 years, and reduced by 46% the relative risk of radiographic progression or death compared to SOC alone (adjusted HR, 0.54; 99.9% CI, 0.41 to 0.71; p&lt;0.0001). It also lowered the number of deaths from 268 to 228, improved the median OS from 4.72 to 5.72 years and reduced by 18% the risk of death from any cause (adjusted HR, 0.82; 95.1% CI, 0.69 to 0.98; p=0.030).

In the ADT+docetaxel (+/-radiotherapy) population, the addition of abiraterone decreased the number of radiographic progression or death events from 211 to 139, increased the median rPFS from 2.03 to 4.46 years and reduced by 50% the relative risk of radiographic progression or death (adjusted HR, 0.50; 99.9% CI, 0.34 to 0.71; p&lt;0.0001). It also reduced the number of deaths from 151 to 121 and extended the median OS while lowering by 25% the risk of death from any cause (adjusted HR, 0.75; 95.1% CI, 0.59 to 0.95; p=0.017).

## 2.4.3. Discussion on clinical efficacy

## · Clinical efficacy of abiraterone in combination with ADT in newly diagnosed mHSPC irrespective of risk category

Abiraterone Mylan is currently indicated for patients with newly diagnosed, high risk mHSPC. With the current application, the MAH is seeking to extend the currently approved indication to mHSPC patients irrespective of their risk category (Variation #1):

Abiraterone Mylan is indicated with prednisone or prednisolone for the treatment of newly diagnosed high risk metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in adult men patients in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) (see section 5.1).

The proposed extension of indication was recently approved in some EU countries via MRP procedure.

The basis for this approval is the multistage, multi-arm study STAMPEDE with adaptive trial design, enrolling over 2,900 men across multiple centres. No concerns regarding GCP compliance or overall study methodology are raised.

Of note, STAMPEDE eligibility criteria permitted the inclusion of metastatic HSPC patients irrespective of their risk. STAMPEDE eligibility permitted metastatic HSPC patients (52%, N=941) and patients with high-risk N0 M0 disease (28%, N= 50); definition 2 of 3 high-risk factors: stage T3/4, PSA &gt;40 ng/ml, or Gleason score 8-10) or N1 M0 disease (20%, N=369; pelvic nodal metastases).

James et al 2017, describe the main results for abiraterone comparison from the STAMPEDE study. The study concluded that men with locally advanced or metastatic prostate cancer treated with ADT plus abiraterone acetate and prednisolone experienced significantly improved overall survival and failure-free survival compared with those receiving ADT alone. In this first publication in the mHSPC patient group was not subdivided according to risk status (low vs high risk).

Later, Hoyle et al 2019, published a post hoc subgroup analysis of the abiraterone comparison within the STAMPEDE trial applying risk stratification criteria derived from the LATITUDE trial to define \"high-risk\" and \"low-risk\" metastatic HSPC.

Hoyle et al. demonstrated that the addition of AAP to ADT provides a clinically meaningful benefit in men with mHSPC, irrespective of metastatic risk or volume stratification. The survival benefit associated with ADT + AAP was observed across the overall M1 population.

Specifically, the post-hoc subgroup analysis of the abiraterone comparison within the STAMPEDE trial demonstrated that, in patients classified as low risk by LATITUDE criteria, treatment with ADT + AAP was associated with approximately 34% reduction in the relative risk of death and a 76% reduction in the relative risk of treatment failure compared with ADT alone. These treatment effects were directionally consistent with those observed in higher-risk subgroups.

These findings support the consistency of the treatment effect across risk and volume categories. James et al 2022., subsequently published the 5-year follow up results from abiraterone comparison of the STAMPEDE trial. These analyses confirm the OS benefit for the mHSPC population regardless of risk status. More in detail, 1003 patients with mHSPC were randomised to receive AAP plus ADT vs ADT alone. 94% were newly diagnosed, 48% had high risk disease and 44% low risk disease (according to LATITUDE definition), 8% were not assessable. Median follow up was 6.1 years in the updated analysis. There was significant and clinically meaningful OS benefit for AAP + ADT compared to ADT alone: adjusted HR = 0.60 (95% CI: 0.50-0.71; p &lt; 0.0001) favouring AAP + ADT. The 5-years survival improved from 41% on SOC alone to 60% on AAP + SOC. This OS benefit was similar in low-risk (HR = 0.54; 95% CI: 0.40-0.74) and high-risk (HR = 0.54; 95% CI: 0.43-0.69) patients with significant improvement. From a statistical perspective, given that the results presented by Hoyle et al. are based on a subgroup analysis at the time of the pre-specified number of events for the primary analysis, it is considered that these results are more suitable for regulatory decision-making, with the updated results provided by James et al. considered to be supportive. Importantly, with reference to the guideline on the investigation of subgroups in confirmatory clinical trials (EMA/CHMP/539146/2013), the evaluation of the low-risk subgroup is in the context of an overall statistically persuasive effect in the primary analysis population, and with no strong signals of inconsistent treatment effects among the high- and low-risk subgroups. A limitation of the STAMPEDE trial is that the control regimens were not designed to reflect contemporary sequential standards of care, including the systematic use of life-prolonging androgen receptor-targeted therapies at disease progression. Subsequent treatments were not mandated by the protocol, and a proportion of patients in the control arms did not receive later-line therapies that are now considered standard. Consequently, the magnitude of benefit observed with early treatment intensification should be interpreted in the context of historical treatment pathways. In addition, STAMPEDE is a platform trial in which treatment arms were introduced sequentially over time, resulting in the use of non-concurrent control groups. While this design improves efficiency and has been addressed through appropriate statistical methodology, it may introduce residual bias and warrants cautious interpretation. It should also be noted that this risk stratification was not prespecified and was performed post hoc, which limits the robustness of conclusions regarding treatment effect modification by risk category and introduces the potential for bias related to multiplicity and retrospective subgrouping.

Notwithstanding these limitations, STAMPEDE has demonstrated a clinically meaningful and durable overall survival benefit with the addition of abiraterone acetate plus prednisolone to androgen deprivation therapy in men with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer across both low-risk and high-risk metastatic subgroups, supported by long-term follow-up data. The claimed indication (Variation #1) is supported.

## · Clinical efficacy of abiraterone in combination with ADT and docetaxel in newly diagnosed mHSPC in patients

With the current application, the MAH sought to extend the approved indication to patients with newly diagnosed mHSPC in combination with ADT and docetaxel (Variation #2):

Abiraterone Mylan is indicated with prednisone or prednisolone for the treatment of newly diagnosed high risk mHSPC in adult patients in combination with ADT and docetaxel (see section 5.1)

The proposed indication extension is supported by the PEACE-1 trial, a randomised, multicentre, open-label phase III study employing a 2 × 2 factorial design in patients with de novo metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer. PEACE-1 evaluated the addition of abiraterone acetate and/or prostate radiotherapy to an evolving standard of care, which included ADT with or without docetaxel.

PEACE-1 demonstrated clinically meaningful improvements in rPFS and OS with the addition of abiraterone to ADT plus docetaxel in the overall study population. The efficacy endpoints were objective and considered clinically relevant despite the open-label design.

Subgroup analyses according to metastatic burden showed a consistent direction of treatment effect, with hazard ratios for OS remaining below unity in both high- and low-volume disease. No clear evidence of a clinically relevant differential treatment effect by metastatic burden was identified. It is acknowledged that metastatic burden was not a stratification factor and that baseline imbalances may have influenced subgroup estimates.

Initially several uncertainties were identified. PEACE-1 was not conducted under the responsibility of the Applicant, and a complete clinical study report was not available. ## 2.5. Clinical safety

## Introduction

Abiraterone acetate is an oral, selective inhibitor of CYP17A1 that blocks extragonadal androgen biosynthesis and, when combined with low-dose glucocorticoid, prolongs survival in several stages of prostate cancer (metastatic castration-resistant and castration-sensitive disease). While efficacy is well established, the safety/tolerability profile is distinct and predictable because of its mechanism (mineralocorticoid excess from upstream steroid shifts plus effects on hepatic enzymes and interactions with corticosteroid co-therapy).

Inhibiting CYP17A1 prevents conversion of pregnenolone and progesterone to 17α-hydroxylated products and downstream androgens; this causes upstream accumulation of mineralocorticoid precursors and relative glucocorticoid deficiency with resultant ACTH-driven mineralocorticoid excess (hypertension, hypokalaemia, fluid retention). Low-dose prednisone (commonly 5-10 mg/day) is co-prescribed to suppress ACTH and reduce these effects, but mineralocorticoid-related events remain the dominant toxicity class. Hepatic enzyme elevations are an additional pharmacologic toxicity that appears dose-related or idiosyncratic in some patients. Finally, systemic corticosteroid exposure (even at low doses) contributes steroid-related adverse effects (hyperglycaemia, weight gain, immunosuppression).

## 2.5.1. Discussion on clinical safety

Abiraterone acetate has a well-characterised safety profile that is largely attributable to CYP17 inhibition and the requirement for concomitant corticosteroid administration. Mineralocorticoid excess (hypertension, hypokalaemia, fluid retention), hepatic enzyme elevations, steroid-related metabolic effects and cardiovascular adverse events represent the principal safety concerns. With appropriate patient selection, baseline assessment and proactive laboratory and clinical monitoring-particularly during the initial months of therapy-these toxicities are generally manageable and do not necessitate treatment discontinuation in the majority of patients.

For Variations 1 and 3, the bibliographic evidence does not indicate a new or materially different safety signal. The known toxicities of abiraterone, corticosteroid treatment, ADT and radiotherapy remain applicable, and no additional Product Information amendment is required beyond the agreed wording.

No further SmPC update is considered necessary for Variations 1 and 3, as the final Product Information reflects the established abiraterone risks and the agreed indication wording.

## · Clinical safety of abiraterone in combination with ADT (Variations #1 and #3)

No new clinical safety data were submitted as part of this application, which is considered acceptable. The safety profile for this combination is considered well established given the extensive post-marketing experience available.

## 3.1.1. Disease or condition

Prostate cancer is a malignant neoplasm originating from the epithelial cells of the prostate gland and is one of the most prevalent cancers among men globally. It represents a significant cause of morbidity and mortality, particularly in aging populations. The prostate, a male accessory reproductive organ situated below the bladder and surrounding the urethra, is responsible for producing seminal fluid, which contributes to sperm motility and viability. Malignant transformation of prostatic cells leads to the development of adenocarcinoma, the predominant histological subtype of prostate cancer (Sandhu et al 2021).

The etiology of prostate cancer is multifactorial, involving a complex interplay of genetic, hormonal, environmental, and lifestyle factors. Advanced age remains the most significant risk determinant, while familial predisposition, racial background, and specific genetic mutations-such as BRCA1, BRCA2, and HOXB13-also contribute to increased susceptibility. Epidemiological data indicate considerable geographical variation in incidence and mortality rates, reflecting differences in screening practices, genetic predisposition, and access to healthcare services (Culp et al 2020; Giri et al 2020).

Advanced metastatic prostate cancer is a heterogeneous disease for which ADT-ARPI combinations are currently the backbone of therapy. All subsequent therapies from among the options available may be used in sequence after an ARP

Effective therapies are therefore available, but a residual need remains for earlier treatment intensification that improves survival and disease control while maintaining manageable toxicity and appropriate patient selection.

The management of prostate cancer has evolved significantly with the advent of precision medicine, minimally invasive surgical techniques, and targeted systemic therapies. Optimal treatment requires an individualized, multidisciplinary approach balancing disease control with functional outcomes and patient-centred care. Continued research into molecular mechanisms, biomarkers, and novel therapeutic targets holds promise for improving survival and reducing disease burden globally.

## 3.7. Benefit-risk assessment and discussion

## 3.7.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

The favourable effects observed with abiraterone acetate, particularly improvements in survival and disease control, are of high clinical relevance in the context of advanced prostate cancer. The identified unfavourable effects are well understood and manageable in clinical practice and are considered acceptable in view of the severity of the disease and the magnitude of the observed benefits.

The present application seeks extensions of indication covering three clinical settings.

## Treatment of newly diagnosed metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in adult patients in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), irrespective of risk category.

(Variation #1)

For Variation #1, the totality of evidence from large randomised clinical trials, including STAMPEDE and LATITUDE demonstrates clinically meaningful and durable improvements in overall survival and other key efficacy endpoints. Although extension beyond the LATITUDE-defined high-risk population relies in part on post hoc subgroup analyses, these analyses consistently demonstrate a treatment effect across risk categories and are considered acceptable for regulatory decisionmaking. The safety profile is well characterised and manageable with appropriate monitoring. The benefit-risk balance for Variation #1 is therefore considered positive.

## Treatment of newly diagnosed high-risk non-metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (HSPC) in adult patients in combination with ADT and radiotherapy (Variation #3)

For Variation #3, evidence from the STAMPEDE abiraterone comparison, supported by long-term follow-up and meta-analytic data, demonstrates clinically meaningful improvements in metastasisfree survival and overall survival when abiraterone is added to ADT and radiotherapy in patients with newly diagnosed high-risk non-metastatic HSPC. The observed safety profile is consistent with the established safety profile of abiraterone, and no new safety concerns have been identified. The benefit-risk balance for Variation #3 is therefore considered positive.

## Treatment of newly diagnosed mHSPC in adult patients in combination with ADT and docetaxel.

## (Variation #2)

For Variation #2, PEACE-1 provides clinically meaningful and statistically robust evidence supporting the addition of abiraterone to ADT plus docetaxel, with significant improvements in radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) and overall survival (OS). The magnitude and consistency of the observed treatment effects support a clinically relevant benefit. Although the complete PEACE-1 clinical study report and underlying source documentation remain unavailable, the resulting limitation is considered evidentiary rather than an outstanding deficiency. The available published and supplementary evidence provides adequate reassurance regarding the reliability of the efficacy findings and the principal multiplicity-control framework. The lack of a direct prospective comparison of the triplet regimen with an ARPI-containing doublet is acknowledged but does not invalidate the randomised comparison relevant to the proposed indication. Overall, the benefits are considered to outweigh the identified and manageable risks. The benefit-risk balance for Variation #2 is therefore considered positive.
