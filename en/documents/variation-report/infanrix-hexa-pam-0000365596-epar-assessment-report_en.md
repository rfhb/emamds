## Overview of study rationale and design

In study H06\\_02TP, the investigational product assessed is altSonflex1-2-3 (GSK4001785A), which is a candidate vaccine in development against Shigellosis that targets Shigella sonnei (S. sonnei) and Shigella flexneri (S. flexneri), serotypes 1b, 2a, and 3a (figure 1 of Critical Expert Overview).

The study included 200 infants 9 months of age at the time of first vaccination that were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1:1 ratio to Dose A, Dose B, Dose C, or control group (with 50 participants in each study group).

AltSonflex1-2-3 vaccine was administered intramuscularly twice (Day 1 and Day 169) at 3 different dose levels (Dose A [low, 3.75 μg OAg], Dose B [medium, 7.5 μg OAg], and Dose C [high, 15 μgOAg]). At the same timepoints, participants were administered the measles and rubella vaccine (MR-VAC) subcutaneously.

In the control group, participants received Typhibev intramuscularly on Day 1, Infanrix hexa intramuscularly on Day 169, and MR-VAC twice subcutaneously on Day 1 and Day 169.

The study completion date was Visit 6 (Day 197).

Active control vaccines were used in the study to provide potential benefit to study participants randomised to the control group. The vaccines selected were not routinely available to the population in the study geographical region, outside the clinical study setting.

## Sample size

Approximately 200 participants were to be randomised to achieve 176 evaluable participants, assuming a drop-out rate of 12% in each intervention group. Participants who withdraw from the clinical study after being randomised were not to be replaced. Sample size was not driven by statistical hypotheses, rather it was determined to support an adequate immunogenicity and safety assessment of the primary study intervention group.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Participant identification numbers were assigned sequentially to the participants who had a consent to participate in the clinical study, according to the range of participant identification numbers allowed for the study.

Targeted enrolment was approximately 200 eligible participants to be randomly assigned in a 1:1:1:1 ratio to the different study groups: Dose A, Dose B, Dose C and Control (approximately 50 participants in each study group).

The allocation of the participant to a study group at the investigator site was performed using a Randomisation and Trial Supply Management System (RTSM), which determined the study group and provided the study intervention number to be used for the first dose.

The study was single (participant) blinded, as only the participants' parent(s)/caregiver/ Legally authorized representative (LAR(s)) were blinded to the study intervention, but the Investigator and sponsor's study team were unblinded to the study intervention assigned.

## Statistical Methods

## Safety Analyses

Descriptive analysis was conducted for safety.

Analyses of solicited and unsolicited AEs were performed using the Exposed Set. For each intervention group, the percentage of participants reporting solicited administration site and systemic events within 7 days after vaccination, and the percentage of participants reporting unsolicited AEs within 28 days after vaccination were tabulated.

Additionally, analyses of SAEs, AEs leading to vaccine or study discontinuation, as well as deviations from reference ranges or baseline values of haematological and biochemistry test results at 7 days after each vaccination were performed using the Exposed Set.

Immunogenicity analyses conducted did not pertain with Infanrix Hexa and are therefore not described in this AR.

## Safety results

The safety analyses were descriptive and performed using the Exposed Set. The Exposed Set included all participants who received at least 1 dose of the study intervention, with the allocation in a group based on the administered intervention.

According to the Applicant, concerning altSonflex1-2-3, as specified in the CSR Synopsis, no safety concerns were identified after administration of altSonflex1-2-3 vaccine; safety and reactogenicity profile appeared generally consistent with that expected for prophylactic vaccines.

In the control group, within 7 days post vaccination, 24 (48.0%) participants had at least 1 solicited or unsolicited AE, 1 (2.0%) participant had at least 1 solicited or unsolicited Grade 3 AE (all nonserious), and 8 (16.0%) participants had at least 1 solicited or unsolicited medically attended AE.

Specifically for solicited or unsolicited AE reported within 7 days post dosing at Visit 4/ Day 169 for the control arm (i.e. when a single Infanrix Hexa dose was administered IM and a second dose of MR-VAX was administered SC) and as summarised in the submitted critical expert overview:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

At least 1 solicited event was reported in 16 (32.7%) participants including 13 (26.5%) participants with at least 1 solicited administration site event and 6 (12.2%) participants with at least 1 solicited systemic event (fever, ≥38.0℃).

- At least 1 solicited administration site event of pain was reported in 13 (26.5%) participants, swelling in 6 (12.2%) participants, and erythema in 4 (8.2%) participants.
- Majority of the solicited administration site events were of Grade 1 intensity except for 4 (8.2%) participants with Grade 2 pain and 1 (2.0%) participant with Grade 3 pain. Pain was the most frequently reported solicited administration site event.
- At least 1 solicited systemic event of fever were reported in 6 (12.2%) participants; of these 6 participants, 5 participants had at least 1 event of fever related to study vaccination as assessed by the investigator. No cases of fever ≥40℃ were reported within the 7 days post vaccination. Three (6.1%) participants had Grade 1 fever and 3 (6.1%) had Grade 2 fever.

A total of 17 (34.7%) participants had at least 1 unsolicited AE within 28 days post vaccination.

The most commonly reported unsolicited AEs by preferred term include upper respiratory tract infection and rhinitis (each, 4 [8.2%] participants) and haemoglobin decreased (3 [6.1%] participants). All other AEs (gastroenteritis, otitis media, pharyngitis, oral candidiasis, otitis media acute, diarrhoea, rash, dehydration, and rhinitis allergic) were reported in 1 (2.0%) participant each.

There were no Grade 3 unsolicited AEs within 28 days post dosing 2 reported for the control group.

Following Infanrix hexa administration, no SAEs, deaths, or AEs leading to vaccine discontinuation/withdrawal were reported.

Safety laboratory test results 7 days post vaccination did not reveal specific trends.

Regarding the clinical chemistry (renal and hepatic panel test results) and haematology analysis, 1 Grade 3 event of low haemoglobin at Visit 5 was reported in the control group 7 days post vaccination; which at retesting at an unscheduled visit (21 days after Visit 5) was Grade 1 and clinically significant according to investigator's assessment.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

SHIGELLA 4 GMMA GVGH-002 (H06\\_02TP) is a Phase 2 single-blind, randomised, controlled, single centre study to assess the immunogenicity and safety of a 2-dose schedule with GVGH altSonflex1-2-3 vaccine in African infants that was conducted in a single centre in Kenya.

Eligible infants aged 9 months at the time of first dosing were randomised into 3 different experimental arms and in an active control arm. In the latter, participants were administered a single intramuscular dose of Infanrix hexa at the age of 15 months concomitantly with a second dose of the measles/rubella vaccine (MR-VAX - responsible for manufacture Serum Institute of India) administered subcutaneously.

Although not specified in the submitted documents, the dose of Infanrix hexa administered at 15 months to study participants is considered as corresponding to the toddler booster dose given the inclusion criteria of previously completed routine childhood vaccinations.

There were no study objectives specifically pertaining to evaluation of Infanrix-specific immune responses.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Safety data were descriptively reported and included data collected for solicited administration-site and systemic events reported for a 7 day period after each study intervention administration (day of study intervention administration and the 6 subsequent days) and for unsolicited adverse events (AEs) reported for a 28 day period after each study intervention administration. All solicited and unsolicited AEs of Grade 1 or above were considered for reporting. The solicited administration site events collected in the study were erythema, pain, and swelling. The solicited systemic event collected in the study was fever (defined as temperature ≥38.0°C), which was assessed for relatedness by the investigator. Serious adverse event (SAE) data were collected throughout the study period in all groups. Haematological, renal, and hepatic panel test results, at 7 days after each vaccination were

also evaluated. A total of 49 subjects were included in the exposed set population that was administered Infanrix hexa and MR-VAX on Day 169.

The limited descriptive safety data collected within study SHIGELLA 4 GMMA GVGH-002 (H06\\_02TP) in paediatric volunteers administered a dose of Infanrix hexa at the age of 15 months concomitantly with a dose of MR-VAX are in line with the approved product information for Infanrix hexa in the European Union (EU).

It is noted that one Grade 3 event of low haemoglobin at Visit 5 was reported in the control group (corresponding to 7 days after administration of Infanrix hexa and MR-VAX); which at retesting at an unscheduled visit (21 days after Visit 5) was Grade 1 and clinically significant according to investigator's assessment. Haemoglobin decreased was also included among the most commonly reported unsolicited AEs by preferred term (3 [6.1%] participants). Haemoglobin decreased is also listed as most frequently recorded medical histories for the participants of study SHIGELLA 4 GMMA GVGH-002 (H06\\_02TP).

It is agreed with the MAH that changes to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for Infanrix hexa are not necessary.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

It is agreed with the MAH, that the limited descriptive safety data collected within study SHIGELLA 4 GMMA GVGH-002 (H06\\_02TP) in paediatric volunteers administered a dose of Infanrix hexa at the age of 15 months concomitantly with a dose of MR-VAX are in line with the approved product information for Infanrix hexa in the European Union (EU) and that changes to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for Infanrix hexa are not necessary.
