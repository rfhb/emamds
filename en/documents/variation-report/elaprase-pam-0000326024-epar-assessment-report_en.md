## 1. Introduction

On 27 January 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Elaprase, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

Study SHP-ELA-401 is not part of a paediatric investigation plan nor part of the clinical development program of Elaprase. This study is a U.S. postmarketing requirement established in the product's approval letter for Elaprase.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The study SHP-ELA-401 is a stand alone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

The pharmaceutical formulation used in study SHP-ELA-401 is concentrate for solution for infusion. The active substance is idursulfase. All participants enrolled in Group 1 received once-weekly IV infusions of Elaprase at a dose of 0.5 mg/kg. The dose of Elaprase administered was calculated based on the participants' weight at each visit. Enrolled participants received their first infusion at Week 1. Thereafter, each enrolled participant received 1 infusion of Elaprase every week, until the point at which each individual participant completed the trial.

Elaprase is indicated for the long-term treatment of patients with Hunter syndrome (mucopolysaccharidosis II, MPS II).

## 2.3.2. Clinical study

## Study SHP-ELA-401; A Long-Term, Open-Label, Multicenter, Phase IV Study to Assess Longitudinal Changes on Height and Weight in Patients with MPS II Who Are Receiving Elaprase and Started Treatment with Elaprase at &lt;6 Years of Age

## Description

Trial SHP-ELA-401 was a long-term, open-label, multicenter phase 4 clinical trial proposed to evaluate longitudinal changes in height and weight among participants with MPS II who began treatment with Elaprase &lt;6 years of age. Elaprase was administered weekly as an IV infusion to participants enrolled in this trial.Participants enrolled in this trial were followed for a minimum of 5 years after initiation of Elaprase treatment, or until they reached their 10th birthday, whichever was longer. Trial SHP-ELA401 planned to enroll a minimum of 20 treatment-naïve participants with the length of on-trial followup for individual participant in this trial ranging from 5 to 10 years, from screening through the EOT.

Analysis of height and weight also included retrospective participants from the HOS registry, which was a global, multinational, voluntary outcome survey that Takeda committed to conduct at the time of the original approval of Elaprase to address a postmarketing commitment in the US. The HOS provided the opportunity to obtain real-world data that increased the understanding of MPS II and the progression of the disease. The HOS was open from 31 October 2005 to 16 February 2023. At survey conclusion, 1332 participants from 157 trial sites in 34 countries were enrolled in the HOS registry.

For this trial, Group 1 (prospective participant group) and Group 2 (retrospective participant group) were prespecified and defined as follows:

Group 1 (prospective participant group): treatment-naïve participants who were &lt;6 years of age at the time of enrollment in Trial SHP-ELA-401. At least 20 participants were to be enrolled in this participant group. A participant was considered included in this group if informed consent was provided and the participant was treated with Elaprase in this trial.

Group 2 (retrospective participant group): participants who were not enrolled in Trial SHP-ELA401 but were enrolled in the HOS registry and treated with Elaprase. While not enrolled in Trial SHPELA-401, their height and weight data from the HOS registry were to be utilized in the primary growth analysis for this trial. A HOS participant was considered included in this participant group if he met the eligibility requirements.

Group 3 (HOS untreated participant group): Untreated participants from HOS were included and this group was used to compare growth data from treated participants. Elaprase untreated participants who had a BMT surgery, or who had been treated with growth hormone or other medications or interventions intended to promote growth in the time period covered by the analysis, were excluded.

## CHMP comments

The MAH explained that study SHP-ELA-401 was executed based on a U.S. postmarketing requirement. It is a multi-centre, single-arm, open-label study to assess longitudinal changes in height and weight among patients with MPS II who began Elaprase treatment &lt;6 years of age. The study contains an external control arm. The use of an external control arm hampers direct comparison to a \"placebo arm\" as the external control arm is not sourced from the same population and/or time.

Currently no update of the SmPC is planned in relation to the outcomes of this study.

## CHMP comments

Study SHP-ELA-401 included MPS II males, which were treatment naïve prior to study entry and started Elaprase treatment before age of 6.

The inclusion criteria for the retrospective patients included in the protocol only describe Elaprase treated patients.

It should be kept in mind that the patient population in Study SHP-ELA-401 might not be comparable to the population enrolled in the HOS registry. The disease is very heterogenous and disease progression and severity varies widely in patients. Especially the group untreated is likely to be different.

Currently information regarding the selection of participants from the HOS registry, which includes in total n=1332 participants, who are used as comparison in the current study, restricted to n=45 participants, is limited. Therefore, any conclusions based on this comparison should be made with ample caution. In case an update is required or planned, the MAH should thoroughly describe and justify the used selection criteria as this impacts the usefulness of the selected population as comparator.

## CHMP comments

It is acknowledged that patients with MPS-II have severe short stature. The impact of Elaprase treatment on height and weight has only been included as an exploratory endpoint in the pivotal study TKT024 study. Therefore, a primary objective to study height and weight development over a 5 year period during Elaprase treatment is appreciated. It should be kept in mind that growth is not linear and growth velocity changes over time, especially when comparing infants to prepuberal children. As the study contains an external control arm, this hampers conclusions to be drawn from the current study, as this set-up is prone to bias and confounding.

The secondary objectives measuring impact on the 6 minute walk test, urinary GAG levels, the effect on liver and spleen size and joint stiffness in addition to quality of life measures are relevant and measure impact on known manifestations of the disease.

## Sample size

The proposed sample size of at least 20 patients for Group 1-Prospective Patient Group represents a number that is feasible to enroll and was determined outside of statistical considerations. Patients who withdraw from the study prior to study completion (dropouts) will not be replaced. A total sample size of approximately 35 patients is expected for analysis from the combination of Group 1-Prospective Patient Group and Group 2-Retrospective Patient Group.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

This is a single-arm, open-label study, therefore no randomisation or blinding was in place.

## CHMP comments

The fact that this study is a single-arm, open label study does hamper a thorough assessment of the impact of treatment.

## Statistical Methods

The analyses were purely descriptive as no predefined hypothesis was tested. The final analyses were performed once the data from all participants were collected in the database, cleaned and final database lock had occurred (02 September 2025).

## Analysis Populations

Combined Population: The Combined Population will include data from all patients in both patient groups (Group 1-Prospective Patient Group and Group 2-Retrospective Patient Group). Selected efficacy analyses will be performed using the Combined Population.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Prospective Population: The Prospective Population will be defined as all patients in Group 1Prospective Patient Group. All safety analyses and selected efficacy analyses will be performed using the Prospective Population.

## General Considerations

All continuous variables will be summarized using descriptive statistics, including the sample size (n), mean, standard deviation (SD), minimum, 25th percentile, median, and 75th percentile. All categorical variables will be summarized using frequency counts and percentages (%). Statistical testing is considered exploratory in this observational study and no adjustment for multiplicity will be performed. Missing growth data will be assumed to be missing at random (MAR) in statistical analyses and no imputation will be performed. Last observation carried forward (LOCF) will be used to impute missing data for selected efficacy outcomes (uGAGs, JROM, 6MWT). Last observation carried forward will not be done for a period beyond 1 year, e.g., if a patient has an assessment at year 2 but not year 3 and year 4, LOCF will only be done up to year 3. Weight and height assessments of treated patients from HOS after the age of 11 years will be excluded from the analyses. The last available assessment prior to age 11 years will serve as the end of study assessment.

## Analytical approach

The effect of treatment on growth at the end of study will be analysed by comparing a) the Combined Set (Groups 1 and 2) with the untreated patients (Group 3) and b) the Efficacy Set (Group 1) with the untreated patients (Group 3), using a linear mixed models with treatment (yes/no), age at time of assessment, race (Asian vs nonAsian) and baseline value of weight/height as factors - for untreated patients, the baseline value would the first available height/weight assessment. Quadratic and cubic terms for age may be added to the model if significant or necessary to better characterize the relationship between physical growth and age over time. Height and weight Z-scores will be calculated based on the WHO-DD growth charts (for age ≤ 24 months) and the CDC growth charts (for age &gt;24 months) normal height/weight-for-age data. Descriptive statistics for the Efficacy Set and Combined Set at each time point for height, weight and Z-scores and for change from baseline will be calculated overall and separately for each subgroup (age group at start of Elaprase ERT treatment (&lt;2 years; ≥2 years), overall antibody status, baseline crossreactive immunologic material (CRIM) status, race (Asian vs non-Asian), genotype class and severity of genetic variant - overall antibody status and baseline CRIM status are only applicable for the Efficacy Set). Height, weight, and the corresponding Z-scores from start of ERT will be plotted against age for the Combined Set. Analyses using linear mixed models with random intercept and slope for age will be fit to the growth data over time from the start of ERT. Quadratic and cubic terms for age may be added to the model if significant or necessary to better characterize the relationship between physical growth and age over time. Predicted growth curves for individuals using the patient specific intercept and slope estimates from the model will be plotted. The effect of factors, such as age group at start of ERT, baseline CRIM status (only for Efficacy Set), and genotype class on growth parameters will be explored. The Least Squares means (LS-means) and standard errors at various ages estimated from the model will be summarized and plotted by factor groups. Similar analyses will be performed on the

Efficacy Set and will assess the effect of baseline CRIM status and antibody status on growth.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

As an additional analysis, a linear mixed model repeated measures (MMRM) will be used as a repeated measures analysis to investigate the growth over time. To account for baseline differences between groups, the model will include fixed categorical effects for visit week and baseline value of each endpoint (height and weight), race (Asian vs non-Asian) and age at baseline as continuous covariates. This will be analysed for the Efficacy Set, the Per-Protocol Analysis Set, the Combined Set and the HOS

untreated patients (Group 3). Interim Analyses

Two interim analyses are planned.

Interim Analysis 1 will examine CRIM assay results with respect to patient genotype and I2S enzyme status. This analysis is intended to evaluate suitability of blood samples for CRIM testing by confirming that the CRIM status determination is consistent with patient genotype. Patients with severe disease presentations, eg, as a result of complete deletion or early truncation of I2S enzyme-coding sequence, would be expected to test CRIM-negative. Interim Analysis 1 will be conducted once the last patient in the Prospective Patient Group enrolls in the study and completes baseline assessments.

Interim Analysis 2 will examine the correlation of CRIM status to prospectively collected data on the antibody response, uGAG levels, liver and spleen volumes, and clinical outcomes. This second analysis will be conducted after the last patient in the Prospective Patient Group completes 2-year assessments.

## CHMP comments

The SAP has been amended very late in the study, just prior to data lock and after last participant last visit. This is considered very late and it is not understood why finalisation of the SAP occurred so late. However, as the study was not intended to test predefined statistical hypotheses about efficacy, this is

not further pursued. Statistical analyses were explorative and descriptive in nature. The use of an external control arm is prone to bias and confounding. The primary endpoint will include a height and weight comparison between the treated and untreated group using linear mixed models (LMM). LMM assumes a linear progression of the outcome measure, however, it is not clear that these outcome measures analysed change linearly over time. This should be kept in mind especially as the rate of disease progression and severity varies widely amongst patients.

No explicit imputation for missing data was conducted for this study. This is understood as the disease is very heterogenous and as stated before disease progression and severity varies widely in patients, therefore predicting values might not be feasible. It is noted that LOCF was used for data which was missing for selected efficacy outcomes (uGAGs, JROM, 6MWT), which is not preferred considering the progressive nature of Hunter syndrome and the fact that the patients are children.

End of the study for the prospective patients is 5 years after start treatment or when the patient reaches his 10 th birthday whichever is later. For patients in the HOS registry, the last available assessment prior to age 11 years will be used as the end of study assessment. The age at end of study visit could therefore differ between the prospective and retrospective patient groups, being 10 years of age for prospective patients and prior to 11 years of age for HOS participants. This will impact the height and weight assessments.

## CHMP comments

In total 23 prospective participants were screened, of which 21 were treated. In total 4 participants discontinued the study: 1 participant died; 1 participant discontinued due to other reasons and 2 participants discontinued due to noncompliance with the trial intervention. The MAH states that the mean treatment compliance in trial participants was 96.88%.

An external treated and untreated control group was included, which were used to assess the impact of treatment. No information was provided on how the selection of participants from the registry was performed. This severely limits the interpretation and conclusions that can be drawn based on the data of this study.

All prospective participants in the study had at least 1 significant protocol deviation. This is unexpected and points to issues with reliability of the data and potentially hampers drawing of conclusions. In case the MAH would want to update the SmPC with the information obtained from this study, the MAH is expected to more thoroughly explain the protocol deviations and how these could impact the overall assessment and reliability of the results. Including, but not limited to a thorough explanation of which endpoint assessments were missed (n=8, 38.1%), what were the deviations regarding trial treatment administration/dispense (n=15, 71.4%) and what is included in other protocol deviations.

The first will examine CRIM assay results with respect to patient genotype and I2S enzyme status and will be conducted once the last patient enrolls in the study and completes baseline assessments. The second will examine the correlation of CRIM status to prospectively collected data on the antibody response, uGAG levels, liver and spleen volume, and clinical outcomes. Additionally, CRIM status will be correlated with changes in height and weight, parameters which are already being assessed in study SHP-ELA-401.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

This submission concerns a multi-centre, open-label, single arm Phase 4 study with the aim to evaluate longitudinal changes in height and weight among participants with MPS II who began treatment with Elaprase &lt;6 years of age. It was conducted to meet the regulatory requirements of the FDA.

The prospective part of Study SHP-ELA-401 included MPS II males, which were treatment naïve prior to study entry and started Elaprase treatment before age of 6. The study also included a retrospective part, which included MPS II males which were either treated with Elaprase with treatment start prior to age of 6 or were untreated. It should be kept in mind that the patient population in prospective part is likely to be different from the population enrolled in the HOS registry, especially the untreated group. Currently information regarding the selection of participants from the HOS registry, is not available.

The primary objective of the study is to evaluate height and weight development over a 5 year period during Elaprase treatment. It should be kept in mind that growth is not linear and growth velocity changes over time, especially when comparing infants to prepuberal children. As the study contains an external control arm, this hampers conclusions to be drawn from the current study, as this set-up is prone to bias and confounding.

The limited data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase. Participants in the study substantial growth over time. Based on the height and weight z-scores it seems that during 5 years of treatment, participants appear to come closer to the normal age-adjusted height and weight. At the cross-sectional analysis of height at 10 years of age, it appears that the untreated group is smaller compared to both the efficacy group and the combined group. However, no firm conclusions can be drawn based on the fact that study design is prone to bias and confounding, lack of information regarding participant selection and analysis methods and handling of missing information that might not be suitable. However, the limited data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase

The lack of a control arm hampers the interpretation of the safety data in the current study. Nevertheless, the TEAEs reported are consistent with the events that can be expected in the population of paediatric patients with Hunter syndrome and with the known safety profile of Elaprase.

In conclusion, no changes to the SmPC are recommended.

In case the MAH wishes to update the SmPC with results of the current study, the MAH is recommended to come for scientific advice as use of an external control arm is prone to bias and confounding. Several issues regarding selection of participants from the HOS registry and representativeness of the population should be addressed, as well as presentation of the data to make comparisons more meaningful.

The benefit-risk of Elaprase remains positive.
