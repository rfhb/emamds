## 2.3.2. Clinical study

Study: PMR-EC-1206 (Parts A, B and C)

Title: A Phase II, Open-label, Multi-Center Study to Compare the Pharmacokinetics of Tacrolimus in Stable Pediatric Allograft Recipients Converted from a Prograf® Based Immunosuppressive Regimen to a Tacrolimus Prolonged Release, Advagraf® Based Immunosuppressive Regimen, Including a LongTerm Follow-Up

## Description

This was a phase 2, open-label, multi-centre study to compare the pharmacokinetics of tacrolimus in stable paediatric allograft recipients converted from a Prograf-based immunosuppressive regimen to a tacrolimus modified release, Advagraf-based immunosuppressive regimen.

Patients undergoing primary heart, kidney or liver transplantation and meeting the inclusion criteria and complying with the exclusion criteria prior to initiation of tacrolimus therapy were enrolled. Patients were randomized to treatment with either Advagraf or Prograf. The randomization was on a 1:1 basis stratified by transplanted organ and age group which was not applicable to Part C. The study was conducted in 19 contracted centres in a total of 8 countries, of which 14 centres in 7 countries enrolled patients.

The study was divided into 3 parts (see Figure 1). Part A lasted 6 weeks, comprised the initial pharmacokinetic part of the study and was designed to collect pharmacokinetic data following administration of Advagraf and Prograf in stable paediatric allograft recipients. Part A was a single arm cross-over study to compare the pharmacokinetics of Prograf and Advagraf. On day 1, patients were converted from their routine Prograf based immunosuppressive regimen to a Prograf based immunosuppressive regimen supplied by the Sponsor as study drug, and patients continued treatment until day 7. The daily dose of the study drug was the same (1:1 [mg:mg]) as the Prograf dose received during the 30 day screening period. On day 7, the first 24-hour pharmacokinetic profile was taken (ending on day 8). Following completion of the pharmacokinetic sampling on day 8, patients were switched to once daily Advagraf on a 1:1 (mg:mg) total daily dose basis, and patients continued treatment until day 14. On day 14, the second 24-hour pharmacokinetic profile was taken (ending on day 15).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Part B comprised a long-term follow-up of 1 year to allow comparison of the safety and efficacy profiles of Advagraf vs Prograf post allograft transplantation.

Part C allowed optional (beyond 12 months), continued long-term provision of Advagraf as study medication to patients completing Part B to whom Advagraf was otherwise not currently commercially available.

## Methods

## Study participants

## Inclusion Criteria

A patient was eligible for the study if all of the following applied:

- 1) The patient was at least 5 years and not older than 16 years of age, inclusive.
- 2) The patient was able to swallow intact study drug capsules.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- 3) The patient received a single solid organ transplant at least 6 months prior to entry into the study.
- 4) The patient's parent(s), or their legal representative(s), were fully informed and gave written informed consent to participate in the study. The patient also gave assent where applicable.
- 5) The patient was receiving a Prograf based immunosuppressive regimen for a minimum of 3 months.
- 6) Female patients of child bearing potential provided a negative urine or serum pregnancy test prior to enrolment in the study.
- 7) Male and female patients who were sexually active agreed to practice effective birth control during the study. (Effective methods of birth control were defined as those which result in a low failure rate (CPMP/ICH/286/95 modified) of less than 1% per year when used consistently and correctly, such as implants, injectables, combined oral contraceptives, some intrauterine devices, sexual abstinence or vasectomized partner).

Additional inclusion criterion on day 1:

- 8) Patient had stable whole blood trough levels of tacrolimus in the range of 3.5 to 15 ng/mL measured on at least 2 separate occasions taken at least 6 days apart within 30 days prior to day 1 and was clinically stable in the opinion of the investigator.

## Exclusion Criteria

A patient was excluded from participation if any of the following applied at baseline:

- 1) The patient had received a multiple organ transplant.
- 2) The patient had experienced any rejection episode within 3 months prior to enrolment in the study, any rejection episode within 6 months prior to enrolment that required antilymphocyte antibody therapy, or 2 or more rejection episodes within 12 months prior to enrolment.
- 3) The patient was receiving rapamycin, everolimus or mycophenolic acid.
- 4) The patient had a chronic dysfunction of the allograft, in the opinion of the investigator.
- 5) The patient had major changes (addition, removal or substitution of any immunosuppressive agent from the regimen, not including dose changes of current immunosuppressive medications) in their immunosuppressive regimen within the last 3 months prior to entry into the study.
- 6) The patient had any unstable medical condition that could have interfered with the study objectives in the opinion of the investigator.
- 7) The patient was known to be positive for either hepatitis B virus, hepatitis C virus, or human immunodeficiency virus.
- 8) The patient was required to be treated with medication or substances known to interfere with tacrolimus metabolism, or had taken such medication within 28 days prior to inclusion.
- 9) The patient was pregnant or breast feeding.
- 10) The patient had any form of substance abuse, psychiatric disorder or condition which, in the opinion of the investigator, could have complicated communication with the investigator.
- 11) The patient was participating or had participated in another clinical trial and/or was taking or had taken an investigational/nonregistered drug in the 28 days prior to enrolment.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

12) The patient was unlikely to comply with the visits scheduled in the protocol.

## Treatments

## Treatments Administered and Selection and Timing of Doses

Patients were treated with Prograf and Advagraf in this study. For a complete characterization of Prograf please refer to the current SmPC. For a complete characterization of Advagraf, please refer to the current SmPC.

## Prograf (Parts A and B)

On day 1, after meeting an additional tacrolimus blood trough level inclusion criterion, patients received Prograf as study drug. The daily dose was the same (1:1, mg:mg) as the Prograf dose received during the screening phase. Prograf capsules were taken orally twice daily, morning and evening, on an empty stomach or at least 1 hour before, or 2 to 3 hours after any meal.

## Advagraf (Parts A, B or C)

All patients were converted from twice daily Prograf (study drug) to once daily Advagraf on a 1:1 (mg:mg) total daily dose basis on day 8, immediately after collection of the final 24 hour pharmacokinetic sample. The dose was maintained for the following 7 days (until day 14). Advagraf capsules were taken orally once daily only in the morning, on an empty stomach, or at least 1 hour before or, 2 to 3 hours after a meal.

For patients continuing into Part C, Advagraf was given in accordance with the investigator's clinical opinion. Other medication was at the discretion of the investigator.

## Objectives

The objectives of the study, by part (A, B, and C), are described below:

Part A: To obtain and compare data on the PK of tacrolimus for the extended-release formulation (Advagraf) with that of the immediate-release formulation (Prograf) in stable paediatric allograft recipients after 1:1 (mg:mg) conversion from Prograf to Advagraf.

Part B: To collect long-term safety and efficacy data for Advagraf over 12 months post conversion from Prograf, and to evaluate the relationship between AUC24 and area under the plasma concentration - time curve at 24 hours (C24) for Advagraf and for Prograf at each PK profile.

Part C: To allow long-term follow-up of Advagraf until Advagraf becomes available to these patients, or patient discontinuation, whichever is the earliest.

## Pharmacokinetic assessments

Whole blood samples were collected in Part A of the study to provide blood concentration-time profiles for pharmacokinetic evaluation. Samples for a 24-hour time period were collected 7 days after the starting the respective study treatment; on Day 7 the profile for Prograf was collected and on Day 14 the profile for Advagraf was collected.

Samples were collected at 0 h (predose) and 1, 2, 4, 6, 12, 13, 14, 16, 18, and 24 hours postdose. The 12 h sample was to be taken before the evening dose was administered for Prograf. The 24 h sample was taken before the morning dose was administered.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Diagnosis and Grading of Rejection Episodes

If clinical and/or laboratory signs indicated the occurrence of a rejection episode, a biopsy was performed, unless clinically contraindicated, prior to the initiation of any antirejection therapy and as soon as possible after the onset of clinical/laboratory signs indicative of possible rejection. The histological evaluation of the biopsy was performed by the local histopathologists following the 'Standardized Nomenclature of the International Society of Heart and Lung Transplantation', the 'Working Formulation For Classification and Grading of Pulmonary Allograft Rejection', the 'Banff '97 Diagnostic categories for renal allograft biopsies - Banff '07 update', the 'Histological Grading of Liver Biopsies for Rejection' or the 'Histological Criteria for Grading of Small Bowel Allograft Acute', as appropriate.

A BPAR episode was defined as any acute rejection episode confirmed by biopsy. For kidney transplant patients, findings at the biopsy of acute antibody-mediated rejection I, II, and III, acute T cell mediated rejection IA, IB, IIA, IIB and III, indicated a biopsy proven acute rejection. For liver transplant patients, the severity of BPAR was categorized using the Banff score as mild, moderate, severe or indeterminate/borderline. A non-BPAR episode was defined as any acute rejection episode not confirmed by biopsy.

## Classification of Acute Rejection Episodes

Acute rejection episodes were classified according to their rejection specific treatment as follows:

## Spontaneously Resolving Acute Rejection

A spontaneously resolving acute rejection was defined as a rejection episode that was not treated with new or increased corticosteroid medication, antibodies or any other medication, and that resolved irrespective of any tacrolimus dose changes.

## Corticosteroid Sensitive Acute Rejection

A corticosteroid sensitive acute rejection was defined as a rejection episode that was treated with new or increased corticosteroid medication only, and that resolved irrespective of any tacrolimus dose changes.

## Corticosteroid-resistant Acute Rejection

A corticosteroid-resistant acute rejection was defined as a rejection episode that did not resolve following treatment with corticosteroids. If a rejection episode was not treated with corticosteroids first, but only with antibodies, it was also included in this category. These episodes were further classified into episodes that resolved with further treatment and those that did not respond to further treatment, or that were ongoing at the time of study end or at patient withdrawal.

This category was divided into 3 subcategories:

- Resolved with further treatment, i.e. any acute rejection with an end date and a treatment other than corticosteroids was used

- Unresolved with further treatment; i.e. any acute rejection with no end date (ongoing at EoS or patient withdrawal) and a treatment other than corticosteroid was used.

- Unresolved with no further treatment, i.e. any acute rejection with no end date (ongoing at EoS or patient withdrawal) and only corticosteroid treatment was used.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

## Statistical Methods

Populations for Analysis

Full Analysis Set (FAS)

The FAS consisted of all patients who received at least 1 dose of any of the study drug (Prograf or Advagraf). The FAS was used for the efficacy and safety analyses of the Part A data only.

## Modified Full Analysis Set (mFAS)

The mFAS consisted of all patients who received at least 1 dose of Advagraf study drug. The mFAS was used for the efficacy and safety analyses of combined Part A + Part B data.

## Pharmacokinetics Analysis Set (PKAS)

The PKAS consisted of all patients who received at least 1 dose of study drug and who provided 2 complete pharmacokinetic profiles. Patients may have been excluded from the PKAS at the discretion of the pharmacokineticist. The PKAS was used for the summary and analysis of pharmacokinetic data in Part A.

## All Enrolled in Part C

All patients who qualified for participation in Part C. This was a subset of the FAS.

## Part B

The purpose of Part B of this study was to collect long-term safety and efficacy data for Advagraf over 12 months post-conversion from Prograf. On Day 15 of Part A, at the end of the second 24-hour PK profile, patients were administered an Advagraf-based immunosuppressive regimen for 52 additional weeks in Part B of the study. Part B covers the key safety and efficacy results during the entire period when patients were treated with Advagraf, during both Parts A and B.

## Part A and Part B

## Overview

After completion of Part B, the safety analyses of combined data of Part A and Part B was reported. No Part B data alone were reported.

The safety of tacrolimus in Part A + Part B of the study was assessed using:

- AEs
- Vital signs (blood pressure, pulse, body temperature, and respiratory rate)
- Clinical laboratory (haematology and biochemistry)

The modified full analysis set (mFAS) consisted of all patients who received at least 1 dose of Advagraf study drug. The mFAS was used for the efficacy and safety analyses of combined Part A + Part B data.

## Patient disposition and exposure

Seventy-nine patients were included in the mFAS; 48 kidney transplant patients, 29 liver transplant patients, and 2 other transplant patients. Of these, 76 patients completed Part B.

Exposure to Advagraf began in Part A and continued during the long-term follow-up in Part B. For the Part A + Part B combined report, the duration of exposure on Advagraf treatment was calculated as the difference between the Advagraf last dose date (Visit 10/EoS date) and the Advagraf first dose date (Visit 3 date) plus 1 day. A data cut-off date (28 Oct 2016) was applied in order to report efficacy and safety analyses at the end of Part B.

The mean (SD) duration of exposure to Advagraf was 361.01 (53.30) days, with the liver and kidney transplant recipient groups within 8 days of each other. The majority of patients (88.6%) received Advagraf for between 253 and 378 days.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

Advagraf is a prolonged-release tacrolimus-containing medicinal product. It is indicated in adults for the prevention of graft rejection following allograft transplantation.

Advagraf was originally authorised in 2007, and as such was not subject to the provisions of the Paediatric Regulation (EC) No 1901/2006 which would otherwise have placed an obligation to investigate the characteristics of the product in the paediatric population for this condition. As such, no recommendations on dose or posology were made regarding the paediatric population, and so the product was not recommended for use in this population for this condition.

Conversion of stable adult patients from Prograf to Advagraf on a 1:1 (mg:mg) total daily dose basis is recommended clinical practice, however the transferability of this approach to paediatric patients is not currently known.

The MAH has submitted a phase 2 study examining the PK, efficacy and safety of paediatric allograft recipients who converted from a Prograf-based immunosuppressive regimen to an Advagraf-based immunosuppressive regimen. Prograf is licenced for prophylaxis of transplant rejection in liver, kidney or heart allograft recipients. Advagraf is licenced for prophylaxis of transplant rejection in adult kidney or liver allograft recipients only, patients in the trial were allowed to enroll with any solid organ transplant.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Both tacrolimus products are also licensed for treatment of allograft rejection resistant to treatment with other immunosuppressive medicinal products.

Patients had to be 6 months post single transplant, aged between 5 and 16 and stable on a Prograf for 3 months. After 7 days of Prograf as a study drug, patients were switched from Prograf to Advagraf on a 1:1 (mg:mg) total daily dose basis, this is in line with the recommended switching posology as per the Advagraf SmPC.

The primary objective of the trial was PK based, comparing steady-state tacrolimus AUC(24) between Advagraf and Prograf in stable paediatric transplant recipients after a 1:1 dose conversion.

81 patients were enrolled in part A of the study, including n=48 kidney transplants, n=31 liver transplants and n=2 other solid organ transplants. 79 patents were included in the mFAS and completed part A. The mean age was 11.5 years old, 58% were male and 88.9% were white. The underrepresentation of patients of other ethnicities is noted which may limit generalisability to more diverse populations or less common transplant groups including patients with heart transplants.

The majority of patients (n=76/79) also completed part B. A lower number of patients went on to enroll in part C (n=46/76). Demographics were similar across all parts of the study.

## PK results

The study design for Part A of the study was mostly acceptable for the objective to compare the PK of tacrolimus for the extended-release formulation (Advagraf) with that of the immediate release formulation (Prograf) following a 1:1 (mg:mg) conversion from Prograf to Advagraf. The sampling time-points after 7 days of each study treatment are considered sufficient, and acceptable to compare the two formulations. It is noted that for the comparison between Prograf and Advagraf exposure parameters that a 90% confidence interval for the ratio of geometric least squares means within the interval of 80% to 125% was considered to show similar exposure by the MAH. Given that tacrolimus is a narrow therapeutic index drug, it would have been appropriate to tighten the acceptance interval to 90 to 111.11% for the AUC in line with the guideline on the investigation of bioequivalence (CPMP/EWP/QWP/1401/98 Rev. 1/ Corr **).

The PK results of Part A demonstrate similar exposure parameters for tacrolimus following administration of Prograf and Advagraf. The MAH tested two statistical models for the analysis and demonstrated minimal differences. The statistical comparison of the pharmacokinetic parameters for the overall data between Advagraf and Prograf showed a geometric mean (90% CI) of 96.66% (92.31 - 101.22%) for AUC24, 93.69% (87.07 - 100.81%) for Cmax, and 90.39% (85.00 - 96.13%) for C24.

The comparison in subjects receiving treatment for kidney transplantation demonstrated overall lower exposure parameters than the mean, and the 90% CI for Cmax (77.02 - 91.36%) fell below the acceptance interval. For subjects receiving treatment for liver transplantation the exposure parameters were slightly higher than the overall mean. The test for treatment-by-organ interaction was significant for AUC24 and Cmax. The MAH has discussed this observed interaction and indicates that the small groups of patients receiving liver, kidney and other (heart) transplants was not balanced for demographics or genetic characteristics, and that demographic differences were apparent. It can be accepted that caution should be taken in interpreting these results.

The comparison between Advagraf and Prograf parameters stratified by age demonstrated that the 90% CI for AUC24, Cmax, and C4 in subjects aged 5 to 7 and for Cmax and C24 in subjects aged 8 to

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

10 years fell below the MAH acceptance criteria of 80 - 125%. No interaction of age of the transplant patient was shown for any parameters.

Overall, tacrolimus exposure was slightly lower overall following conversion from Prograf to Advagraf in the overall comparison, and in patients with kidney transplants and subjects aged 5 to &lt; 14 years. A similar trend has also been observed in adult studies. This is not considered a concern given the posology of Advagraf indicates careful monitoring of treatment and dose adjustment if necessary.

The PK results from this study demonstrate overall comparable PK between Advagraf and Prograf. However, given the concerns highlighted above i.e. acceptance interval, lower exposure in specific groups, and the statistically significant difference for treatment-by-organ AUC24 and Cmax, the PK results from this study would not be sufficient alone to support the paediatric indication for Advagraf. Considering the MAH is not seeking a paediatric indication claim for Advgraf the highlighted issues are not pursued. Given that both Prograf hard capsules and Modigraf prolonged-release granules are available with the paediatric indication, it is considered acceptable to not seek the paediatric indication for Advagraf.

## Efficacy results

There were no cases of rejection episodes (n=0/81, 0%) and all patients and grafts survived in part A of the study.

In part B of the study, there were 2 cases of acute rejection (n=2/79, 2.5%), both were also listed as SAEs. One case of a BPAR occurred on day 281, 273 days after the start of Advagraf therapy in a kidney transplant patient, identified as acute T-cell-mediated rejection, grade IB and acute antibodymediated rejection, grade II and was corticosteroid-resistant. The second case also occurred in a kidney transplant patient, was not biopsy proven, occurred on day 145, 136 days after the start of Advagraf therapy and was treatable with corticosteroids and plasmapheresis. There were 3 cases of efficacy failure, including the BPAR discussed above, and 2 cases with unknown outcomes. All patients and grafts survived in part B of the study.

In part C, there was one case (1/46, 2.2%) of acute rejection in a kidney transplant patient, that was not biopsy proven, occurred after treatment with Advagraf for 1050 days (2.9 years) and was resolved. No other efficacy data was collected or analysed.

While the approval of Advagraf was mainly based on treatment of patients with de novo transplants, a number of phase II studies examined the conversion of twice daily Prograf to Advagraf. The number of biopsy confirmed acute rejections up to 2 years post conversion was in the range of 0 to 6% (kidney, liver and heart transplants) (Advagraf EPAR). In a study in 18 stable paediatric liver patients converted from Prograf to Advagraf there were no cases of acute rejection or graft loss up to one year after conversion (Heffron TG, 2007) 1 .

Overall, the number of rejection episodes throughout parts A, B and C of the was low study (n=0-2, 0-2.5%) and in the range expected for adult patients.

## Safety results

In Part A + B of the study, 79 participants were exposed to once-daily Advagraf treatment for up to 12 months. The majority of patients (88.6%) received Advagraf for between 253 and 378 days with a mean (SD) duration of exposure of 361.01 (53.30) days. Forty-six patients contributed long-term follow-up safety data in Part C of the study.

Part C was an open-label, long-term follow-up available at the discretion of the investigator to patients who had completed Part B and to whom Advagraf was otherwise not commercially available. ## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

The MAH is not proposing any updates to the product information based on this submission. Based on the PK data presented in Part A of Study PMR-EC-1206, it is agreed that the use of Advagraf in paediatric patients is not supported. Concerns regarding the use of an appropriate acceptance interval, lower exposure in specific groups, and a statistically significant difference in treatment organ preclude an indication for use in paediatric patients from a PK perspective.

The number of rejection episodes throughout parts A, B and C of the was low study (n=0-2, 0-2.5%) and in the range expected for adult patients.

Based on data presented in the final study reports, no new safety concerns have been identified from the completion of Study PMR-EC-1206. Overall, although the data set is too limited to draw robust conclusions, the findings from this study in paediatric allograft recipients is generally consistent with known safety profile for Advagraf in the adult population.

The MAH has not proposed any updates to product information and does not intend to pursue a paediatric indication for Advagraf. Given that both Prograf hard capsules and Modigraf prolongedrelease granules are presentations of tacrolimus with a paediatric indication there is no clinical need for a paediatric indication for Advagraf. The CHMP agrees that no updates are required based on the data submitted in this procedure.

## 7. Assessment of applicant response to 2 nd Request for supplementary information

## Question 1

One patient had peritoneal dialysis and renal allograft loss categorized as an \"Other\" reason for discontinuation. The MAH is asked to provide rationale for why this discontinuation was categorized as \"Other\" and not \"Retransplantation, graft loss or acute rejection episodes\" as per the protocol. Additionally, the narrative for this patient and assessment of causality is requested.

## Summary of the MAH's response

After reviewing the CRF options for withdrawal for Part C of the study, it was noted that \"Retransplantation / Graft loss\" was not available as a selectable discontinuation option. Therefore, \"Other\" was considered the most appropriate option. Additional rationale was then added via the freetext explanation: \"peritoneal dialysis and renal allograft loss\".

On Day 125, the patient experienced procedural pain of moderate intensity from a biopsy and was admitted to the hospital. The event was serious and treatment included paracetamol. A percutaneous renal transplant biopsy showed severe chronic changes, severe chronic vascular changes, and severe chronic allograft coagulopathy. It is reported that the patient was losing their renal allograft, and the reason for this was multifactorial with the biopsy being undertaken for chronic allograft dysfunction.

On Day 184, the patient experienced hypertension and blood creatinine increased. Both events were serious and were severe in intensity. The patient was admitted to hospital and treatment initiated. On Day 189, the patient underwent peritoneal dialysis, and fluid intake was reduced. The catheter insertion was uncomplicated, and the patient followed the procedure well. Blood creatinine remained high. The event hypertension resolved on Day 191, and the patient was discharged from the hospital with view to starting peritoneal dialysis on Day 195, once peritoneal dialysis catheter was fully formed.

On Day 195, the patient experienced renal transplant failure which was severe and serious, and the patient was admitted to the hospital. It was reported that the event 'procedural pain from biopsy' was related to the biopsy procedure. The investigator provided failing renal graft and stage 5 chronic kidney disease as alternative aetiology for the events renal transplant failure, blood creatinine increased, and hypertension.

The patient was permanently discontinued from the study on Day 212. The patient reported 17 AEs during Part C of the study, of which 4 were serious (procedural pain from biopsy, blood creatinine increased, hypertension, and renal transplant failure). None of the events were considered related to treatment with Advagraf.

## Assessment of the MAH's response

After review, the Sponsor confirmed that \"Retransplantation, graft loss or acute rejection episodes\" was not included in the CFR as an option for reason for withdrawal, as per protocol. This explains why the reason \"Other\" was given for this patient, with \"peritoneal dialysis and renal allograft loss\" provided as free text to further elaborate the reason for withdrawal.

Regarding the assessment of causality for this patient, the majority of reported AEs were mild, not related, and had an outcome of resolved (weight gain, torn ligament, nose bleeds, head cold, high temperature, and sore stomach). Of those events that were not resolved, the reported AEs, including acidosis, hyperkalaemia, worsening anaemia, hyperphosphatemia, and increased blood creatinine, were clinically consistent with declining renal allograft function. In view of the patient's renal allograft loss and subsequent initiation of peritoneal dialysis, these events are considered likely attributable to the underlying graft failure rather than representing a distinct treatment related safety signal.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The investigator's assessment that the event 'procedural pain from biopsy' did not have a causal relationship and was attributed to procedural complications is agreed. The assessment of the events of blood creatinine increased, hypertension, and renal transplant failure as not causally related to Advagraf treatment is also accepted.

## Conclusion

This issue is considered fully resolved.
