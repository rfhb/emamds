## 1. Introduction

On 30 April 2025, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study, Study CP-4-006 LT-OLE (C0311009), for Ngenla, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

Study CP-4-006 was part of the MAH's clinical development program for MOD-4023 (somatrogon).

Study CP-4-006 was a Phase 3, open label, randomized, multi-centre, 12 months, efficacy and safety study of weekly somatrogon compared to daily Genotropin (somatropin) therapy in pre-pubertal children with growth hormone deficiency (GHD). Eligible patients who successfully completed the 12month main study could enrol in a single-arm LT-OLE treatment period with somatrogon (until authorisation) regardless of which treatment they received in the main study.

Study CP-4-006 was the pivotal study submitted in support of the initial marketing authorisation application (MAA), with the main study having been completed (19 April 2017) and the OLE ongoing at that time. Data from the earlier phase of the OLE were submitted in support of the MAA (cut-off date 01 November 2019).

The MAH states that Study CP-4-006 is a standalone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

In Study CP-4-006, somatrogon was provided as a solution for injection in 20 or 50 mg/ml strengths in a single-patient multi-dose disposable pre-filled PEN.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## 2.3.1. Introduction

The MAH has provided a supplemental abbreviated (Last Patient Last Visit) clinical study report with final study results for Study CP-4-006 (LT-OLE); a phase 3, open-label, randomized, multicenter, 12 months, efficacy and safety study of weekly somatrogon compared to daily somatropin therapy in prepubertal children with growth hormone deficiency.

## 2.3.2. Clinical Study

## Description

Study ID: Study CP-4-006 (C0311009)

Study title: A phase 3, open-label, randomized, multicenter, 12 months, efficacy and safety study of weekly MOD-4023 compared to daily Genotropin therapy in pre-pubertal children with growth hormone deficiency.

## Methods

The study was divided into 2 parts: Main Study Period (main study) and LT-OLE Period. The main study was a 12-months, open-label, multi-centre, randomized, active controlled, parallel group study comparing efficacy and safety of weekly somatrogon to daily GH, somatropin. Eligible patients who successfully completed the 12-month main study could enrol in a single-arm LT-OLE treatment period with somatrogon regardless of which treatment they received in the main study. See Figure 1 below.

## Study participants

Prepubertal children (boys 3-11 years, girls 3-10 years) diagnosed with GHD who had no prior exposure to any r-hGH therapy, had impaired height and HV, and with a baseline IGF-1 level of at least 1 SD below the mean IGF-1 level standardized for age and sex (IGF-1 SDS ≤ -1) were enrolled in this study.

Patients who successfully completed 12 months of treatment in the main study and continued to meet inclusion/exclusion criteria were eligible to continue treatment with somatrogon in the OLE period.

Inclusion criteria specific to the OLE included:

- Completion of the main study (12 months of treatment) with adequate compliance.
- Willing and able to provide written informed consent of the parent or legal guardian of the patient and written assent from pediatric patients (where applicable based on age and country regulation).
- Agreement to refrain from sexual activity during the LT-OLE, i.e. observe complete sexual abstinence as the only acceptable contraceptive measure during the LT-OLE (for pubertal and post-pubertal patients).

## Exclusion criteria specific to the OLE included:

- Concomitant administration of other treatments that may have an effect on growth such as anabolic steroids, or sex steroids (other than for hormonal replacement), with the exception of ADHD drugs or hormone replacement therapies (thyroxin, hydrocortisone, testosterone, oestrogen/progesterone, desmopressin [DDAVP])

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Change in medical condition during the treatment period (such as, but not limited to, development of a serious inter-current critical illness, a severe adverse drug reaction, etc.)
- Unresolved drug related (MOD-4023 or Genotropin®) SAE from the treatment period as per Medical Monitor judgement.

## Treatments

Somatrogon was provided as a solution for SC injection containing 20 or 50 mg/mL somatrogon in a single patient use, multi-dose, disposable pre-filled pen (PEN). Somatrogon was administered, preferably (but not required) in the morning hours once weekly, into the upper arms, buttocks, thighs, or abdomen (8 locations).

At the start of the LT-OLE period, patients who completed 12 months of somatropin treatment in the main study switched to somatrogon (0.66 mg/kg/week), while those who had received somatrogon for 12 months continued at the same dose that they had been receiving.

The dose of somatrogon was adjusted every 3 months in the LT-OLE, based on body weight, and dose decreases were permitted for safety reasons (e.g., AE or repeated IGF-1 values &gt;2 SDS).

## Objective

The LT-OLE objective was to assess the long-term efficacy, safety and immunogenicity of somatrogon.

## Outcomes/endpoints

Below are the endpoints analysed as per the supplemental CSR for the LT-OLE period:

Efficacy Endpoints:

- Annualized HV (cm/year): Annualized HV at each 12 months, based on the difference between the height at each 12 months and the height at the previous 12 months
- Change in height SDS, calculated as the difference between the height SDS at each scheduled visit in the LT-OLE period and the height SDS at the main period baseline
- Change in bone maturation at each 12-month visit in the LT-OLE period
- IGF-1 levels and IGF-1 SDS levels on Day 4 (-1) after somatrogon dosing at every scheduled visit in the LT-OLE Year 1 and at every 6 months thereafter. Change in IGF-1 SDS, calculated as the difference between the IGF-1 SDS values in the LT-OLE period and the IGF-1 SDS at the main period baseline.
- IGFBP-3 levels and IGFBP-3 SDS levels on Day 4 (-1) after somatrogon dosing at every scheduled visit in the LT-OLE Year 1 and at every 6 months thereafter. Change in IGFBP-3 SDS, calculated as the difference between the IGFBP-3 SDS values in the LT-OLE period and the IGFBP-3 SDS at the main period baseline.

Safety Endpoints:

- Incidence of AEs and SAEs
- Laboratory data
- Vital signs

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Incidence of ADA (including the characterization of the Ab and neutralizing properties).

## Sample size

Approximately 220 eligible patients were planned to be included in the main study and randomized 1:1 to the weekly somatrogon arm or the daily somatropin comparator arm. Eligible patients that completed the main study would be allowed to continue in the LT-OLE.

A total of 228 patients were randomized and 224 received at least 1 dose of study intervention in the 12-month main study; 4 patients did not receive study drug and withdrew from the study.

Of 222 patients who completed the 12-month main study, 212 entered the LT-OLE period Year 1.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

In the main study, eligible patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either somatrogon or somatropin treatment arms for 12 months. Eligible patients who successfully completed the 12-month main study could enrol in a single-arm LT-OLE treatment period with somatrogon regardless of which treatment they received in the main study.

This was an open label study.

## Statistical Methods

The assessment of safety and efficacy (auxology/clinical and biochemical endpoints) during the LT-OLE was based on descriptive statistics and summarized by patient's originally randomized treatment and overall. No hypothesis testing was performed. The descriptive statistics included means, SDs, quartiles/ranges for continuous variables, and counts with percentages for categorical data. 95% CI was used to further describe the clinical endpoints.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

In accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No 1901/2006, the MAH has submitted the final study data for Study CP-4-006 (LT-OLE). Results from this Phase 3, open label, randomised, multi-centre, 12 months, efficacy and safety study of weekly somatrogon, administered s/c in a pre-filled pen device, compared to daily somatropin therapy in pre-pubertal children with GHD were submitted in support of the initial MAA for somatrogon while the OLE phase was ongoing.

The objective of Study CP-4-006 OLE was to assess the long-term efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity of somatrogon in the 6-year OLE period.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The full analysis set, and safety analysis set included 212 participants who entered the OLE period of the study and took at least 1 complete dose of somatrogon.

## Efficacy

From an efficacy perspective, patients treated in the OLE period of the Phase 3 study demonstrated sustained improvement in clinical parameters of growth. Over the 6-years, the mean (SD) annualized HV remained stable in OLE Years 1 and 2, as would be expected as patients aged, and slowly decreased thereafter. This trend was similar between groups irrespective of initial main study randomisation to somatrogon or somatropin.

Height SDS improved progressively over the LT-OLE years and were comparable irrespective of initial main study randomisation.

Similarly, increases in mean annual bone maturation were observed throughout the study. Bone maturation with weekly somatrogon did not advance discordantly relative to advancement in chronological age.

Mean absolute serum IGF-1 levels generally increased across OLE period visits, as did mean IGF-1 SDS values, while remaining within normal range.

No clinically meaningful differences were observed in annualised HV and other growth parameters between ADA-positive and ADA-negative patients throughout the OLE period.

## Safety

In Study CP-4-006 OLE, the mean (SD) duration of somatrogon treatment during OLE period was similar between groups randomised initially to somatrogon and somatropin, 1237 (400) and 1180 (500) days, respectively.

Of the 222 patients who completed the 12-month main study, 212 entered OLE Year 1. The number of participants who completed 12-Month OLE period Year 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 was 205 (96.7%), 174 (82.1%), 166 (78.3%), 72 (34.0%), 4 (1.9%), and 0 (0%), respectively. Completion rates were generally high in Years 1 to 3 and declined significantly in Years 4 and 5 as the sponsor discontinued the study and somatrogon became available as an authorised medicinal product. Although a discrepancy is noted in the reporting of the number of patients who discontinued the study due to an adverse event in the supplemental CSR (Tables 3 and 6, corresponding to Tables 1 and 3, respectively, of this report), this matter will not be pursued further. It appears from tabulated data (supplemental CSR Table 14.3.1.1.5.1) that study discontinuation due to an adverse event was highest in OLE Year 1 (7 participants). Subsequently, 2 patients discontinued due to an adverse event in Year 2.

With regards to adverse events, the incidence of all-causality TEAEs was highest in OLE Years 1, 2 and 3 (~72%), with a higher incidence rate observed in OLE Year 1 in those patients initially randomised to somatropin versus somatrogon. The incidence of all-causality TEAEs declined in Years 4 (54.3%) and 5 (53.8%) and none was reported in Year 6.

The most commonly recorded all-causality TEAEs during the OLE period were reported under the SOCs General disorders and administration site conditions, Infections and Infestations, and Gastrointestinal disorders.

The majority of all-causality TEAEs were considered mild or moderate in severity. The incidence of severe TEAEs was highest in Year 1 (5.7%) and declined thereafter, with no severe TEAEs being reported in Years 4 through 6.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The incidence of treatment-related TEAEs generally declined with time in the OLE study (31.6%, 22.6%, 17.1%, 12.3%, 17.3% and 0% for OLE Years 1 to 6, respectively), with a higher incidence rate observed in OLE Year 1 in those patients initially randomized to somatropin versus somatrogon (41.7% versus 21.2%). The most common treatmentrelated TEAEs by PT that occurred in ≥5% of participants in the OLE period were the PTs Injection site pain and Injection site erythema.

There were no deaths reported in the OLE period. There were 19 reported treatment-emergent SAEs reported in 14 patients, with the highest incidence in Year 1. No treatment-emergent SAEs were considered by the investigator to be related to study intervention. Except for Appendicitis, no SAE, by PT, was reported more than once in the OLE study.

Adrenal cortical hypofunction, arthralgia, glucose metabolism impairment, immunogenicity, injection site reactions, musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders, neoplasms, pancreatitis, scoliosis, thyroid function impairment were evaluated as AESIs during the Phase 3 study.

The incidence of patients reporting AESIs by PT was &lt;5%, except for injection site pain (25.9%, 19.8%, 14.7%, 10.5%, 15.4% in OLE Years 1 - 5) and vomiting (7.1% in OLE Year 1). Most of the reported AESIs were mild to moderate in severity, with the incidence of severe AESIs being low (3.8%, 2.3%, 1.2% in OLE Years 1, 2, 3 respectively; none reported in OLE Years 4 - 6).

The incidence of study discontinuation due to a TEAE was generally low. Treatment-related TEAEs leading to permanent study discontinuation were reported in 5 participants in OLE Year 1. All related to injection site reactions.

Overall, no notable trends of concern were observed in the reporting of AEs, SAEs or AESIs in the OLE period of the Phase 3 trial. The incidence of all generally declined during the OLE period. No new safety concerns were identified.

Similarly for clinical laboratory, vital signs and physical examinations, no notable trends of concern were observed. While a high incidence of laboratory test abnormalities (without regard for baseline) was observed for basophils and eosinophils, these did not result in notable adverse event reporting. Clinical laboratory findings, including glycaemic, hormonal, and lipid parameters, were generally stable throughout somatrogon treatment.

From an immunogenicity perspective, the overall incidence of ADA positivity was high, but declining with time; 65.6%, 55.4%, 61.2%, 53.7%, 42.3%, respectively, in OLE Years 1 - 5. However, ADA positivity was shown to have no discernible influence on safety outcomes as measured by incidence of TEAEs, SAEs, AEs leading to discontinuation or on the incidence of AESIs.

Overall, the submission of final data for this LT-OLE study did not give rise to any new concerns with respect to the long-term safety and efficacy of somatrogon in the treatment of children and adolescents from 3 years of age with growth disturbance due to insufficient secretion of growth hormone. Despite the high incidence of injection site reactions, annual completion rates in the OLE period were high and somatrogon appeared to be well tolerated and showed sustained improvement in clinical parameters of growth.

Longer-term safety (incidence rates of neoplasm, diabetes type 2, immunogenicity, medication errors) is subject to ongoing evaluation as part of the ongoing non-interventional Post Authorisation Safety Study (PASS), 'An Active Surveillance Study to Monitor the Real-World Long-Term Safety of Somatrogon Among Paediatric Patients in Europe', which was imposed as an Annex IID condition to the Marketing Authorisation for Ngenla. The evaluation of long-term efficacy (by measuring IGF-1 levels and height in paediatric patients treated with somatrogon) is listed as a secondary objective of this PASS for which the first interim study report, dated 04 November 2024, has been reviewed by PRAC (Reference: EMEA/H/C/005633/MEA/001.3).
