## 1.7. CHMP outcome

## 1.7.1. Considerations related to paediatrics

The requirements for the submitted dossier in relation to paediatrics are described in section 1.4. of this report. In this procedure, the CHMP reviewed the available paediatric data of studies conducted in accordance with the agreed PIP P/0068/2023, namely studies KALOS and LOGOS. The results of these studies are reflected in the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) and, as appropriate, the Package Leaflet for Riltrava Aerosphere 5μg /14.4μg/16μg, pressurised inhalation, suspension. Further, these results were pivotal in supporting this application. EMA has deferred the paediatric development of this medicinal product. The relevant paediatric statement has been included in Section 5.1 of the SmPC.

## 1.7.2. Opinion

Based on the CHMP review of data on quality, safety and efficacy, the CHMP considers by consensus decision that the benefit-risk balance of Riltrava Aerosphere 5μg/14.4μg/160μg, in the maintenance treatment of asthma in patients 12 years of age and older who are not adequately controlled by a combination of a medium dose inhaled corticosteroid and a long-acting beta2-agonist, is favourable.

## 1.7.2.1. Periodic safety update reports

## 2. Introduction

## Therapeutic Context

Asthma is a serious and life-threatening heterogeneous disease that affects adults and children with a prevalence of 1% to 29% worldwide (Global Initiative for Asthma; GINA 2024), characterised by wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, cough, variable expiratory airflow limitation and chronic airway inflammation. Symptoms and airflow obstruction are triggered by various factors, including exercise, allergen or irritant exposure, changes in weather, or viral respiratory infections. These may resolve spontaneously or in response to medication, or they may worsen resulting in an asthma exacerbation that in some cases, might be life-threatening. The long-term goals of asthma management are achieving symptom control, maintaining normal activity levels, and minimising the risk of asthma-related mortality, exacerbations, persistent airflow limitation, and treatment side effects. The GINA guideline describes 2 tracks, both of which specify 5 treatment steps for asthma, each step outlining options for higher levels of treatment for controlling asthma. Within this stepwise approach, GINA proposes a classification of asthma severity based on the type and intensity of medications required to control the disease (Steps 1 to 5). Asthma reliever therapies alleviate acute symptoms. Controller therapies aim to improve asthma symptom control by dampening chronic inflammation. This classification of asthma severity is then assessed retrospectively once the patient is on regular controller treatment for several months. The level of asthma control obtained with treatment determines the need to step up or step down to the next treatment step to achieve optimum control with the minimum amount of medication. Most asthma patients (GINA Step 1-3 patients) can achieve and maintain clinical control with standard regimens including maintenance ICS with or without inhaled LABAs. For patients whose asthma is not well controlled despite the use of ICS with or without a LABA (GINA Step 4&amp;5 patients), treatment options include adding a LAMA such as tiotropium and, in certain subsets of patients, biologic agents (e.g. benralizumab, omalizumab, mepolizumab), bronchial thermoplasty, and low-dose oral corticosteroids.

LAMAs can provide an important step up in care before escalation to systemic or invasive therapies.

## 2.1. Aspects of development

The BGF MDI clinical development programme was conducted to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the ICS/LAMA/LABA triple therapy combination products, BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg and BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg. The two pivotal studies supporting this submission in the use of BGF MDI in patients with inadequately controlled asthma are the global phase 3 studies KALOS and LOGOS. The differences between the two were their sub-studies. In addition to the pivotal studies, 2 supportive studies provide information on the qualified comparator (LITHOS and VATHOS studies for BFF MDI), and another 2 supportive studies (PT001101 and PT001102) were glycopyrronium dose-ranging studies that helped determine the two glycopyrronium doses selected for evaluation in KALOS and LOGOS. Results from VATHOS (D5982C00006) and LITHOS (D5982C00005), provided evidence to qualify BFF MDI as the active ICS/LABA comparator to BGF MDI in KALOS and LOGOS and showed the added benefits of the components to the overall effects of the BGF triple combination.

## 2.2. Description of the product

A different strength of formoterol /glycopyrronium bromide /budesonide, with HFO-1234ze(E) propellant, is already approved in the EU for the maintenance treatment in adult patients with moderate to severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) who are not adequately treated by a combination of an inhaled corticosteroid and a long-acting beta2-agonist or combination of a long-acting beta2-agonist and a long-acting muscarinic antagonist. Proposed indication: Riltrava Aerosphere is to be indicated as a maintenance treatment of asthma in patients 12 years of age and older who are not adequately controlled by a combination of a medium or high dose inhaled corticosteroid and a long-acting beta2-agonist. Proposed posology: the recommended and maximum dose is two inhalations twice daily (two inhalations in the morning and two inhalations in the evening). Patients are advised to take Riltrava Aerosphere every day even when asymptomatic. If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible, and the next dose should be taken at the usual time. A double dose should not be taken to make up for a forgotten dose. Mechanism of action: Budesonide is a glucocorticosteroid which when inhaled has a rapid (within hours) and dose dependent anti-inflammatory action in the airways. Glycopyrronium is a long-acting, muscarinic antagonist, which is often referred to as an anticholinergic. The major targets for anticholinergic drugs are muscarinic receptors located in the respiratory tract. In the airways, it exhibits pharmacological effects through inhibition of M3 receptor at the smooth muscle leading to bronchodilation. Antagonism is competitive and reversible. Prevention of methylcholine and acetylcholine-induced bronchoconstrictive effects was dose-dependent and lasted more than 12 hours. Formoterol is a selective β2-adrenergic agonist that when inhaled results in rapid and long-acting relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle in patients with reversible airways obstruction. The bronchodilating effect is dose dependent, with an onset of effect within 1-3 minutes after inhalation. The duration of effect is at least 12 hours after a single dose.

## 3.1. Introduction

This is a line extension application for a new strength of the medicinal product Riltrava.

Each single actuation (delivered dose, ex-actuator) of the currently authorised Riltrava is a pressurised suspension for inhalation containing 5 micrograms of formoterol fumarate dihydrate, glycopyrronium bromide 9 micrograms, equivalent to 7.2 micrograms of glycopyrronium, and 160 micrograms of budesonide per delivered dose (the dose leaving the mouthpiece of the inhaler) and it is indicated as a maintenance treatment in adult patients with moderate to severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) who are not adequately treated by a combination of an inhaled corticosteroid and a long-acting beta2-agonist or combination of a long-acting beta2-agonist and a long-acting muscarinic antagonist.

## 3.3. Finished medicinal product

## 3.3.1. Description of the product and pharmaceutical development

The finished product is a pressurised inhalation suspension, also referred as pressurised metered dose inhaler (MDI), containing three active substances (budesonide, glycopyrronium bromide and formoterol fumarate dihydrate) in a fixed-dose combination, suspended, thanks to the presence of 1,2-distearoyl-snglycero-3-phosphocholine and calcium chloride dihydrate, which constitute the 'porous particles', in a in a medium of norflurane (a hydrofluoroalkane (HFA) propellant, HFA-134a).

The finished product is presented as a white suspension.

The container closure system consists of a fluorinated ethylene propylene coated aluminium can sealed with a retention type metering valve that delivers a nominal 50μL metered dose upon actuation. The can and valve assembly are referred to as the canister. A shield-style dose indicator is attached to the top of the canister and the canister/dose indicator assembly is fitted with a yellow polypropylene actuator and grey plastic dust cap. The product is foil overwrapped in a foil laminate pouch containing a desiccant sachet and packed into a carton.

A minimum of 130 actuations per canister are considered necessary as four priming actuations are performed prior to first use and 120 inhalations are intended for patient use. Six re-priming actuations are performed after weekly cleaning (2 priming actuations after weeks 1, 2 and 3 of continuous use). The product is formulated to contain approximately 171 actuations.

The only change to the formulation is the increase in the content of glycopyrronium, which is double the quantity included in the authorised product. The same excipients and the same quantities of excipients are used in the manufacture of the authorised finished product. All excipients are well known pharmaceutical ingredients. There are no novel excipients used in the finished product formulation. The list of excipients is included in section 6.1 of the SmPC and in paragraph 3.1 of this report.

The pharmaceutical development was aimed to minimise the risks associated with this change. In terms of characterisation of the inhaler, only temperature cycling and verification of the appropriateness of the proposed patient instructions have been performed. The temperature cycling studies showed that changes in the storage temperature did not have any impact on the performance of the finished product. The patient use simulation studies showed that the proposed instruction for use, which are the same as those approved for the BGF MDI product, are adequate for the BGF+ MDI formulation. The applied product is indicated for adolescent patients from 12 to 17 years of age and its compliance with \"Guideline on pharmaceutical development of medicines for paediatric use\" (EMA/CHMP/QWP/805880/2012 Rev. 2) has been confirmed. Compliance with the 'Guideline on the Pharmaceutical Quality of Inhalation and Nasal Products' was already demonstrated for the authorised product.

The applicant has confirmed that the formulation and the manufacturing process intended for the commercial product are the same as those used in the clinical studies. The only change made to the product is the colour of the actuator, which is further discussed later in this report. In the SmPC, it is indicated that patients with difficulties in the coordination of actuation with inhalation may use the inhaler with a spacer to ensure proper administration of the medicinal product. The Aerochamber Plus Flow-Vu is specified in the SmPC.

The delivered dose and fine particle mass of budesonide and formoterol fumarate have been determined for the BGF+ MDI batches used in the phase III clinical batches and compared to those obtained from the dual formulation BFF MDI used in the same studies. The mean differences between the triple BGF+ and dual BFF MDI products are within 10% for both budesonide and formoterol delivered dose and fine particle mass. During the procedure, a major objection (MO) was raised by the CHMP requesting additional characterisation studies for the increased dose of glycopyrronium. The following information was requested in the MO: a. The delivered dose of glycopyrronium obtained from clinical batches of BGF+ MDI used in the pivotal phase III studies; b. full aerodynamic particle size distribution (APSD) profiles of glycopyrronium obtained from clinical batches of BGF+ MDI with and without the use of the Aerochamber Plus Flow-Vu spacer; c. in-vitro therapeutic equivalence should be demonstrated for all the active substances in the finished product with and without the use of the above-mentioned spacer. The MO was satisfactorily addressed by providing information on the delivered dose of glycopyrronium from the clinical batches of BGF+ MDI. It was confirmed that it is double the quantity delivered by the currently authorised BGF MDI product. Batch-to-batch consistency between clinical and registration batches has been fully demonstrated with and without the use of the spacer by provision of APSD data, delivered dose uniformity and spacer characterisation. The same overage used in the authorised strength is proposed for the BFI+ MDI product, which has been adequately justified by characterisation studies on the actuator/mouthpiece deposition. The container closure system proposed for the BGF+ MDI product is the same as that used for the authorised product. As the extractable profile is expected to be the same, it is acceptable to use the extraction studies performed on the BGF MDI as the starting point for the leachable study on the higher strength product. The leachables profile of the BGF+ MDI product has been well characterised and it has been demonstrated that the quantities of all the identified components in the canister are such that each leachable is not delivered at levels above the TTC of 1.5 µg/day for mutagenic compounds. No additional controls for leachables are required.

## 3.3.4. Stability of the product

Stability data were provided from three commercial-scale batches of finished product manufactured at the proposed commercial site and packaged in the proposed commercial container closure system. The batches were stored for up to 36 months under long-term conditions (25°C/60% RH), for up to 36 months under intermediate conditions (30°C/75% RH) and for up to 6 months under accelerated conditions (40°C/75% RH) in accordance with ICH guidelines, although a higher relative humidity was used for the intermediate storage condition (75 % instead of 65 % RH). The stability samples were stored inverted (valve down), which is considered to be the worst scenario, under protected conditions.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Samples were tested for description, appearance, total can assay, degradation products, delivered dose uniformity throughout canister life, number of actuations per canister, moisture content, APSD by next generation impactor (NGI) and microbial limits. The analytical procedures used are the same as those used for release testing and have been appropriately validated. The analytical methods are stabilityindicating.

All results complied with the proposed specifications at all test points under long-term storage conditions. No significant changes or adverse stability trends were observed for appearance, description, delivered dose uniformity, number of actuations per canister, moisture content, APSD or microbial quality.

An in-use study was performed using inhalers removed from the protective foil overwrap and stored unprotected in the horizontal orientation, representing the most likely storage position during patient use. Samples were evaluated at the beginning, mid and end of shelf life under 25°C/75% RH and 30°C/75% RH conditions for up to 6 months and the storage condition 'Do not store above 30ºC' implemented.

Based on the available stability data, the proposed shelf life of 3 years, including an in-use period of 3 months following removal from the protective foil overwrap, is acceptable. The proposed storage conditions as stated in SmPC section 6.3 'Do not store above 30 °C. Do not expose to temperatures higher than 50 °C. Do not pierce the pressurised container. Store in a dry place.' are considered adequately supported by the stability data.

## 5.2.2. Pharmacokinetics

## 5.2.2.1. Introduction

The focus of PK tests was to assess the steady state PK of budesonide, glycopyrronium, and formoterol following treatment with BGF MDI in participants with inadequately controlled asthma in a PK sub-study.

## 5.2.2.2. Evaluation and qualification of models

## 5.2.2.2.1. Population pharmacokinetics

The population PK analysis for the triple-combination of budesonide/glycopyrronium/formoterol in asthma participants from KALOS was presented. Key findings of the final models are outlined below.

Budesonide: In total, 1202 samples from 125 participants treated with BGF MDI, BFF MDI, and/or Symbicort pMDI were included in the population PK analysis of budesonide. The final model was a twocompartment model with first-order absorption. The typical budesonide estimates were Ka = 4.95hr⁻¹ and CL/F = 125.67L/h, compared with Ka = 2.91hr⁻¹ and CL/F = 122L/h in COPD. The central and peripheral volumes in asthma were 481.05L and 447.52L, respectively, yielding a steady-state volume of distribution (Vss) of 928.57L for a typical individual. This was slightly lower than the Vss of 1,209L reported in the previous COPD model. The effective half-life was estimated to be approx. 5.2 h. Based on the effective half-life, budesonide concentrations are expected to reach steady state after approx. 1.1 days of BID dosing and the extent of exposure is approx. 1.25 times higher than after the first dose.

Glycopyrronium: In total, 505 samples from 56 participants treated with BGF MDI were included in the population PK analysis of glycopyrronium. The final model was a two-compartment model with first-order absorption. The Ka was estimated at 58.89hr⁻¹, CL/F at 225.68L/h, and Vc/F at 1559.36L. These values are similar to estimates from the COPD model (Ka 38.7hr⁻¹, CL/F 166L/h, Vc/F 1120L). The apparent steady-state volume of distribution (Vss/F) was calculated as 5009L, which is slightly lower than the corresponding value of 5520 L in the COPD cohort. Based on the estimated effective half-life of 11.67h, glycopyrronium concentrations are expected to reach steady state after approx. 2.4 days of BID dosing and the extent of exposure is approx. 1.96 times higher than after the first dose.

Formoterol: In total, 920 samples from 112 participants treated with BGF MDI, BFF MDI, and/or Symbicort pMDI were included in the population PK analysis of formoterol. The final model for formoterol was a two-compartment model with first-order absorption. Age was a statistically significant covariate of formoterol apparent clearance; however, the changes in drug exposure were not considered clinically meaningful at the planned dosages or for the studied inhaled therapies. The Ka was 7.71hr⁻¹, CL/F was 144.19L/h and Vc/F was 1445.63L. These are comparable to COPD values (Ka 9.71hr⁻¹, CL/F 124L/h, and Vc/F 1240 L). The apparent steady-state volume of distribution (Vss/F) for formoterol was calculated as 2696.87 L in the asthma cohort and 2370 L in COPD. Based on the estimated effective half-life of 10.73h, formoterol concentrations are expected to reach steady state after approx. 2.2 days of BID dosing, and the extent of exposure is approx. 1.85 times higher than after the first dose.

## 5.2.2.3. Pharmacokinetics in the target population

KALOS: This was a phase 3, randomised, double-blind, double dummy, parallel group, multicentre, variable length study to assess the efficacy and safety of BGF MDI (320/28.8/9.6μg and 320/14.4/9.6μg) relative to BFF MDI (320/9.6μg) and Symbicort pressurised metered dose inhaler (320/9μg) in adult and adolescent participants with inadequately controlled asthma. A 12-hour PK sub-study was conducted in a subset of participants across treatment groups to characterise the steady state PK of budesonide, glycopyrronium, and formoterol fumarate. All study treatments were administered as two actuations twice daily (BID) for a minimum of 24 weeks and a maximum of 52 weeks. A total of 144 study participants (adults and adolescents), across treatment groups, were included in the PK sub-study. Budesonide: Budesonide concentration-time profiles following chronic dosing with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg, BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg, BFF MDI, and Symbicort pMDI showed that budesonide concentrations were highest for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg followed by Symbicort pMDI and BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg with BFF MDI having the lowest budesonide concentrations. Higher budesonide concentrations were observed based on geometric mean Cmax and AUC0-12 values for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg and BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg compared with BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI. Glycopyrronium: Glycopyrronium concentration-time profiles following chronic dosing with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg and BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg were similar in shape. Higher glycopyrronium concentrations were observed based on geometric mean Cmax and AUC0-12 values for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg compared with BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg. Formoterol: Formoterol concentration-time profiles following chronic dosing with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg, BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg, BFF MDI, and Symbicort pMDI showed that formoterol concentrations were higher for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg and BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg compared with BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI. Higher formoterol concentrations were observed based on geometric mean Cmax and AUC0-12 values for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg and BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg compared with BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI. Adolescent PK results: Several adolescent participants provided PK data; either dosed with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg or with BFF MDI. This fulfilled the PIP requirement to obtain PK data from adolescent participants in KALOS. Descriptive statistics were provided for the several adolescent participants (in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group and in the BFF MDI group) that were included in the PK analysis set per the SAP criteria. No analyses were performed on the adolescents alone due to the small sample size.

## 5.3. Clinical efficacy

## 5.3.1. Dose response studies

The doses of budesonide and formoterol fumarate from phase III studies with BGF MDI and BFF MDI in COPD were carried through to the BGF MDI asthma programme. The choice to evaluate 2 GP doses (14.4μg; 28.8μg) was supported by two studies in participants with asthma:

Study PT001101: A randomised, double-blind, chronic-dosing (14 days), 5-period, placebo-controlled, incomplete block, cross-over, multicentre, phase II study that assessed the efficacy and safety of 5 doses of GP MDI (28.8, 14.4, 7.2, 3.6 and 1.9μg) BID compared with placebo MDI BID and open-label salmeterol 50μg BID. A total of 248 subjects were randomised to 1 of 24 pre-defined treatment sequences. Each sequence included GP MDI 14.4μg and GP MDI 7.2μg, 75% of sequences included Placebo MDI and/or SAL, and 50% of the sequences included GP MDI 28.8μg, GP MDI 3.6μg, and/or GP MDI 1.9μg. A dose-ordered response in peak change from baseline in FEV1 was observed across GP doses. The GP MDI 28.8μg dose demonstrated the greatest improvement in change from baseline in morning trough and peak FEV1 and change from baseline FEV1 AUC0-3 vs the placebo MDI group. While GP MDI 14.4μg had marginal efficacy at the end of the dosing interval, it had an efficacy that was similar to GP MDI 28.8μg for endpoints such as peak FEV1 and FEV1 AUC0-3. Dose 14.4μg was utilised in the phase III trials for COPD and approved for use in COPD for both GFF MDI and BGF MDI in the EU. Hence, both GP MDI 28.8μg and 14.4μg were further evaluated in asthma as part of the triple combination product.

Study PT001102: A randomised, double-blind, parallel group, multi-centre, phase II/III dose confirmation study comparing 3 doses of GP MDI (28.8, 14.4, and 7.2μg, all BID) to placebo MDI and open-label Spiriva Respimat (2.5μg, once daily) for 24 weeks. Patients had to have a pre-dose FEV1&gt;40% and &lt; 85%, reversibility to albuterol had to be demonstrated during screening, and were randomised to receive 1 of the 5 treatments (each of the 3 doses of GP, placebo MDI and Spiriva Respimat, resp.). Out of 1077 subjects, 1066 were in the ITT/mITT populations.

No dose difference was observed among the GP MDI doses for the primary analyses; the GP 14.4μg and 28.8μg doses were comparable. As the comparison of GP MDI 28.8μg vs placebo MDI was not statistically significant, none of the subsequent primary and secondary efficacy analyses were either. With respect to the secondary endpoint, while all 3 GP MDI doses produced numerical reductions in the rate of moderate or severe asthma exacerbations vs placebo MDI of approx. 20% or greater, none of these was nominally significant, although the study was not powered to demonstrate an effect in asthma exacerbations. Some positive treatment effects for the 28.8μg dose were observed through post-hoc analyses. There were no new safety concerns observed with any glycopyrronium dose in PT001102.

## 5.3.2. Main studies

## 5.3.2.1.1. KALOS (D5982C00007) and LOGOS (D5982C00008)

With the exception of individual sub-studies, these trials were designed as replicates; therefore, a unified description of their shared methodology is provided below.

## 5.3.2.1.2. Study title

KALOS: A Randomised, Double-Blind, Double Dummy, Parallel Group, Multicenter Variable Length Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of PT010 Relative to PT009 and Symbicort in Adult and Adolescent Participants With Inadequately Controlled Asthma

LOGOS: A Randomised, Double-Blind, Double Dummy, Parallel Group, Multicenter Variable Length Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of PT010 Relative to PT009 and Symbicort in Adult and Adolescent Participants With Inadequately Controlled Asthma

## 5.3.2.1.3. Study design

KALOS and LOGOS were phase III, randomised, double-blind, double dummy, 4-arm parallel group, multicentre 24-to-52-week variable length studies that compared the efficacy and safety of 2 doses of BGF MDI (320/28.8/9.6μg and 320/14.4/9.6μg) to 2 active comparators, BFF MDI (320/9.6µg) and Symbicort pMDI (320/9µg), in adult and adolescent patients with inadequately controlled asthma controlled (ACQ-7 total score ≥1.5) despite treatment with a medium or high dose ICS/LABA, aiming to demonstrate the contribution of glycopyrronium to the overall effects of the BGF triple combination in the asthma population. KALOS had 3 sub-studies in addition to the main study, A 12-hour PFT sub-study, a 12-hour PK and a 24-Hour Holter Monitor Sub-Study. LOGOS listed one additional sub-study a 12-hour PFT study. PFT sub-studies were pooled for analysis.

The screening/run-in period consisted of 4 screening Visits scheduled prior to randomisation at visit 5. At screening visit 1, informed consent was collected, and some eligibility criteria were assessed. If required criteria were met, participants discontinued their medium or high dose ICS/LABA and initiated a 4-week run-in period with BFF MDI 320/9.6μg taken BID until the evening prior to visit 5 when the run-in BFF MDI was discontinued. If an asthma exacerbation occurred during screening, the period could be extended up to 10 weeks to allow for treatment and a 4-week washout prior to randomisation. The treatment period was variable in length. After randomisation, treatments were continued for a minimum of 24 weeks and a maximum of 52 weeks. Participants completed a 52-week dosing period, except those randomised within 28 weeks of the last randomised participant that completed the study period. After randomisation, participants had 6 to 10 treatment period visits. The exact number of visits varied depending on individual participant schedules and protocol-defined visit windows. The study ended when the last randomised participant completed 24 weeks of randomised study treatment and a 2-week safety follow-up phone call, unless study treatment was discontinued prior to week 24. ## 5.3.2.1.3.1. Treatment

Study treatments were masked in a double dummy fashion, whereby participants randomised to BGF MDI or BFF MDI were given placebo pMDI to match Symbicort pMDI and participants randomised to Symbicort pMDI were given placebo MDI to match BGF/BFF MDI. Run-in BFF MDI was provided during the screening period. Placebo was included to support the double-blind, double-dummy design; patient received two inhalers, one active and one placebo, hence, all used identical regimens regardless of the assigned arm.

## 5.3.2.1.3.2. Randomisation

Participants were centrally randomised to one of the treatment arms using a Randomisation and Trial Supply Management system. Randomisation was initially 1:1:1:1 across treatment groups (BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg, BGF 320/28.8/9.6μg, BFF MDI, and Symbicort pMDI). However, due to recruitment considerations, the BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg treatment group recruitment was stopped following approval of protocol version 4.0. Participants were subsequently randomised 1:1:1 to the remaining treatment groups (BGF 320/28.8/9.6μg, BFF MDI, and Symbicort pMDI). Participants who were randomised to BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg remained on the same dose until the end of the treatment. Randomisation was conducted separately for adults and adolescents, and stratification was applied as follows: Adults: country; baseline pre-bronchodilator percent predicted FEV₁: ≤55% vs &gt;55%; severe exacerbation history in the 12 months prior to visit 1: 0, 1, or ≥2; ICS dose - medium vs high; Adolescents: country; baseline pre-bronchodilator percent predicted FEV₁: ≤75% vs &gt;75%.

## 5.3.2.1.3.3. Blinding

A double-blind, double-dummy design was implemented to ensure blinding across different inhaler types, with placebo inhalers used to match active treatments. The sponsor retained the right to break the blind in cases of serious adverse events that were unexpected and suspected to be causally related to the study intervention, and which required expedited reporting to regulatory authorities.

## 5.3.2.1.3.4. Patient population

Inclusion criteria: age at least 12 to 80 years of age incl., documented history of physician-diagnosed asthma ≥1 year prior to visit 1 who had been regularly using a stable daily medium or high ICS/LABA for at least 4 weeks prior to visit 1, ACQ-7 total score ≥1.5 at visits 1, 3, and 5, pre-bronchodilator FEV1 &lt;80% predicted normal value at visits 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 for participants ≥18 years of age or &lt;90% predicted normal value for participants 12 to &lt;18 years of age, reversibility to albuterol (defined as a post-albuterol increase in FEV1 of ≥12% and ≥200 mL for adults OR ≥12% for adolescents), acceptable inhaler technique. Exclusion criteria: life-threatening asthma, hospitalisation for asthma within 2 months of visit 1, completed treatment for respiratory infection or asthma exacerbation with systemic corticosteroids in the 4 weeks prior to Visit 1, evidence of a clinically significant disease, use of LAMA in the 12 weeks prior to visit 1, (oral or IV) corticosteroid use within 4 weeks of visit 1; use of oral beta2agonists or depot corticosteroids and other specified medicines, current/former smokers.

## 5.3.2.1.4.3. Statistical methods for estimation and sensitivity analysis on primary estimands

The following sets were defined for the statistical analysis: Screened Analysis Set (all participants who sign the ICF); Randomised Analysis Set (all participants who are randomised to study intervention); Efficacy Analysis Set (all participants who are randomised to study intervention and receive any amount of study intervention. Participants will be analysed according to the study intervention they were assigned at randomisation, regardless of the actual study intervention received); Per-Protocol Analysis Set (all participants in the Efficacy Analysis Set without an important protocol deviation impacting efficacy at randomisation. Participants will be analysed as randomised since receiving the wrong treatment would be an important protocol deviation); Safety Analysis Set (all participants who are randomised to study intervention and receive any amount of the study intervention. Participants will be analysed according to the actual study intervention received rather than randomised); Pulmonary Function Test Sub-Study Analysis Set (all participants in the Efficacy Analysis Set who consented and have at least one postbaseline spirometry assessment in the sub-study (after the first dose of study medication)).

The analysis of the primary endpoint, change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 over 24 weeks, was conducted using a repeated measures analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model which included treatment, visit, prior ICS dose, and treatment-by-visit interaction as categorical covariates and baseline trough FEV1 and percent albuterol reversibility as continuous covariates. Statistical testing was based on the differences in least-squares means over 24 weeks between treatment groups, at a 5% significance level following a hierarchical testing procedure for type I error control. Bayesian borrowing was conducted to contribute information to the adolescent population from the adult population for the analysis of the primary endpoint. A robust mixture prior approach was used, where the prior was a mixture of an informative (primary analysis results fitted to a normal distribution) and uninformative element (empirical Bayes unit-information). The proportion of borrowing was represented as a weight of the effective sample size of the prior. There was a primary pooled analysis on the rate of severe exacerbations in KALOS and LOGOS, to satisfy the primary pooled objective of assessing the effect of BGF MDI relative to ICS/LABA on the rate of severe asthma exacerbations. It was to be performed on a combined exacerbation dataset from the two studies, and its model was adjusted for baseline trough FEV1, percent reversibility, baseline severe exacerbation history, prior ICS dose, region, and study. The significance level for hypothesis testing at this endpoint was determined by following the hierarchical testing procedure.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Sensitivity analyses - primary endpoint: A tipping point analysis was conducted to assess how extreme departures, from the missing at random (MAR) assumption in the primary analysis model, would have to be in order to reverse the conclusions of the primary analysis. The effect of BGF was incrementally worsened and the effect of ICS/LABA was incrementally improved to the point where the treatment difference is no longer statistically significant. This was achieved by a multiple imputation using a Monte Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) method. Missing data were imputed sequentially using a regression imputation model adjusted with the same terms as the primary analysis model, except for visit and treatment-by-visit interaction. The imputed values for the active group were increased by a pre-specified constant, and the imputed values for the control were decreased by another pre-specified constant. The results from the individual imputed datasets were combined using Rubin's rules. Separate tipping analyses were conducted for the two treatment comparisons of interest, the BGF 320/28.8/9.6 µg group or the BGF 320/14.4/9.6 µg group vs the ICS/LABA group. The same tipping point analysis methodology was to be applied to the pooled primary endpoint.

Supplementary analyses - primary endpoint: For the primary endpoint, a While-on-Treatment strategy was used to handle all pre-specified ICEs, where data after the ICE were not used in analysis.

## 5.3.2.1.4.4. Secondary objectives

The secondary objectives of these studies were to assess the effect of BGF MDI compared to ICS/LABA with respect to lung function, symptoms, patient-reported outcomes (PROs), and safety. The secondary endpoints included: change from baseline in FEV1 AUC0-3 over 24 weeks, percentage of responders in ACQ-7 over 24 weeks, percentage of responders in ACQ-5 over 24 weeks, percentage of responders in AQLQ(s)+12 over 24 weeks, percentage of responders in SGRQ over 24 weeks, and other endpoints as per study protocol. The secondary objective pooled between KALOS and LOGOS assessed the effect on asthma exacerbations of BGF MDI compared to ICS/LABA in: the rate of severe asthma exacerbations for participants with percent predicted FEV1 ≤55% at baseline, the rate of severe asthma exacerbations for participants with ≥1 severe exacerbation in the 12 months prior to visit 1.

## 5.3.5. Supportive studies

Two supportive studies were submitted, providing evidence to qualify BFF MDI as the active ICS/LABA comparator to BGF MDI in KALOS and LOGOS.

## 5.3.5.1. VATHOS (D5982C00006)

This was a 24-week study that evaluated the efficacy and safety of BFF MDI 320/9.6μg BID compared with BD MDI and open-label Symbicort TBH 320/9μg. BFF MDI 160/9.6μg BID was also included to evaluate dose response. The aim was to assess the benefits and safety of BFF MDI on lung function, asthma control, and asthma HRQoL in a population consisting of adult and adolescent patients with asthma who remained inadequately controlled, despite treatment with medium dose ICS or ICS-LABA. Budesonide and Formoterol fumarate MDI 160/9.6µg was included to evaluate dose response by comparing to BFF MDI 320/9.6µg. SYMBICORT TBH 320/9μg was included as a marketed comparator for regions that required this. All participants were taking a stable daily medium dose ICS or ICS-LABA for at least 8 weeks prior to visit 1. After meeting all eligibility criteria, participants discontinued the medium dose ICS or ICS-LABA at visit 1 and initiated run-in BD MDI 320μg taken BID until the evening prior to randomisation, when run-in BD MDI was discontinued. Participants randomised in a 2:1:2:2 scheme to BD MDI 320μg, BFF MDI 160μg or 320μg, or open-label SYMBICORT TBH 320/9μg. Key inclusion criteria: 12-80 years, documented history of physician-diagnosed asthma ≥6 months prior to visit 1, regular/stable daily medium dose ICS or an ICS-LABA regimen for at least 8 weeks prior to visit 1, ACQ-7 total score ≥1.5 at visits 1&amp;4, pre-bronchodilator/pre-dose FEV1&lt;90% predicted normal value at visits 1, 2 &amp; 3, and a pre-dose FEV1 of 50% to 90% at visit 4, defined post-albuterol increase in FEV1. Key exclusion criteria: life-threatening asthma, hospitalisation for asthma within 8 weeks of visit 1, completed treatment for respiratory infection/asthma exacerbation treated with systemic corticosteroids and/or additional ICS treatment in the 8 weeks prior to visit 1 and throughout the screening period, smokers as defined on the protocol. Study participants: The demographics of the patient populations were consistent with the inclusion/exclusion criteria. Demographics, background disease, treatments and lung function were balanced across the groups. Primary endpoint: the BFF MDI 320/9.6μg treatment group demonstrated a statistically significantly greater improvement in mean change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 over 24 weeks compared with the BD MDI 320μg treatment group using the primary strategy for handling ICEs in VATHOS. Following the type 1 error control procedure, the secondary endpoints, change from baseline in FEV1 AUC0-3 over 24 weeks and onset of action were also met where the BFF MDI 320/9.6μg treatment group demonstrated statistically significantly greater improvements for the respective comparisons with the BD MDI 320μg treatment group. The endpoints AQLQ(s)+12 and rescue medication use were nominally significant, given placement in the type I error procedure. Although the endpoints ACQ-5 and ACQ-7 were not significant, the BFF MDI 320/9.6μg treatment group had a numerically higher responder rate over 12 weeks compared with the BD MDI 320μg treatment group. Secondary endpoints: BFF MDI 320/9.6μg was superior to BD MDI 320μg on lung function as demonstrated by statistically significantly greater improvement from baseline in FEV1 AUC0-3 over 24 weeks. In conclusion: BFF MDI 320/9.6μg was superior to BD MDI 320μg on primary and secondary lung function endpoints; numerically greater improvements were observed for BFF MDI 320/9.6μg compared with BD MDI 320μg for asthma control and HRQoL as assessed by responder rate in ACQ-7, ACQ-5, and AQLQ(s)+12; BFF MDI 320/9.6μg had a nominally significant reduction in rescue medication use over 24 weeks compared with BD MDI 320μg. BFF MDI 320/9.6μg and BFF MDI 160/9.6μg were well tolerated. Over 24 weeks of treatment, there were no new or unexpected safety findings in adult and adolescent participants with inadequately controlled asthma.

## 5.3.5.2. LITHOS (D5982C00005)

This was a phase 3 randomised, double-blind, active comparator, parallel group, multicentre study comparing efficacy and safety of BFF MDI 160/9.6μg to BD MDI 160μg in adults and adolescents who had asthma which was not controlled on low dose ICS or ICS-LABA. Study interventions were administered as 2 actuations BID for 12 weeks. Participants were taking a stable daily low dose ICS or ICS-LABA for at least 8 weeks prior to visit 1. After meeting all eligibility criteria, patients discontinued on low dose ICS or ICS-LABA at visit 1 and initiated run-in BD MDI 160μg (BID) until the evening prior to randomisation, when run-in BD MDI was discontinued. Participants eligible for the study were randomised in a 1:1 scheme to BFF MDI 160/9.6μg or BD MDI 160μg. Randomisation was stratified by baseline asthma treatment. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are similar to the Vathos study and are specified in the study protocol. The demographics of the patient populations were consistent with the inclusion/exclusion criteria. Demographics were balanced across treatment groups. Background disease characteristics were balanced across treatment groups, as well as the lung function parameters, asthma control, and HRQoL measures at baseline. In accordance with the CSP requirement, all participants were taking a stable daily low dose ICS (24.6%) or ICS-LABA (75.4%) for at least 8 weeks prior to visit 1. The proportion of smoker patients was higher in the BFF MDI 160/9.6μg treatment group vs the BD MDI 160μg. Primary endpoint: the BFF MDI 160/9.6μg treatment group demonstrated a statistically significantly greater improvement in mean CFB in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 over 12 weeks compared with the BD MDI 160μg treatment group using the primary strategy for handling ICEs. Following the Type I error control procedure, the secondary endpoints, such as the mean CFB in FEV1 AUC0-3 over 12 weeks, onset of action, and rescue medication were also met, where the BFF MDI 160/9.6μg treatment group demonstrated statistically significantly greater improvements for the respective comparisons with the BD MDI 160μg treatment group. Although the endpoints ACQ-7, ACQ-5, and AQLQ+12 were not significant per the Type I error control procedure, the BFF MDI 160/9.6μg treatment group had a numerically higher responder rate over 12 weeks compared with the BD MDI 160μg treatment group.

Change from baseline in FEV1 AUC0-3 over 24 weeks: The results across subgroups for the comparison of BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg vs pooled BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI were directionally consistent with the overall population improvements, noting the small sample size resulting in wide CIs in some subgroups.

Overall, the results did not demonstrate a patient-perceived benefit of BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg beyond that observed with the pooled dual ICS/LABA comparators (BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI). In KALOS, BGF MDI failed to achieve statistical significance across all PRO and HRQoL endpoints, except for SGRQ, which reached nominal significance under the Type I error control procedure. Similarly, in LOGOS, nominal significance was observed only for ACQ-7. Given that ACQ-7 incorporates pre-bronchodilator FEV₁% into its scoring algorithm, it is likely that the observed favourable outcome was largely driven by the improvement in lung function. This interpretation is further supported by the less favourable results for ACQ-5, which excludes lung function parameters. Although the patient perceived benefit data is not statistically robust, values are directionally positive across both KALOS and LOGOS. Nominal significance was achieved for ACQ-7 in LOGOS and SGRQ in KALOS. Additionally, the magnitude of treatment effect was comparable with approved SITT products. ## 5.3.7. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical efficacy

## 5.3.7.1. Discussion

The BGF MDI is a fixed-dose triple inhaler combining budesonide, glycopyrronium, and formoterol fumarate (two inhalations of 160/14.4/4.8μg, e.g. 320/28.8/9.6μg per daily dose). Dose-finding: The choice to evaluate 2 GP doses (14.4μg and 28.8μg) in the replicate phase 3 studies was supported by data from two phase 2 dose-ranging studies (PT001101 and PT001102) in patients with asthma, and while this evidence was somewhat limited especially for GP MDI 14.4μg, the rationale for selecting two GP doses in the pivotal studies was generally accepted during CHMP scientific advice (SA). Nevertheless, recruitment in the BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg treatment group was stopped via protocol amendment in the pivotal programme (see 5.3.2.), due to challenges to recruit. Hence, BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg is the proposed dose for the MAA.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Therapeutic indication: Pivotal phase 3 studies (see 5.3.2.), aimed to demonstrate the superiority of triple (ICS/LABA/LAMA) over dual therapy (ICS/LABA) in support of the proposed use in patients ≥12 years with inadequately controlled asthma despite medium or high dose ICS/LABA for at least 4 weeks prior to screening. According to EMA guideline on the clinical development of fixed dose combination (FDC) medicinal products (EMA/CHMP/158268/2017), a demonstration that each active substance in a FDC contributes to efficacy and/or benefit-risk balance is needed. In addition, the add-on effect of the additional active substance needs to be quantified. A pragmatic approach was taken in the SA by accepting that efficacy of the dual combination BFF MDI (ICS/LABA) was proven by previous evidence, and that, therefore, this should be the primary comparator. The trial design is therefore considered appropriate to quantify the effect contribution of GP in the triple combination. However, as noted in the SA, the design does not allow quantification of GP's contribution compared to high-dose ICS/LABA, since no high-dose ICS/LABA comparator was included. The MAH's justification that high dose of ICS/LABA was included to also evaluate the triple therapy (BGF MDI) corresponding to GINA step 5, constituting a potential new paradigm that provides additional step-up care in GINA Step 4 and 5 patients before escalation to systemic/invasive therapies, cannot be agreed by the CHMP. Furthermore, enrolment of patients on high ICS/LABA at baseline without a direct evidence of superiority of the triple combination over high dose ICS/LABA, is not sufficiently supported for the proposed indication Based on the CHMP's evaluation, the indication was restricted and reference to \"high dose\" removed (see below). Of note, the proposed indication allows for use in a somewhat broader asthma population than other authorised ICS/LABA/LAMA combinations, as it does not refer to prior exacerbations. This was discussed by the CHMP and supported by data demonstrating comparable efficacy in patients with or without a severe exacerbation in the previous 12 months. Additionally, patients without a history of exacerbations were shown to be still at risk, as 31% in the ICS/LABA control arm had a severe exacerbation during the trial. Similar effects observed in adults to support this broader application were also seen in adolescent patients (see discussion below). Therefore, this indication wording is acceptable: Riltrava Aerosphere is indicated as a maintenance treatment of asthma in patients 12 years of age and older who are not adequately controlled by a combination of a medium dose inhaled corticosteroid and a long-acting beta2-agonist. Pivotal phase 3 studies: KALOS and LOGOS: randomised, double-blind, double-dummy, parallel-group, multicentre trials with treatment periods 24 and 52 weeks, were considered acceptable with respect to their design, treatment and dose choice, eligibility criteria, choice of treatments and study endpoints. In line with the EMA Guideline on the clinical investigation of medicinal products for the treatment of asthma (CHMP/EWP/2922/01 Rev.1), the studies are also considered of sufficient length and duration. Efficacy assessment methods based on standardised pulmonary function testing, recording of asthma exacerbation history (in accordance with EMA guidance (CHMP/EWP/2922/01 Rev.1)), and the completion of validated PROs, were consistent across treatment arms and in accordance with clinical practice and regulatory guidance. The population consisted of patients older than 12 and younger than 80 years old, with a history of physician-diagnosed asthma ≥1 year, that is inadequately controlled on medium or high dose ICS/LABA therapy for at least 4 weeks prior to screening. This population is consistent with GINA Steps 4 and 5 [GINA 2025]. As stated above, the SA recommended to only enrol patients inadequately controlled on medium dose ICS/LABA as the study design does not allow quantification of the contribution of GP to the overall effect of the triple combination as compared to high dose ICS/LABA as a high dose ICS/LABA comparator was not used. With respect to exacerbation history, initially adult participants were required to have a documented history of at least one severe asthma exacerbation in the 12 months prior to enrolment. Due to challenges in recruiting, this criterion was removed (protocol amendment 2), leading to a broader studied population. Consequences of these aspects are reflected in the agreed indication wording, as mentioned above.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Despite the replicate study design, differences between studies were observed, i.e. in KALOS, a significantly higher proportion of participants had received high-dose ICS prior to enrolment (42.5%) compared to LOGOS (26.3%). There was a longer median time since asthma diagnosis in KALOS (16 years) vs LOGOS (14.5 years). Additionally, a greater percentage of participants in KALOS had experienced one or more severe asthma exacerbations in the 12 months preceding enrolment (67.5% vs 49.3% in LOGOS). It would appear that KALOS participants had more severe asthma characteristics than those in LOGOS. Initially, it was thought this was due to the timing of the removal of the exacerbation history criteria. However, similar randomisation figures for KALOS and LOGOS at the time of protocol amendment 2 (14.2% vs 14.9%, respectively) suggest that the patient populations in these trials were unlikely to have been significantly affected by this. Any differences were likely due to random fluctuations between studies. It is acknowledged that changes to inclusion criteria, particularly those with an impact on the primary endpoint, are strongly discouraged. Nevertheless, as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, recruitment was directly and significantly affected and so this amendment was permissible under these circumstances. Results of study endpoints: Results focus on the strength proposed for registration, BGF MDI 160/14.4/4.8μg which gives a dose of BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg (two actuations). The co-primary endpoint for lung function, change from baseline in pre-dose FEV₁ at week 24, was met in the pivotal studies, with statistically significant treatment differences observed for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg in both KALOS (0.056L; 95% CI: 0.031, 0.082; p&lt;0.001) and LOGOS (0.097L; 95% CI: 0.070, 0.123; p&lt;0.001) compared with BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI. While the treatment effect was evident and sustained through week 52 in KALOS, a trend toward reduced treatment difference was noted after week 24 in LOGOS. By week 52, the LS mean difference in trough FEV₁ between BGF MDI 320/28/9.6μg and the dual therapy comparators remained statistically significant but was notably smaller (0.054 L; 95% CI: 0.018, 0.097; p = 0.004). Data imputation was given as a reason and since patients were not followed for longer than 52 weeks, no additional information is available. Post-hoc FEV₁ responder analyses were conducted in KALOS, LOGOS and the pooled data and the percentage of responders in the in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg treatment arm achieving ≥100 and ≥200 ml change in pre-dose trough FEV1 was nominally significant from comparators over 24 weeks across KALOS, LOGOS and pooled analyses. These data, although limited due to post-hoc nature, can be considered supportive towards clinical benefit on lung function. The improvements observed in KALOS and LOGOS over dual ICS/LABA therapy are comparable to other approved SITT products and are clinically relevant, showing a clinically meaningful benefit of triple therapy over medium-dose ICS/LABA. Exacerbations: The absolute number of severe asthma exacerbations and the adjusted annual exacerbation rates were low across all groups in the pooled analysis of the pivotal studies. Nevertheless, the adjusted exacerbation rate per year (SE) was lower in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group compared to the pooled ICS/LABA comparator group. This corresponds to a statistically significant rate ratio of 0.855 (95% CI: 0.757, 0.966; p = 0.012), indicating 14.5% relative reduction in the rate of severe asthma exacerbations with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg. Overall, the pooled analysis met the co-primary endpoint for severe exacerbation rate reduction. MAH's discussion on subgroup analyses of exacerbation outcomes, both 'severe' and 'moderate or severe' was limited and the results showed that the rate of moderate/severe exacerbations was similar to that of severe exacerbations, due to the conservative definition for moderate exacerbations use. Further secondary analyses of the pooled data showed numerically lower rates of severe exacerbations in participants with either baseline FEV₁ ≤ 55% or ≥1 prior exacerbation, but they did not reach statistical significance. Time-to-event analyses also indicated a reduced risk of first severe or moderate/severe exacerbation with BGF MDI, though these findings were nominally significant only, falling outside type I error control procedure. Patients without exacerbation history have a lower expected incidence of future exacerbations. In the ICS/LABA control arm, the mean annualised severe exacerbation rate was 0.552 in patients with no exacerbation history, with 31% of participants in this arm having a severe exacerbation in the trial. This compares with a mean annualised severe exacerbation rate of 0.721 and incidence of 43% among patients with at least one exacerbation in the previous 12 months. Although lower than patients with a history of exacerbations, a 31% incidence of severe exacerbations could still be considered clinically meaningful and may indicate that patients without a recent exacerbation history remain at risk and have need for a treatment escalation option. The subgroup without a prior exacerbation history demonstrated that the risk for severe exacerbations was reduced in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group relative to the pooled ICS/LABA group, with a comparison rate ratio of 0.828 (0.675, 1.015). In the pooled KALOS and LOGOS data, the number needed to treat (NNT) for the subgroup of participants with no exacerbations in the prior year was 11, with an absolute reduction in risk (AAR) of 0.095 (0.457 vs 0.552). This is comparable to the ≥1 exacerbation subgroup (AAR= 0.100, NNT=10) and currently available SITT products. Therefore, it is accepted that clinical benefit has been shown in patients with 0 severe exacerbations in the previous year, as concluded by the CHMP and reflected in the approved indication. With respect to the selected key secondary endpoint, change from baseline in FEV₁ AUC0-3 over 24 weeks, BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg demonstrated statistically significant improvements over pooled comparators in both pivotal studies. Patient-reported outcomes (ACQ-5 and ACQ-7) and HRQoL measures (AQLQ+12 and SGRQ) were also evaluated as secondary endpoints and the results did not demonstrate a significant patient-perceived benefit of BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg beyond that observed with the pooled dual ICS/LABA comparators (BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI). Although the patient perceived benefit data is not statistically robust, values are directionally positive across both trials; hence a modest patient perceived benefit has been shown. Subgroup analyses of the change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 over 24 weeks and change from baseline in FEV1 AUC0-3 over 24 weeks outcomes by age, sex, race, baseline history of severe exacerbations, baseline pre-bronchodilator % predicted FEV₁, prior ICS dose, % reversibility, and geographic region, showed that all of these subgroups were directionally similar across primary and secondary endpoints in both studies. Lung function and exacerbation data stratified by baseline disease characteristics, exacerbation history and ICS-dose were supportive of the primary analysis. Adolescent data: A limited number of adolescents were enrolled in the phase 3 studies, with 70 in KALOS and 58 in LOGOS, totalling 128 participants (3% of overall population), in line with the agreed PIP. In the active treatment arm, only 16 adolescents were included in KALOS and 14 in LOGOS, but the studies were not powered to show efficacy in adolescents. Within individual studies, results for adolescents were generally consistent with overall findings. For the co-primary lung function endpoint (change from baseline in trough FEV₁), subgroup analyses showed treatment differences between BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg and dual therapy comparators in KALOS, in LOGOS and in the pooled analysis. For the second co-primary endpoint (rate of severe asthma exacerbations), pooled subgroup analysis in adolescents yielded a rate ratio of 0.510 (95% CI:0.175, 1.483). Bayesian borrowing analyses were prespecified and applied to pooled data to enhance precision by leveraging adult data for select endpoints. In adolescents treated with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg, a numerical improvement in pre-dose trough FEV₁ was observed. However, borrowing from adults yielded a smaller and less favourable reduction in severe exacerbation rate. A large fraction of the effective sample size was borrowed from the adult population for the adolescents. Given the actual small, recruited sample size for the adolescent population, this may have been too optimistic. Nevertheless, international asthma guidelines provide broad support for the extrapolation and considering that budesonide and formoterol are used in children from low age and these may be combined with the LAMA, it is acceptable to extrapolate efficacy data from adults. It should also be taken into account that the pathophysiology of asthma in adults and adolescents is similar and that efficacy findings in adolescents were directionally consistent with those in the adults. In summary, considering the totality of the data, a partial extrapolation approach, supported by bibliographic references and combined with clinical efficacy and safety data from the overall asthma pivotal studies supports the approval of BGF MDI in the adolescents. For plans for long-term safety monitoring in this population, see section 5.4.11.

## 5.3.7.2. Conclusions on the clinical efficacy

The CHMP considered that the overall clinical efficacy development programme for Riltrava Aerosphere in the asthma indication, including the pivotal and supportive studies, as well as the extrapolation concept for the adolescent population is adequate to justify the efficacy of Riltrava Aerosphere as a maintenance treatment of asthma in patients 12 years of age and older who are not adequately controlled by a combination of medium dose inhaled corticosteroid and a long-acting beta-2 agonist.

## 5.4. Clinical safety

Please refer to the table of studies in section 5.3.2. For the purpose of this document, the following definitions apply:

'Adverse event - AE' means any untoward medical occurrence in a subject to whom a medicinal product is administered and which does not necessarily have a causal relationship with this treatment.

'Serious adverse event - SAE' means any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose requires inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity, results in a congenital anomaly or birth defect, is life-threatening, or results in death. The definition (in line with ICH E2A) includes important medical events that may not be immediately life-threatening or result in death or hospitalisation but may jeopardise the patient or may require intervention to prevent one of the other outcomes listed in the definition above.

'Adverse Drug Reaction - ADR' means any untoward and unintended response to a medicinal product related to any dose administered, for which, after a thorough assessment, a causal relationship between the medicinal product and the adverse event is at least a reasonable possibility, based for example, on their comparative incidence in clinical trials, or findings from epidemiological studies and/or on an evaluation of causality from individual case reports.

## 5.4.1. Safety data collection

Pooled analyses of safety data from studies KALOS and LOGOS studies were presented, as their characteristics offer a suitable foundation for combining safety datasets. Analyses were performed after clinical data lock and unblinding. The purpose of data pooling was the potential identification of rare AEs, adverse events of special interest (AESIs) including MACE, and exploration of AEs and SAEs in subgroups in a larger participant set than that available from a single trial.

Safety variables collected and methods of evaluation included exposure, AEs (common AEs, SAEs with outcome of death, other SAEs, AEs leading to discontinuation of IP, possibly related AEs/SAEs, AEs associated with use of ICS, LAMAs, and LABAs, adjudicated events), study population (disposition, demographics, baseline and disease characteristics, medical history, medications).

Safety data have been collected through safety assessments during scheduled visits in both studies. The main safety analysis was carried out once the last randomised patient completed week-52 assessment, with the aim of providing long-term safety data in asthma patients. All continuous variables were summarised using descriptive statistics, while categorical variables were summarised with frequency counts and percentages. No formal hypothesis testing was performed, however, comparisons between treatment groups were made for key safety variables through estimation of risk difference in event rates per 100 participant-years (exposure-adjusted incidence rate) for the on-treatment period.

## 5.4.6. Safety in special populations

Intrinsic factors: There were no clinically meaningful differences, new or unexpected safety findings by subgroups of age, sex, race, and BMI for risks of any safety parameter between BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg vs BFF MDI or Symbicort pMDI, except for the minor variabilities expected based on the smaller sample size of subgroups.

Extrinsic factors: No clinically meaningful differences were observed by region for risks of any safety parameter between BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg vs BFF MDI or Symbicort pMDI, apart from the minor expected variabilities.

Pregnancy and lactation: No animal reproductive toxicology studies were conducted with BGF MDI. Budesonide is known to induce embryofoetal toxicity in rats and rabbits (class effect of glucocorticoids). At high doses/systemic exposure levels, formoterol is known to cause implantation losses and decreases in birth weight/ early postnatal survival, while glycopyrrolate had no significant effects on reproduction. As per the SmPC, BGF MDI should only be used during pregnancy if the expected benefits outweigh the potential risks.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Breast-feeding: No pregnant or breast-feeding women were recruited, nor permitted continuation of study treatment in women who became pregnant during the asthma pivotal studies. Therefore, this programme cannot provide information on whether BGF MDI or its metabolites are excreted in breast milk post administration. Administration of BGF MDI to women who are breast-feeding should only be considered if the expected benefit to the mother is greater than any possible risk to the child, as per the SmPC.

Fertility: No fertility studies were conducted with BGF MDI in humans. Studies in rats showed slight reductions in fertility only at dose levels higher than the maximum human exposure to formoterol. Budesonide and glycopyrronium individually, did not cause any AEs on rats' fertility. Therefore, it is unlikely that BGF MDI administered at the recommended dose will impact fertility in humans.

## 5.4.11. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical safety

## 5.4.11.1. Discussion

As BGF MDI (320/14.4/9.6μg BID) is approved for the treatment of COPD in adults and has a wellestablished safety profile in this indication, there is extensive safety data on the individual components in BGF MDI. The safety evaluation for BGF MDI in asthma focuses on the proposed new formulation for use in this indication: 5 micrograms of formoterol fumarate dihydrate, glycopyrronium bromide 18 micrograms, equivalent to 14.4 micrograms of glycopyrronium, and budesonide 160 micrograms. The proposed posology of BGF MDI in asthma is 2 inhalations twice daily. Throughout the safety summary, reference is made to developed BGF MDI as BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg to reflect the total dose administered on a twice daily (BID) basis (as all doses reflect the sum of two actuations).

## 5.4.11.1.1. Overall assessment of available safety data

Safety in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg asthma clinical programme is supported by 2 completed pivotal studies, KALOS and LOGOS, which were pooled to evaluate BGF MDI safety the treatment of adults and younger patients &gt;12-18 years with inadequately controlled asthma at a dose of 320/28.8/9.6μg. The safety of 2 doses of BGF MDI (320/28.8/9.6μg and 320/14.4/9.6μg) were individually compared with 2 ICS/LABA active comparators, BFF MDI 320/9.6μg and Symbicort pMDI 320/9μg, in adult and adolescent participants with inadequately controlled asthma. The pooling approach is considered acceptable.

The pooled safety population included 4309 participants. The disposition of participants in the pivotal studies in asthma was generally balanced across the groups. The mean age in the safety population was 52.1 years; the majority of participants in the safety population were aged between 18 and 65 years (approximately 75% per treatment group). About 20% of participants across treatment groups were over the age of 65 years, but none were &gt;85 years. No specific safety concerns were identified for elderly patients &gt;65 years. There were 128 participants in the ≥12 to &lt;18 years age group (n=19 in the BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg group, n=30 in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group, n=39 in the BFF MDI 320/9.6μg group and n=40 participants in the SYMBICORT pMDI 320/9μg group). The overall number of exposed adolescent patients was in line with the agreed updated PIP. The pooled safety database met the ICH E1 requirements; over 1103 patients completed a full 24 weeks of treatment with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg.

Supportive studies (LITHOS, VATHOS) were also submitted. No new safety findings were reported in supportive studies LITHOS and VATHOS.

The overall incidence of AEs in the pooled safety population was similar between BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg vs BFF MDI or Symbicort pMDI treatment groups (54.2% in BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg, 55.2% in BFF MDI 320/9.6μg and 58.4% in Symbicort pMDI 320/9μg). The number of patients with AEs assessed as 'possibly related' by the investigator was numerically higher in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg treatment group compared to the other treatments. However, the percentage of patients in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg treatment group with SAEs assessed as 'possibly related' was similar compared with the BFF MDI or Symbicort pMDI treatment groups. The number of on treatment fatal SAEs was comparable between the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg, BFF MDI or Symbicort pMDI groups.

The most frequently reported treatment emergent AEs in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group in the safety population were 'upper respiratory tract infection', 'nasopharyngitis', 'COVID-19' and 'headache' which were mild to moderate. The overall incidence of the most frequently reported AEs during the ontreatment period was generally comparable among the treatment groups.

Oral candidiasis: cases of 'oral candidiasis' were numerically higher in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group vs other treatment groups, with exposure adjusted incidence rate of 0.88 in this group vs 0.58 in BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg, 0.68 in BFF MDI 320/9.6μg and 0.77 in Symbicort pMDI 320/9μg. ## 9. Benefit-risk assessment

## 9.1. Therapeutic context

## 9.1.1. Disease or condition, proposed therapeutic indication

Asthma is a serious and life-threatening condition affecting adults and children, who experience wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness and cough, together with variable expiratory airflow limitation and chronic airway inflammation. Symptoms are triggered by various factors, including exercise, allergen or irritant exposure, changes in weather, or viral respiratory infections. Asthma management includes symptom control, maintaining normal activity levels, and minimising the risk of asthma-related mortality, exacerbations, persistent airflow limitation, and treatment side effects.

Asthma in adolescents can be considered comparable to asthma in the adult population, with the exception of factors such as increased comorbidities in adults. In general, asthma is characterised by the same core pathophysiological features in both age groups, including airway inflammation, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, variable expiratory airflow limitation, and risk of exacerbations. Additionally, both adults and adolescents have the same treatment strategy, treatment-escalation approaches (i.e. stepwise approach) and therapeutic targets as outlined under GINA guidelines.

Riltrava Aerosphere (also known as BGF MDI) contains three drug substances, each individually wellestablished in the treatment of asthma: budesonide, a corticosteroid that exhibits potent glucocorticoid and weak mineralocorticoid activity, glycopyrronium a LAMA that exerts bronchodilatory effect via muscarinic receptors located on smooth muscle cells within the trachea and bronchi, and formoterol fumarate, a potent and selective LABA that, when inhaled, acts locally in the lungs as a bronchodilator. The indication initially proposed by the MAH was: maintenance treatment of asthma in patients 12 years of age and older who are not adequately controlled by a combination of a medium or high dose inhaled corticosteroid and a long-acting beta2-agonist (for effects on symptoms control and prevention of exacerbations see section 5.1).

## 9.1.2. Available therapies and unmet medical need

Available therapies: Currently, there are 2 fixed-dose triple combination products for the maintenance treatment of asthma in patients aged ≥ 18 years authorised in the EU:

beclomethasone/formoterol/glycopyrronium (Trimbow/Trydonis),

mometasone/indacaterol/glycopyrronium (Enerzair/Zimbus Breezhaler). Hence, there are limited options for patients in whom an MDI is the preferred method of delivery. There are currently no fixed-dose triple combination products for the maintenance treatment of asthma in adolescent patients aged 12 to 17 years authorised in the EU.

Unmet medical need: There is a group of patients with sub-optimal asthma control and/or acute exacerbations despite the use of therapies containing medium-or high- dose ICS (difficult-to-treat asthma). Asthma control remains inadequate for around 50% of GINA Step 4 and 5 patients and GINA

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

recommends inhaled LAMA in addition to ICS/LABA. Triple therapy has been associated with a substantially improved lung function and reduced exacerbations. The benefits of including ICS/LAMA/LABA components in a single triple therapy inhaler may include: a) clinical improvements of 3 different pharmacological classes with unique mechanisms of action; b)optimising lung function symptoms while minimising risk of exacerbations; c )possible improved patient adherence with a single MDI and elimination of the selective use of bronchoactive vs anti-inflammatory maintenance treatments; limits potential errors, and d) addition of LAMAs to ICS/LABA could provide an important step-up in care before escalation to systemic or invasive therapies.

## 9.2. Main clinical studies

For a detailed description of the main clinical studies supporting this application, please refer to section 5.3.2. KALOS and LOGOS were phase 3, randomised, double-blind, double-dummy, parallel-group, multicentre trials evaluating BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6 µg in patients aged ≥12 years with inadequately controlled asthma despite medium or high-dose ICS/LABA therapy. Following a 4-week run-in with BFF MDI 320/9.6 µg BID, participants were randomised to receive one of four treatments: BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6 µg, BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6 µg, BFF MDI 320/9.6 µg, or Symbicort pMDI 320/9 µg. All treatments were administered as two inhalations BID for 24 to 52 weeks. Due to recruitment challenges partially related to the COVID-19 pandemic, the lower-strength BGF MDI arm (320/14.4/9.6 µg) was discontinued mid-study, though enrolled participants continued treatment. Subsequent randomisation was adjusted to a 1:1:1 ratio across the remaining arms. Blinding was maintained via double-dummy methodology. Across both studies, 4320 participants were randomised, with 1,179 in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6 µg arm and 2400 in the pooled comparator arms. Thirty adolescent patients were on active treatment. Primary and key secondary endpoints included lung function, rate of severe asthma exacerbations, asthma control, and HRQoL. Data from both studies were pooled to enhance statistical power for exacerbation analyses and subgroup evaluations.

## 9.3. Favourable effects

Effects on lung function: For the co-primary endpoint (change from baseline in morning pre-dose trough FEV1 for both pivotal studies over 24 weeks), statistically significant treatment differences in trough FEV₁ were observed for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg in both KALOS (0.056L; 95% CI: 0.031, 0.082; p &lt; 0.001) and LOGOS (0.097L; 95% CI: 0.070, 0.123; p &lt; 0.001) compared with pooled comparators, BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI. The key secondary endpoint was change from baseline in FEV₁ AUC0-3 over 24 weeks. BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg demonstrated statistically significant improvements over pooled comparators in both pivotal studies: an adjusted mean difference of 0.069 L (95% CI: 0.044, 0.094; p &lt; 0.001) in KALOS and 0.112 L (95% CI: 0.087, 0.138; p &lt; 0.001) in LOGOS. Subgroup analyses for spirometry endpoints generally showed no meaningful differences across predefined groups (age, sex, race, baseline history of severe exacerbations, baseline pre-bronchodilator % predicted FEV₁, prior ICS dose, % reversibility, and geographic region). Lung function findings from the individual studies were supported by pooled analyses: in the combined dataset from KALOS and LOGOS (N=1,179), treatment with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg resulted in a nominally significant 0.166L increase from baseline (LS mean change) in the morning pre-dose trough FEV1 over 24 weeks compared with 90 mL for pooled ICS/LABA control (difference: 0.076L, 95% CI: 0.057, 0.094). Similar nominally significant improvements were seen for change from baseline in FEV1 AUC0-3 over 24 weeks for the combined analysis of KALOS and LOGOS, which resulted in a 0.090L improvement compared to BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg ICS/LABA control. Improvements in lung function were sustained over 52 weeks.

Effects on exacerbations: The annualised rate of severe asthma exacerbations was evaluated as a coprimary endpoint based on the pooled analysis of KALOS and LOGOS. The adjusted exacerbation rate per year (SE) was lower in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group [0.541 (0.028)] vs to the pooled ICS/LABA comparator group [0.633 (0.022)]. This corresponded to a rate ratio of 0.855 (95% CI: 0.757, 0.966; p = 0.012), indicating a statistically significant 14.5% relative reduction in the rate of severe asthma exacerbations with BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg. The rate of severe asthma exacerbations over the treatment period was evaluated as a secondary endpoint in each pivotal study. In KALOS, BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6 µg demonstrated a nominally statistically significant reduction in exacerbation rate compared with pooled BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI (rate ratio: 0.803; 95% CI: 0.684, 0.943; p = 0.007), corresponding to an approximate 20% relative rate reduction. In contrast, LOGOS did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction (rate ratio: 0.925; 95% CI: 0.768, 1.113; p = 0.407), corresponding to an approximate 7.5% relative rate reduction. Subgroup analyses of the pooled severe asthma exacerbation endpoint did not reveal any meaningful differences across predefined subgroups (age, sex, race, baseline history of severe exacerbations, baseline pre-bronchodilator % predicted FEV₁, prior ICS dose, % reversibility, and geographic region), with a consistent trend favouring BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg. In the subgroup without a prior exacerbation history in the previous year, the risk for severe exacerbations was reduced in the BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg group relative to the pooled ICS/LABA group, the comparison rate ratio (95% CI) was 0.828 (0.675, 1.015) with absolute risk reduction of 0.095 (0.457 vs 0.552). These results are comparable to the subgroup with ≥1 exacerbations in the previous year, comparison rate ratio (95% CI) 0.861 (0.740, 1.003), with an absolute risk reduction of 0.100 (0.621 vs 0.721). Patient Reported Outcome and Health-related Quality of Life endpoints: Nominally significant improvements were observed only for the St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) in the KALOS study (odds ratio: 1.31; 95% CI: 1.04, 1.64; p = 0.021) and the Asthma Control Questionnaire-7 (ACQ7) in the LOGOS study (odds ratio: 1.32; 95% CI: 1.06, 1.64; p = 0.012). In the combined analysis of KALOS and LOGOS, all groups showed clinically relevant improvements from baseline in symptom control and health related life quality outcomes (indicated by changes in ACQ-7 and AQLQ+12 score). The proportion of patients achieving the minimum clinically important difference in ACQ-7 was 70% for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg compared with 65% for pooled ICS/LABA treatment arms over 24 weeks (odds ratio [OR] 1.26; 95% CI: 1.08, 1.47). The proportion of patients achieving MCID ≥0.5 in AQLQ+12 was 58% for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg compared with 57% for pooled ICS/LABA treatment arms over 24 weeks (OR 1.10, 95% CI: 0.94, 1.29). Responder rates with ACQ-5 (73%) were similar to those with ACQ-7 over 24 weeks. Adolescents: A total of 128 adolescents aged 12 to&lt;18 years with uncontrolled asthma were randomised in KALOS and LOGOS and received treatment with 2 inhalations twice daily of Riltrava Aerosphere 5/14.4/160μg (N=30)/Riltrava Aerosphere 5/7.2/160μg (N=19) compared with 2 inhalations twice daily of pooled FOR/BUD MDI 5/160μg and FOR/BUD MDI 4.5/160μg (N=79). ## 9.3.1. Uncertainties and limitations about favourable effects

Effect on lung function: in both pivotal studies, the change from baseline in pre-dose FEV₁ at week 24 was statistically significant with treatment differences between BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg, BFF MDI and Symbicort pMDI. In KALOS, the treatment effect was sustained through week 52, however, in LOGOS, a trend toward lower treatment difference was seen after week 24, although it must be noted results remained nominally significant. Nevertheless, the patients were not followed for longer than 52 weeks, and hence, it is not possible to determine whether this trend would have persisted with longer follow-up. Effect on exacerbations: while the co-primary endpoint for reduction in annualised severe exacerbation rate was met, the magnitude of the relative reduction was modest (14.5%). This effect was observed in patients with or without a history of exacerbations as the relative reductions were 13.9% and 17.2%, respectively. These values fall marginally below the 20% threshold typically proposed for clinical relevance. Furthermore, in relation to the treatment effect on severe exacerbations, and due to recruitment challenges, a pooled co-primary endpoint analysis was pursued, contrary to SA. The individual study exacerbation analyses were evaluated as secondary endpoints instead. Although the pooled analysis met the co-primary endpoint according to the confirmatory hierarchical testing strategy of each study, the approach introduced potential for Type I error inflation at the submission level as the propagation of alpha in both the KALOS and LOGOS studies effectively led to a situation where statistical significance can be tested on the same pooled data twice. Whilst it is agreed that the type I error rate was controlled sufficiently at an individual study level, and is therefore acceptable, a more conservative approach would have been preferred. Patient Reported Outcome (PRO) and Health-related Quality of Life (HRQOL) endpoints: Findings were inconsistent across studies, with only nominal significance observed for SGRQ (KALOS) and ACQ-7 (LOGOS). The ACQ-7 result may reflect lung function improvements rather than true patient-perceived benefit, as supported by less favourable ACQ-5 outcomes. When results were pooled across KALOS and LOGOS, only ACQ-5, ACQ-7 and AQLQ+12 were presented. Of these, only ACQ-7 achieved nominal significance, as it was outside the Type I error control procedure. Nonetheless, PRO/HRQOL endpoints are subject to high inter-patient variability and absence of statistical significance does not necessarily preclude a clinically meaningful effect. Adolescents: Although pivotal studies were not powered to show efficacy in adolescents, within individual studies, results for adolescents were generally directionally consistent with overall findings for co-primary endpoints. However, only 128 adolescents were enrolled across both pivotal studies, with limited representation in the proposed treatment arm (N=30). No substantial supportive information can be taken from exposure data comparing the adolescent and adult population, as only 1 adolescent patient who received BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg was analysed for PK. Therefore, pre-specified Bayesian borrowing analyses were applied to pooled efficacy data to enhance precision by leveraging adult data for select endpoints. In conducting these analyses, large fraction of the effective sample sizes were borrowed from the adult population to support the adolescent data. Although the overall data support the extrapolation of the Riltrava Aerosphere indication to adolescents, the limited sample size is to be acknowledged. Treatment of adolescent patients will be monitored post-authorisation.

## 9.4. Unfavourable effects

The three components of Riltrava Aerosphere have well-characterised safety profiles, being already approved for the COPD management. The AEs seen in the pivotal clinical trials in the asthma safety population were generally in line with the established class effects of ICS/LABA/LAMA. The most frequently reported treatment emergent AEs were 'upper respiratory tract infection', 'nasopharyngitis', 'COVID-19' and 'headache', which are not unexpected. Rates of these AEs were comparable to those reported in the BFF MDI or Symbicort pMDI treatment groups. The most frequent AE considered possibly related to the study drug which is reflected in the PI was dysphonia (1.4%), with a higher incidence compared with the BFF MDI (0.7%) or Symbicort pMDI (0.8%). In the overall 'on-treatment' comparison of AEs in the pooled safety population, reports of 'oral candidiasis' were highest in the study drug group compared to all other groups. Oral candidiasis is listed as an ADR in sec. 4.8 of the SmPC. Eye disorders were reported at a low frequency but were more frequently reported in the BGF MDI 320/14.4/9.6μg treatment group and are included in the PI. A trend towards a slightly higher numerical incidence of exposure adjusted reports of 'atrial fibrillation' in BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg was seen, although the number of reports was low. Atrial fibrillation is listed as an ADR in sec. 4.8 of the SmPC. A low number of patients (n=10 across all treatment groups) in the pooled safety population had 'on treatment' SAEs with outcome death; the numbers were comparable across the treatment groups with no new safety concerns identified.

Adolescents: Exposure in adolescent patients was limited in the asthma pivotal trials. Based on the pooled data from the KALOS and LOGOS, BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg was well tolerated in this subgroup, with a safety profile similar to that of overall (incl. adult) population. No new or unexpected safety findings were observed. No deaths were reported. There was only one AE associated with the use of ICS, LAMAs, and LABAs reported for BGF 320/28.8/9.6μg in the adolescent population. This was a non-serious event of bronchitis bacterial which was unrelated to treatment.

## 9.4.1. Uncertainties and limitations about unfavourable effects

The safety profile of Riltrava Aerosphere in the asthma population is overall consistent with the already available safety data of the mono-components in the COPD population; no new safety signals were identified. Oral candidiasis, although well established in the setting of the actives in the triple combination BGF MDI is a common AE which could impact compliance. Similarly, the higher frequency of reports of dysphonia, including discontinuations, with BGF MDI treatment in the asthma population raises uncertainty in relation to long term compliance or discontinuations. Dysphonia severity and its impact on quality of life are variable for patients. The relevant ADRs and warnings are reflected in the SmPC.

In addition, topics of special interest including ocular and systemic effects, MACE, sudden cardiac death and pneumonia will have to be monitored and discussed as safety concerns in the PSURs. These events are known for ICS/LABA/LAMA combination products, and their inclusion in routine PSUR monitoring reflects standard pharmacovigilance practice, which is considered appropriate.

Adolescents: Although no new major safety signals have been identified in the adolescent population, there are uncertainties regarding the extent to which the safety profile particularly at the proposed higher strength of glycopyrronium is characterised in the adolescent population, particularly with long term use due to limited exposure in the pivotal asthma studies. Therefore, safety in adolescents (incl. ocular and systemic effects), and their long-term safety, will be subject to monitoring in the PSURs.

## 9.6. Benefit-risk assessment and discussion

## 9.6.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

The participants enrolled in the KALOS and LOGOS studies are representative of patients with asthma not adequately controlled despite treatment with inhaled medium or high-dose ICS in combination with a LABA, consistent with GINA Steps 4 and 5. For these patients, treatment options include the addition of a LAMA or switching to a triple combination therapy (ICS/LABA/LAMA). In certain subsets, escalation to biologics, bronchial thermoplasty, or low-dose oral corticosteroids may be considered.

The initial indication proposed for BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6µg was maintenance treatment of asthma in patients aged 12 years and older who are not adequately controlled by a combination of medium or highdose ICS and LABA. This population includes adolescents and patients with and without a documented exacerbation in the prior year.

Adults: The trough FEV₁ endpoint was met with statistical significance in both pivotal studies. The magnitude of effect size is varied but a significant and clinically relevant treatment effect has been demonstrated across KALOS and LOGOS. The pooled analysis of the second co-primary endpoint, rate of severe exacerbations, showed a statistically significant 14.5% relative rate reduction. However, individual study results were inconsistent. KALOS showed a nominally significant ~20% relative reduction, while LOGOS showed a nominally non-significant ~7.5% relative reduction. Importantly, a similar magnitude of effect was shown in patients with and without a history of exacerbations in the previous year. While no validated MCID exists for exacerbation reduction, a 20-40% reduction is generally considered clinically meaningful in randomised controlled trials. Although the observed 14.5% reduction falls below this range, it was consistent regardless of exacerbation history and can be considered clinically relevant in patients whose asthma is inadequately controlled despite treatment with medium dose ICS/LABA therapy.

The overall safety profile of BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg was consistent with the already available data from the COPD development and the established safety profiles for the individual components in this ICS/LAMA/LABA triple combination, with no new major safety signals identified, which is reassuring. The most common serious adverse reactions are listed in sec 4.8 of the SmPC. Ongoing monitoring in the PSURs will help characterise the long-term safety profile, particularly with respect to the identified topics of special interest.

Adolescents: While the limited representation of adolescent patients in the BGF MDI efficacy and safety database limits a full interpretation, it is acknowledged that no specific trends indicating differences in the overall efficacy or safety profiles were observed in the pivotal clinical studies in this population. Based on the duration of treatment/follow up, up to 1 year, uncertainty about the longer-term safety including local and systemic effects is acknowledged, however, appropriate warnings regarding potential systemic side effects have been included in the SmPC sec 4.4. Continued monitoring through the PSURs will allow the adolescent long-term safety profile to be better characterised as additional data become available. The limitations of the observed data in adolescents were offset by an extrapolation exercise, in which the totality of data from a number of different data sources was considered, including data from the pivotal asthma studies reference to bibliographic data/data on the established safety profiles and pharmacology of the individual active substances in the triple combination product, in addition to current international asthma guidelines. The approach was considered sufficient to provide assurance to support the approval of the product in adolescents, notwithstanding the limited direct clinical experience in this population.

## 9.6.2. Balance of benefits and risks

Participants enrolled in the KALOS and LOGOS trials are representative of asthma patients not adequately controlled despite their treatment with inhaled ICS in combination with LABA. Their treatment options include: addition of LAMA such as tiotropium; switch to an authorised fixed-dose triple combination therapy (ICS/LABA/LAMA). The currently authorised triple ICS/LABA/LAMA combinations Trimbow(/Trydonis) and Enerzair(/Zimbus) Breezhaler are approved for use in adults whose asthma is inadequately controlled with maintenance ICS and LABA. Trimbow has both medium and high-dose ICS combination presentations, while Enerzair is indicated for those not adequately maintained on high-dose ICS+LABA therapy. Therapeutic escalation to these products requires patients to have experienced at least one exacerbation in the previous year. Adults: The trough FEV₁ endpoint was met with statistical significance in both studies (0.056L in KALOS and 0.097L in LOGOS). The magnitude of effect size varies but a significant and clinically relevant treatment effect is demonstrated. While no universally accepted minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for FEV₁ in asthma exists, improvements of ≥100 mL are generally considered clinically meaningful. In patients already receiving ICS/LABA, the MCID may be lower as baseline bronchodilation limits the potential for further gains. For reference, Enerzair Breezhaler's authorisation was supported by a 0.065L difference in trough FEV₁, deemed \"borderline clinically relevant\". Trimbow showed improvements of 0.057L and 0.073L (for medium- and high-dose comparisons, respectively), considered \"modest\". The lung function data observed across KALOS and LOGOS are hence considered comparable to currently approved products and of modest clinical relevance. The pooled analysis of the second coprimary endpoint, rate of severe exacerbations, showed a statistically significant 14.5% relative rate reduction. Though modest, the observed effect on severe exacerbation rate can be considered clinically relevant for patients whose asthma is inadequately controlled despite treatment with medium ICS/LABA therapy. Importantly, a similar magnitude of effect was shown in patients with and without a history of exacerbations in the previous year. The overall safety profile of BGF MDI 320/28.8/9.6μg was generally consistent with the already available data from the COPD development and the established safety profiles for the individual components in this ICS/LAMA/LABA triple combination. Generally, no new safety signals have been identified. The SmPC provides appropriate guidance and relevant warnings for HCPs on managing the risks of this product for the intended patient population. There remains some uncertainty in relation to safety in longer term use beyond one year. A number of outcomes of interest including MACE, sudden cardiac deaths, ocular events, pneumonia, systemic safety e.g. impact on growth, BMD, endocrine effects, HPA axis will continue to be monitored through the PSURs. Adolescents: While the limited representation of adolescents in the BGF MDI efficacy and safety database somewhat restricts the interpretation, it is acknowledged that no specific trends to indicate differences in the overall efficacy or safety profiles were observed in the BGF MDI pivotal clinical studies in this population. The extrapolation exercise in which the totality of data from a number of different data sources was considered, including data from the pivotal asthma studies, reference to bibliographic data/data on the established safety profiles and pharmacology of the individual active substances in the triple combination product, in addition to current international asthma guidelines is supportive. Due to the short duration of treatment/follow up, up to 1 year, there is uncertainty about the long-term safety including local and systemic effects however, appropriate warnings regarding potential systemic side effects have been included in the SmPC sec. 4.4 and systemic safety will be closely monitored in the postmarketing setting, via regular PSURs.
