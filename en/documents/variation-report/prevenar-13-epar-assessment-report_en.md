## 1. Introduction

On 4 June 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Prevenar 13, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that Study B7471024 - A Phase 3 Randomized, Double-Blind Trial to Evaluate the Safety and Immunogenicity of a 20-Valent Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine Given in a Series of 3 Infant Doses and 1 Toddler Dose in Infants in India and Taiwan is a stand alone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

13vPnC is a sterile liquid suspension formulation containing saccharides from pneumococcal serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, and 23F individually conjugated to CRM197. The vaccine is formulated to contain 2.2 µg of each saccharide, except for 4.4 µg of 6B, per 0.5-mL dose. The vaccine contains succinate buffer, sodium chloride, polysorbate 80, and 125 µg aluminum as aluminum phosphate, per 0.5-mL dose.

20vPnC contains the serotypes present in 13vPnC, and 7 new serotypes (8, 10A, 11A, 12F, 15B, 22F, and 33F), individually conjugated to CRM197. 20vPnC uses the same platform and contains the same excipients as 13vPnC (Prevnar 13®/Prevenar 13®).

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## Introduction

The MAH submitted a final report for:

- Study B7471024 - A Phase 3 Randomized, Double-Blind Trial to Evaluate the Safety and Immunogenicity of a 20-Valent Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine Given in a Series of 3 Infant Doses and 1 Toddler Dose in Infants in India and Taiwan.

The marketing authorisation for 13vPnC for the immunisation of children 6 weeks to 5 years of age was approved in the EU on 09 December 2009 with the following indication:

- Active immunisation for the prevention of invasive disease, pneumonia, and AOM caused by S pneumoniae in infants and children from 6 weeks to 5 years of age.

Later on, based on results from additional clinical trials, the indication of 13vPnC was extended for all children from 6 weeks of age and adults. Since 26 February 2015, the current EU-approved indications for the use of 13vPnC are therefore:

- Active immunisation for the prevention of invasive disease, pneumonia and acute otitis media caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae in infants, children and adolescents from 6 weeks to 17 years of age;
- Active immunisation for the prevention of invasive disease and pneumonia caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae in adults ≥18 years of age and the elderly.

## Clinical study

Study B7471024 - A Phase 3 Randomized, Double-Blind Trial to Evaluate the Safety and Immunogenicity of a 20-Valent Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine Given in a Series of 3 Infant Doses and 1 Toddler Dose in Infants in India and Taiwan

## Description

The purpose of this Phase 3, multicenter, randomised, double-blind study was to assess the safety and immunogenicity of 20vPnC in infants and to provide key safety data to support licensure in this population in India and Taiwan. The targeted age of the population for this study, infants ≥42 to ≤84 days of age, has been selected as this is the range that encompasses the routinely recommended age for initial vaccination with PCVs and other vaccines in infants in India and Taiwan. The specific dosing was given according to a local recommended schedule. 13vPnC has been used as an active comparator in this study. Please see the details of the study design in figure below.

## Study participants and treatments

Approximately 540 healthy infants ≥42 to ≤84 days of age in India (approximately 350 infants) and Taiwan (approximately 190 infants) were to be enrolled and randomised (1:1) to receive either 20vPnC or 13vPnC. The infants were to receive vaccine according to a schedule of 3 infant doses and a toddler dose. The specific dosing was to be given according to the local recommended schedule. ## Sample size

The study size is not based on any formal statistical hypothesis test as this is a descriptive study. The sample size calculation for Indian cohort based on primary immunogenicity objective and for Taiwanese cohort on primary safety objective.

In the India Cohort (excluding Site 1012),

- 250 (99.6%) participants were included in the safety population: 125 (100.0%) and 125 (99.2%) participants in the 20vPnC and 13vPnC groups, respectively.
- 235 (93.6%) participants were included in the Dose 3 evaluable immunogenicity population: 119 (95.2%) and 116 (92.1%) participants in the 20vPnC and 13vPnC groups, respectively.
- 216 (86.1%) participants were included in the Dose 4 evaluable immunogenicity population: 112 (89.6%) and 104 (82.5%) participants in the 20vPnC and 13vPnC groups, respectively.

In the Taiwan Cohort,

- 190 (100.0%) participants were included in the safety population: 94 (100.0%) and 96 (100.0%) participants in the 20vPnC and 13vPnC groups, respectively.
- 186 (97.9%) participants were included in the Dose 3 evaluable immunogenicity population: 92 (97.9%) and 94 (97.9%) participants in the 20vPnC and 13vPnC groups, respectively.
- 180 (94.7%) participants were included in the Dose 4 evaluable immunogenicity population: 88 (93.6%) and 92 (95.8%) participants in the 20vPnC and 13vPnC groups, respectively.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

This was a double-blind study. All participants were randomized to study intervention using an IRT.

## Statistical Methods

The primary safety objective was evaluated by descriptive summary statistics for local reactions, systemic events, and AEs (including SAEs). AEs were categorized according to the MedDRA. Local reactions (redness, swelling, and pain at the injection site) and systemic events (fever, decreased appetite, drowsiness/increased sleep, and irritability) were prompted for and collected daily by the participants' parents/legal guardians using an e-diary application, for 7 days following each dose (Days 1 through 7, where Day 1 was the day of vaccination).

AEs were collected from the time the participants' parents/legal guardians provided informed consent through 1 month after Dose 3 (Visit 4) and from Dose 4 (Visit 5) through 1 month after Dose 4 (Visit 6). SAEs were collected from informed consent through 1 month after Dose 4 (Visit 6).
