## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Introduction

## 2.1.1. Problem statement

## Disease or condition

Haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII [FVIII] deficiency) is a bleeding disorder caused by a deficiency of coagulation FVIII, a key component of the intrinsic pathway. In patients with haemophilia A, the primary platelet-driven haemostasis is not affected, but generation of a stable, fibrin-rich clot is defective because inadequate amounts of thrombin are generated.

## Claimed therapeutic indication

The currently authorised indication is treatment and prophylaxis of bleeding in previously treated patients (PTSs) ≥ 7 years of age with haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency).

The MAH applied for the following extension of indication to include previously untreated patients: treatment and prophylaxis of bleeding in patients ≥ 7 years of age with haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency).'

## Epidemiology and risk factors, screening tools/prevention

Haemophilia A is clinically characterised by recurrent bleeding into tissue and joints leading to progressive joint destruction. The disease occurs worldwide with an estimated incidence of 1 to 2 in 10,000 births, affecting predominantly males.

## Clinical presentation, diagnosis

Hemophilia A is usually diagnosed by measuring FVIII clotting activity (FVIII:C) level in the plasma of a patient. There is a direct correlation between FVIII activity levels and clinical manifestations. Hemophilia A is defined as severe if the plasma FVIII:C level (measured as IU/dL) is &lt; 1%, moderate if it is between 1% and 5%, and mild if it is between &gt; 5% and 40% of normal.

Hemophilia A can result in spontaneous and life-threatening bleeding events or excessive bleeding in response to trauma. Bleeds occur in muscle, central nervous system, organs, soft tissue and most frequently in joints, which leads to joint damage and severe disability, with major effects on the physical, psychosocial, quality of life, and financial conditions of the haemophilia patients.

## Management

Per the current recommendations of the World Federation of Hemophilia (WFH) guidelines for the management of haemophilia as well as of many national and international haemophilia organisations, the standard of care for all patients with severe haemophilia A is primary prophylaxis, defined as regular therapy with FVIII replacement products or other haemostasis products to prevent spontaneous bleeding. Primary prophylaxis should be initiated early in life prior to the onset of joint disease, before the second clinically evident joint bleed and before 3 years of age to prevent musculoskeletal complications from recurrent joint and muscle bleeds.

Hemlibra (emicizumab) is indicated for routine prophylaxis of bleeding episodes in patients with haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) with or without factor VIII inhibitors.

Efanesoctocog alfa (Altuvoct) is a modified recombinant factor VIII that is designed to enhance the haemostatic function. It works by binding to activated factor X (Xa) to facilitate the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin, thereby promoting the clotting cascade effectively. Its unique structure allows for stable interaction with the coagulation system, improving its efficacy in controlling bleeding.

Both Hemlibra and Altuvoct can be used in all age groups.

A gene therapy of haemophilia A, valoctocogene roxaparvovec (Roctavian), an adeno-associated viral serotype 5 (AAV5) vector containing a B-domain-deleted variant of human FVIII had been authorized in the EU for the treatment of severe haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) in adult patients without a history of factor VIII inhibitors and without detectable antibodies to adenoassociated virus serotype 5 (AAV5). However, it has since been withdrawn in Europe.

Episodic or on-demand FVIII treatment, i.e., treating bleeds when they occur, is no longer considered the standard of care for the management of haemophilia as it does not alter the natural history of frequent and recurrent bleeding and related complications. Patients suffering from severe haemophilia A usually receive prophylactic treatment with FVIII products, which requires regular infusion every 2 to 3 days.

## 2.1.2. About the product

The active substance, damoctocog alfa pegol, is a site specifically PEGylated B-domain deleted recombinant human coagulation factor VIII, produced in baby hamster kidney cells (BHK), with a 60 kDa branched polyethylene-glycol (two 30 kDa PEG) moiety. The molecular weight of the protein is approximately 234 kDa.

Jivi is produced without the addition of any human or animal derived protein in the cell culture process, purification, PEGylation or final formulation.

Damoctocog alfa pegol is a PEGylated form of rFVIII. Site-specific PEGylation reduces clearance of factor VIII resulting in an extended half-life while maintaining the normal functions of the B-domain deleted rFVIII molecule (see section 5.2). Damoctocog alfa pegol does not contain von Willebrand factor.

## 2.4. Clinical efficacy

## 2.4.1. Main study

Alfa-PROTECT study (21824) (Part B) A Phase 3, single-group treatment, openlabel, study to evaluate the safety of BAY 94-9027 infusions for prophylaxis and treatment of bleeding in previously treated children aged 7 to &lt;12 years with severe hemophilia A.

## Methods

An open-label, single-group, uncontrolled, prospective, multicenter study evaluating the safety of Jivi in children aged 7 to &lt;12 years with severe hemophilia A. The main study (Part A) lasted 6 months with a visit frequency of 2x/week for the first 4 exposure days (EDs), and monthly thereafter, and the extension study (Part B) extended for 18 months. In Part A, participants received Jivi intravenously (IV) as prophylactic treatment 2x/week, with a dose of 40 IU/kg (up to 60 IU/kg at the investigator's discretion). In Part B, participants could continue the same regimen or have it adjusted to 60 IU/kg IV every 5 days, at the investigator's discretion.

## Study participants

## Treatments

PROTECT Kids and Alfa-PROTECT extension studies in patients 7 to &lt;12 years of age (pooled analysis).

A total of 74 PTP (7 to &lt; 12 years) received prophylaxis treatment, 42 of them twice weekly (2560 IU/kg) with a mean dose of 46 IU/kg. The median (Q1; Q3) ABR was 0.0 (0.0; 1.4).

A total of 28 patients received prophylaxis treatment every 5 days (45-60 IU/kg) with a mean dose of 55.0 IU/kg. The median ABR was 1.4 (0.7; 5.0). Each participant could continue their prophylaxis dose as prescribed in Part A (40 - 60 IU/kg, 2x/week). Dose frequency could be decreased to every 5 days 60 IU/kg at the investigator's discretion.

## Objectives

To assess safety and tolerability of Jivi replacement therapy in previously treated patients 7- &lt;12 years of age with severe hemophilia A.

To describe clinical efficacy of Jivi.

## Outcomes/endpoints

Primary endpoints were the following: Adverse events of special interest (AESI, hypersensitivity and LoE) associated with the first 4 exposure days (EDs) leading to discontinuation.

Adverse drug reactions (ADRs), anti-drug antibody (ADA) development and inhibitor development were included as secondary endpoints.

Assessment of efficacy was included as a secondary objective. The following efficacy endpoints were assessed: annualised bleeding rate (ABR), Jivi consumption, number of infusions/month and year (Annualised Infusion Rate).

## Sample size

The study was not designed to test any predefined hypothesis. Planned enrollment: ≥30 participants.

## Randomisation

Not Applicable.

## Blinding (masking)

The study was an open-label, single-group, uncontrolled study.

## Statistical methods

The study was not designed to test any predefined hypothesis. Therefore, endpoints were analysed by descriptive statistical methods. For the efficacy analysis, the annualised bleeding rate (ABR) was summarised using descriptive statistics and estimated using a negative binomial regression model.

## Annualised infusion rate

Participants received 4087 infusions during Part B, primarily for prophylaxis (98.8%). The mean number of infusions per participant per year (all infusions) was 98.1 (SD: 11.5), or approximately 8.2 infusions per month.

As expected by the dosing regimens, the 2x/week group averaged 104.2 infusions per year (8.7 per month) compared to 75.3 per year (6.3 per month) in every 5 days group.

## FVIII recovery

FVIII recovery was assessed throughout the study, with assessment doses ranging from 42.6 to 74.3 IU/kg (mean: 58.53 IU/kg), reflecting the individual prophylaxis dose.

The median FVIII recovery across all timepoints was 1.72 kg/dL (Q1, Q3: 1.52, 1.88). Throughout Part B, median values ranged from 1.64 kg/dL at Month 12 in Part B to 2.01 kg/dL at the end of Part A/start of Part B.

## Patient-reported outcomes questionnaire

Health-related quality of life improved from baseline to the end of Part B, as measured by the Haemo-QoL questionnaire. The mean Total score improved from 27.26 (SD: 12.32) at baseline to 18.62 (SD: 11.57) at the final visit. The largest improvements were observed in the Sports, Friends, Feelings, and Physical Health domains. Disease severity assessments showed improvements from baseline to the end of Part B. The proportion of caregivers reporting \"no problems at all\" increased from 42.9% to 75.0%. Similarly, the proportion of participants reporting \"no problems at all\" increased from 64.3% to 71.4%. Global impression of change ratings at the final visit showed that 82.1% of caregivers and 89.3% of participants reporting being \"much improved\" or \"very much improved\". In the 2x/week group (N=21), 85.7% of caregivers and 95.2% of participants reported being \"much improved\" or \"very much improved\", compared with 75.0% for both caregivers and participants in the every 5 days group (N=4).

## Post hoc analyses

Post hoc analyses of the combined 24-month period (Part A and Part B) were conducted to evaluate long-term efficacy. The observed median (Q1, Q3) ABRs were consistent between the entire 24-month period and Part B: all bleeds (24-month: 1.00 [0.50, 3.04], Part B: 0.72 [0.0, 3.39]) and treated bleeds (24-month: 0.53 [0.00, 1.07]), Part B 0.00 [0.00, 1.33]). Annual consumption and infusion patterns also remained stable across the full study period.

## 2.4.2. Discussion on clinical efficacy

## Design and conduct of clinical studies

To support the proposed extension of indication to include previously untreated patients (PUPs) and a new dosing schedule (every-5-days) in children 7-12 years of age, the results of Part B of the Alfa-PROTECT study and a pooled analysis of the results of the PROTECT Kids and the Alfa-Protect study have been submitted.

Part B of the Alfa-PROTECT study assessed long-term (18 months) efficacy and safety of JIVI in children treated in Part A, including every-5-days dosing. Moreover, health-related quality of life was evaluated.

The PROTECT Kids was a Phase 3, open-label, uncontrolled multicentre study in previously treated children &lt;12 years of age with severe haemophilia A.

In Alfa-PROTECT Part B, 30 patients were included. 22 subjects received 2x/week regimen, and 5 patients received every 5 days regimen. 3 patients received variable frequency dosing. In a pooled analysis, which included 74 patients, 42 subjects received 2x/week and 28 received every 5 days dosing regimens. Four patient received variable frequency dosing.

In the Protect Kids and Alfa-PROTECT study, mean dose with every 5 days dosing regimen was 52.4IU/kg (range: 42-66 IU/kg) and 66.8 IU/kg (range 64-70IU/kg), respectively. For 2x/week dosing, the mean dose was 34.7 IU/kg (range 20-52 IU/kg) and 56.9 IU/kg (range 41-69 IU/kg), respectively. Median treated ABRs were 2.93 and 0.00 in PROTECT Kids and Alfa-PROTECT, respectively.

## 2.5. Clinical safety

## Introduction

Following the initial marketing authorisation of Jivi (BAY 94-9027) for patients ≥12 years of age, the Alfa-PROTECT study (21824) was conducted as an open-label, single-group, uncontrolled, prospective, multicenter, Phase 3 study to evaluate safety and efficacy in previously treated children aged 7 to &lt;12 years with severe haemophilia A. The primary objective was to assess safety and tolerability. A total of 35 patients were included in the study. Based on results from the PROTECT Kids main study and Alfa-PROTECT main study (Part A), the indication was extended to include patients 7 to &lt;12 years of age.

The Alfa-PROTECT study comprised two parts:

- Part A assessed safety and efficacy during the first 6 months of treatment, with particular focus on potential immune responses to PEG leading to loss of efficacy and/or hypersensistivity (AESI) during the first 4 exposure days in children aged 7 to &lt;12 years with severe hemophilia A. The primary objective was to assess safety and tolerability. The safety results confirmed that AESIs related to the immune response to PEG only occured during the first 4 EDs, with an estimated incidence of 1.6% (based on a pooled analysis of AlfaPROTECT and PROTECT Kids), and are transient and with no long-term immune memory observed.
- Part B further evaluated longer-term outcomes over an additional 18 months in children treated in Part A, with focus on:
- ο Clinical efficacy with flexible dosing regimens, including every-5-day dosing
- ο Long-term safety data beyond the initial 6-month treatment period up to an additional 18 months
- ο Impact of Jivi on health-related quality of life

## Patient exposure

A total of 32 participants who completed Part A received Jivi treatment for a median duration of 507 days (range: 35 to 550), corresponding to 141 EDs (range 10 to 159).

## Adverse events

71.9% of participants experienced at least 1 TEAE, primarily of mild (13 participants, 40.6%) or moderate (8 participants, 25.0%) intensity. Upper respiratory tract infection was the most common moderate TEAE (4 participants), all 4 events recovered by the end of the study.

TEAEs with severe intensity were reported in 2 participants (6.3%). These were pulpless tooth and tooth infection in 1 participant (both TEAEs resolved), and ankle fracture in 1 participant (the TEAE was resolving by the end of study).

The most commonly reported TEAEs by MedDRA SOCs were: Infections and infestations (46.9%), Injury, poisoning and procedural complications (40.6%), Musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders (25.0%), and Gastrointestinal disorders (21.9%).

By Preferred Term, TEAEs occurring in ≥10% of participants were upper respiratory tract infection (18.8%), nasopharyngitis (15.6%), cough (15.6%), pain in extremity (12.5%), and headache (12.5%). 1 participant (3.1%) experienced a TEAE (infusion site pain) considered to be study drug related.

## 3. Benefit-Risk Balance

## 3.1. Therapeutic Context

## 3.1.1. Disease or condition

Haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII [FVIII] deficiency) is a bleeding disorder caused by a deficiency of coagulation FVIII, a key component of the intrinsic pathway. In patients with haemophilia A, the primary platelet-driven haemostasis is not affected, but generation of a stable, fibrin-rich clot is defective because inadequate amounts of thrombin are generated.

Jivi is currently authorised for the treatment and prophylaxis of bleeding in previously treated patients ≥ 7 years of age with haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency).

The MAH applied to extend the indication to include also previously untreated patients (PUPs) based on the available data from PTPs and in line with the guideline on the clinical investigation of recombinant and human plasma-derived factor VIII products EMA/CHMP/BPWP/144552/2009 rev 2 recommendations.

## 3.1.2. Available therapies and unmet medical need

In line with the current recommendations of the WFH Guidelines for the Management of Haemophilia as well as of many national and international haemophilia organisations, the standard of care for all patients with severe haemophilia A is primary prophylaxis, defined as regular therapy with FVIII replacement products or other haemostasis products to prevent spontaneous bleeding. Primary prophylaxis should be initiated early in life prior to the onset of joint disease, before the second clinically evident joint bleed and before 3 years of age to prevent musculoskeletal complications from recurrent joint and muscle bleeds.

Hemlibra (emicizumab) is indicated for routine prophylaxis of bleeding episodes in patients with haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) with or without factor VIII inhibitors.

Efanesoctocog alfa (Altuvoct) is a modified recombinant factor VIII that is designed to enhance the hemostatic function. It works by binding to activated factor X (Xa) to facilitate the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin, thereby promoting the clotting cascade effectively. Its unique structure allows for stable interaction with the coagulation system, improving its efficacy in controlling bleeding.

Both Hemlibra and Altuvoct can be used in all age groups.

A gene therapy of haemophilia A, valoctocogene roxaparvovec (Roctavian), an adeno-associated viral serotype 5 (AAV5) vector containing a B-domain-deleted variant of human FVIII has been authorised in the EU for the treatment of severe haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) in adult patients without a history of factor VIII inhibitors and without detectable antibodies to adenoassociated virus serotype 5 (AAV5). However, it has since been withdrawn in Europe.

Episodic or on-demand FVIII treatment, i.e., treating bleeds when they occur, is no longer considered the standard of care for the management of haemophilia as it does not alter the natural history of frequent and recurrent bleeding and related complications. Patients suffering from severe haemophilia A usually receive prophylactic treatment with FVIII products, which requires regular infusion every 2 to 3 days.

## 3.1.3. Main clinical studies

Following the initial marketing authorisation of Jivi for patients ≥12 years of age, the Alfa-PROTECT study (21824) was conducted as an open-label, single-group, uncontrolled, prospective, multicenter, Phase 3 study to evaluate safety and efficacy in previously treated children aged 7 to &lt;12 years with severe hemophilia A. The primary objective was to assess safety and tolerability. A total of 35 patients were included in the study. Based on results from the PROTECT Kids main study and Alfa-PROTECT main study (Part A), the indication was extended to include patients 7 to &lt;12 years of age.

The Alfa-PROTECT study comprised two parts:

- Part A assessed safety and efficacy during the first 6 months of treatment, with particular focus on potential immune responses to PEG leading to loss of efficacy and/or hypersensistivity (AESI) during the first 4 exposure days in children aged 7 to &lt;12 years with severe hemophilia A. The primary objective was to assess safety and tolerability. The safety results confirmed that AESIs related to the immune response to PEG only occurred during the first 4 EDs, with an estimated incidence of 1.6% (based on a pooled analysis of AlfaPROTECT and PROTECT Kids), are transient and with no long-term immune memory observed.
- Part B further evaluated longer-term outcomes over an additional 18 months in children treated in Part A, with focus on:
- o Clinical efficacy with flexible dosing regimens, including every-5-day dosing
- o Long-term safety data beyond the initial 6-month treatment period up to an additional 18 months
- o Impact of Jivi on health-related quality of life

## 3.7.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

No previously untreated patients (PUPs) were included in the clinical development programme. Nonetheless, based on efficacy and safety data available from PTPs and in line with the EMA guideline recommendations, extension of the indication to include PUPs is acceptable.

In Part B the mean annualised bleeding rates (SD) for treated bleeds were 0.42 (0.66) and 1.58 (2.51) for 2x/week and every 5 days regimens, respectively. The mean annualized bleeding rates (SD) for all bleeds were 1.21 (1.81) and 3.80 (2.52) in 2x/week and every 5 days regimens, respectively. In total 5 patients received every 5-days dosing, compared to 22 patients who received 2x/week dosing.

In addition, in a pooled analysis, a model-estimated ABR was 0.88 (0.57, 1.35) and 1.95 (1.20, 3.18) for treated bleeds in 2x/week and every 5 days regimens, respectively. For trauma bleeds, a model-estimated ABR was 0.58 (0.36, 0.94) and 1.58 (0.92, 2.72) for treated bleeds in 2x/week and every 5 days regimens, respectively.

Furthermore, severe bleeds were reported in 1.7% treated 2x/week compared to 16.2% patients treated every 5 days.

Generally, no new or unexpected safety issues have been reported.

## 3.7.2. Balance of benefits and risks

The benefit-risk of Jivi is positive for the treatment and prophylaxis of bleeding in patients ≥7 years of age with haemophilia A (congenital FVIII deficiency).
