## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## Introduction

As required per Regulation No 1901 /2006, Article 46, the MAH submitted a final report for:

- Study MS100070-0087: A single-arm, multicentre Phase 1/1b study of avelumab + lenvatinib in children with primary CNS tumour

Study MS100070-0087 was a single-arm Phase 1/1b dose escalation study of avelumab + lenvatinib in participants 2 to less than 18 years of age with primary high grade (WHO grade III and IV) CNS malignancies who had received at least 1 prior therapy.

In a previous dose-finding study of avelumab monotherapy in 21 paediatric patients with various solid tumours (Study MS 100070-0306, discussed in PAC/PAM procedure EMEA/H/C/004338/P46/009), 4 out of 8 patients with CNS tumours had stable disease (SD) and 2 had sustained SD &gt; 6 months. The study was however discontinued beyond phase I due to the failure of avelumab monotherapy to lead to objective responses.

The rationale behind the current study MS100070-0087 was an anticipation that paediatric patients with CNS tumour with high unmet medical need might receive clinical benefit from treatment with a combination of avelumab and lenvatinib. However, in absence of a clear signal of antitumor activity during the dose escalation part (see below) and following agreement by the EMA based on the related amendments of the PIP, the sponsor discontinued enrolment into the dose escalation part (part 1) and the expansion part (part 2) was removed from the study.

Based on the outcome of the study, the MAH suggests that no urgent regulatory activities are necessary, considering the following:

- Avelumab is not indicated for paediatric conditions / combinations tested in study MS100070\\_0087

## Clinical study

Study MS100070-0087: A single-arm, multicentre Phase 1/1b study of avelumab + lenvatinib in children with primary CNS tumour

## Description

Study MS 100070-0087 was a single-arm multicentre, open-label, Phase I/lb dose escalation study, designed to determine safety and tolerability, anticancer activity, and PK of avelumab + lenvatinib in participants 2 to &lt; 18 years of age with primary high grade (WHO Grade III and IV) CNS malignancies who had received at least 1 prior therapy. The study centres were in Canada, France, Germany and South Korea.

The study was intended to consist of a dose-escalation part (Part 1) and - after determination of the recommended avelumab and lenvatinib dose for expansion - a dose expansion Part 2 in children with diffuse midline glioma H3 K27M-positive and possibly other high-grade CNS tumour types who have received at least one prior therapy.

In absence of a clear signal of antitumor activity during the dose escalation part (see Efficacy results below) and following agreement by the EMA and UK MHRA based on the related amendments of the PIP, the sponsor discontinued enrolment into the dose escalation part (part 1), and the expansion part (part 2) was removed from the study. This decision was unrelated to safety concerns with the combination in the target population.

## Methods

## Study participants

The dose-escalation Part 1 consisted of 2 populations for avelumab dosing based on body weight and age:

- Population A (Pop A, flat dose): Avelumab 800 mg in children ≥12 to&lt; 18 years old and body weight ≥40 kg.
- Population B (Pop B, weight-based dose): Avelumab 15 mg/kg in children ≥2 and&lt; 12 years old or body weight &lt; 40 kg.

## Treatments

## Avelumab

- Population A: Avelumab 800 mg intravenously once every 2 weeks + Lenvatinib (dose escalation), orally, once daily (Pop A).
- Population B: Avelumab 15 mg/kg intravenously once every 2 weeks + Lenvatinib (dose escalation), orally, once daily (Pop B).

These doses were selected based on noncompartmental analysis and population PK modelling/simulations of serum concentration data from the monotherapy Study EMRl 00070- 0306 (n=21). These preliminary analyses and simulations predicted that the selected paediatric dosing

## Lenvatinib

- Dose level 1 (starting dose): 11 mg/m2
- Dose level 2: 14 mg/m2
- Dose level -1: 9 mg/m2

The first cohorts of 3 participants each were enrolled at the lenvatinib starting dose level 1 and received avelumab flat or weight-based doses. Based on the number of participants experiencing dose limiting toxicities (DLTs) at the completion of each cohort, the dose of lenvatinib for the next cohort could be increased to dose level 2 or decreased to dose level -1.

The starting dose of lenvatinib in combination with avelumab in this study was 11 mg/m 2 daily, which corresponds to 80% of the recommended Phase II dose of 14 mg/m 2 daily of lenvatinib monotherapy and lenvatinib combined with chemotherapy (ifosfamide + etoposide) in children and young adults (2 to 25 years old) (Gaspar 2017).

The number of participants from Populations A and B was balanced for each dose-escalation level for lenvatinib.

The duration of one cycle was 14 days. Treatment continued until confirmed disease progression, unacceptable toxicity or withdrawal of consent.

## Objective(s)

## Part 1

Primary:

- To evaluate the safety and tolerability of avelumab + lenvatinib
- To determine the recommended avelumab and lenvatinib dose for expansion

## Secondary:

- To further evaluate the safety and tolerability of avelumab + lenvatinib
- To assess antitumor activity of avelumab + lenvatinib by overall response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DoR)
- To assess the progression-free survival (PFS) based on Investigator assessments and overall survival (OS)
- To characterize the PK profile of avelumab and lenvatinib when administered in combination
- To characterize the immunogenicity of avelumab in combination with lenvatinib

## Baseline data

Across the 4 dosing regimens, 11 participants (64.7%) were male, and 6 participants (35.3%) were female, with a median age of 12.0 years (range: 3 to 16).

All participants met the inclusion criterion for histologically confirmed diagnosis of primary CNS malignancy. Across the 4 dosing regimens, all 17 participants (100.0%) had brain as the site of primary tumour. The median time since initial cancer diagnosis was 21.7 months (range: 6 to 136). All participants had either WHO Grade III (4 participants, 23.5%) or Grade IV (13 participants, 76.5%) disease at study entry.

Median PFS and median OS times were 3.48 months (95% CI: 1.81, 8.21) and 10.91 months (95% CI: 5.52, 26.15), respectively (Table 2).

One participant had an unconfirmed PR following surgery. In addition, 5 participants (29.4%) were reported with a best overall response (BOR) of SD, including the participant with the unconfirmed PR (Table 3).

MAH Efficacy Conclusion: The clinical activity observed with the avelumab + lenvatinib combination in paediatric participants with primary CNS tumours did not provide a clear signal of antitumor activity to support continuation into the dose expansion Part 2 of the study. The results align with the limited effect of lenvatinib in paediatric CNS tumours as observed with lenvatinib monotherapy. These results were not available at the time when study MS100070-0087 was initiated in 2021.

## Safety results

The patterns of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were consistent with the known avelumab and lenvatinib safety profiles, with increased incidence of hypothyroidism with the combination. Hypothyroidism, reported in 15 participants (88.2%; 11 ongoing at the last visit/or cutoff date) was the most frequently reported TEAE, with 11 participants (64.7%) reported with lenvatinib related TEAE and 10 participants (58.8%) reported with avelumab-related TEAE of hypothyroidism. Hypothyroidism was manageable with levothyroxine treatment.

There were no mean or median changes from baseline in haematology, biochemistry, coagulation, hormonal, or vital signs parameters that were either of clinical concern or raised a new previously unknown safety signal.

Overall, 1 participant (5.9%) was reported with a DLT as identified by the Study monitoring committee (SMC) and the Investigator - nonserious Grade 3 QTc prolongation - in the lenvatinib 14 mg/m 2 population B group.

MAH Safety conclusion: Overall, the combination of lenvatinib at doses 11 mg/m 2 and 14 mg/m2 administered once daily orally with avelumab at doses 15 mg/ kg body weight or 800 mg intravenously once every 2 weeks was generally well tolerated by paediatric participants in this study and no new safety concern was identified for this combination in the target population. ## Discussion on clinical aspects

The MAH submitted the final report for Study MS 100070-0087, evaluating efficacy and safety of avelumab in combination with lenvatinib in 17 paediatric patients with primary CNS tumours, who had received at least 1 prior therapy.

The study was a part of the PIP for Bavencio and was originally planned to include a dose-finding part and a dose expansion part. However, due to limited tumour response in the dose-finding part, the study was terminated before initiation of the dose expansion part. This was made in agreement with the EMA/PDCO, and the PIP was amended accordingly.

In absence of a clear signal of antitumor activity during the dose escalation part the study was terminated and there were no new safety concerns. The adverse event profile was in line with the known safety profile of avelumab and lenvatinib. Not unexpectedly, the combination led to a high incidence or hypothyroidism, which is reported as a Common (avelumab) or very common (lenvatinib) AE for the respective monotherapies. This AE was manageable with replacement therapy.

The SmPC section 4.2 previously includes a standard text that efficacy and safety, or a recommended dose, in paediatric patients has not been established. The lack of objective responses to avelumab monotherapy in a previously submitted paediatric study, Study MS 100070-0306 (discussed in PAC/PAM procedure EMEA/H/C/004338/P46/009) is described in section 5.1 of the SmPC, and pharmacokinetics of avelumab in paediatric patients based on data from the same study is presented in section 5.2. This study included a total of 21 paediatric patients of which 8 with CNS tumours.

As the lack of efficacy of avelumab in paediatric CNS tumours is already described in the SmPC, no further updates of the product information is considered warranted based on the results of Study MS 100070-0087.

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

The final report of Study MS 100070-0087 was submitted, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended. The results of the study did not give a clear signal of antitumor activity to support continued evaluation of the combination of avelumab with lenvatinib in paediatric patient with primary CNS tumour, and do not warrant an updated of the Product information.
