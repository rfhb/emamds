## 1. Introduction

On 9 June 2026, the MAH submitted the final non-clinical study report 2022-0059 for Byfavo 20 mg powder for solution for injection and Byfavo 50 mg powder for concentrate for solution for injection/infusion, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The juvenile toxicity study number 2022-0059, conducted in the rat with administration on Post-Natal Day (PND) 7, is part of the non-clinical development program related to the Paediatric Investigation Plan (PIP) of Byfavo.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Remimazolam besylate was administered at 40 mg/kg/dose, in a volume of 8 mL/kg per administration. Remimazolam was administered dissolved in a vehicle containing 4.34% lactose. The lactose-based formulation is prepared by dilution of a lactose-based master stock formulation of 10 mg/mL remimazolam (as free base) with 0.9% NaCl to the final concentration at 5 mg/mL.

While the remimazolam formulation intended for clinical intravenous (IV) use contains dextran 40, this excipient was not part of the formulation administered by subcutaneous (SC) injection in PND 7 rats. Dextran 40 should be avoided in injectable formulations in rat because of a species-specific acute nonantibody-mediated anaphylactoid reaction to dextran 40 (not present in humans). Moreover, as a nondiffusible inert polysaccharide, dextran 40 would be expected to remain largely trapped at the SC injection site producing local edema and interfering with the systemic distribution of remimazolam.

## 2.3. Non-Clinical aspects

The study was conducted in accordance with the EMA PIP EMEA-001880-PIP02-M04 (decision no. P/0110/2023), under Art. 46 of Regulation (EC) No 1901/2006. The study report was finalised in July 2025. The purpose of this study was to determine the potential neurotoxicity when remimazolam was administered to male and female Sprague Dawley rat pups on PND 7 using a repeat dose scheme over six hours in order to maintain pups in a status of continuous sedation. The study was designed to compare remimazolam with a negative control (vehicle) and with two positive controls, ketamine and midazolam, respectively. that are well-known approved anesthetics with some neurotoxic liabilities. A comparative approach was considered more suitable, based on the general adverse properties of anaesthetic treatments on the developing brain, instead than establishing a NOAEL for the exposure to the study item. Remimazolam besylate was administered SC to rat pups on PND 7, using a dose series of 5 administrations, 90 minutes apart, at 40 mg/kg/dose. For the comparators, ketamine and midazolam, a comparable depth and duration of sedation was obtained with a series of five intraperitoneal injections of ketamine at 20 mg/kg, at intervals of 90 minutes, or a series of five subcutaneous injections of midazolam, at 15 mg/kg at intervals of 90 minutes. Both routes of administration were considered valid alternatives to mimic the pediatric IV injection/infusion route, which is not feasible in 7-day old rat pups. A group of additional satellite animals were used for the collection of blood samples for toxicokinetics assessment. Pups were closely monitored after each administration on PND 7 to determine the sedation status through the surface righting reflex test. The surface righting reflex was also monitored on the day after dosing, for those animals with terminal sacrifice on PND 8 or PND 90. The level of sedation was measured by assigning the score as follows: Score 1 - Loss of righting

Pups were sacrificed at three different time-points:

- 2 h after the last dose (10 pups/sex/group);
- 16 h after the last dose (10 pups/sex/group);
- At PND 90 (20 remaining pups/group.

At the scheduled sacrifices animals were necropsied, gross examination was conducted, and the brain collected and processed for histopathological evaluations. Animals were evaluated for neurotoxicity on PND 7 and 8 (10 pups/sex/group for each of the two days) with standard histology and with immunohistochemistry for markers of neurodegeneration (CC3 staining as a marker of apoptosis) and for glial cells (Iba1 and GFAP staining as markers of microglia and astrocytes). On PND 90 the brain of animals (20 /sex/group) was evaluated with standard histology or with immunohistochemistry (10 /sex/group) for glial cells (Iba1 and GFAP).

In pups on PND 7, treated with a series of five injections of remimazolam or ketamine or midazolam, at selected doses, at intervals of 90 min, pharmacologically-related deep/intermediate sedation (score 1 or 2) was generally reached 5 minutes after the first dosing and maintained up to the end of the observation period, i.e. 120 minutes after the fifth dose for animals sacrificed 2 h after the last dose or 180 minutes for those animals that returned to their mother. On PND 8 surface righting reflex returned to normal (score 3) for those animals sacrificed at 16 h after the last dose or for those allowed to reach adulthood. Therefore, the doses used of the three anesthetics were able to maintain pups in a comparable status of sedation during the day of dosing with a complete recovery from the day after.

From a safety assessment perspective, treatment with remimazolam on PND 7 did not affect pup survival and no clinical signs of toxicity were seen. For animals allowed to reach adulthood, body weight gain pre-weaning was slightly lower than controls (vehicle) only from PND 7 to 10 and then fully recovered during further development.

The histopathological evaluation of the brain revealed that at PND 7, 2 h after the last dosing, both in males and females, there was a significant increase in the number of CC3 positive apoptotic cells per mm 2 in the cortex and hippocampus for all sedative-treated groups, with remimazolam effects being less intense than ketamine and ketamine being less intense and less prolonged than midazolam. In fact, for remimazolam and ketamine, on PND 8 (16 h after the last dose), there was a return to baseline levels of apoptotic cells per mm 2 , while in midazolam-treated animals the value remained significantly higher than in the vehicle-treated control.

Moreover, treatment with remimazolam did not alter microglia and astrocyte function. No changes in quantification of the % area of Iba1-positive microglia and the % area of GFAP-positive astrocytes in the cortex and hippocampus compared to control vehicle were seen for remimazolam, either on PND 7, 8 or PND 90. However, for the two comparators, ketamine and midazolam, a slight effect was recorded for microglia and astrocytes areas on PND 8 and PND 90.

On pups scheduled to reach adulthood, CNS-related developmental tests were conducted before weaning, namely evaluation of Startle Reflex (auditory function) from PND 11, Air Righting Reflex (dynamic postural correction) from PND 14, Pupil Reflex (visual function) on PND 21. After weaning, the following behavioral tests were performed: modified Irwin's test on PND 50-51, Water T Maze (learning and memory ability) on PND 59-64, Open Field (locomotor activity and exploratory behavior) on PND 64-66, and Sociability (social approach behavior) on PND 78-84.

The behavioral test performed pre-weaning, showed a one-day delay in reaching 100% acquisition of the auditory function (startle reflex to a noise stimulus in female pups treated with remimazolam and in pups of both sexes treated with ketamine and midazolam) in comparison to controls, while no delay on the dynamical postural correction (air righting reflex) and on the visual function (pupil reflex) was seen in any of the groups. With respect to behavioral tests performed postweaning, the learning and memory ability or changes in motor activity in the novel open field arena or in the sociability approach behavior appeared comparable between groups that received any of the sedative treatments on PND 7 and the vehicle control group.

Concerning the toxicokinetics assessment, the plasma profile of remimazolam in PND 7 pups following the five-partite subcutaneous injection series suggests exposure exceeding clinical plasma levels targeted for general anesthesia and associated with loss of consciousness. The mean Cmax concentrations observed after the first and after the last dose in pups exceeded the plasma concentration associated with loss of consciousness in patients (which is around 0.65 µg/mL remimazolan) by more than 4-fold and more than 8-fold, respectively. The trough levels noted suggest non-interrupted exposure to efficacious plasma levels during the entire treatment.

## 3. Overall conclusion and recommendation

The assessment of potential neurotoxicity, when remimazolam was administered to juvenile animals (male and female Sprague Dawley rat pups on PND 7) using a repeat dose scheme over six hours, in order to maintain pups in a status of continuous sedation, is based on a comparative approach with ketamine and midazolam, that are well-known approved anesthetics with some neurotoxic liabilities.

This study was conducted in accordance with EMA PIP EMEA-001880-PIP02-M04 (decision P/0110/2023).

Under the conditions of this study remimazolam was less neurotoxic than ketamine, and ketamine less neurotoxic than midazolam, when the sedatives were all administered at equipotent doses to juvenile Sprague Dawley rats at PND 7. Moreover, no additional safety findings were identified which have an impact on the non-clinical safety profile previously established for remimazolam in the approved indication.

In conclusion, the non-clinical assessment of remimazolam neurotoxic potential has been adequately addressed. The Juvenile Toxicity Study (2022-0059, report finaliSed in July 2025) submitted according to Art. 46 of Regulation (EC) No 1901/2006 / PIP: EMEA-001880-PIP02-M04, is suitable to support a CHMP positive opinion.
