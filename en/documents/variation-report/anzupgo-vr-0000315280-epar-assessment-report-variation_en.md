## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Introduction

## 2.1.1. Problem statement

## Disease or condition

Chronic hand eczema (CHE) is a multifactorial heterogenous chronic inflammatory skin disorder located anywhere on the hands and wrists. It is a disabling condition which impacts quality of life and occupational performance.

According to the European Society of Contact Dermatitis guideline, CHE refers to hand eczema, which persists for more than 3 months or returns twice or more often within 12 months. 1

## Therapeutic indication

The therapeutic indication sought by the MAH is as follows: Anzupgo is indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe chronic hand eczema (CHE) in adults and adolescents 12 years and older for whom topical corticosteroids are inadequate or inappropriate (see section 5.1).

## Epidemiology and risk factors, screening tools/prevention

HE is a common skin disease with a 1-year prevalence of at least 9.1% in the general population (6.4% in men and 10.5% in women) and a lifetime prevalence of 14.5% 2 . CHE represents the most prevalent occupational skin disease, and the higher prevalence in women is probably explained by a difference in distribution of exposure both domestically and occupationally. The incidence rates of CHE ranges from 5.5 to 8.8 cases per 1000 person-years. Self-reported CHE in women peaks at between age 19 and 29 years, and decreases with age, while in men the incidence increases gradually with age. The incidence rate of HE is similar between adults and adolescents. Regarding paediatric population, the 1-year prevalence from Scandinavian school children aged between 12 and 16 years old was 7%, while for adolescents aged between 16 and 19 years it was 10% 3, 4, 5 .

## Biologic features

CHE is characterised by skin barrier dysfunction, immune cell infiltration of the skin compartment and alteration of the skin microbiome. The pathophysiology of CHE is complex comprising different clinical patterns with multiple, potentially overlapping aetiologies and risk factors. Environmental factors explain up to 59% of the aetiology of hand eczema (HE). Risk factors often associated with HE include atopic dermatitis (AD) in childhood, persistent/severe AD, previous CHE, and low age at onset of CHE, being contact allergic, being exposed to wet work, cold/dry weather conditions, and decreased indoor humidity as well as being active in certain occupations. Lifestyle factors, including tobacco smoking, have been reported to influence the prognosis of occupational CHE.

It is often difficult to establish which etiological factor plays the most important role in the development of this condition. The latest European guidelines suggest distinguishing between allergic contact dermatitis (ACD), irritative contact dermatitis (ICD), protein contact dermatitis/contact urticaria and atopic HE on an etiological basis. However, ICD and ACD are often found to coexist together with an atopic substrate.

ICD is characterised by a predominance of the Th1/Th17 axes, with consequent activation of interleukin (IL)-1α, IL-1β, IL-8, tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNFα) and granulocyte-macrophage colony stimulating factor (GM-CSF). On the other hand, the immune signature of ACD varies according to the nature of allergens. In the skin of patients with AD there is overexpression of Th2 and Th22, which produce a great amount of IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13. Hyperkeratosis, one of the key features of CHE, derives from the release by keratinocytes of cytokines such as TNFα. Besides, epidermal proliferation is promoted by IL-17, produced by both Th1 and Th2, through induction of IFNγ and IL-4, respectively. In patients with an atopic diathesis, the Janus kinase (JAK) signalling pathways play an important role, as well as Th2-released IL-31, which is a major contributor to pruritus in this category. 6

Taken together, these distinct immune pathways highlight the heterogeneity of CHE. The topical panJAK inhibitor delgocitinib interferes with multiple cytokine pathways by blocking JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2, thereby attenuating the signalling of several proinflammatory cytokines downregulating the immune and inflammatory responses. By inhibiting both Th1/Th17 and Th2/Th22 pathways, delgocitinib provides a treatment option across the various immunologic phenotypes of CHE. 7

## Clinical presentation, diagnosis and stage/prognosis

CHE is a heterogeneous disease and shows variable morphology, typically with more erythema, oedema, vesicles, and oozing in the acute phase, as well as erythema, xerosis, scales, lichenification, hyperkeratosis, and fissures in the chronic phase. CHE patients typically experience itch, pain, and burning sensation, which can impede the performance of activities of routine daily living, work, and recreation. The disease is often characterised by recurring flares and a poor prognosis.

In the context of this variation application, 'moderate to severe' CHE is defined by signs and intensity of CHE (score of 3 or 4) in the Investigator's Global Assessment for chronic hand eczema (IGA-CHE) scale, which is a clinician-reported outcome (ClinRO) measure developed and validated by the MAH (Table 8 of this AR).

## Management

Basic management strategies in CHE include education, avoidance of clinically relevant allergens, protection from irritants, and frequent use of emollients. Current medical management typically relies on potent topical corticosteroids (TCS). Current management strategies are frequently inadequate; many patients either fail to respond to corticosteroids or experience adverse effects from prolonged use, including skin atrophy and an increased risk of infection associated with extended use of TCS. Calcineurin inhibitors such as tacrolimus and pimecrolimus are used as steroid-sparing alternatives in adults, adolescents and children with AD, but are not authorised for the treatment of other forms of CHE in the EU. Oral alitretinoin can be used for adult patients with severe CHE not responding to potent TCS. Consequently, there is an unmet medical need for treatment options for CHE especially in the adolescent population.

## 2.1.2. About the product

Delgocitinib is a pan-JAK inhibitor that targets the activity of all 4 members of the JAK family of enzymes consisting of JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2 in a concentration dependent manner. JAKs are intracellular enzymes specifically associated to the different cytokine receptors in either a heterodimeric or homodimeric complex and are essential for cytokine signalling. JAKs are activated upon cytokine-receptor interaction and thereafter phosphorylate and activate signal transducers and activators of transcription (STATs). Activated STATs, in turn, activate the expression of cytokineresponsive genes to induce specific biological responses in target cells. Thus, the JAK/STAT signalling pathway provides direct translation of extracellular signals, cytokines, into specific transcriptional responses and plays a key role in driving a broad range of physiological and pathological processes involving the innate and adaptive immune system. Delgocitinib cream is currently approved for the treatment of moderate to severe chronic hand eczema (CHE) in adults for whom topical corticosteroids are inadequate or inappropriate.

## 2.2.3. Toxicology

## Repeat dose toxicity

Two studies were reported and assessed during the initial MAA:

- 10-week oral dose toxicity study in juvenile rats with 12-week recovery period,
- Preliminary 1-month dermal dose toxicity study in juvenile minipigs.

In the rat study, a decrease in ulnar length was observed from the lowest dose of 3 mg/kg in both sexes (M:87-fold to human exposure, F: 130-fold to human exposure). This finding was associated with slight decreases in body weight gain and food consumption observed in same animals.

In the minipig study, dermal administration of delgocitinib resulted in very low plasma concentrations and no toxicity was reported in juvenile minipigs.

## 2.2.5. Discussion on non-clinical aspects

The primary pharmacodynamic profile of delgocitinib cream was extensively investigated at time of registration in biochemical and cell-based in vitro studies and relevant in vivo animal models, supporting the proof of concept in CHE.

One new in vitro pharmacology study was submitted by the MAH within this type II variation application. This study was a human whole-blood immunoassay using anti-CD3/CD28 stimulation, which induces JAK-dependent cytokine release from activated T cells, showing dose-dependent inhibition of delgocitinib on release of IL-4 in human whole blood cells (IC₅₀ = 55.5 nM). As IL-4 is a pro-inflammatory cytokine capable of activation of several essential pathways in the immune response and IL-4 receptors are present in several immune and non-immune cells, these results support the potential of delgocitinib to suppress the pan-JAK inhibitory activity in human.

The juvenile toxicity data was assessed already in the initial MAA and are included in SmPC section 5.3. Those data suggest a decrease in ulnar length in rats with associated decreases in body weight gain and food consumption in all dose levels. All exposures markedly exceeded the maximum human

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

exposure following topical application. As these effects were observed only at exposures considered to be sufficiently in excess of the maximum human exposure following topical application (M:87x human exposure, F: 130x human exposure), no specific hazard for humans was identified based on this oral juvenile toxicity study. After dermal application in juvenile minipigs, only low systemic exposure was reached and no toxicity was reported.

The updated ERA did not suggest a risk to any environmental compartments.

## 2.2.6. Conclusion on the non-clinical aspects

No new data has been provided concerning the toxicological profile of delgocitinib in juvenile animals.

Based on the updated data submitted in this type II variation application, the new/extended indication does not lead to a significant increase in environmental exposure.

Considering the above data, delgocitinib is not expected to pose a risk to the environment.

## 2.3.2. Pharmacokinetics

The MAH provided clinical PK data from study 1426, in which the efficacy, safety and PK of twice daily topical applications of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g for a 16-week treatment period in adolescents (1217 years of age, n =74 subjects with delgocitinib cream and n = 24 with cream vehicle), with moderate to severe CHE have been studied. The PK sampling was performed after 8 and 16 weeks of treatment (one sample, 2-6 hours post morning dose).

## Analytical methods

Delgocitinib plasma concentration was determined with a previously validated method using solidphase extraction with LC-MS/MS detection in a range of 0.005 ng/ml - 5 ng/ml. A partial validation was successfully conducted to transfer the method to Labcorp, Harrogate, UK, where the study samples were analysed.

All 138 plasma samples from individuals receiving an active dose were analysed. The samples were analysed within the established stability period, and all runs were acceptable showing satisfactory method performance, including overall accuracy and precision of the QC samples. Three samples were reanalysed due to result above the upper limit of quantification or high internal standard response. Assay reproducibility was confirmed with 24 samples subjected to incurred sample reanalysis (ISR).

## 2.3.3. Pharmacodynamics

## Mechanism of action

Delgocitinib is a pan-JAK inhibitor that targets the activity of all four members of the JAK family of enzymes (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2) in a concentration dependent manner.

In human cells, inhibition of the JAK-STAT pathway by delgocitinib attenuates the signalling of several pro-inflammatory cytokines (including IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-13, IL-21, IL-23, GM-CSF, and IFN-α) downregulating the immune and inflammatory responses in cells of relevance to CHE pathology.

No pharmacodynamic assessment was included in study 1426.

## 2.3.5. Discussion on clinical pharmacology

The baseline characteristics between the treatment groups were generally similar in study 1426. Following twice daily application of delgocitinib 20 mg/g cream in adolescents' patients (DELTA TEEN), the geometric mean plasma concentrations observed 2-6 hours after application were 0.28 ng/mL at Day 57 and 0.22 ng/mL at Day 113. One subject had higher plasma concentration (i.e., 13.4 ng/ml at week 16) than any other subjects. At week 8, the subject has had delgocitinib plasma concentration of 1.5 ng/ml. There were no safety concerns for the subject. Also, the HECSI score of the subject at baseline (63) decreased during the study and was low at both week 8 and week 16; and the subject's IMP did not increase during the study (the baseline HECSI score and IMP use have been identified as factors with a potential impact on the systemic exposure of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in adults). Thus, the MAH considered that a plausible explanation for the higher concentration could be the IMP contamination at the site of phlebotomy for PK assessment, this is agreed by CHMP.

The MAH provided comparisons of the PK results from study 1426 in adolescents and from study 1402 in adults. In study 1402, the PK sampling was performed as a single sample at weeks 1, 4, and 16 (26 hours post morning dose). Thus, the PK sampling weeks have been different, however, in both studies the PK sampling has been performed at week 16 and these results can be compared in detail, since the timing of the PK samples (i.e., 2-6 hours post morning dose) has been the same.

When comparing the plasma concentrations from week 8 (study 1426 in adolescents) to weeks 1 and 4 (study 1402 in adults), it can be concluded that the plasma concentrations are of similar level. In the adult study 1402, there was also one subject having higher plasma concentration (29.20 ng/ml at week 4) than other subjects and also in this case, it was thought that the explanation for the higher concentration was the IMP contamination at the site of phlebotomy for PK assessment.

At week 16, the systemic exposure was slightly higher in adolescents compared to adults, however, in both groups the plasma concentrations were low. The difference could not be explained by any differences in %BSA, baseline HECSI score, or IMP use, which were similar between adolescents and adults.

In the previous clinical studies in adult patients with CHE (1273, 2285 and 1402), systemic exposure of delgocitinib was observed to decrease over time presumably because of improved skin barrier integrity associated with delgocitinib cream treatment. Steady-state was observed to be reached within one week after the start of the treatment.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Pharmacodynamics of delgocitinib were investigated at time of marketing authorisation in adults and were found acceptable by the CHMP. It is thus acceptable that no PD assessment was included in study 1426.

SmPC section 5.2 was updated to add the geometric mean plasma concentrations observed 2-6 hours after application at Day 57 and Day 113; and to reflect that the systemic exposure was similar between adults and adolescents. This is agreed by CHMP.

## 2.3.6. Conclusions on clinical pharmacology

Minimal systemic exposure was seen in adolescents in study 1426 following topical application of a 20 mg/g cream to the hands and wrists of patients with moderate to severe CHE. The delgocitinib plasma concentrations when compared in adolescents (study 1426) and in adults (study 1402) were low and of similar level. Overall, the PK of topically applied delgocitinib in adolescents was adequately characterised. The information and results obtained in the clinical pharmacology programme have been adequately reflected in the SmPC section 5.2.

## Study participants

Adolescents 12-17 years of age with moderate to severe CHE who had an inadequate response to, or for whom TCS were not advisable were randomised in trial 1426. The main criteria for inclusion and exclusion are presented below.

## Main criteria for inclusion:

- Age 12 to 17 years at screening and baseline.
- Diagnosis of CHE, defined as hand eczema that has persisted for more than 3 months or returned twice or more within the last 12 months.
- Disease severity graded as moderate to severe at screening and baseline according to IGA-CHE (i.e. an IGA-CHE score of 3 or 4, see Table 1).
- Subjects who have a documented recent history of inadequate response to treatment with topical corticosteroids (at any time within 1 year before the screening visit) or for whom TCS are documented to be otherwise medically inadvisable (e.g. due to important side effects or safety risks).
- o Inadequate response is defined as a history of failure to achieve and maintain a low disease activity state (comparable to an IGA-CHE score of ≤2) despite treatment with a daily regimen of TCS of class III-IV (potent to very potent) for Europe and Australia and class IV-I (medium potency to very/ultra-high potency) for Canada, applied for at least 28 days or for the maximum duration recommended by the product prescribing information, whichever is shorter.
- o Important side effects or safety risks are those that outweigh the potential treatment benefits and include intolerance to treatment, hypersensitivity reactions, and significant skin atrophy as assessed by the physician.

## Main criteria for exclusion:

- Concurrent skin diseases on the hands, e.g. tinea manuum.
- Clinically significant infection (e.g. impetiginised hand eczema) on the hands.
- Systemic treatment with immunosuppressive drugs (e.g. methotrexate, cyclosporine, azathioprine), immunomodulating drugs, retinoids (e.g. alitretinoin), or corticosteroids within 28 days prior to baseline (steroid eyedrops and inhaled or intranasal steroids corresponding to up to 1 mg prednisolone for allergic conjunctivitis, asthma, or rhinitis are allowed).
- Use of tanning beds, phototherapy (e.g. UVB, UVA1, PUVA), or bleach baths on the hands within 28 days prior to baseline.
- Previous or current treatment with JAK inhibitors (including delgocitinib), systemic or topical.
- Cutaneously applied treatment with immunomodulators (e.g. PDE-4 inhibitors, pimecrolimus, tacrolimus) or TCS on the hands within 14 days prior to baseline.
- Use of systemic antibiotics or cutaneously applied antibiotics on the hands within 14 days prior to baseline.
- Other cutaneously applied therapy on the hands (except for the use of subject's own emollients) within 7 days prior to baseline.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Cutaneously applied treatments in regions other than the hands, which could interfere with clinical trial evaluations or pose a safety concern within 7 days prior to baseline.
- Any disorder which is not stable and could
- o Affect the safety of the subject throughout the trial.
- o Impede the subject's ability to complete the trial.

Examples include but are not limited to cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, hepatic, renal, neurological, musculoskeletal, infectious, endocrine, metabolic, haematological, immunological, and psychiatric disorders, and major physical impairment.

The eligibility criteria in trial 1426 were the same as in trials 1401 and 1402 in adults, data from which were used in the Bayesian analysis, with 2 exceptions:

- Trial 1426 was conducted in adolescents (12-17 years), whereas Trials 1401 and 1402 were conducted in adults (&gt;18 years).
- HESD itch score (weekly average) ≥4 at baseline was not an inclusion criterion for Trial 1426 but it was an inclusion criterion for Trials 1401 and 1402.

## Background, rescue and concomitant treatment

No background treatment was required in this trial. The subjects were required not to change their usual skin care routine for the hands regarding use of emollients. However, emollients should preferably not have been used on the affected areas within 2 hours before and after application of IMP.

If medically necessary (i.e. to control intolerable CHE symptoms), rescue treatment for CHE could be prescribed to trial subjects at the discretion of the investigator. Rescue treatment was defined as treatment (medication or procedure) initiated to treat intolerable CHE symptoms during the treatment and follow-up periods. The investigators had to make every attempt to conduct efficacy and safety assessments (e.g. disease severity scores, safety laboratory assessments) immediately before administering any rescue treatment. If rescue treatment was initiated, the subject had to stop treatment with IMP immediately and was not allowed to restart treatment with IMP. Subjects who discontinued IMP were asked to attend an early termination visit, a follow-up visit (via phone or a site visit if needed) approximately 2 weeks after the last application of IMP, and also the primary endpoint visit at Week 16.

Investigators could prescribe concomitant medications or treatments to provide adequate supportive care as deemed necessary, except for specifically prohibited medications and procedures (Table 5).

## Description of endpoints

## Primary endpoint: Investigator's Global Assessment for chronic hand eczema (IGA-CHE) total score (TS)

IGA-CHE total score TS refers to a score of 0 [clear] or 1 [almost clear] with a ≥2-step improvement from baseline.

The HESD score is derived as an average of the 6 items; a high score is indicative of severe CHE signs and/or symptoms. A minimum of 4 HESD scores per week were required throughout

## Children's Dermatology Life Quality Index (CDLQI)

The CDLQI is a validated questionnaire consisting of 10 items addressing the subject's perception of the impact of their skin disease on different aspects of their quality of life over the last week: dermatology-related symptoms and feelings, friendships, leisure, school or holiday, adverse comments, sleep, and the treatment. Each item is scored on a 4-point Likert scale (0 = 'not at all /not relevant'; 1 = 'a little'; 2 = 'a lot'; 3 = 'very much'). The total score is the sum of the 10 items (0 to 30); a high score is indicative of a poor quality of life. The CDLQI was completed at the trial site according to the schedule of trial procedures (see Figure 2).

## Secondary safety endpoint

The secondary safety endpoint was the number of treatment-emergent adverse events (AEs) from baseline up to Week 18. An event was considered treatment-emergent if started after the first application of IMP or if started before the first application of IMP and worsened in severity after first application of IMP. In addition, evaluations included clinical laboratory tests, vital signs, ECG and physical examination at screening and end of treatment; use of rescue treatment; and subjects' assessment of local tolerability.

## Sample size

92 subjects were to be randomised 3:1 to delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g or cream vehicle. Based on response rates from the phase 2b dose-ranging trial in adults (LP0133-1273), a one-sided significance level at 2.5% and a sample size of 92 subjects would provide at least 80% power for detecting a treatment difference in the primary endpoint assuming an IGA-CHE TS response at Week 16 of 40.2% for delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g and 10.5% for cream vehicle. When the response rates from LP01331401 and LP0133-1402 were included in the sample size calculation, this resulted in a power which was lower than first anticipated.

The power obtained when using the Bayesian approach on a sample size of 92 subjects is explained below.

The design characteristics were based on 92 randomised subjects (69 in the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g group and 23 in the cream vehicle group). For each endpoint, the choice of the between-trial heterogeneity parameter (t) and the weight on the non-informative part of the prior was described. In addition, the degree of borrowing was described by the ESS and finally, the influence of the type I error and power investigated.

## Overview of the Bayesian procedure

The R Bayesian evidence synthesis tools (R package RBesT) was applied. The steps in the analysis are listed below.

1. Create informative prior: The meta-analytic-predictive (MAP) approach, using a hierarchical model for between-study heterogeneity, was applied to derive an informative prior from historical data (the pivotal trials LP0133-1401 and LP0133-1402 and where applicable, phase 2b trial LP0133-1273) for the cream vehicle as well as for the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g treatment group.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

2. Parametric mixture densities: The expectation maximisation algorithm was used to approximate parametric mixture densities to the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) samples representing the MAP prior. The mixture providing the best Akaike information criterion value was selected.

3. Robustification of the informative prior: The informative mixture prior was robustified by adding a non-informative component which protects against type I error inflation in presence of prior-data conflict, i.e. if the adolescent trial data strongly deviate from the historical information. The weight put on the non-informative part was prespecified for each endpoint. The influence of the weight on the ESS, the type I error and power for the trial was to be described.

4. Posterior distribution: Once the data from the adolescent trial was unblinded, the posterior distributions for the proportion of responders for each treatment group, given the robust mixture priors, were calculated.

5. Treatment effect evaluation: Conclusion on treatment effect were based on the calculated difference in the posterior distributions.

a. Statistically significant treatment effect was concluded if the probability that the difference (delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g minus cream vehicle) was larger than zero, was larger than 0.975.

b. 95% credibility interval was generated as the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of the difference in posterior distributions.

c. Median and mean were calculated from the difference in posterior distributions.

6. Sensitivity/tipping point analyses (given the unblinded results) were done to evaluate the influence of the prespecified selected t and the prespecified selected weight parameter for the non-informative part of the prior.

## Evaluation of design characteristics (type I error, power and bias) for the Bayesian analysis of the primary endpoint

The between-trial heterogeneity for the primary endpoint IGA-CHE TS was set to t = 0.5, the weight on the non-informative part of the prior was set to 0.2 (20%). This ensured a type I error &lt;5% for efficacy rates &lt;10% (average observed efficacy rates for cream vehicle was 8.4% in the adult trials LP0133-1401 and LP0133-1402) and a high power (&gt;75%) for efficacy levels above observed efficacy levels (average difference in adult trials is 15.9%). For all key secondary endpoints, the same t and weight were used.

The selection of the parameters for the primary endpoint IGA-CHE TS were justified. Overall, the type I error decreases as t and weight increase, and it peaks just above the average observed response rate in the historical data. The power is increasing with increasing difference and lower t and lower weight for the non-informative part of the prior distribution.

The choice of t = 0.5 and a weight = 0.2 corresponded to an effective sample size (ESS) of approximately 55 for the cream vehicle arm and approximately 20 for the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g arm for the primary endpoint IGA-CHE TS. The larger degree of borrowing of information in the cream vehicle arm as compared to the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g arm was due to less variability in the historical data for cream vehicle. For the key secondary endpoints the ESS were approximately 70 and 110 for HECSI-90, approximately 40 and 90 for HESD, approximately 60 and 90 for HESD itch, and approximately 35 and 40 for HESD pain for the cream vehicle arm and for the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g arm, respectively.

## Randomisation

The subjects were centrally randomised in a 3:1 ratio to receive treatment with either delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g or cream vehicle and stratified by baseline IGA-CHE score (3 or 4) by using a central interactive response technology (IRT) system.

## Blinding (masking)

Study 1426 was a double-blind trial. The packaging and labelling of the IMPs contained no evidence of their identity. It is not considered possible to differentiate between the IMPs solely by sensory evaluation.

If emergency unblinding was required, the investigator could unblind a subject's treatment via an unblinding function in the IRT system or unblinding CRO. Only the investigator was to be unblinded and was required to keep treatment allocation confidential. The MAH had to remain blinded until the end of the trial (except designated unblinded personnel). A local contact number for emergency unblinding CRO was provided on the subject card to be used (by e.g. emergency room physician) if investigator could not be reached.

## Protocol deviations

Protocol deviations (PDs) were classified as important or non-important and categorised according to a set of predefined categories and subcategories. Important PDs were considered those that could significantly affect a subject's rights, safety, or well-being, or significantly impact the completeness, accuracy, or reliability of key trial data. A total of 26 important PDs were reported during the trial.

Four (4) important PDs were related to violation of eligibility criteria: ICF for adolescents becoming of legal age not signed (1), ICF signed only by parent as required, but the subject did not sign the form until after having completed the trial (1). Additionally, two PDs in the same subject: violation of exclusion criterion 10 (cutaneously applied treatment with immunomodulators or TCS on the hands within 14 days prior to baseline) and violation of exclusion criterion 11 (use of systemic antibiotics or cutaneously applied antibiotics on the hands within 14 days prior to baseline). This subject was thus randomised in error but was still included in the analysis sets.

Fourteen (14) important subject-level PDs not related to violation of eligibility criteria were considered to impact interpretability of trial results for the individual subject but did not lead to exclusion from any trial analysis sets:

- 5 were related to primary endpoint assessment missing/late.
- 7 were related to missing/late/incomplete efficacy assessments (other than primary endpoint).
- 1 was related to assessments performed by non-authorised staff.
- 1 was related to wrong trial product administered.

## HECSI-90

During the procedure, the MAH submitted correction to the logistic regression analyses provided in the CSR. The identified error did not affect the primary endpoint but concerned the key secondary endpoints (HECSI-90, ≥4-point reduction in HESD itch, HESD pain, and HESD scores), and the exploratory endpoints (HECSI-90, HECSI-75, ≥4- and ≥3-point reductions in HESD itch, HESD pain, and HESD scores).

In Trial 1426, the logistic regression analyses were adjusted for the baseline IGA-CHE score. For these analyses, baseline IGA-CHE score was required to be specified as a categorical variable (i.e. \"Moderate\" and \"Severe\"). This specification ensures correct estimation of response rates by treatment group, risk differences with 95% confidence intervals, and p-values. This was done correctly for the primary endpoint, IGA-CHE TS. For the original outputs of the key secondary endpoints and the exploratory endpoints listed above, the baseline IGA-CHE score was specified as a numeric variable (\"3\" and \"4\") rather than as a categorical variable (\"Moderate\" and \"Severe\"). The numeric variable (\"3\" and \"4\") for baseline IGA-CHE score produced incorrect results in the statistical outputs for these endpoints, which were submitted within this Type II variation application. As a corrective measure, the affected analyses were updated using the categorical variable (\"Moderate\" and \"Severe\"). The corrected results are presented in this AR instead of those in the original submission.

## 2.4.3. Discussion on clinical efficacy

## Design and conduct of clinical studies

The MAH conducted one randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, parallel-group, multi-site trial in adolescents to support the current variation i.e., study LP0133-1426. This was a phase 3 clinical trial to evaluate efficacy and safety of twice-daily applications of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g compared with cream vehicle for a 16-week treatment period in adolescents 12-17 years of age with moderate to severe CHE.

After completion of pivotal phase 3 trials in adults, the originally planned sample size of study 1426 was considered by the MAH potentially too small to have power to show a difference, as the response rates for adults for the primary endpoint were lower in Trials 1401 and 1402 than in Trial 1273. Therefore, the MAH amended the statistical methodology of study 1426 to a Bayesian analysis where data from the adult population from completed pivotal phase 3 trials (LP0133-1401 and LP0133-1402) was leveraged to analyse efficacy endpoints in the adolescent population. Upon PDCO's request, a frequentist analysis was added and the number of evaluable subjects was specified. However, the PDCO noted that the conducted primary analysis deviated from the PIP opinion. Taking note of the

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

discussions during the modification procedure, the PDCO nonetheless confirmed compliance, as a frequentist analysis had also been conducted. This is issue is further discussed below. Furthermore, the MAH submitted no paediatric extrapolation concept or extrapolation plan for support of use of efficacy and safety data obtained from adults for extrapolation to the adolescents, as recommended in the Reflection paper on the use of extrapolation in the development of medicines for paediatrics (EMA/189724/2018) and the ICH E11A Guideline on pediatric extrapolation (EMA/CHMP/ICH/205218/2022). The MAH stated that \"extrapolation from adults to adolescents was considered justified based on similarity of CHE pathophysiology in adults and adolescents, low and comparable systemic exposure in adults and adolescents following topical application of delgocitinib, and expected consistency in the treatment effect of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g.\" Nevertheless, no reference or discussion was however provided by the MAH for the claimed similarity of CHE pathophysiology in adults and adolescents. The MAH stated that few studies have dealt with hand eczema in paediatric populations and that there is limited information on the prevalence of hand eczema in paediatrics. Nonetheless, the MAH refered to the Scandinavian data on prevalence of CHE in the paediatric population, according to which the incidence rates of CHE were similar among adult and adolescent subjects (please see Section 2.1.1 of this AR). The MAH also referred to data showing a strong association between CHE in adolescence and AD in childhood, but has, however, not discussed any association between CHE in adolescence and adult age. Since the conducted clinical trial in the end demonstrated the efficacy of delgocitinib cream in comparison to vehicle cream in adolescents with CHE also using a frequentist analysis, an extrapolation from adults to adolescents are not ultimately deemed necessary for assessment of efficacy.

The MAH provided a comparison table of key inclusion and exclusion criteria in pivotal trials 1401 and 1402 vs. study 1426. The criteria are the same except for age (≥18 years in studies 1401 and 1402 and 12-17 years in study 1426), and the criterion on HESD itch score (weekly average≥4 at baseline) required only in pivotal trials 1401 and 1402.

The endpoints of studies 1401 and 1402 and study 1426 were almost identical. The efficacy assessments in Trial 1426 included both investigator-rated efficacy assessments (IGA-CHE and HECSI) and subject-rated efficacy assessments (HESD and CDLQI). These efficacy assessments were also included in Trials 1401 and 1402, except CDLQI, which is the age-appropriate version of the DLQI questionnaire developed for adults. Further, the estimands in study 1426 were similar to those used in studies 1401 and 1402 also in terms of handing of intercurrent events and population level effect measure.

Overall, the study settings of study 1426 and studies 1401 and 1402 are similar, which supports the eligibility of adult data from studies 1401 and 1402 to be used for the extrapolation using the Bayesian analysis. The decision to change from a Frequentist analysis to Bayesian borrowing was done while the adolescent study was blinded but the adult data were known. The reason for the change was the observed lower response rates in the adult phase 3 studies vs in the phase 2 study, which was used for the sample size calculation. The Bayesian approach used data from the adult trials to construct an informative prior to be used in the analysis of adolescents. A so-called robust prior - a mixture of the informative part based on the MAP approach informed by the adult data and a non-informative (or vague) part - was used. Major tuning parameters of the approach were the assumed between-trial heterogeneity, τ, and the weight put on the non-informative part of the prior. The smaller the assumed heterogeneity and the smaller the weight put on the non-informative part of the prior, the larger the amount of adult evidence that was used and, consequently, the larger the effective sample size (ESS). The result is meant to be a mixture of the effect estimate in adult and in adolescents, with ESS reflecting the amounts of evidence taken from adults.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Borrowing from the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g arm and from the cream vehicle arm in the adult studies was uneven. The larger degree of borrowing of information in the cream vehicle arm as compared with the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g arm, for the primary endpoint, was explained by the lower variability in response rates across the two adult phase 3 studies for cream vehicle. Instead of borrowing information on the response rates separately from the two arms in the adult studies, the effect size could have been borrowed. Indeed, when combining information across studies (e.g. in a meta-analysis) a standard premise is that there may be a study-specific factor impacting all subjects within that study, but this nuisance parameters cancels out when estimating treatment effect within studies. Assuming that a study-specific (or adult-specific) factor exists, the estimation using uneven amounts of historical adult data by treatment group is biased. The results would likely not have changed much though even if information effect size had been borrowed instead.

Aside from the above concern about the specific way of borrowing, it is questioned whether the small effect size estimated among adults is a good justification for Bayesian borrowing. Essentially, the use of Bayesian borrowing suggests that a lower standard for independent, confirmatory evidence in adolescents applies. The opposite might be true: it may be argued that the need for compelling confirmatory evidence in adolescents is amplified by the effect observed in adults.

Due to these uncertainties in the Bayesian analysis, the results were requested to be presented in the SmPC section 5.1 according to the Frequentist analysis results/ which was agreed and implemented by the MAH.

There were few intercurrent events impacting efficacy evaluation by Week 16. Also missing data was not an issue. Stratified (Cochran-) Mantel-Haenszel tests and estimates and stratified logistic regression were applied with stratification by baseline ICE-CHE, i.e. the factor considered in randomisation. These methods are considered appropriate.

There were some protocol deviations during the trial, none of which are considered to have had an impact on subject safety or the interpretation of trial results.

## Efficacy data and additional analyses

In Trial 1426, 98 subjects were randomised 3:1 to delgocitinib cream (n=74) or cream vehicle (n=24). Close to 80% of subjects were European, hence, the study population is considered well representative of European adolescent population. The mean (SD) age of study subjects was similar in both study groups: 14.6 (1.7) in the delgocitinib group vs. 14.7 (1.6) in the vehicle group. There were more female (62.2%) than male (37.8%) subjects in the delgocitinib group, whereas in the vehicle group the gender distribution was equal (50% of both sexes). Also, the youngest adolescents were well represented, with 23/74 subjects aged 12 to 13 years in the delgocitinib vs. 4/24 in the vehicle group. There were no differences in demographic or baseline characteristics in study 1426 that are considered to affect the interpretation of efficacy, safety, or PK results, including the small difference in gender distribution in the study groups.

All study subjects were exposed to investigational medicinal product (IMP) and were included in the full analysis set (FAS). In the delgocitinib group, 71/74 (95.9%) subjects completed trial vs. 22/24 (91.7%) subjects in the vehicle group. During the 16-week treatment period, rescue treatment was prescribed to 1 subject in the delgocitinib group and 2 in the vehicle group; and during the safety follow-up period, to 1 subject in the delgocitinib group. Three subjects in both groups discontinued the IMP. The reasons for discontinuation were adverse event (1 in both groups), lack of efficacy (2 in the delgocitinib and 1 in the vehicle group) and lost to follow-up (1 in the vehicle group).

## Primary endpoint

Based on the confirmatory analysis of the primary endpoint IGA-CHE TS at Week 16 using the composite estimand, the efficacy of delgocitinib cream was superior to that of cream vehicle at Week 16. Altogether 47/74 (63.5%) of subjects achieved IGA-CHE TS at Week 16 in the delgocitinib group compared with 7/24 subjects (29.2%) in the cream vehicle group, with a difference of 37.9 % according to Bayesian analysis (95% Cred. Int. 13.5, 58.2) and probability of 0.999. The primary endpoint analysis using the primary estimand was also performed using the Frequentist approach. Using composite strategy for the primary estimand, there were 63.5% responders in the delgocitinib group and 29.2% responders in the vehicle group, with a difference of 34.3% (CI 13.1% to 55.4%), p=0.0015, based on logistic regression analysis. Logistic regression analysis using treatment policy strategy and analyses using Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test for both the composite and treatment policy strategies yielded similar results with a statistically significant difference between the study groups in favour of the delgocitinib group.

## Secondary efficacy endpoints

The primary and all key secondary endpoints in study 1426 (IGA-CHE TS, HECSI-90, and ≥4 reduction in HESD itch, HESD pain, and HESD score) were evaluated based on a pre-specified testing hierarchy. Delgocitinib cream was superior to cream vehicle on all key secondary endpoints, based on the confirmatory Bayesian analysis. The additional analyses (Frequentist) of the key secondary endpoints supported the results of the Bayesian analysis, as the estimated treatment effects were in favour of delgocitinib cream. Thus, the conclusions from the Bayesian analyses are overall the same as those from the Frequentist analyses.

Delgocitinib cream demonstrated early onset of action for itch and pain, as greater improvements were observed with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g than with cream vehicle in ≥ 4-point reduction of HESD itch at Week 1 and HESD pain at Week 2. As this information is considered clinically relevant for healthcare professionals, its inclusion in SmPC section 5.1 was ultimately agreed.

The secondary endpoint related to subject's quality of life, the mean change in CDLQI score from baseline to Week 16, was larger for delgocitinib cream than for cream vehicle (LSMean: -5.6 vs -2.9, nominal p-value: 0.004). Compared to studies in adults, the estimated treatment effect in adolescents was overall more pronounced.

The dosing regimen investigated in study 1426 was delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g applied twice daily on hands, fingers, fingertips, and wrists in a thin layer covering the affected areas. This is the same dosing regimen that has been approved for moderate to severe CHE in adults. Based on results from study 1426, the same dosing for delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g is considered appropriate for adolescents as the approved dosing in adults.

The efficacy results of adolescents were requested to be presented in the SmPC as analysed by the Frequentist analysis instead of Bayesian analysis, even though the latter was after the amendment of the primary analysis of the study, due to uncertainties regarding validity of the Bayesian analysis. Furthermore, it is considered more appropriate to include the results from adolescents only in this part of Section 5.1 of the SmPC, as the Bayesian analyses contain also adult data. The MAH amended the SmPC as requested, and the results are now presented as analysed by logistic regression.

One endpoint (HECSI, LS mean % change from baseline) was requested to be omitted from Section 5.1 of the SmPC, as it merely duplicates the results of HECSI-90 already presented and thus does not provide additional value for the prescriber, and the MAH agreed to this change. Furthermore, the nonmultiplicity-controlled results on HECSI-75 and CDLQI were also requested to be removed and the MAH also agreed to this change.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Sensitivity analyses and supplementary estimands confirmed the results of the primary analysis. For the Bayesian analyses, sensitivity and tipping point analyses indicated that the selected τ and weight had little effect on the results of the study. Of note, the outcome of the study would have been the same also without the mid-study change to a Bayesian analysis. The originally planned Frequentist analysis of the primary and key secondary endpoints were statistically significant, except reduction of HESD score (weekly average) of &gt;= 4 points from baseline to Week 16 (p=0.0611), which was tested last in the hierarchy. Overall, the Bayesian results were very similar to the Frequentist results.

The MAH has proposed to include additional information on statistical methodology in the presentation of results in adults in tables 3, 4 and 5 of SmPC section 5.1 without changes to factual information. The changes are considered acceptable.

## 2.4.4. Conclusions on the clinical efficacy

Based on results from study 1426, delgocitinib cream is effective in the treatment of moderate to severe chronic hand eczema in adolescents 12-17 years of age. The same dosing approved in adults for delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g is considered appropriate for adolescents.

## 2.5. Clinical safety

## Introduction

LP0133-1426(DELTA TEEN) was a phase 3 clinical study to evaluate efficacy and safety of twice-daily applications of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g compared with cream vehicle for a 16-week treatment period in adolescents 12-17 years of age with moderate to severe CHE.

## Safety data comparisons

The primary evaluation of safety is based on data from adolescents in study 1426. Two of the safety pools used in the initial MAA, the 'Primary pool' and the 'Exposure pool' (Table 41), were compared side-by-side with study 1426 to evaluate and compare the safety observed with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in adolescents to that of adults with moderate to severe CHE.

- The Primary pool: The primary evaluation of safety in adults was based on the data from three 16-week vehicle-controlled trials in adults pooled into the Primary pool (Trials 1273, 1401, and 1402). The 2 pivotal phase 3 trials in adult subjects, Trials 1401 and 1402 were of similar design as Trial 1426 in adolescents.
- The Exposure pool included safety data from clinical trials with delgocitinib cream of variable trial designs in CHE i.e. 1273, 1401, 1402, 1403, 1528, 2285 and AD Trials 1275 and 1181. This pool was used for the evaluation of less frequent safety findings.

## Adverse events by causal relationship

In Trial 1426, few AEs were assessed as probably or possibly related to IMP by the investigator. delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g: 2 AEs (1 PT application site pain and 1 PT flushing) in 2 subjects (2.7%, R: 7.76). No severe AEs were reported with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in Trial 1426. All AEs reported during the trial period were of mild or moderate severity, except for 3 severe AEs in 3 subjects in the cream vehicle group (12.5%, R: 36.68).

## Dermal safety

## Application site reactions

Application site reactions were identified, by the MAH as an ADR in the original MAA in adults, based on the consistent pattern of onset shortly after treatment initiation in about 1% of subjects, and the consistent investigator-judged relationship to IMP.

In Trial 1426, 2 subjects reported 2 events of PT application site pain were reported: 1 in each treatment group. Both events were non-serious, mild, judged to be probably related to IMP, and resolved. Both events had onset within 8 days of first IMP dose. These findings were consistent with the findings in the Primary pool.

Application site reactions are included as ADRs for adults in SmPC section 4.8 with a frequency 'common'. This ADR is also proposed by the MAH as a common ADR for adolescents.

## Local skin infections

In Trial 1426, 'Local skin infections' were reported by 1 subject on delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g (1.4% of subjects, R: 3.88) and none on cream vehicle, and by 5 subjects on delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in the Primary pool (0.7% of subjects, R: 2.26). The 1 event of 'local skin infections' in the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g treatment group was a moderate, non-serious PT acrodermatitis considered not related to IMP by the investigator. While the AE was reported as lesional/ perilesional, the RT was 'scabies whole body'. The event had onset on Day 48 at which point the subject was withdrawn from trial and treated with concomitant medication. The event resolved by Day 55.

One additional local skin infection (1 RT 'Hand Eczema infection' (verbatim), PT localised infection) was reported with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g on Day 18 but not captured by the safety focus area search terms. The event was mild, non-serious and judged not related to IMP. The localised infection recovered in 6 days, with subject withdrawal 6 days later than this due to 'lack of efficacy'.

In the Primary pool there was no imbalance in the reporting of 'local skin infections' between delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g and cream vehicle.

## Acnes

In Trial 1426, 2 AEs of 'acnes' were reported, both in the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g group (both PT acne) (2.7% of subjects, R: 7.76). Both events were distant, non-serious, of mild severity, were judged not related to IMP, and did not lead to any change in dose or other action with the IMP. By the end of trial one event had resolved while continuing treatment while the other had not.

The rate of 'acnes' in adolescents in Trial 1426 was higher than that reported with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in the Primary pool (0.3% of subjects, R: 0.92), possibly due to the more common occurrence of acnes in adolescents.

## Local tolerability

In Trial 1426, the IMP was generally well tolerated; in both treatment groups, most subjects reported no or mild tolerability issues (i.e. 'worst stinging/burning' in connection with IMP application graded as 'none' or 'mild' in the eDiary) during the 16 weeks of treatment. The tolerability to the IMP improved in both treatment groups during the trial, assessed as a higher percentage of subjects reporting 'worst stinging/burning' as 'none' during the last weeks of treatment versus the first weeks of treatment. Local tolerability assessed by subjects in Trial 1426 was consistent with local tolerability assessments reported in the Primary pool.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

There were no AEs reported as allergic contact dermatitis or irritant contact dermatitis.

## Hypersensitivity reactions

The 'hypersensitivity reactions' search was based on the SMQ Hypersensitivity (narrow) which includes PTs such as dermatitis contact, hand dermatitis, dermatitis atopic, and dermatitis allergic. This search was called 'allergic reactions'.

In Trial 1426, hypersensitivity reactions were reported by 6.8% of subjects (R: 19.41) with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g and 4.2% of subjects with cream vehicle (R: 12.23). The percentage of subjects with hypersensitivity reactions and the rates of these were similar between delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in Trial 1426 and the Primary pool (5.4% of subjects, R: 20.05).

In the Trial 1426 delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g treatment group, 3 of the 5 events were skin related: 1 lesional/perilesional PT hand dermatitis, 1 distant PT dermatitis atopic and 1 distant eczema. The lesional/perilesional PT hand dermatitis was reported near the end of the subject's safety follow-up period (Day 123, last day of IMP Day 113), and thus not while on delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g. The 2 non-cutaneous events captured were PTs rhinitis allergic and conjunctivitis allergic.

All 5 events in the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g group were non-serious, of mild or moderate severity, and were judged not related to IMP. 4 events did not lead to action to IMP and 1 had action to IMP as not applicable.

In the cream vehicle group, 1 severe, lesional PT hand dermatitis was reported; this event led to drug withdrawal.

## Laboratory findings

There were no clinically relevant changes or differences between treatment groups in any of the haematology, liver, kidney, or lipid parameters in Trial 1426, consistent with the evaluation in the Primary pool. No findings related to vital signs, ECG, or physical examination in adolescent Trial 1426.

## Safety in special populations

In Trial 1426, the potential effects of intrinsic factors on the safety profile of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g was examined for the subgroups of sex and baseline IGA-CHE score. No clinically relevant differences were observed for subjects with different sex (male and female) or baseline disease severity, as measured by the IGA-CHE score (moderate and severe).

Minor variations in the AE profile were observed across some of the subgroups, particularly with the cream vehicle. According to the MAH, these observations were based on relatively low number of subjects and events and any variations should be considered in this context.

No extrinsic factors were examined.

## Safety related to drug-drug interactions and other interactions

No clinical drug interaction studies have been performed with delgocitinib cream. Given the limited metabolism of delgocitinib, application to a limited BSA (hands and wrists), and minimal systemic exposure of topically applied delgocitinib, there is a low potential for interaction with systemic medications.

## Discontinuation due to adverse events

In Trial 1426 2 AEs in 2 subjects (2.7%, R: 7.76) in the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g group led to permanent discontinuation of IMP and withdrawal from trial, both reported within the SOC infections and infestations. These were 1 mild AE of PT localised infection and 1 moderate AE of PT acrodermatitis. These events are discussed in the 'local skin infections'. In all, 1 severe AE (PT hand dermatitis) (4.2%, R: 12.23) in the cream vehicle group led to permanent discontinuation of IMP.

None of these events in Trial 1426 were assessed as related to IMP by the investigator and all subjects had recovered or were recovering from the events by the end of the trial.

The proportion of subjects and rates of AEs leading to withdrawal or permanent discontinuation of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g was slightly higher in Trial 1426 than in the Primary pool (6 subjects, 1.0%, R: 3.00). According to the MAH, this difference may be due to differences in the trial size or age group.

## Post marketing experience

## Anzupgo

Anzupgo has not yet been approved or marketed for use in adolescents in the EU. As of 22 July 2025, the cumulative patient exposure of Anzupgo is estimated to be 2,889 patient years of on-use exposure in adults, and 71 post marketing cases have been received, of which one case report was serious. The events reported have been reviewed and assessed in the context of the cumulative information. The SAE, hand dermatitis (rapidly deteriorating eczema with open lesions) was considered related by the physician. According to the MAH, the patient's underlying conditions, which included palmoplantar pustular psoriasis, psoriasis vulgaris, and AD, make it more likely that the flare-up was related to these conditions in an elderly patient. The MAH does not consider causality between the reported event and the use of Anzupgo. The data were in line with the current known safety profile with no new safety concerns identified, and no changes were made to the reference safety information.

Of note, CORECTIM (delgocitinib ointment) was approved in January 2020 for treatment of AD and is marketed in Japan. Subsequently, in March 2021, the indication was expanded for treatment of AD in adolescents (aged from 2 to 17 years) and later also expanded to infants (aged 6 to 24 months). The MAH has not identified or received any new information regarding a safety concern related to CORECTIM ointment since the approval of Anzupgo.

## 2.5.1. Discussion on clinical safety

For the assessment of safety in adolescents, the MAH conducted a single randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, parallel-group, multi-site trial in adolescents. LP0133-1426 (DELTA TEEN) was a phase 3 clinical trial to evaluate efficacy and safety of twice-daily applications of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g compared with cream vehicle for a 16-week treatment period in adolescents 12-17 years of age with moderate to severe CHE.

## Safety data pools

Safety data from pivotal Trial 1426 were presented side-by-side with safety data from the Primary and the Exposure pools of adults trials evaluated in the initial MAA. A pooled approach was utilised, with distinct data pools created for assessment of different safety aspects. The 'Primary pool' (Trials 1401, 1402 and 1273) represented a selected source population of adult CHE patients in trials of similar design, with same indication, dose and duration of treatment as the pivotal study in the target population of adolescents. The 'Exposure pool' included adult trials, 1 of which also included children and adolescents of variable designs and indications (CHE and AD) including double- and single-blind, vehicle- and active-controlled trial designs (Trials 1401, 1402, 1273, 1528, 1275), and single-arm trials or treatment periods (Trials 1403, 1181, 2285).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Overall, the MAH's principles of safety assessment are endorsed.

## Extent of exposure

In Trial 1426, 74 adolescents (25.8 PYO) were treated with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g compared with 24 adolescents (8.2 PYO) treated with cream vehicle, for up to 16 weeks.

In the Primary pool, 691 adults with CHE were exposed to delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g (214.72 PYO) vs 371 subjects exposed to cream vehicle (109.91 PYO) for up to 16 weeks.

In the Exposure pool, 1596 subjects with CHE or AD were exposed to delgocitinib cream in the clinical trial program across the indications, in trials of varying design, for a total of 833.65 PYO.

As such, the overall size of the safety database in adolescents is somewhat limited, as per the general minimum requirements expressed in ICH E1 Guideline, particularly in terms of longer-term exposure. However, based on the available data, this is not considered to be of concern, as discussed below.

## Trial 1426 in adolescent

## Adverse events

The overall numbers of AEs was higher for delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g 37 (50.0%) in comparison with the vehicle treatment arm 8 (33,3%). The most commonly reported AEs were within the SOC infections and infestations, with AEs reported in around one third of subjects in each treatment group. The most frequently reported AEs were PTs nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infection, and headache. There appeared to be more upper respiratory tract infections in the delgocitinib treatment group (6.8 %; R=19.41) vs. none in the vehicle group. When including all related PTs (upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis, rhinitis, sinusitis, viral pharyngitis, viral upper respiratory tract infection), the two groups experienced 21 and 5 events, respectively. As infections are a known class effect of JAK inhibitors, the MAH was requested to justify why upper respiratory tract infections should not be included in SmPC section 4.8. The MAH provided adequate justifications in their response supporting the conclusion that upper respiratory tract infections were not related to delgocitinib treatment in Trial 1426. No temporal associations were seen between occurrence of respiratory tract infections with delgocitinib cream treatment. No serious or severe infections were reported. The number of all events in the class of Infections and infestations was 19/74 (25.7%) in the delgocitinib group vs. 6/24 (25.0 %) in the vehicle group. The distribution of infection events between the study groups was also even. Furthermore, the systemic exposure to delgocitinib is low with local application of the delgocitinib cream.

In the Primary pool, the most frequent AEs reported by ≥5% of subjects with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g were PTs nasopharyngitis, COVID-19 and headache.

All AEs reported with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g were non-serious, and mild or moderate in severity. No severe AEs were reported. The majority of all AEs resolved during the trial.

No SAEs, deaths, or pregnancies were reported with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g. There was no indication that the intrinsic factors analysed, sex or baseline disease severity, affected the AE profile of adolescent subjects. It is noted that no extrinsic factors were examined. Only two AEs in two subjects (2.7%, R: 7.76) in the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g group (one mild local skin infection and one acrodermatitis) led to permanent discontinuation of IMP and withdrawal from trial. Neither of the two were agreeably assessed as related to the IMP. The analysis of common AEs supports an overall favourable safety profile.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Safety focus areas

The previously identified ADR for adults 'application site reactions' is also considered an ADR for the adolescent population by the MAH. This ADR was reported once by 1 subject on delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in study 1426. According to the MAH, no new ADRs were identified in adolescents. Upon CHMP's request, the MAH presented detailed descriptions of the five hypersensitivity cases in the delgocitinib group and provided sufficient information confirming that the observed hypersensitivity reactions were unlikely to be caused by the delgocitinib cream, as they either occurred distant from the application site, the timing did not suggest association with treatment with delgciotinib cream, or the nature of the reaction was not typical for a reaction to local application on skin (allergic rhinitis, conjunctivitis), or the subjects suffered from atopy or seasonal allergic reactions already at baseline. No further safety concern arose from analysis of other event belonging to 'dermal safety', namely 'acnes', 'local tolerability', or 'local skin infections'.

No adverse events were reported in Trial 1426 for other focus areas of 'skin atrophy, 'serious or severe infections', 'herpes viral infections' including eczema herpeticum and herpes zoster, 'vaccination side effects, including covid-19 vaccination', 'embolic and thrombotic events', 'cardiovascular events of interest', 'all-cause mortality', 'Torsade de pointes/QT prolongation', 'malignancies including NMSC', or 'rare events'.

No clinically relevant differences in vital sign measurements (blood pressure and pulse) were observed in any of the treatment groups from baseline to end of treatment in Trial 1426.

## Systemic exposure

The first timepoint at which plasma delgocitinib concentrations were determined in the study 1426 was at 8 weeks. The MAH was, therefore, requested to compare all safety data before and after the 8 weeks timepoint in study 1426 to clarify if differences were seen and to clarify at which timepoint from start of treatment the most common AEs of infections and infestations (SOC and PT) occurred. There was a numerically slightly higher rate of AEs in the first 8 weeks compared with the period after 8 weeks in both study groups (delgocitinib cream and vehicle cream groups). The number (%) of subjects with AEs was 24/74 (32.4%) during the first 8 weeks and 21/42 (29.3%) after 8 weeks in the delgocitinib group. In the vehicle group, the number (%) of subjects with AEs was 6/24 (25.0%) during the first 8 weeks and 5/22 (22.7%) after 8 weeks. There were no serious AEs in either group. The difference was not driven by any pattern or clustering of events but rather by reporting of more single events across the SOCs infections and infestations, gastrointestinal disorders, and respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders. Notably, the observed difference in the overall rate of AEs before and after 8 weeks was similar in the active and comparator groups. The observed minor differences in AE rates between the time periods are considered to be random.

In addition, and as requested, the MAH analysed, at which timepoint from start of treatment the most common AEs of infections and infestations (SOC and PT) occurred. No peaks or pattern in time of occurrence of common respiratory tract infections were seen during the 16-week treatment period. All respiratory tract infections were non-serious and mild or moderate in severity. The results do not indicate any increased risk of infectious events in the first weeks of treatment.

## Clinical laboratory parameters

There were no clinically relevant changes or differences between treatment groups in any of the haematology, liver, kidney or lipid parameters parameters in Trial 1426. No cardiac safety concerns were identified based on the evaluation of vital signs and ECG. Overall, these findings were in line with the safety findings from the Primary pool.

## Safety in special populations

No clinically relevant differences were observed for subjects with different sex (male and female) or baseline disease severity, as measured by the IGA-CHE score (moderate and severe). No extrinsic factors were examined. This is agreed.

## Safety related to drug-drug interactions and other interactions

No clinical drug interaction studies have been performed with delgocitinib cream. Given the limited metabolism of delgocitinib, application to a limited BSA (hands and wrists), and minimal systemic exposure of topically applied delgocitinib, there is a low potential for interaction with systemic medications. This is agreed.

## Discontinuation or withdrawal from trial due to adverse events

The proportion of subjects and rates of AEs leading to withdrawal or permanent discontinuation of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g (2 subjects, 2.7%. R: 7.76) was slightly higher in Trial 1426 than in the Primary pool (6 subjects, 1.0%, R: 3.00). According to the MAH, this difference may be due to differences in the trial size or age group, which can be agreed with.

## Post marketing safety data

To date, Anzupgo has not yet been approved or marketed for use in adolescents in the EU. In the post marketing setting, as of 22 July 2025, the cumulative patient exposure of Anzupgo is estimated to be 2,889 patient years of on-use exposure in adults, and 71 post marketing cases have been received, of which one case report was serious. The SAE, hand dermatitis (rapidly deteriorating eczema with open lesions) was considered related by the physician. According to the MAH, the patient's underlying conditions, which included palmoplantar pustular psoriasis, psoriasis vulgaris, and AD, make it more likely that the flare-up was related to these conditions in an elderly patient. The MAH does not consider causality between the reported event and the use of Anzupgo. This is agreed. The data were in line with the current known safety profile with no new safety concerns identified, and no changes were made to the reference safety information.

## Extrapolation of safety from the adult source population to the target adolescent population

The MAH did not submit paediatric extrapolation concept or extrapolation plan for support of use of efficacy and safety data obtained from adults for extrapolation to adolescents, even though recommended in the Reflection paper on the use of extrapolation in the development of medicines for paediatrics (EMA/189724/2018) and the ICH E11A Guideline on paediatric extrapolation (EMA/CHMP/ICH/205218/2022). The MAH stated that extrapolation from adults to adolescents was considered justified based on similarity of CHE pathophysiology in adults and adolescents, low and comparable systemic exposure in adults and adolescents following topical application of delgocitinib and expected consistency in the treatment effect of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g. However, no references or discussion were provided by the MAH. Only short-term safety results in adolescents are available from the current study. Further, the size of the adolescent safety database is relatively limited, especially for longer-term exposures. Nevertheless, the overall safety profile of delgocitinib cream in the adolescents' population is not expected to differ from the adult's one.

In addition, the PASS \"Enhanced safety surveillance of NMSC cases\" that ensures gathering of relevant information specifically related to NMSC has been modified to also include adolescents (see RMP Section). Therefore, no extrapolation concept and plan are considered necessary for this variation.

## Comparative analysis of safety data

Overall, the safety profile of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g twice daily for a duration of 16 weeks in treatment of adolescents with CHE appeared similar in comparison to the safety profile of adults from the overall source population of adults, acknowledging the limitations of this type of across study comparisons.

In conclusion, the results of the study 1426 showed a relatively benign safety profile in treatment of adolescents 12-17 years of age with moderate to severe CHE with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g twice daily for 16 weeks of treatment. The safety results are comparable to those in adults. SmPC section 4.8 was updated accordingly.

Upon request, the MAH agreed to include adolescents in this planned PASS (see RMP).

## 3. Benefit-Risk Balance

## 3.1. Therapeutic Context

## 3.1.1. Disease or condition

Chronic hand eczema (CHE) refers to hand eczema which persists for more than 3 months or returns twice or more often within 12 months. CHE is a multifactorial heterogenous chronic inflammatory skin disorder located anywhere on the hands and wrists. It is a disabling condition which impacts quality of life and occupational performance. The prevalence of CHE is similar in adolescents as adults.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 3.1.2. Available therapies and unmet medical need

Management of CHE include education, avoidance of allergens, protection from irritants, and frequent use of emollients. Current medical management relies on potent topical corticosteroids (TCS). However, many patients either fail to respond to corticosteroids or experience adverse effects from prolonged use, including skin atrophy and an increased risk of infection. Calcineurin inhibitors such as tacrolimus and pimecrolimus are used as steroid-sparing alternatives in adults, adolescents and children with atopic dermatitis (AD) but are not authorised for the treatment of other forms of CHE in the EU. Oral alitretinoin can be used for adult patients with severe CHE not responding to potent TCS. Consequently, there is an unmet medical need for treatment options for CHE especially in the adolescent population.

## 3.1.3. Main clinical studies

The MAH conducted one randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, parallel-group, multi-site trial in adolescents to support the current variation. Study LP0133-1426 was a phase 3 clinical trial to evaluate efficacy and safety of twice-daily applications of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g compared with cream vehicle for a 16-week treatment period in adolescents 12-17 years of age with moderate to severe CHE.

Study subjects (n=98) were randomised 3:1 to delgocitinib cream (n=74) or cream vehicle (n=24), all of whom were exposed to IMP and were included in the full analysis set (FAS). In the delgocitinib group, 71/74 (95.9%) subjects completed trial vs. 22/24 (91.7%) subjects in the vehicle group.

After completion of pivotal phase 3 trials in adults, the originally planned sample size of study 1426 was considered by the MAH potentially too small to have power to show a difference, as the response rates for adults for the primary endpoint were lower in Trials 1401 and 1402 than in Trial 1273. Therefore, the MAH amended the statistical methodology of study 1426 to a Bayesian analysis where data from the adult population from recently completed pivotal phase 3 trials (LP0133-1401 and LP0133-1402, \"studies 1401 and 1402\") was leveraged to analyse efficacy endpoints in the adolescent population. Upon PDCO's request, a Frequentist analysis was added, and the number of evaluable subjects was specified. The analyses of data were based on full analysis set (all subjects randomised and exposed to IMP).

## 3.2. Favourable effects

The confirmatory Bayesian analysis of the primary endpoint IGA-CHE TS at Week 16 using the composite estimand demonstrated that the efficacy of delgocitinib cream was superior to that of cream vehicle. Altogether 47/74 (63.5%) of subjects achieved IGA-CHE TS at Week 16 in the delgocitinib group compared with 7/24 subjects (29.2%) in the cream vehicle group, with a difference of 37.9 % (95% Cred. Int. 13.5, 58.2) and probability of 0.999. Similar results were obtained by using the Frequentist approach by logistic regression analysis and by Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test for both the composite and treatment policy strategies on the primary endpoint. The efficacy of delgocitinib cream was also statistically significantly higher than that of vehicle cream as analysed by the Frequentist methodology without extrapolation from adult data. At week 16, using composite strategy for the primary estimand, there were 63.5% responders in the delgocitinib group and 29.2% responders in the vehicle group, with a difference of 34.3% (CI 13.1% to 55.4%), p=0.0015, based on logistic regression analysis.

The primary and all key secondary endpoints in study 1426 (IGA-CHE TS, HECSI-90, and ≥4 reduction in HESD itch, HESD pain, and HESD score) were evaluated based on a pre-specified testing hierarchy.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Delgocitinib cream was superior to cream vehicle on all key secondary endpoints, based on the confirmatory Bayesian analysis and the additional Frequentist analyses.

Delgocitinib cream demonstrated early onset of action for itch and pain in ≥4-point reduction of HESD itch at Week 1 and HESD pain at Week 2.

The mean change in the quality-of-life measure CDLQI score from baseline to Week 16 was numerically larger for delgocitinib cream than for cream vehicle (LSMean: -5.6 vs -2.9).

Compared to studies in adults, the estimated treatment effect in adolescents was overall more pronounced.

## 3.3. Uncertainties and limitations about favourable effects

None.

## 3.4. Unfavourable effects

In study 1426, there were more reported AEs in the delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g treatment group n=37 (50%) compared to the cream vehicle treatment group, n=8 (33.3%). The most common AEs were in the SOC category of Infections and infestations and were reported for 27 subjects (36.5%) with delgocitinib vs. 7 subjects (29.2%) with vehicle treatment. The most common AEs by PT were 'nasopharyngitis' (reported for 10 subjects (13.5%) with delgocitinib vs. 4 subjects (16.7%) with vehicle); 'upper respiratory tract infection' (reported for 5 subjects with delgocitinib (6.8%) vs. 0 % with vehicle); 'hypersensitivity reactions' (reported for 5 subjects with delgocitinib (6.8%) vs. 1 subject (4.2% subject) with vehicle); 'headache' (reported for 4 subjects with delgocitinib (5.4%) vs. for 1 subject (4.2%) with vehicle); 'application site reactions' (reported for 1 subject with delgocitinib (1.4%) vs. for 1 subject (4.2%) with vehicle); 'SOC gastrointestinal disorders' (reported for 7 subjects (9.5) vs. 1 subject (4.2) with vehicle).

## 3.5. Uncertainties and limitations about unfavourable effects

## 3.7. Benefit-risk assessment and discussion

## 3.7.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

The overall goal when treating CHE is to reduce inflammatory skin lesions and improve the clinical signs and symptoms, including itch and pain. The results of study 1426 demonstrated consistent results across endpoints and robust improvements in CHE signs and symptoms (proportion of subjects with IGA-CHE TS, HECSI-90, HESD itch ≥4-point improvement, HESD pain ≥4-point improvement, and HESD ≥4-point improvement) and numerical improvements in health-related quality of life (change in CDLQI).

From the safety point of view, results of study 1426 showed a relatively benign safety profile in adolescents 12-17 years of age with moderate to severe CHE when treated with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g twice daily for 16 weeks of treatment. The size of the adolescent safety database is quite limited, especially for longer-term exposures. Nevertheless, the safety results were comparable to those in adults. Further, at time of initial MA, the risk of NMSC at long-term use could not be ruled out with sufficient certainty due to a possible role of local immunosuppression in its pathophysiology. Therefore, a general warning advising patients on periodic skin examination in SmPC section 4.4 was deemed warranted by the CHMP and a category 3 PASS, including adolescents, was agreed to further address this important potential risk (see RMP).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 3.7.2. Balance of benefits and risks

Delgocitinib cream was demonstrated to be efficacious in the treatment of moderate to severe CHE in adolescent. The overall effect was consistent across endpoints covering signs and symptoms of CHE and health related quality of life of patients. Beneficial effects were more pronounced in this age group than in adults, for whom delgocitinib cream is already approved for the treatment of moderate to severe CHE.

From the safety point of view, the safety profile is relatively benign when treating adolescents with moderate to severe CHE with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g twice daily after up to 16 weeks of treatment.
