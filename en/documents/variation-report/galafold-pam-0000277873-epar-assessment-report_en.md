## 1. Introduction

On 29 May 2025, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Galafold, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that Study AT1001-036 is a standalone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Migalastat was supplied as 150 mg capsules. Migalastat capsules contained 123 mg migalastat free base, which is equivalent to 150 mg migalastat HCl. Migalastat was administered once every other day (QOD) during the treatment period. Capsules were taken with water at the same time of day during the QOD dosing schedule. The same dose was used for adults and adolescent patients aged 12 to &lt; 18 years and weighing ≥ 45 kg.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## 2.3.1. Introduction

## 2.3.2. Introduction

The MAH submitted a final report for:

Study AT1001-036: A Long-term, Open-label Study to Evaluate the Safety, Pharmacodynamics, and Efficacy of Migalastat in Subjects &gt; 12 Years of Age with Fabry Disease and Amenable GLA Variants. This study included patients that completed study AT1001-020. The PK data from this study served as the basis for a paediatric extension of indication (variation II-29). The final CSR of study AT1001-020 has been assessed in variation II-34.

## 2.3.3. Clinical study

## Study AT1001-036

Long-term, open-label, uncontrolled, multicentre study to evaluate the safety, pharmacodynamics, and efficacy of migalastat treatment in subjects over 12 years of age with Fabry disease and amenable GLA variants. Subjects enrolled in this study following the completion of migalastat Study AT1001-020. Enrolment into Study AT1001-036 immediately followed the completion of Study AT1001-020 in order to maintain continuity of treatment. Assessments performed at the end of treatment visit for Study AT1001-020 were utilised for Visit 1 (baseline) assessments for this study.

## Methods

## Study participants

Subjects who met all of the following criteria were eligible for inclusion into the study:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

1. Male or female subjects, diagnosed with Fabry disease &gt; 12 years of age who completed Study AT1001-020
2. Subject's parent or LAR was willing and able to provide written informed consent and authorisation for use and disclosure of personal health information or research-related health information, and subject provided assent, if applicable
3. If of reproductive potential, both male and female subjects agreed to use a medically accepted method of contraception throughout the duration of the study and for up to 30 days after their last dose of migalastat

Subjects who met any of following criteria were excluded from participating in the study:

1. Subject's last available estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in the previous study was &lt; 60 ml/min/1.73m 2
2. Subject had advanced kidney disease requiring dialysis or kidney transplantation
3. Subject received any investigational/experimental drug, biologic, or device within 30 days before baseline, with the exception of migalastat
4. Subject anticipated starting gene therapy during the study period
5. Subject had any intercurrent illness or condition at Visit 1 that may have precluded the subject from fulfilling the protocol requirements or suggested to the investigator that the potential subject may have an unacceptable risk by participating in this study
6. Subject had a history of allergy or sensitivity to migalastat (including excipients) or other iminosugars (e.g. miglustat, miglitol)
7. Subject required treatment with Replagal (agalsidase alfa) or Fabrazyme (Agalsidase beta)
8. Subject required treatment with Glyset (miglitol) or Zavesca (miglustat)
9. Female subject was pregnant or breast-feeding, or was planning to become pregnant during the study period
10. In the opinion of the investigator, the subject and/or parent or LAR was unlikely or unable to comply with the study requirements

Subjects may have discontinued study drug, withdrawn from the study, or been withdrawn from the study for any reason. The reason for and date of discontinuation or withdrawal were to be recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF) and in source documents. All subjects who discontinued study treatment were encouraged to complete an early termination visit and follow-up safety assessments.

## Treatments

This was an open-label study in which all subjects received migalastat.

Subjects were instructed not to eat for at least 2 hours before and for 2 hours after administration of study drug. Water could be consumed during this period.

Study drug was not to be taken on 2 consecutive days. If a dose was missed entirely for the day, the subject could take the missed dose of study drug only if it was within 12 hours of the time the dose normally was taken. If more than 12 hours had passed, the subject was to resume taking study drug at the next planned dosing day and time according to the every-other-day dosing schedule.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Subjects continued in Study AT1001-036 until reaching the age eligible to receive reimbursed commercial product in their respective country. As such, the duration of treatment varied among subjects.

## Objective(s)

Primary Objective:

- To assess the long-term safety of migalastat treatment in adolescent subjects diagnosed with Fabry disease who had variants in the gene encoding α-galactosidase A (α-Gal A) amenable to treatment with migalastat

Secondary Objectives:

- To characterise the pharmacodynamics (PD) of migalastat in adolescent subjects diagnosed with Fabry disease and who had GLA variants amenable to treatment with migalastat
- To assess the efficacy of migalastat in adolescent subjects diagnosed with Fabry disease and who had GLA variants amenable to treatment with migalastat

## Outcomes/endpoints

Safety endpoints were as follows:

- incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), SAEs, and AEs leading to discontinuation of study drug
- change from baseline in clinical laboratory test results over time
- change from baseline in vital signs over time
- change from baseline in physical examination findings over time
- change from baseline in body weight and height over time
- change from baseline in ECG results over time
- change from baseline in ECHO parameters over time
- change from baseline in Tanner Stage

Efficacy endpoints were as follows:

- change from baseline in eGFR (Schwartz formula)
- change from baseline in urine protein and albumin levels
- change from baseline in LVMi
- change from baseline in FPHPQ scores
- change from baseline in PedsQL scores

The PD endpoint was the change from baseline in plasma levels of lyso-Gb3.

## Sample size

As this was an open-label long-term extension study that enrolled subjects from Study AT1001-020, no formal sample size calculation was performed. The number of subjects enrolled was dependent on the number of eligible subjects completing migalastat treatment in Study AT1001-020 who consented to enroll into Study AT1001-036.

## Efficacy results

## Pharmacodynamic results

Overall, the mean (SD) plasma lyso-Gb3 at baseline was 14.97 (35.603) ng/ml, with a median of 3.63 ng/ml The mean was largely influenced by an elevated result (146 ng/ml) in 1 subject. At Month 24 (n = 8), the mean (SD) change from baseline was 0.05 (1.127) ng/ml.

Mean lyso-Gb3 levels were notably higher at baseline in males (22.16 ng/ml) compared to females (2.98 ng/ml); however, that was driven by the outlier value. The median lyso-Gb3 at baseline was 7.60 ng/ml and 2.55 ng/ml for males and females, respectively. At Month 24, males (n = 4) had increased levels of lyso-Gb3 relative to baseline (mean [SD] = 0.16 [1.708]), while females (n = 4) decreased (mean [SD] = -0.06 [0.132]).

The median lyso-Gb3 at baseline were generally comparable by prior ERT status (3.63 ng/ml for ERTnaïve subjects and 4.69 ng/ml for ERT-experienced). At Month 24, mean (SD) change from baseline in ERT-naïve subjects (n = 5) was 0.49 (1.065) ng/ml, compared to -0.68 (0.944) ng/mL in ERTexperienced subjects (n = 3). As noted above, results for male and ERT-experienced subjects were driven by 1 subject. This subject has a GLA variant of unknown significance that was not seen previously in migalastat studies. The variant was deemed amenable to migalastat when processed through the migalastat amenability assay. The subject's plasma lyso-Gb3 results were not of concern to the investigator who offered that the subject was responding from a clinical perspective and might have a spliced mutation or other intronic mutations. An ad-hoc analysis was performed excluding this subject. In this analysis, the mean (SD) plasma lyso-Gb3 at baseline was 6.23 (7.070) ng/ml, with a median of 3.21 ng/ml. At Month 24 (n = 8), the mean (SD) change from baseline was 0.05 (1.127) ng/ml.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Results were generally comparable by prior ERT status in the ad-hoc analysis excluding the outlier.

## Renal parameters

Per the study protocol, eGFR was calculated using the modified Schwartz formula for creatinine clearance. Estimated glomerular filtration rate was measured throughout the study; however, given the limited number of subjects with measurements at later time points, results presented here focus on change from baseline to Month 24 (n = 8).

Overall, mean eGFR remained within normal range for adolescents throughout the study based on the reference laboratory. The mean (SD) eGFR at baseline for all subjects was 113.7 (16.11) ml/min/1.73m 2 . Females demonstrated greater hyperfiltration at baseline than males, with mean (SD) eGFR (Schwartz formula) of 118.7 (14.09) and 110.7 (17.19) ml/min /1.73m 2 , respectively (Table 4).

At Month 24 (n = 8), the mean (SD) eGFR was 89.4 (10.42) ml/min /1.73m 2 , a mean (SD) change from baseline of -27.8 (8.88) ml/min/1.73 m 2 . At Month 30 and beyond, the number of subjects reduced considerably to 4 and fewer due to subjects aging out from the study and subsequently continuing treatment with commercial migalastat. The observed decrease in mean (SD) eGFR over time is likely representative of hyperfiltration at baseline and normalisation of eGFR over time.

Two males (aged 13 and 15 years) and 2 females (aged 13 and 16 years) had eGFR &lt; 80 ml/min/1.73m 2 at the last post-baseline observation. Three of the 4 subjects were ERT-experienced, and all completed the study and continued treatment with commercial migalastat.

While the Schwartz formula was the standard at the time of study and validated for use in a CLIA lab for paediatric patients, calculation of eGFR was also performed using the CKD-EPI40 equation (Björk, Nyman et al. 2021) as an ad-hoc analysis to understand the changes in eGFR over time in this growing adolescent population. The CKD-EPI40 equation, which is based on sex-specific creatinine growth curves, provides more accurate adjustment due to growth and maturation in the adolescent population and may minimise bias caused by hyperfiltration seen with the Schwartz formula in young adults. Values obtained using the CKD-EPI40 equation yielded comparable results to those using the Schwartz formula (Table 5).

## Cardiac parameters

Left ventricular mass index (LVMi) was assessed as a measure of cardiac impairment in the study subjects. Echocardiogram results were reported by both M-mode and 2D mode imaging.

The M-mode is consistent with previous migalastat studies in adults and allows for a comparative look of the data between adolescents and adults. At baseline and throughout the treatment period, LVMi results were within normal limits for all but 1 subject.

At the end of treatment visit (Day 1459), One subject had abnormal results using both M-mode and 2D mode imaging, which were reported as a TEAE considered unrelated to migalastat by the investigator. All prior LVMi results for the subject were within normal limits.

The LVMi reported by both modes were relatively stable throughout the study (Table 6). At Month 24 (n = 11), the overall mean (SD) change from baseline was 6.01 (9.014) g/m2 as reported by M-mode.

Mean LVMi results by visit were generally similar by sex, prior ERT status, and age group.

## 2.3.4. Discussion on clinical aspects

## Efficacy

In this art 46 procedure, the MAH submitted the results of study AT1001-036, A Long-term, Open-label Study to Evaluate the Safety, Pharmacodynamics, and Efficacy of Migalastat in Subjects &gt; 12 Years of Age with Fabry Disease and Amenable GLA Variants. This study included patients that completed study AT1001-020. The PK data from this study served as the basis for a paediatric extension of indication (II-29). The final CSR of study AT1001-020 has been assessed in variation II-34.

As the data from study AT1001-020 covered one year of observation, the longer-term results from study AT1001-036 are considered of importance to confirm the clinical benefit of migalastat in adolescent patients.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Patients were treated with a dosage conform the SmPC for paediatric patients (of 12 years and older). The study objectives were to assess long term safety and efficacy of migalastat. The corresponding endpoints are suitable for these objectives. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are in line with previous study. In study AT1001-020, 22 patients were enrolled, 21 received migalastat during the study. Sixteen (16) patients rolled over to study AT1001036. Reasons for not enrolling in the LTFU study are not provided in the CSR. The MAH clarified that this was due to the age of the participants at the end of study AT1001-020. If they were above 18 years of age, they continued on commercially available migalastat treatment. As there was no restriction for age in the inclusion criteria of study AT1001-036, this is not understood but not further pursued. Patients were 13-17 years of age at time of enrolment. Half of the 16 enrolled patients were ERT-naïve at the start of study AT1001-020. As the patients in this study were all pre-treated with migalastat in study AT1001-020, it is expected that lyso-Gb3 levels remain relatively constant under continued treatment. This is indeed observed, the data show a continued pharmacodynamic effect on long term treatment with migalastat. At baseline, some patient presented with hyperfiltration, which is a known characteristic in adolescents with Fabry disease and is an early sign of kidney involvement. The median filtration rate was within normal range for adolescents (between 90-120 ml/min). Over the 2-year observation period, large decreases in eGFR were observed, with a mean of -18.5 ml/min/1.73m 2 in the overall population. Although the majority of the patients remained within normal range during the study, the steepness of the decline is concerning. In the responses, the applicant refers to a pooled analysis from study AT1001-020 and AT1001-036. However, the presented numbers and analyses cannot be verified and there seem to be inconsistencies that should be clarified (OC). Four subjects (aged 13 and 16 years) had eGFR &lt; 80 ml/min/1.73m 2 at the last post-baseline observation. This could be seen as a potential lack of efficacy. The MAH has indicated potential concomitant medications that could have influences kidney function in these 4 patients, however, no causality can be concluded. LVMi increased with 6.01 g/m 2 over the 24 months observation period. The effect was more pronounced in males (mean increase of 10.42 g/m 2 ). In previous study AT1001-020, LVMi stayed fairly stable, with a small mean decrease of -3.9 over the 1-year observation period. The observed increase in LVMi is thus not in line with the results of the previous study although the observation period of 1 year was not considered sufficient at the time to conclude. The observed increase of LVMi can be seen as a progression of disease under migalastat treatment and is not reassuring. In the clarification, new analyses were presented that cannot be verified and there seem to be inconsistencies in the data (OC). Data on the FPHPQ is not easily interpreted, as improvements were observed for some items, while decline was observed for others. In addition, the results are not presented in a comprehensive way as results for all items on the questionnaire are reported separately. Some improvements on the PedsQL were observed under migalastat treatment, but data at two-year timepoint is limited. Overall, it seems that long term treatment of migalastat does not lead to stabilisation of the disease in all patients, but that disease progression is observed despite treatment. This is not in accordance with the results from adult Fabry patients, where stabilisation was observed up to 5 years of treatment with migalastat. At the moment the SmPC contains a warning in section 4.4 indicating that clinical signs and symptoms of Fabry disease should be monitored and in case of disease progression, treatment with migalastat should be reconsidered. Dependent on the responses to the questions, a paediatric specific warning should be included and inclusion in section 5.1 could be requested.

## Safety

Overall, the observed safety profile observed was mild and in line with the known safety profile as represented in section 4.8 of the SmPC. No concerning SAE's occurred and there were no deaths in the study. The MAH states that there are no new safety findings observed in the study. This is not fully agreed. Increased AST/ALT was observed in 3 patients. In the assessment of the study results of AT1001-020 (variation II-34), one patient presented with increased AST/ALT. The MAH was requested to include aminotransferase increases in section 4.8 of the SmPC. The MAH did not agree and argued that all the observed increased were from 1 patient, mild of nature, and recovered without consequences for migalastat treatment. However, the results of study AT1001-036 warranted further discussion on the potential inclusion of AST/ALT increased in section 4.8. In the responses, the MAH has analysed the reported cases from clinical studies, reports in literature and adverse event databases. It is agreed that causality cannot be established as generally, the events occur a very long time after treatment initiation. Inclusion in section 4.8 is not warranted.

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

The data from the long-term study AT1001-036 is of interest to assess long term efficacy and safety of long term migalastat treatment in paediatric patients with Fabry disease.

Observed efficacy is not in line with that observed in adults. Further discussion by the MAH is requested (see RfSI). An update of the SmPC (section 4.8, 5.1 and possibly 4.4) might be necessary via a type II variation after conclusion of the current procedure.

## MAH response

While the EMA has highlighted data points in Study AT1001-036 thought to indicate disease progression, further analyses conducted by Amicus of estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and left ventricular mass index (LVMi) data from both the AT1001-020 and AT1001-036 studies, along with global safety database review, confirms that there is no evidence of lack of efficacy or disease progression while on long-term migalastat treatment. Study AT1001-036 was a long-term, open-label, single-arm study designed to evaluate the safety, pharmacodynamics (PD), and efficacy of migalastat in subjects aged 12 years and older with Fabry disease and amenable GLA variants. Subjects enrolled in Study AT1001-036 immediately after completing Study AT1001-020, which involved 12 months of migalastat treatment. The primary objective of Study AT1001-036 was to assess the long-term safety of migalastat. Secondary objectives

included evaluation of PD and efficacy, with eGFR and LVMi serving as key efficacy endpoints.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The study demonstrated that long-term migalastat treatment was generally safe and well-tolerated in adolescent subjects with Fabry disease. Results remained consistent with the known safety profile of migalastat, with no new or unexpected safety findings observed in this population. No subject was considered to have experienced a loss of efficacy or signs of disease progression during the study. Of the 16 subjects enrolled in Study AT1001-036, one withdrew consent, and the remaining 15 completed the study. Of note, all 15 were considered by the principal investigators (PIs) as having received satisfactory treatment and subsequently continued Fabry disease therapy with commercial migalastat. An overview of efficacy in renal and cardiac parameters is summarized below, providing additional context for results obtained in Study AT1001-036. eGFR Overall, mean eGFR remained within normal range for adolescents throughout the study based on the reference laboratory values. The mean (standard deviation [SD]) eGFRCKD-EPI40 (CKD-EPI with age and creatinine values adjusted to 40 years for patients &lt; 40 years of age) at baseline for all subjects was 110.75 (8.101) ml/min/1.73m 2 . At Month 24 (n = 8), the mean (SD) eGFR was 92.21 (12.483) ml/min/1.73m 2 , a mean (SD) change from baseline of -18.50 (9.942) ml/min/1.73m 2 . Amicus conducted a thorough review for the 4 subjects specifically highlighted in the Assessment Report with eGFR values below 80 ml/min/1.73 m 2 in Study AT1001-036. Of these, for 3 subjects, ongoing treatment of ibuprofen, which can cause interstitial nephritis, is assessed as a confounder (Ibuprofen 400 mg Summary of Product Characteristics [SmPC], 2025). Additionally for one subject, carbamazepine, which can cause renal impairment/failure, is assessed as a confounder (Tetgretol 100 mg SmPC, 2025). Three out of 4 subjects also received the BioNTech/Pfizer COVID-19 vaccine, which may lead to various forms of glomerular disease after the first or second dose of vaccination and is assessed as a confounding factor across these 3 subjects (Umezawa, Suzuki et al., 2025). Importantly, none of the 4 subjects with eGFR &lt; 80 ml/min/1.73m 2 at the last post-baseline observation reported any renal-related adverse events (AEs) or events indicative of lack of efficacy during the study. In summary, all 15 subjects who completed Study AT1001-036 were considered by the PIs as having satisfactory treatment and subsequently continued treatment for Fabry disease with commercial migalastat (Galafold). Renal parameters - including eGFR, urine protein, and urine albumin - remained within the normal range and were largely stable throughout Study AT1001-020 and Study AT1001036. These data indicate no evidence of clinically meaningful renal decline or disease progression. LVMi In Study AT1001-036, a total of 16 subjects had baseline LVMi measurements; 10 and 8 subjects completed LVMi measurement at 12 and 24 months, respectively. For the overall group, the mean change from baseline in LVMi was 9.41 g/m 2 at 12 months and 6.01 g/m 2 at 24 months as reported by M-mode. The standard deviations for these values were relatively high, 10.016 and 9.014, respectively, indicating high variability. Similar changes and variability were observed for LVMi by 2DMode. Due to the lack of a control group and relatively small sample size, the mean changes in LVMi could be chance findings or the result of outlying values. All subjects except 1 had normal LVMi values throughout the study. One subject had 1 abnormal LVMi value at the end of treatment visit (year 4). In addition, there were no clinically relevant changes in mean ejection fraction, mean posterior wall thickness, or mean septal wall thickness through Month 24 for the group overall. To further characterize any potential long-term changes in LVMi and provide a complete view of cardiac outcomes throughout the entire migalastat treatment period, a supplementary pooled analysis with Study AT1001-020 data was conducted on 8 subjects who had echocardiography M-mode evaluations through the 24-month observation period of Study AT1001-036, representing at least 36 months of continuous treatment from initial baseline. The results of this supplementary analysis are shown in Table 1. It should be noted that the subjects in these studies had aged 3 years since their baseline assessments in Study AT1001-020 and would be expected to have continued growth and development of the heart throughout adolescence. The results indicate that the group of 8 subjects (7 had baseline echocardiograms) had negative mean changes (indicating reductions) in LVMi at all time points up to Month 36 (Month 24 in Study AT1001-036). In the male subgroup, the point estimates of mean change from baseline in LVMi were positive for some time points, however, the sample size for this group is small and the 95% CIs contain zero. Given the small sample size and variability in LVMi changes, as reflected by wide CIs and high standard deviations, chance findings and outliers may have influenced the results. Furthermore, the clinical assessment of LVMi in children and adolescents differs from that in adults. Given the rapid and variable growth that occurs during these ages, linear curves cannot be expected and absolute changes in LVMi are less informative or clinically meaningful. Rather, values &lt; 95% CI can be considered normal, and those &gt; 95% CI can be considered abnormal (Khoury, Mitsnefes et al., 2009). In conclusion, in Study AT1001-036, all but 1 subject maintained normal LVMi values throughout the entire treatment period. Other echocardiographic parameters - including ejection fraction, posterior wall thickness, and septal wall thickness - showed no clinically relevant changes over 24 months. Together, these data indicate no evidence of cardiac structural pathology. A supplementary pooled analysis of subjects with 36 months of treatment from Studies AT1001-020 and AT1001-036 showed negative mean changes from baseline in LVMi. This approach offers a more longitudinal view of cardiac outcomes over the full treatment duration, compared to evaluating Study AT1001-036 in isolation. In addition to the review of the clinical dataset, Amicus conducted a review of the global safety database to identify all cases indicating lack of efficacy in the Galafold-treated adolescent age group under the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) system organ class (SOC) \"General disorders and administration site conditions\" (Data Lock Point [DLP]: 31 Jul 2025). One spontaneous report was identified from this search concerning a patient. However, this case was excluded from further analysis as the patient's medical history of generalized body pain was considered as a confounder for the event of \"the medicine wasn't helping the patient as the patient continued to suffer from pain throughout her body\" (MedDRA preferred term [PT] \"drug ineffective\"). Based on this review, Amicus concluded that there are no confirmed cases indicative of lack of efficacy associated with Galafold use in the adolescent population.

Taken together, further analyses of eGFR and LVMi data from Studies AT1001-020 and AT1001-036, along with global safety database review, show no evidence of lack of efficacy or disease progression while on migalastat treatment.

## Summary of the MAH responses to 2 nd Request for supplementary information

To address the question regarding renal function trends in adolescent Fabry patients treated with migalastat, supplementary analyses using pooled data from Study AT1001-020 and its open-label extension (OLE) Study AT1001-036 are presented. These analyses aimed to provide a more comprehensive and physiologically appropriate interpretation of estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) changes over time, particularly in the context of growth and puberty. The following sections outline the rationale, methodology, and data sources supporting these analyses. Variability in Creatinine and eGFR: Estimated glomerular filtration rate formulas based on serum creatinine (sCr) measurements can yield fluctuating results due to factors unrelated to actual changes in kidney function. When interpreting eGFR data, it is essential to recognize the inherent variability in sCr, which directly affects eGFR calculations (Hilderink, van der Linden et al. 2018). This variability arises from 2 main sources: analytical and biological. Analytical variability, which reflects the precision of laboratory measurements, was mitigated in Amicus studies by utilizing the same central laboratory for both studies. Biological variability, on the other hand, encompasses intra-individual changes influenced by factors such as diet, physical activity, circadian rhythm, and hydration status. These physiological influences can cause transient shifts in creatinine clearance, leading to apparent changes in eGFR values even when renal function remains stable. Furthermore, medications that affect creatinine secretion and medical conditions impacting creatinine production add complexity to eGFR interpretation. Recent literature (Hilderink, van der Linden et al. 2018) suggests that overall variability in eGFR can be up to 20% due to these sources of variation as demonstrated in Annex to the Response: eGFR Subject Data. Spaghetti plots of eGFR data over time according to age at visit in Figure 1 illustrate the variabilities in eGFR data within the subjects. This underscores the importance of interpreting eGFR trends over time, such as annualized slopes, rather than relying solely on isolated point estimates, particularly in adolescent populations where growth and maturation further influence renal function markers. Effect of Puberty:

Assessing eGFR in pediatric populations poses greater challenges than in adults due to additional physiological variables beyond race and sex. In particular, puberty introduces dynamic changes in growth and development that significantly influence muscle mass, which directly affects sCr levels - the basis for most GFR estimation formulas. Changes in skeletal muscle mass, body weight, and height during adolescence can alter sCr independently of renal function (Stevens, Coresh et al. 2006; Schwartz and Furth, 2007; Schwartz and Work, 2009; Hogg, Furth et al. 2003). In the present study population, substantial growth and developmental changes were observed, as demonstrated in Annex to the Response: eGFR Subject Data, especially during long-term follow-up. Rapid increases in muscle mass and anthropometric parameters likely contributed to the upward trend in creatinine levels, with a more pronounced rise in males during adolescence compared to females. For example, one subject at enrolment in Study AT1001-020 grew at the end of treatment (Month 57). During this period, sCr increased from 44 μmol/l to 80 μmol/l, which likely contributed to the observed decline in eGFRCKD-EPI40 from 116.0 to 88.4 ml/min/1.73m 2 (Combined Studies AT1001-020/036).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

During the development of the Study AT1001-036 clinical study report (CSR) and manuscript, it became evident that the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration 40 (CKD-EPI40) equation was more appropriate than the Schwartz formula for this adolescent study population, as it incorporates sex-specific creatinine growth curves. As described in the Study AT1001-036 CSR: \"While the Schwartz formula was the standard at the time of study and validated for use in a CLIA lab for pediatric patients, calculation of eGFR was also performed using the CKD-EPI40 equation (Björk, Nyman et al. 2021) as an ad-hoc analysis to understand the changes in eGFR over time in this growing adolescent population. The CKD-EPI40 equation, which is based on sex-specific creatinine growth curves, provides more accurate adjustment due to growth and maturation in the adolescent population and may minimize bias caused by hyperfiltration seen with the Schwartz formula in young adults.\"

This approach supports a more reliable interpretation of renal function in adolescents by accounting for the physiological changes associated with puberty.

## Interpreting Longitudinal Trends: Slope Versus Change from Baseline

Given the inherent variability in sCr and eGFR, particularly in pediatric populations undergoing puberty, relying solely on change from baseline at discrete time points may lead to misleading conclusions. To more accurately characterize renal function trajectory and account for physiological variability, all available eGFR data from both Study AT1001-020 and its OLE Study AT1001-036 were analyzed across the entire migalastat treatment period.

Furthermore, treatment durations varied among participants, making slope-based metrics (ie, annualized rate of change in eGFR) a more robust and representative measure of renal function over time. This approach offers greater sensitivity to longitudinal trends and helps mitigate confounding effects such as growth-related changes and visit-to-visit variability.

Supporting this methodology, the 2018 National Kidney Foundation (NKF), US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) workshop analyses (Inker, Heerspink et al. 2019; Greene, Ying et al. 2019) demonstrated that eGFR slope provides a more precise and unbiased estimate of renal trajectory than comparisons at a single time point, reinforcing its value in evaluating treatment effects in growing adolescent populations.

## Summary:

The supplementary analyses presented here offer a more physiologically appropriate and longitudinal interpretation of renal function in adolescent Fabry patients treated with migalastat.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

By pooling all available eGFR data from Studies AT1001-020 (ASPIRE) and AT1001-036 (OLE), and applying the CKD-EPI40 equation, which accounts for sex-specific creatinine growth curves, the analyses provide a more accurate reflection of renal function over time, particularly in the context of puberty-related variability. The use of slope-based metrics (annualized rate of change) rather than isolated point estimates mitigates confounding factors such as growth and differences in treatment duration. For the overall population (n = 20), the median eGFRCKD-EPI40 slope was -1.0 ml/min/1.73 m²/year, with a 95% confidence interval of [-3.5, 2.0], which are consistent with those reported in adolescents without Fabry disease. Taken together, these findings support the conclusion that there was no clinically meaningful decline in renal function over the course of treatment in Study AT1001020 and Study AT1001-036.

## Assessment of MAH response

The MAH has provided further clarification on the supplementary analysis of study AT1001-020 and AT1001-036. A discussion was provided indicating that the CKD-EPI40 formula was more reliable for assessing renal function in adolescents (it should be noted that this was demonstrated for healthy children and not for patients suffering from Fabry disease). The difficulty to reliably estimate eGFR in adolescents is acknowledged and the use of the CKD-EPI40 equation might be more suitable in this patient population. However, no consensus seems to exist in the field which formula is most reliable in this patient population and in general, the Schwartz formula is the standard also in recent published literature. It is agreed with the MAH that eGFR slope is the most useful method to express renal function over time. Individual slopes were presented as well as a calculated mean slope. It is noticed that the mean is quite heavily influenced by patients with shorter follow up duration that showed stable renal function. It is clear from the individual slopes, that especially for the patients that were followed up for a longer duration, up till 5 years, there is a clear decrease in renal function over time. Especially in females, this could be a worrying signal. Published literature on expected decline in renal function in adolescents if left untreated, or when treated with ERT is quite fragmented. Study cohorts are often small and methods for measuring eGFR vary. It is therefore challenging to properly contextualize the data currently presented. The results presented in section 5.1 of the SmPC indicate a larger decline in eGFR (-1.6 (15.4) ml/min/1.73m 2 (n=19) than currently presented with the pooled data. The estimation in the SmPC is therefore on the conservative side and it is agreed with the applicant that the long term data in a handful of patients are not sufficiently robust to warrant an update in the SmPC text. The current SmPC 4.4 text includes a warning to frequently monitor patients that are switched to migalastat and to switch to other treatment option in case a relevant clinical deterioration is observed. At the moment, this warning is considered sufficient to mitigate the potential lack of efficacy of migalastat in some paediatric patients.

Conclusion: Issue resolved.
