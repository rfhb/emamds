## 1. Introduction

On 4 June 2026, the MAH submitted the completed paediatric study B7471024 for Prevenar 20, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

These data are also submitted as part of the post-authorisation measure.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that study B7471024 is a stand-alone study. The purpose of the study was to assess the immunogenicity (after a 3-dose infant primary series and after the toddler booster) of 20vPnC in infants (≥42 to ≤84 days of age) and to provide key safety data to support licensure in this population in India and Taiwan. Prevenar 13 (13vPnC) served as control vaccine.

In the EU, a pediatric indication for Prevenar 20 was approved in 2024 (EMEA/H/C/005451/II/0012).

No amendments to the Product Information are being submitted as part of this P46 procedure.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the studies

The 20vPnC vaccine is a sterile liquid suspension for intramuscular administration of capsular polysaccharide antigens of S pneumoniae serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 8, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 18C, 19A, 19F, 22F, 23F, and 33F each individually conjugated to the nontoxic variant of diphtheria toxin CRM197, as carrier protein.

Each prefilled syringe of 20vPnC is designed to deliver 2.2 µg of each serotype-specific polysaccharide conjugate, except for 6B, which is 4.4 µg/dose. The vaccine is formulated in succinate buffer containing sodium chloride and polysorbate 80, at pH 5.8, and containing aluminum phosphate at 0.25 mg/mL aluminum as an adjuvant. The excipients (polysorbate 80, succinate buffer, sodium chloride) and aluminum phosphate components have been conserved between 13vPnC and 20vPnC.

Each 1-mL syringe contains a 0.5-mL dose of vaccine, supplied as a single-dose injection for parenteral administration, with no preservative.

## Clinical study

Study B7471024 - A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind Trial to Evaluate the Safety and Immunogenicity of a 20 Valent Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine Given in a Series of 3 Infant Doses and 1 Toddler Dose in Infants in India and Taiwan

## Description

Study B7471024 was a multicentre study that investigated the safety and immunogenicity of 3 primary infant doses of 20vPnC (administered to subjects ≥42 to ≤84 days of age) and a toddler booster vaccination. According to local recommendations, the infant doses were given at approximately 6, 10, and 14 weeks of age in India and in Taiwan, the infant doses were given at approximately 8, 16, and 24 weeks of age. All toddler doses were given at approximately 12 to 15 months of age.

The study was conducted at 6 sites India and 5 sites in Taiwan.

Safety and immunogenicity data from all participants enrolled at one of the India sites (Site 1012) were excluded from the analysis presented in the CSR body due to a quality event identified in an audit.

The study was conducted between 02 May 2024 and 20 September 2025 (India cohort) and between 16 September 2022 and 22 May2024 (Taiwan cohort).

## Methods

## Study participants

Approximately 540 healthy male or female infants ≥42 to ≤84 days of age in India (approximately 350 infants) and Taiwan (approximately 190 infants) were planned to be enrolled.

Written informed consent was obtained from the subject's parent(s) / legal guardian(s).

Subjects with a history of severe adverse reaction associated with a vaccine and/or severe allergic reaction (eg, anaphylaxis) to any component of investigational product or any diphtheria toxoidcontaining vaccine were excluded from the study. In addition, subjects with history of seizure and immunosuppressed subjects were excluded.

## Treatments

Study participants were randomised (1:1) to receive either 20vPnC or 13vPnC. The infants were to receive vaccine according to a schedule of 3 infant doses and a toddler dose. Participants received 1 dose of 20vPnC or 13vPnC intramuscularly by injecting 0.5 mL into the anterolateral thigh muscle of the left leg, at each vaccination visit (Visits 1, 2, 3, and 5).

## Objectives

The primary immunogenicity objective was to describe the immune responses of 20vPnC in healthy infants in India by investigating IgG GMCs for the 20vPnC serotypes at 1 month after Dose 4

The secondary immunogenicity objectives were to further describe the immune response by examining IgG GMCs and the percentages of participants with predefined IgG concentrations for the 20vPnC serotypes by country 1 month after Dose 3 and Dose 4, respectively.

The primary safety objective was to describe the safety profile (prompted local and systemic events, AEs, SAEs) of 20vPnC in participants receiving at least 1 dose.

## Immunogenicity endpoints

Approximately 540 infants (350 in India and 190 in Taiwan) are planned to be randomized. Assuming a non-evaluable rate of 10% at most, the expected number of evaluable participants is 243 (158 in India and 85 in Taiwan in each vaccine group).

A precision measure for a given serotype-specific IgG GMC is defined as the relative distance from either limit of the 2-sided 95% CI for the GMC to the GMC, calculated as the percentage of the upper confidence limit of the GMC relative to the GMC ([upper limit of CI/GMC] × 100). A precision measure of 120% for a serotype means that the upper 95% confidence limit of the GMC is 20% higher than the GMC for that serotype.

For the primary immunogenicity objective, with 158 evaluable India participants in the 20vPnC group, the upper 95% confidence limits of the IgG GMCs would be 13% (serotype 7F) to 20% (serotype 23F) higher than the corresponding GMCs among all 20 serotypes. For Taiwan participants in the 20vPnC group, the upper 95% confidence limits of the IgG GMCs would be 18% (serotype 7F) to 29% (serotype 23F) higher than the corresponding GMCs among all 20 serotypes.

The evaluable sample size will allow estimation of the proportion of participants with predefined serotype-specific IgG concentrations to within 2% to 11% precision within each vaccine group (across both India and Taiwan subpopulations).

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

All participants were randomized to receive study intervention using an IRT system (IWR). The site personnel (study coordinator or specified designee) was required to enter or select information including but not limited to the user's ID and password, the protocol number, and the participant number.

The site personnel was then provided with a vaccine assignment, randomization number, and DU or container number when study intervention is being supplied via the IRT system. The IRT system provides a confirmation report containing the participant number, randomization number, and DU or container number assigned. The confirmation report must be stored in the site's files.

The study will be participant and investigator blinded. Sponsor personnel directly involved in evaluating participant data will be blinded to vaccine assignment until the first analysis of study data for participants in that country (Taiwan or India) following the principles outlined in the International Council for Harmonisation E9 guideline on Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials. A data blinding plan will be created to describe the blinding requirements and unblinding events. Laboratory personnel performing the immunologic assays will be blinded until all assays have been completed and assay results finalized.

At the initiation of the study, the investigator site will be instructed on the method for breaking the blind for an individual participant. The method will be an electronic process. Blinding codes should be broken only in exceptional circumstances when knowledge of the actual treatment code is absolutely essential for further management of the participant. Investigators are encouraged to discuss with a member of the study team if they believe that unblinding is necessary. ## Statistical Methods

The primary safety objective was evaluated by descriptive summary statistics for local reactions, systemic events, and AEs (including SAEs). AEs were categorized according to the MedDRA.

The primary objective was evaluated by descriptive summary statistics for serotype-specific IgG GMCs for 20vPnC serotypes 1 month after Dose 4. The secondary immunogenicity objectives were evaluated by descriptive summary statistics by country for IgG GMCs and percentages of participants with predefined serotype-specific IgG concentration for the 20vPnC serotypes and 1 month after Dose 3 and 1 month after Dose 4.

## Results

## Participant flow

In India (excluding Site 1012), 251 participants were randomized; 241 (96.0%) participants completed all 3 infant doses, 240 (95.6%) participants completed the follow-up visit after Dose 3, 226 (90.0%) participants received all 4 doses, and 224 (89.2%) participants completed all visits per protocol. A total of 27 (10.8%) participants were withdrawn from the trial due to: withdrawal by parent/guardian (7), protocol deviation (7, participants received prohibited vaccines), lost to follow-up (6), no longer meeting eligibility criteria (6), and physician decision (1).

In Taiwan, 190 participants were randomized; 188 (98.9%) participants completed all 3 infant doses, 187 (98.4%) participants completed the follow-up visit after Dose 3, 184 (96.8%) participants received all 4 doses, and 182 (95.8%) participants completed all visits per protocol. A total of 8 (4.2%) participants were withdrawn from the trial due to: no longer meeting eligibility criteria (5), withdrawal by parent/guardian (2), and AE (1, COVID-19). Of the 5 participants no longer meeting eligibility criteria, 3 were related to caregiver(s) no longer agreeing to study interventions (refusing blood collection) and 2 were due to the family moving away from treatment area. The 1 withdrawal due to an AE of COVID-19 was due to that participant receiving immunoglobulin treatment for COVID-related neurologic symptoms.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

Prevenar 20 (20vPnC) is approved in the EU for active immunisation for the prevention of invasive disease, pneumonia, and acute otitis media caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae in infants, children, and adolescents from 6 weeks to less than 18 years of age and active immunisation for the prevention of invasive disease and pneumonia caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae in individuals 18 years of age and older.

The study submitted with this P46 procedure (Study B7471024) was a stand-alone study to support licensure in India and Thailand. The study was not part of the PIP (EMEA-002330-PIP01-18-M03) that supported EU approval. In the EU, the pediatric indication for Prevenar 20 was approved in 2024 (EMEA/H/C/005451/II/0012).

No amendments to the Product Information have been submitted as part of this P46 procedure.

## Study Design &amp; conduct

Study B7471024 was a Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, multicentre study that investigated the safety and immunogenicity of 3 primary infant doses of 20vPnC (administered to subjects ≥42 to ≤84 days of age) and a toddler booster vaccination (at 12 to 15 months of age) in India and Taiwan. Subjects were randomized 1:1 to receive either 20vPnC or 13vPnC (control). Due to local recommendations, the infant immunisation schedules varied. In India, infant doses were given at approximately 6, 10, and 14 weeks of age. In Taiwan, the infant doses were given at approximately 8, 16, and 24 weeks of age. Therefore, infant vaccination in Taiwan usually starts 2 weeks later and intervals between infant doses are prolonged in Taiwan with 8 instead of 4 weeks. Comparison between schedules may shed some light on possible differences in immunogenicity/safety.

Currently, the EU SmPC recommends the following (3+1) primary infant immunisation schedule: The first dose at 6 weeks - 2 months of age. Dose 2 and 3 should be administered with intervals of at least 4 weeks. The 4th (booster) dose is recommended between 11 and 15 months of age.

## IgG GMCs 1 month after Dose 3

In India, IgG GMCs ranged from 0.39 µg/mL (serotype 6B) to 2.63 µg/mL (serotype 19F) in the 20vPnC group, and from 0.61 µg/mL (serotype 6B) to 2.96 µg/mL (serotype 19F) in the 13vPnC group. ## IgG GMCs 1 month after Dose 4

In India, IgG GMCs against the 13 serotypes ranged from 1.19 μg/mL (serotype 3) to 13.65 μg/mL (serotype 6A) in the 20vPnC group, and from 1.83 μg/mL (serotype 3) to 23.45 μg/mL (serotype 6A) in the 13vPnC group. Although comparison of data collected at different sites in India and Taiwan should be interpreted with caution, it was quite apparent that generally higher titres were obtained across serotypes in Taiwan where the interval between doses was 8 weeks compared to 4 weeks in India (samples were analysed in the same central laboratory in USA). Indeed, greater intervals between vaccine doses could increase the overall effect of vaccination from an immunological perspective.

## Percent of participants with predefined pneumococcal IgG concentration ≥ 0.35 µg/ml

As secondary analysis, the MAH evaluated also the percent of participants reaching the WHO threshold (pneumococcal IgG concentration ≥ 0.35 µg/ml) against vaccine serotypes. The analysis also showed overall comparable immunogenicity between vaccine groups for the 13 shared serotypes and generally higher proportions of subjects reaching the threshold after vaccination in Taiwan compared to India, particularly after Dose 3. For the 13 matched serotypes, the percentages of participants with predefined serotype specific IgG concentrations 1 month after Dose 3 were in India ≥76.5% in the 20vPnC group and ≥85.1% in the 13vPnC group (≥90.2% and ≥97.1% 1 month after Dose 4). In Taiwan, the percentages after 20vPnC or 13vPnC Dose 3 were ≥97.8% and ≥94.7%, respectively (≥95.5% and ≥97.8% 1 month after Dose 4). Therefore, also the results of WHO threshold analysis suggest a possible benefit for prolonged primary vaccination intervals. However, as Study B7471024 was not designed to compare vaccination schedules, this could also be a chance finding. Additional studies are needed to clarify whether prolongation of primary vaccination intervals can ameliorate serotype-specific pneumococcal immunity.

## Solicited adverse events

Prompted adverse reactions were recorded for 7 days after each vaccination. In India, the percentages of participants with prompted local reactions were comparable in the 20vPnC (32.2% - 63.7%) and 13vPnC (39.8% - 70.4%) groups after each dose. The most frequently reported local reaction was pain at the injection site (20vPnC: 32.2% - 58.1%; 13vPnC: 35.2% - 68.0%). In both vaccine groups, most local reactions were mild or moderate and resolved within one week. The percentages of participants with prompted systemic events were comparable in the 20vPnC (28.0% - 79.8%) and 13vPnC (25.0% - 80.0%) groups after each dose. The most frequently reported systemic events were irritability (20vPnC: 18.6% - 66.1%; 13vPnC: 19.4% - 67.2%) and drowsiness (20vPnC: 6.8% - 32.3%; 13vPnC: 6.5% - 33.6%). The percentages of participants with fever (≥38.0°C) were comparable in the 20vPnC (1.7% to 18.5%) and 13vPnC (2.8% to 15.2%) groups. High Fever (&gt;38.9°C) occurred rarely and predominately after Dose 1 (20vPnC: 1.6%; 13vPnC: 0.8%). Most systemic events were mild or moderate and resolved within one week. In Taiwan, the percentages of participants with prompted local reactions were comparable in the 20vPnC (29.8% - 43.0%) and 13vPnC (25.5% - 38.5%) groups after each dose. The most frequently reported local reaction was swelling (20vPnC: 9.6% - 27.8%; 13vPnC: 15.6% - 30.2%). Most local reactions were mild or moderate and resolved within one week. The percentages of participants with prompted systemic events were comparable in the 20vPnC (50.0% - 74.2%) and 13vPnC (72.9% - 57.4%) groups after each dose. The most frequently reported systemic events were irritability (20vPnC: 31.1% - 43.0%; 13vPnC: 35.1% - 46.9%) and drowsiness (20vPnC: 23.3% - 52.1%; 13vPnC: 33.0% - 50.0%). The percentages of participants with fever (≥38.0°C) were comparable in the 20vPnC (4.3% - 15.6%) and 13vPnC (6.3% - 13.8%) groups. High Fever (&gt;38.9°C) occurred rarely (20vPnC: up to 1.1%;13vPnC: up to 2.1%). Most systemic events were mild or moderate and resolved within one week.

## Conclusion

In summary, Study B7471024 showed a comparable safety profile of 20vPnC and 13vPnC and no new safety signals were identified. Antibody responses against serotypes shared between vaccines were slightly lower after 20vPnC compared to 13vPnC (as already observed in previous studies) but robust responses against the 7 additional serotypes were only elicited by 20vPnC. No amendments to the Product Information are considered necessary following this P46 procedure.
