## 1. NAME OF THE MEDICINAL PRODUCT

Sepalna 20 mg hard capsules

## 2. QUALITATIVE AND QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITION

Each hard capsule contains 20 mg senaparib.

For the full list of excipients, see section 6.1.

## 3. PHARMACEUTICAL FORM

Hard capsule (capsule).

White hard capsule of approximately 23.5 mm in length containing white to off white powder.

## 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

Sepalna is indicated as monotherapy for the maintenance treatment of adult patients with advanced (FIGO Stages III and IV) epithelial high-grade ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer who are in response (complete or partial) following completion of first-line platinum-based chemotherapy.

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration

Treatment with Sepalna should be initiated and supervised by a physician experienced in the use of anticancer medicinal products.

## Posology

The recommended dose is 100 mg senaparib (five 20 mg capsules), once daily.

Patients should start the maintenance treatment with Sepalna no later than 8 weeks after completion of their final dose of the platinum containing regimen.

## Duration of treatment

Patients can continue treatment until radiological disease progression, unacceptable toxicity or completion of 2 years treatment.

## Missed dose

If the patient misses a dose by more than 4 hours of the time it is usually taken, the patient should not take the missed dose and should take the next dose at the regularly scheduled time.

Dose adjustments for adverse reactions The recommended dose modifications for adverse reactions are listed in Tables 1 and 2.

Dose adjustments for co-administration with CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors (see sections 4.4 and 4.5)

Concomitant use of strong or moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors is not recommended, and alternative agents should be considered.

If concomitant administration with a strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor cannot be avoided, the recommended Sepalna dose reduction is 20 mg once daily.

If concomitant administration with a moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor cannot be avoided, the recommended Sepalna dose reduction is 40 mg once daily. Subsequent dose reduction to 20 mg once daily may be considered with moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors based on tolerability.

Caution should be made when senaparib is co-administered with weak CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors.

Patients receiving concomitant treatment with CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors should be closely monitored for adverse reactions.

## Special populations

## Renal impairment

No dose adjustment is needed for patients with mild to moderate renal impairment. Senaparib is not recommended to be used in patients with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease undergoing haemodialysis and should only be used in these patients if the potential benefit outweighs the risk. If used, patients should be monitored closely (see section 5.2).

## Hepatic impairment

No dose adjustment is needed in patients with mild hepatic impairment. Senaparib is not recommended to be used in patients with moderate and severe hepatic impairment as safety and pharmacokinetics have not been studied in these patients. It should only be used in patients with moderate and severe hepatic impairment if the potential benefit outweighs the risk. If use is considered necessary, patients should be carefully monitored (see section 5.2).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Elderly

No adjustment in starting dose is required for elderly patients from 65 to 84 years of age. There are no clinical data in patients aged 85 or over (see section 5.2).

## Paediatric population

There is no relevant use of senaparib in the paediatric population in the treatment of ovarian cancer, fallopian tube cancer and peritoneal cancer. The safety and efficacy of senaparib in children and adolescents below 18 years of age have not yet been established. No data are available.

## Method of administration

Sepalna is for oral use. The capsules should be swallowed whole with water. They should not be chewed or crushed.

They should be taken at least one hour before or two hours after the ingestion of food.

## 4.3 Contraindications

Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1.

Breast-feeding (see section 4.6).

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

## Haematological toxicity

During treatment with Sepalna, events of myelosuppression (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, and lymphopenia, see section 4.8) have been reported, including one fatal case of bone marrow failure.

Testing complete blood count before treatment with Sepalna is recommended (see section 4.2). Sepalna should not be started until patients have recovered from haematological toxicity caused by prior anti-tumour therapy (absolute neutrophil count should recover to ≥1.5 x 10 9 /L, platelet count should recover to ≥75 x 10 9 /L, and haemoglobin should recover to ≥10 g/dL).

Testing complete blood counts is recommended biweekly for the first three months, followed by monthly monitoring for the next 3 months of treatment, and periodically after this time, to monitor for clinically significant changes in any haematologic parameter during treatment. Patients with low body weight/BMI, low baseline platelet values and hypoalbuminemia may be at increased risk of haematological toxicity and closely monitoring is required.

For haematologic toxicity-related events, the dose should be adjusted timely (see section 4.2). For severe or transfusion-dependent haematologic toxicity, treatment should be interrupted and relevant haematologic testing should be performed. Bone marrow analysis and / or blood cytogenetic analysis are recommended if some blood parameters remain clinically abnormal after 4 weeks of dose interruption.

## Myelodysplastic syndrome / acute myeloid leukaemia

Myelodysplastic syndrome / acute myeloid leukaemia (MDS / AML), including cases with fatal outcome, have been reported in patients who received senaparib (see section 4.8). The duration of therapy with senaparib in patients who developed MDS/AML varied from 392 days to 1007 days.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

If haematologic toxicity does not recover to ≤CTCAE Grade 1 for a long time (&gt;4 weeks), or MDS / AML is suspected, the patient should be further consulted by a haematologist. If MDS / AML is confirmed during the treatment, Sepalna must be discontinued immediately, and appropriate treatment should be given.

## QT interval prolongation

Sepalna should be used with caution in patients at risk of QT prolongation and arrhythmias, including patients with electrolyte disturbances, genetic predisposition, co-prescription of QT-prolonging medicinal products, or intermittent clinical conditions predisposing to hypokalaemia. Periodic monitoring of electrocardiograms and serum electrolytes (potassium, magnesium) during treatment in patients at risk should be considered.

## Embryofoetal toxicity / pregnancy / contraception

Senaparib may cause embryo and foetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman based on its mechanism of action (PARP inhibition).

It should not be used during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential who are not using effective contraception. Women of childbearing potential are required to have a pregnancy test before treatment to rule out pregnancy. Given uncertainties whether senaparib may reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives, women using systemically acting hormonal contraceptives should add a barrier method during treatment and for at least 6 months after the last dose of senaparib (see sections 4.5 and 4.6).

## Interactions

Concomitant use of senaparib with strong or moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inducers is not recommended. In the event that a patient already receiving senaparib requires treatment with a strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducer, the prescriber should be aware that the efficacy of senaparib may be substantially reduced (see section 4.5).

Concomitant use of senaparib with strong or moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors is not recommended. In the event that a patient already receiving senaparib requires treatment with a strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors, senaparib dose may be reduced (see sections 4.2 and 4.5)

The effect of weak CYP3A4 inhibitors on senaparib exposure has not been studied. Caution should be exercised when senaparib is co-administered with weak CYP3A4 inhibitors. Patients should be monitored for adverse reactions.

Based on in vitro data, senaparib may affect exposure to medicinal products that are substrates of CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19. Caution should therefore be exercised when these enzymes substrates are co-administered with senaparib including medicinal products with a narrow therapeutic index where clinical monitoring to ensure the medicinal product efficacy and safety in patients is recommended (see section 4.5).

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

## Effect of other medicinal products on senaparib

Senaparib is primarily metabolized via CYP3A4 (see section 5.2).

## Strong or moderate CYP3A4 / P-gp inhibitor

A clinical study evaluating the impact of itraconazole, a strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor, showed that co-administration increased senaparib mean Cmax by 79% and mean AUC by 280%, decreased metabolite M9 mean Cmax by 77% and mean AUC by 50%. Known strong CYP3A4 inhibitors include itraconazole, telithromycin, clarithromycin, protease inhibitors boosted with ritonavir or cobicistat, boceprevir, telaprevir. If a strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors must be co-administered, the senaparib dose should be reduced to 20 mg once daily (see section 4.2 and 4.4).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The effect of moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors on senaparib exposure has not been studied. Since a clinically relevant increase in senaparib exposure cannot be excluded, concomitant use of moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors is also not recommended. Known moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors include erythromycin, diltiazem, fluconazole, verapamil, or grapefruit juice. If a moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor must be co-administered, the senaparib dose should be reduced to 40 mg once daily. Subsequent dose reduction to 20 mg once daily may be considered with moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors based on tolerability (see sections 4.2 and 4.4).

## Weak CYP3A4 inhibitor

Caution should be exercised when senaparib is co-administered with weak CYP3A4 inhibitors. In case of adverse reactions, the dose adjustment recommendations in Table 1 should be followed (see section 4.2).

## Strong or moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inducer

A clinical study to evaluate the impact of rifampicin, a known strong CYP3A4/P-gp inducer, has shown that co-administration with senaparib decreased its mean Cmax by 59% and mean AUC by 83%, increased metabolite M9 mean Cmax by 64% and mean AUC by 18%. The effect of moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inducers on senaparib is not established.

Therefore, concomitant use of strong or moderate CYP3A4/P-gp inducer is not recommended, as the efficacy of senaparib may be substantially reduced (see section 4.4). Known strong CYP3A inducers include phenytoin, rifampicin, rifapentine, carbamazepine, phenobarbital and St John's Wort. Known moderate CYP3A4 inducers include efavirenz and rifabutin.

## Weak CYP3A4 inducer

It is recommended to use weak CYP3A4 inducers with caution.

## Effect of senaparib on other medicinal products

## Drug substrates of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19

The clinically relevant induction effect of senaparib and M9 on CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19 cannot be excluded based on in vitro results (see section 5.2). Therefore, co-administration with substrates of these enzymes may result in reduced substrate exposure and reduced efficacy.

Caution is recommended when senaparib is used concomitantly with substrates of CYP1A2 (e.g., clozapine, theophylline, ropinirole), CYP2B6 (e.g., methadone, ketamine, bupropion, efavirenz), and CYP3A4 (e.g., cyclosporine, tacrolimus, sirolimus, everolimus, irinotecan, docetaxel, quinidine), CYP2C8 (e.g., repaglinide, paclitaxel), CYP2C9 (e.g., S-warfarin, phenytoin) and CYP2C19 (e.g., clopidogrel, voriconazole, sertraline).

Particular caution should be exercised with substrates having a narrow therapeutic index where clinical monitoring to ensure the medicinal product's efficacy in patients is recommended including but not limited to warfarin, tolbutamide (CYP2C9 substrate), cyclophosphamide (CYP2B6 substrate), mephenytoin, phenytoin, valproic acid (CYP2C19 substrate), paclitaxel (CYP2C8 substrate), theophylline (CYP1A2 substrate).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Drug substrates of CYP3A4

The clinically relevant inhibition effect of senaparib and M9 on CYP3A4 cannot be excluded. In vitro results showed that senaparib is both CYP3A4 inhibitor and inducer and M9 is a CYP3A4 inducer (see section 5.2). The net effect on CYP3A4 drug substrates is unknown. Therefore, concomitant treatment with CYP3A4 drug substrates should be avoided.

If co-administration with sensitive CYP3A4 substrates cannot be avoided caution should be exercised including CYP3A4 drug substrates with narrow therapeutic index, and appropriate monitoring of the safety and efficacy of the CYP3A4 substrate is recommended. Narrow therapeutic index CYP3A4 substrates include but are not limited to some immunosuppressants such as cyclosporine, sirolimus, tacrolimus, some antiarrhythmics such as quinidine, or quinine.

## Substrates of transporters

The clinically relevant inhibition effect on BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3，OCT1, OCT2 and MATE1 transporters cannot be excluded (see section 5.2).

Caution is recommended when senaparib is combined with substrates of BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3 (e.g. statins, bosentan, empagliflozin), OCT1, OCT2 and MATE1 (e.g. metformin), as increased exposure of the co-administered medicinal product may occur. Monitoring of the patient for adverse reactions related to the co-administered substrate is recommended.

The clinically relevant inducing effect on P-gp transporters cannot be ruled out. In vitro results showed both inhibitory and induction potential of P-gp by senaparib (see section 5.2). The net effect of senaparib on P-gp drug substrates is unknown. Therefore, senaparib upon co-administration may modify the exposure to substrates of P-gp affecting thereby the medicinal product's efficacy and / or safety. Close monitoring is recommended for patients receiving medicinal products with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g. digoxin).

## Hormonal contraceptives

It is currently unknown whether senaparib may reduce the effectiveness of systemically acting hormonal contraceptives. Therefore, women using systemically acting hormonal contraceptives should add a barrier method during senaparib treatment and for at least 6 months after the last dose (see section 4.6).

## QT interval prolonging medicinal products

Sepalna should be used with caution in patients co-prescribed with QT-prolonging medicinal products (see section 4.4).

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation

## Women of childbearing potential / Contraception in females

Women of childbearing potential should not become pregnant while on treatment with Sepalna and should not be pregnant at the beginning of treatment. A pregnancy test should be performed on all women of childbearing potential prior to treatment.

Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during therapy and for 6 months after receiving the last dose of Sepalna. It is currently unknown whether senaparib may reduce the effectiveness of systemically acting hormonal contraceptives, therefore women using systemically acting hormonal contraceptives should add a barrier method during treatment and for at least 6 months after the last dose of Sepalna (see sections 4.4 and 4.5).

## Pregnancy

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

There are no clinical data from the use of senaparib in pregnant women. Animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have not been conducted (see section 5.3). Based on its mechanism of action and demonstrated class effects, senaparib could cause embryonic or foetal harm, including embryolethality and malformations, when administered to a pregnant woman. Sepalna should not be used during pregnancy and in women of childbearing potential not using contraception.

## Breast-feeding

It is unknown whether senaparib or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A risk to the newborns / infants cannot be excluded. Breast-feeding is contraindicated during treatment with Sepalna and for 1 month after receiving the last dose of Sepalna (see section 4.3).

## Fertility

Based on the mechanism of action of senaparib, there is a risk for impaired fertility in both males and females.

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines

Sepalna has minor influence on the ability to drive and use machines.

Dizziness, fatigue, and asthenia have been reported in some patients who received senaparib, and caution when driving or operating machines is advised for patients presenting with these symptoms.

## 4.8 Undesirable effects

## Descriptions of selected adverse reactions

The following information summarises clinical data on specific adverse reactions of senaparib from four clinical studies with a total of 447 tumour patients treated with senaparib ≤100 mg/day, and guidelines for the management of specific adverse reactions are provided in section 4.4.

## Haematologic toxicity

In tumour patients treated with ≤100 mg/day of senaparib, the overall incidence of haematologic toxicity was 83.4%, with ≥Grade 3 events occurring in 51.7% of patients. One fatal case of bone marrow failure was reported in clinical studies. Risk mitigation and management are provided in section 4.2 and 4.4.

The incidence of anaemia was 71.8%, and the incidence of ≥Grade 3 anaemia was 30.2%. The median time from the first dose to the first onset was 32 days (range: 7 to 673 days), and the median duration was 31 days (range: 3 to 648 days). The incidence of treatment interruption, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation due to anaemia was 34.2%, 24.8% and 2.0%, respectively.

The incidence of leukopenia was 62.6%, and the incidence of ≥Grade 3 leukopenia was 12.3%. The median time from the first dose to the first onset was 29 days (range: 2 to 723 days), and the median duration was 17 days (range: 2 to 400 days). The incidence of treatment interruption, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation due to leukopenia was 22.8%, 11.2%, and 0.2%, respectively.

The incidence of neutropenia was 59.7%, and the incidence of ≥Grade 3 neutropenia was 20.1%. The median time from the first dose to the first onset was 30 days (range: 2 to 723 days), and the median duration was 15 days (range: 2 to 391 days). The incidence of treatment interruption, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation due to neutropenia was 25.1%, 13.0% and 0.2%, respectively.

The incidence of thrombocytopenia was 58.2%, and the incidence of ≥Grade 3 thrombocytopenia was 20.8%. The median time from the first dose to the first onset was 28 days (range: 7 to 784 days), and the median duration was 22 days (range: 2.5 to 361 days). The incidence of treatment interruption, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation due to thrombocytopenia was 30.4%, 20.1% and 2.9%.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The incidence of lymphopenia was 13.0%, and the incidence of ≥Grade 3 lymphopenia was 2.2%. The median time from the first dose to the first onset was 56.5 days (range: 2 to 674 days), and the median duration was 13.75 days (range: 2 to 84 days). The incidence of treatment interruption and dose reduction due to lymphopenia was 1.8% and 0.2%, and no patient permanently discontinued treatment.

## Gastrointestinal toxicities

Nausea and vomiting are frequently reported with senaparib, with the incidence of 26.8% and 15.2% respectively. However, most of them were Grade 1 or 2. Incidence of ≥Grade 3 nausea and vomiting were both 0.7%.

The median time from the first dose to the first onset of nausea was 6 days (range: 1 to 603 days), and the median duration was 18 days (range: 1 to 657 days). The incidence of treatment interruption and dose reduction due to nausea was 1.6% and 0.4%, respectively. No patient permanently discontinued treatment.

The median time from the first dose to the first onset of vomiting was 19.5 days (range: 1 to 721 days), and the median duration was 1 day (range: 1 to 1151 days). The incidence of treatment interruption, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation due to vomiting was 2.0%, 0.4% and 0.2%, respectively.

The incidence of diarrhoea was 13.6%, and the incidence of ≥Grade 3 diarrhoea was 0.7%. The median time from the first dose to the first onset was 45 days (range: 1 to 689 days), and the median duration was 3 days (range: 1 to 387 days). The incidence of treatment interruption and dose reduction due to diarrhoea was 1.3% and 0.2%, respectively. No patient permanently discontinued treatment.

The incidence of intestinal obstruction was 4.0%, and the incidence of ≥Grade 3 was 1.8%, with a fatal outcome in 1 patient (0.2%). The underlying disease may play a role in the development of intestinal obstruction in patients with ovarian cancer. The median time from the first dose to the first onset was 63 days (range: 2 to 664 days), and the median duration was 9 days (range: 2 to 106 days). The incidence of treatment interruption, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation due to intestinal obstruction was 2.0%, 0.4% and 0.2%, respectively.

## Myelodysplastic syndrome / acute myeloid leukaemia

With a median follow-up duration of 51.17 months, 6/270 patients in senaparib group reported MDS / AML in the FLAMES study. A total of 7 patients in clinical studies developed MDS / AML, representing an overall incidence of 1.6%, including events reported during the long-term survival follow up. The median duration of therapy prior to developing MDS / AML was 729 days (range: 392 to 1007 days). The median time from the first dose to the first onset of MDS / AML was 1035 days (range: 393 to 1514 days). All patients had potential contributing factors for the development of MDS / AML, such as prior chemotherapy with platinum agents. Some also received platinum and paclitaxel during the period of senaparib discontinuation and the onset of MDS / AML.

## 5. PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Antineoplastic agents, poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors, ATC code: L01XK07

## Mechanism of action

Senaparib is a poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor, with inhibitory effect on PARP-1 and PARP-2 enzyme activities. Cytotoxicity induced by senaparib has been observed in tumour cell lines both with and without BReast CAncer (BRCA) 1/2 tumour suppressor genes deficiencies. Although senaparib is an inhibitor of PARP1/2, other PARP enzymes of the family (PARP 3, 5A, 5B, 6, 7, 11, 12 and 15) are also inhibited at the clinical concentrations.

## Clinical efficacy

## First-line maintenance therapy for advanced ovarian cancer

The FLAMES study is a phase III randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of senaparib maintenance therapy in patients with advanced (FIGO stage III-IV) ovarian, fallopian tube cancer or primary peritoneal carcinoma (N=404) who have had a complete or partial response after first-line platinum-based chemotherapy. Patients were randomized at a 2:1 ratio to senaparib group or placebo group after completion of first-line platinum-based chemotherapy plus surgery.

Randomization stratification factors included response to first-line platinum-based chemotherapy (complete response vs. partial response) and baseline BRCA status (BRCA-mutated vs. wild-type). The BRCA testing of tumour tissue or blood samples was conducted using next-generation sequencing (NGS) technology, which comprehensively covered all coding exons and adjacent flanking regions of the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes. The HRD (Homologous Recombination Deficiency) status was determined retrospectively as an exploratory endpoint.

After randomization, patients started to receive senaparib 100 mg or placebo once daily. Treatment was continued until radiological progression of the underlying disease, unacceptable toxicity or for up to 2 years. The primary efficacy endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS) based on Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours, version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1), as assessed by blinded independent central review (BICR). The secondary efficacy endpoints included overall survival (OS). Tumour assessments were performed every 12 weeks (±1 week) from the date of randomization until 120 weeks, then every 24 weeks (±1 week) until objective radiological disease progression (PD) / initiation of new anti-tumour therapy.

Demographic and baseline characteristics were generally balanced between senaparib and placebo groups. The median age of all the patients was 55 years, and all were Asian women. In the overall population, 40.1% had ECOG performance status of 0. All patients had undergone at least one tumourrelated surgery, with 99.0% undergoing cytoreductive surgery. Ovary was the primary tumour site in 94.3% of the patients, and the most common histological type was serous (99.8%). Regarding disease stage, 70.3% of patients presented with FIGO stage III and 29.5% with FIGO stage IV disease. All patients had received first-line platinum-based therapy, and 88.1% achieved a complete response to the first-line therapy. Patients received a median of 7.0 cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy, with a median interval of 48.0 days from the last cycle to randomization. None of the patients received vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) / vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) inhibitors in combination or maintenance treatment during the course of first-line therapy or in other prior setting. BRCA mutations were found in 34.2% of the patients, while 65.8% had wild-type BRCA (BRCAwt). Based on prespecified exploratory testing, HRD results were available for 261 patients; of these, 71.6% had tumours classified as HRD and 28.4% as HRP (homologous recombination proficient).

As of the primary analysis with a data cut-off (DCO) on 16 March 2023, the median follow-up period for all patients was 22 months. ## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

## Absorption

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Following oral administration of senaparib, absorption is rapid with median peak senaparib plasma concentrations typically achieved 2 hours after dosing.

In patients with advanced solid tumours administered senaparib 100 mg once daily, the mean steadystate Cmax was 2735 ng/mL and AUC0-24h was 26624 h ng/mL for a typical patient (body weight was 65 kg and albumin was 44 g/L) based on the population pharmacokinetic (PopPK). The geometric means of Cmax and AUC0-24h of pharmacologically active metabolite M9 at steady-state were 633 ng/mL and 8805 ng h/mL via PopPK. Following multiple oral doses of senaparib 100 mg once daily, the AUC accumulation ratio of senaparib and M9 was 1.19 folds and 1.73 folds, PK appeared to be time-dependent to a small extent.

In a dedicated food-effect study conducted in healthy participants, administration of a single 100 mg dose of senaparib with a high-fat meal using the modified formulation (i.e., the formulation used in the pivotal clinical study) resulted in a decrease in Cmax of 24%, an increase in AUC0-inf of 28%, and a delay in median Tmax to 5 hours, compared with fasted conditions.

No dedicated food-effect study has been conducted with the commercial formulation. Bioequivalence between the modified formulation and the commercial formulation was demonstrated. Therefore, any conclusions regarding the effect of food on the commercial formulation are based on an indirect between-study comparison.

To ensure consistent exposure, Sepalna should be taken at least one hour before or two hours after the ingestion of food (see section 4.2).

## Distribution

Mean human plasma protein binding of senaparib was 96.13%. Senaparib and its major pharmacologically active metabolite M9 are mainly bound to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (AAG) (94.5%).

The results of the human mass balance study ([ 14 C]-senaparib) showed that the geometric mean ratio of whole blood/plasma partitioning was 0.540 to 0.557. The apparent distribution volume (Vd/F) at steady state was 27.7 L in the typical patients via PopPK.

## Biotransformation

The main metabolic enzyme of senaparib is CYP3A4.

The results of the human mass balance study ([ 14 C]-senaparib) showed the predominant radioactive component in plasma was unchanged senaparib, accounting for 74.80% of the total radioactivity exposure in plasma. In addition to the unchanged senaparib, the major metabolite identified in plasma was piperazine cyclomono-oxidation product M9, which accounted for 18.08% of the total plasma radioactivity exposure in plasma, and other metabolites accounted for less than 10% of the total radioactivity exposure in plasma.

## In vitro pharmacokinetic interaction studies

## CYP450 enzymes

At clinically relevant concentrations, neither senaparib nor the major circulating metabolite M9 represent a risk of inhibition of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6. While M9 is not expected to be a clinically relevant CYP3A4 inhibitor, senaparib showed potential time-dependent inhibition in vitro on CYP3A4/5 at clinically relevant concentrations. Furthermore, senaparib may show direct inhibition potential of CYP3A4 at clinically relevant concentrations in the gastro-intestinal tract.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Based on in vitro results, induction of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 by senaparib could not be excluded at clinically relevant concentrations.

M9 was not an inducer of CYP2C8 but induction of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 could not be excluded at clinically relevant concentrations.

## Drug transporters

In vitro, senaparib and metabolite M9 were identified as P-gp substrates.

In vitro, neither senaparib nor M9 inhibit OAT1, OAT3 and MATE2-K, and metabolite M9 does not inhibit P-gp, BCRP, OATP1B3, OCT1, OCT2 and MATE1 at clinically relevant concentrations.

Based on in vitro data, senaparib inhibits OCT2 and MATE1, and the active metabolite, M9, inhibits OATP1B1, at clinically relevant concentrations, and the clinically relevant inhibition effect by senaparib on BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1 and P-gp cannot be excluded (see section 4.5).

## Elimination

Following single oral administration of [ 14 C]-senaparib in healthy subjects, 84.55% was recovered in faeces and 15.18% was recovered in urine, indicating that faeces were the major excretion route while urinary excretion represented a minor pathway. The main identified forms in faeces are the unchanged drug (accounting for 47.38% of administered dose) and metabolite M9 (accounting for 15.39% of the administered dose). The apparent total clearance (CL/F) of senaparib was 3.76 L/h in cancer patients. The effective half-life (t1/2,eff) of senaparib and M9 was 9.06 hours and 18.8 hours in patients with cancer following senaparib 100 mg taken once daily.

## Linearity

Senaparib exposure increases less than dose proportionally from 80 mg to 150 mg, suggesting a trend toward absorption saturation at higher doses (&gt;80 mg). Dose proportionality has not been established within 2-80 mg.

## Special populations

## Renal impairment

In the PopPK analysis, 89 patients with normal renal function (eGFR ≥90 mL/min), 75 patients with mild renal impairment (eGFR of 60 to 89 mL/min) and 24 patients with moderate renal impairment (eGFR of 30 to 59 mL/min) were included. The results showed that the steady-state exposure of senaparib was comparable between patients with normal renal function and those with mild or moderate renal insufficiency (see section 4.2).

There are no data in patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15 to 29 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease (eGFR &lt;15 without dialysis or eGFR &lt;15 requiring dialysis) (see section 4.2).

## Hepatic impairment

In the PopPK analysis, 165 patients with normal hepatic function (total bilirubin (TB) &amp; aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≤ upper limit of normal (ULN) ) and 24 patients with mild hepatic impairment (either AST &gt; ULN and TB ≤ ULN or TB &gt;1.0 - 1.5 x ULN and any AST) were included. The results showed that the steady-state exposure of senaparib was comparable between patients with normal hepatic function and those with mild hepatic impairment (see section 4.2).

No data are available for patients with moderate (TB &gt;1.5 - 3.0 x ULN and any AST) or severe hepatic impairment (TB &gt;3 x ULN and any AST) (see section 4.2).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Elderly patients

Patients included in population PK (PopPK) analysis were aged 31 to 83 years (145 (76.7%) &lt;65 years, 35 (18.5%) 65-74 years, and 9 (4.8%) &gt;75-84 years). The PopPK analysis showed that the steady-state total exposures (AUC) of senaparib in patients aged 65-74 and patients aged 75-84 were 26% and 55% higher than that aged &lt;65. No data was available for patients aged 85 or over (see section 4.2).

## Race, body weight

Based on population PK analysis (PopPK), no clinically significant relationships were identified between predicted steady-state exposure and patient's race and body weight. Patients included in the population PK study were 80% were Asian, 15% were Caucasian, and had body weights between 42 kg and 103 kg (median body weight was 63 kg).

The consistent 10-15% difference in exposure between Caucasian and Asian patient populations observed in the clinical studies is judged not sufficiently significant for a dose-adjustment based on racial considerations. However, data in the non-Asian population are limited.

The consistent 12-23% difference in exposure between patients weighing 85 kg and 110 kg patient populations observed in the clinical studies is judged not sufficiently significant for a dose-adjustment based on weight considerations. However, data on patients with extremely high body weight are limited.

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data

## Repeat-dose toxicity

In repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and dogs, the main target organs from repeat-dose toxicity were bone marrow, spleen, thymus and liver. Erythrocyte count, leukocyte count and lymphoid decreased resulted in secondary effects caused by weakened immunity. These findings occurred at exposures below those observed in patients treated at the recommended dose, and were generally reversible or partly reversible. These toxicity findings are consistent with the pharmacology of senaparib. Additionally, skin effects were also observed. In the 9-month dog toxicity study, a dog with dysplasia in retinal was found with optical disc edge unclear in eyes, but the relationship with senaparib was uncertain.

## Genotoxicity

The result of in vitro Ames assay (bacterial reverse mutation assay) was negative, the results of in vitro chromosome aberration test in Chinese hamster ovary cells (CHO) and in vivo micronucleus test in sprague-dawley rats were positive, this chromosome breakage effect is consistent with the mechanism of senaparib, indicating that senaparib may have potential for genotoxicity in humans and therefore malignancy.

## Reproductive toxicology

Reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have not been conducted with senaparib.

## Carcinogenicity

Carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted with senaparib.

## 1. What Sepalna is and what it is used for

Sepalna contains the active substance senaparib. Senaparib is a type of cancer medicine called a PARP (poly adenosine diphosphate-ribose polymerase) inhibitor.

Sepalna is used in adults for the treatment of high-grade (fast-growing) cancers of the ovary, the fallopian tubes (part of the female reproductive system that connects the ovaries to the uterus), or the peritoneum (the membrane lining the abdomen), following an initial treatment with platinum-based chemotherapy which has shrunk or cleared the cancer.

PARP is an enzyme (protein) that helps cells repair damaged DNA. The active substance of Sepalna, senaparib, blocks PARP and thereby prevents cancer cells from repairing their DNA. This causes the cancer cells to die, which helps to control the cancer.

## 2. What you need to know before you take Sepalna

## Do not take Sepalna

- if you are allergic to senaparib or any of the other ingredients of this medicine (listed in section 6).
- if you are breast-feeding.

## Warnings and precautions

Talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse before or while taking Sepalna if any of the following apply to you:

## Low blood cell counts

Sepalna can cause myelosuppression, a condition in which the bone marrow cannot make enough blood cells. This results in lower blood cell counts, such as your red blood cell count (anaemia), blood platelet count (thrombocytopenia), white blood cell count (leukopenia), neutrophil count (neutropenia), lymphocyte count (lymphopenia) or even bone marrow failure. Signs and symptoms you need to look out for include fever or infection, and abnormal bruising or bleeding (see section 4 for more information). Your doctor will test your blood regularly throughout your treatment.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Myelodysplastic syndrome / acute myeloid leukaemia

Rarely, low blood cell counts may be a sign of more serious problems with the bone marrow such as 'myelodysplastic syndrome' or 'acute myeloid leukaemia'. Your doctor may want to test your bone marrow to check for these problems.

## Children and adolescents

Children under 18 years of age should not be given Sepalna. This medicine has not been studied in this age group.

- certain medicines to treat hypercholestrolemia (e.g. rosuvastatin, atorvastatin, pravastatin, simvastatin, empagliflozin)

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- certain medicines to treat diabetes (e.g. metformin)

## Pregnancy and breast-feeding

Sepalna should not be taken during pregnancy as it could harm your unborn baby. If you are pregnant, think you may be pregnant or are planning to have a baby, ask your doctor for advice before taking this medicine.

It is not known if senaparib passes into breast milk, so do not breast-feed if you are taking Sepalna and for 1 month after receiving the last dose of Sepalna. Ask your doctor for advice before taking this medicine.

If you are a woman who could become pregnant, you must use highly effective contraception (birth control) while you are taking Sepalna, and for 6 months after taking your last dose. Sepalna may interfere with how well oral hormonal contraceptives work. Discuss with your doctor the most appropriate methods of contraception. Your doctor will ask you to take a pregnancy test before starting your treatment with Sepalna to confirm that you are not pregnant. Contact your doctor straight away if you become pregnant while you are taking Sepalna.

## Driving and using machines

Sepalna may have a minor influence on your ability to drive and use machines. If you feel dizzy, weak or tired while taking Sepalna, exercise caution when driving or using machines.

## 3. How to take Sepalna

Always take this medicine exactly as your doctor or pharmacist has told you. Check with your doctor or pharmacist if you are not sure.

The recommended starting dose is 100 mg (5 x 20 mg capsules) once daily. This means you take a total of 5 capsules each day.

- Swallow the capsules whole with water. Do not chew or crush the capsules. This will ensure the medicine works as well as possible.
- Take Sepalna at least one hour before or two hours after the ingestion of food.
- Take the capsules at approximately the same time each day.

If you have certain side effects or if you are taking, have recently taken, or might take any other medicines, your doctor may recommend a lower dose, or temporarily stop your treatment.

## If you take more Sepalna than you should

If you take more than your normal dose, contact your doctor immediately.

## If you forget to take Sepalna

If you forgot to take Sepalna you can take the missed dose within 4 hours, otherwise take your next dose at your regularly scheduled time. Do not take a double dose to make up for a forgotten dose.

## 4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, this medicine can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them.

## Tell your doctor straight away if you notice any of the following serious side effects - you may need urgent medical treatment:

Very common (may affect more than 1 in 10 people)

- shortness of breath, feeling very tired, pale skin, or fast heartbeat - these may be signs of a low red blood cell count (anaemia).
- fever or infection - these may be sign of low neutrophil count or white blood cell count which can increase your risk of infection. Signs may include fever, chills, feeling weak or confused, cough, pain or a burning feeling when passing urine.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- bruising or bleeding for longer than usual if you hurt yourself - these may be signs of a low blood platelet count (thrombocytopenia).

## Common (may affect up to 1 in 10 people)

- blockage in the gut or bowel
- low blood cell counts may be a sign of more serious problem with the bone marrow such as myelodysplastic syndrome, acute myeloid leukaemia or bone marrow failure.

Other side effects can include:

## Very common (may affect more than 1 in 10 people)

- feeling sick (nausea)
- tiredness (fatigue)
- abdominal (belly) pain
- vomiting
- dizziness
- diarrhoea
- decreased appetite
- decreased number of a type of white blood cell called lymphocytes (lymphopenia)
- blood in the urine (haematuria)
- headache
- joint pain (arthralgia)

## Very common side effects that may show up in blood tests

- increased blood levels of liver enzymes: transaminases, alkaline phosphatase or gammaglutamyltransferase
- increased blood levels of bilirubin (hyperbilirubinaemia)

## Common (may affect up to 1 in 10 people)

- increased blood levels of uric acid (hyperuricaemia)
- abnormal or reduced skin sensations (paraesthesia or hypoaesthesia)
- swelling in the face, legs, ankles, and/or hands (oedema)
- muscle pain (myalgia)
- inflammation of the lining of the mouth (stomatitis)
- hair loss (alopecia)

## Common side effects that may show up in blood tests

- low albumin levels in your blood
- low levels of magnesium in your blood
- low levels of potassium in your blood

## Uncommon (may affect up to 1 in 100 people)

- changes in the electrical activity of the heart (seen on an electrocardiogram as 'QT prolongation'), which may lead to a fast or irregular heartbeat
- abnormal liver function
