## 1. Introduction

On 13 th of September 2024, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Elaprase, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

This is a stand-alone PAM (P46). A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

Elaprase is indicated for long-term treatment of patients with Mucopolysaccharidosis II (MPS II, also known as Hunter syndrome).

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that study TAK-665-5001 is a standalone study and that it is not part of a paediatric investigation plan nor part of the clinical development program of Elaprase.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

The frequency of Elaprase administration was once per week in all patients, per IV infusion. Older cohorts received Elaprase per the recommended dose (0.5 mg/kg) whilst the younger cohorts received a mean dose of 0.6 mg/kg. Most patients did not have modifications to the dose and/or frequency after treatment initiation.

The dose for children and adolescents is the same as for adults, 0.5 mg/kg body weight weekly (SmPC).

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## 2.3.1. Introduction

The MAH submitted a final report for:

- Study TAK-665-5001; a global, multi-centre, non-interventional retrospective chart review analysis of pairs of siblings with Mucopolysaccharidosis type II (MPS II) to evaluate the effectiveness of idursulfase in patients 12 months of age and younger.

## 2.3.2. Clinical study

## Study TAK-665-5001

## Description

Study TAK 665-5001 was conducted to address the lack of scientific evidence of the effectiveness of Elaprase in patients with MPS II aged ≤12 months and the impact of early initiation of Elaprase on clinical outcomes. Study TAK-665-5001 was a global, multi-centre, non-interventional retrospective chart review study to evaluate the real-world effectiveness of Elaprase in patients with MPS II who initiated treatment at ≤12 months of age (primary objective).

## CHMP comments

As the MAH explains, the current scientific substantiation for treatment of very young paediatric MPS II patients is limited. Study TAK 665-5001 was performed to address this, which is supported.

This study concerns a multi-centre, non-interventional retrospective chart review study with the aim to study the effectiveness of Elaprase treatment in MPS II patients who started treatment before 12 months of age. Time of index is the moment of Elaprase treatment initiation. Information from the pre-index period and post-index period of a patient is gathered from hospital charts. There is no untreated control group to match the treated patients, which limits the interpretation and conclusions that can be drawn based on the data of this study.

## Methods

## Study participants

Inclusion criteria:

- The patient is male.
- The patient has been diagnosed with MPS II (biochemically and/or genetically).
- The patient is a sibling of, at least, one male patient diagnosed with MPS II who received Elaprase treatment.
- The patient and his sibling(s) received Elaprase for treatment of MPS II for at least 2 years.
- The younger sibling must have started Elaprase treatment at a maximum age of 20 months and there is a confirmed minimum of 12 months age difference at Elaprase treatment initiation between the younger and the older siblings.

## Exclusion criteria:

- Patients or sibling(s) who received treatment for MPS II with an ERT or an investigational product other than idursulfase (IT or IV) prior to or within 2 years of initiating Elaprase treatment.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Patients or sibling(s) who underwent BMT or HSCT prior to or within 2 years of initiating Elaprase treatment.

## CHMP comments

This study included MPS II male siblings, of which one sibling should have received Elaprase for at least 2 years. The younger sibling must have initiated Elaprase treatment before the age of 20 months. The younger sibling must have initiated treatment with at least a 12 month age difference compared to the older brother, in order to study the effect of an earlier start of treatment.

## Treatments

This is a non-interventional retrospective chart review study. However patients receiving Elaprase treatment for at least 2 years were included.

## Objective(s)

The MAH states that the overarching goal of this study is to gain a more complete understanding of the real-world practice of early treatment initiation with Elaprase in very young patients with MPS II and evaluate the impact of Elaprase initiated at an early age. This study will be conducted with the aim to support the implementation of newborn screening programs for MPS II, as well as a potential label extension of Elaprase to younger patients.

To achieve this goal, the study has the following objectives:

- I. Primary Objective
- To evaluate real-world effectiveness of Elaprase in patients with MPS II who initiated this treatment at 12 months of age and younger.
- II. Exploratory Objectives:
- To assess the impact of Elaprase treatment at an early age by comparing effectiveness parameters in children who initiated this treatment at 12 months of age and younger and their siblings who initiated this treatment at 36 months of age and older.
- To compare effectiveness parameters in children who initiated Elaprase treatment at up to 20 months of age with their siblings who initiated Elaprase treatment at an older age, on condition that the older sibling initiated Elaprase treatment at an age, at least, 12 months older than the age of the younger sibling, at Elaprase treatment initiation

## CHMP comments

The primary objective of is to evaluate effectiveness of Elaprase in MPS II patients who started treatment at or before the age of 12 months. The study included sibling of which the youngest sibling can have started treatment at or before 20 months of age. Thus not all included young siblings will contribute to gathering data for the primary objective.

Exploratory objectives are to assess the impact of treatment at an early age by comparing the effectiveness of the younger sibling to the effectiveness observed in the older sibling. The study setup does not allow for a randomized and well controlled comparison and thus the ability to draw firm conclusions is limited.

Thus MPS II patients regardless of age, are covered by the current indication. The extension of the label is likely aimed at extending the label approved in the US.

## CHMP comments

At MAA, efficacy of Elaprase has been (amongst others) determined by assessing the 6 minute walk test and forced vital capacity for lung function. It is understandable that these measures are not determined in this chart review study, as these measures are not assessable in these very young patients and furthermore might not be part of routine clinical practice. Urinary GAG levels, left ventricular mass index and the effect on liver and spleen size are considered relevant clinical outcomes and have also been assessed in clinical studies included in the dossier of the MAA. Also joint stiffness, the ENT parameters (infections, enlarged tonsils) and respiratory tract infections are known manifestations of the disease. The studied outcomes are considered relevant for assessing effect of Elaprase treatment on MPS-II.

However as explained by the MAH, the rate of disease progression and disease severity vary widely amongst patients with MPS II, clinical manifestations are often noticed at the age of two to four years in children. Thus in this study, in these young patients, disease might not yet have manifested with a clinical profile, which limits the interpretation.

No safety assessment was performed.

## Statistical Methods

Analyses were performed once the data from all patients were collected in the database, cleaned and database lock had occurred. Based on the data obtained, the SAP was amended to include analysis methods best suited to the final data, and the final analyses was performed.

## Methods

All relevant chart data were abstracted and entered by the site principal investigator (PI) or delegated staff into an online electronic case report form (eCRF) programmed by the CRO. If any relevant data were not available from medical charts, the eCRF offered an unknown/not available answer option where needed.

Appropriate descriptive summary statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics and treatment variables. This was to evaluate real world effectiveness of Elaprase in patients with MPS II who initiated this treatment at 12 months of age and younger (Cohort 1: YSu12) compared with their matching older siblings (Cohort 2: OS).

For a selected list of categorical and continuous variables, descriptive summary statistics were reported at specific time points in 6-month intervals.

The Kaplan-Meier (KM) method was used to describe cumulative incidence of \"first events\" from birth and from age at diagnosis by cohort (per objective) for key variables. KM curves were presented graphically. The hazard ratio and its 95% confidence interval for the cohort variable (A vs B) was calculated from a Cox proportional model, including the cohort as a single covariate. Corresponding pvalues were also reported, but the interpretation of the hazard ratio was only descriptive.

For a selected number of binary and continuous variables, and based on completeness of information, an adjusted analysis with covariates was performed using a generalized linear mixed model (GLMM). GLMMs are recommended to analyse data with a complex correlation structure. This makes it possible to account for the correlation within sibling pairs as well as correlation of observations over time. The GLMM was run only for objectives and cohorts that had an adequate number of patients. Parameter coefficients and odds ratios (OR) were presented, and linearity assumption was also checked.

This analysis was purely descriptive in the sense that no pre-defined hypothesis was tested. However, for some analyses and statistical procedures, such as Cox proportional hazard models and GLMM, confidence intervals and p-values were calculated; however, these should not be interpreted in the strict sense of confirmatory testing but rather served as an indicator of precision and uncertainty that is associated with the derived estimates.

## Missing data

Steps were taken to minimize the amount of missing data and optimize the integrity of the collected data, by ensuring clarity in the eCRF. In addition, the eCRF allowed for an \"unknown\" option for most questions, so information that was missing from a patient's chart was made explicit. Data fields indicated as \"unknown\", reflecting missing data in the patient's chart, were addressed at the analytic stage. Data that was \"unknown\" or not available for a specific time point was accounted for. Descriptive statistics were restricted to the subset of patients for which the data were available, and the relevant denominator information was provided in the results tables. No imputation of missing data was conducted for this study.

## Results

## Participant flow

Overall, 38 patients (19 sibling pairs) were included for analyses in this study. Primary and exploratory analyses were performed using subpopulations defined by age of Elaprase treatment initiation and sibling relationship as follows:

- Cohort 1 consisted of 15 patients who were all younger siblings that initiated Elaprase treatment before or at 12 months of age.
- Cohort 2 consisted of 15 patients taking Elaprase who were the matched older sibling of patients in Cohort 1.
- Cohort 3 consisted of 11 patients who were the matched older sibling of a patient in Cohort 1 but also initiated Elaprase treatment at or after 36 months of age.
- Cohort 4 consisted of 19 patients who were all younger siblings that initiated Elaprase treatment before or at 20 months of age.
- Cohort 5 consisted of 19 patients who were the matched older sibling of a patient in cohort 4, who initiated Elaprase treatment at an older age; and initiated Elaprase treatment at least 12 months older than the age of the younger sibling at Elaprase initiation.

## CHMP comments

The plan was to include 25 sibling pairs (50 patients) with MPS II and in case enrolling 25 sibling pairs was not achievable, a minimum of 15 sibling pairs (30 patients) would be included. In total 38 patients (19 sibling pairs) were included in the study, of which 15 sibling pairs were included in cohort 1 and 2 for assessing the primary objective.

## CHMP comments

Data was collected between March 2022 and February 2023 in 14 hospitals.

Article 46 requires the MAH to submit information on studies conducted in children of authorised medicines within six months of completion of each study. The MAH clarified that although the end of data collection occurred on 08 February 2023, the protocol had to be revised afterwards to take into consideration the smaller than expected sample size impacting the ranking of the study objectives. The overall analyses were therefore completed on 25 June 2024. The submission is performed as shortly as possible following the completion of the analysis.

The requirement of submitting paediatric study data within six months of completion of the trial was not met. All protocol and SAP amendments were made after the end of the data collection, which is not acceptable and compromises the integrity of the data.

## Baseline data

Patient characteristics at the time of index date (ie, Elaprase treatment initiation) for all cohorts are summarised in Table 3.

The mean and median ages at MPS II diagnosis were lower among Cohort 1 than Cohort 3 (3.1 months vs 53.9 months and 2.0 months vs 44.0 months, respectively); more generally, younger siblings appeared to be diagnosed at an earlier age than their older siblings. The mean and median time from diagnosis to treatment initiation was lower among Cohort 1 than Cohort 3 (2.0 months vs 7.9 months and 1.0 month vs 6.0 months, respectively).

The percentage of patients with the presence of cognitive impairment was high in Cohorts 2, 3, and 5 (12 [80.0%], 10 [90.9%], and 16 [84.2%], respectively). The most common diagnosis method for cognitive impairment across all cohorts was clinical judgement, with Cohort 5 having the highest percentage of patients (57.9%) diagnosed with cognitive impairment by clinical judgement, followed closely by Cohort 3 (54.5%). The highest proportion of patients with severe cognitive impairment was found in Cohorts 2, 3, and 5.

## CHMP comments

As could be expected, the age at MPS-II diagnosis was lower and time from diagnosis to treatment shorter in cohorts 1 and 4 (the younger siblings) than in the cohorts with the older siblings. The cohorts with the older siblings also contain a higher percentage of patients with the presence of cognitive impairment.

The older siblings received a dose of 0.5 mg/kg once per week, conform SmPC. The younger siblings received a mean dose of 0.6 mg/kg once per week, which is higher than the recommended dose. This off-label use has, according to the MAH, been reported elsewhere. However, it is not clear to the assessor where this has been reported and what the motivation was for this off-label use. The MAH is requested to submit these reports and clarify whether off-label use with a higher dose is used more often in very young patients and what the reason is for this (OC). The fact that the younger age cohort received a different dose than the older siblings, compromises the exploratory comparison between the two groups.

## Compliance and discontinuation

A high percentage of patients missed infusions in Cohorts 1 and 2 (8 [53.3%] patients) and Cohort 3 (6 [54.5%] patients). The mean (SD) number of missed infusions was highest amongst patients in Cohort 3 (23.2 [19.4]) and lowest amongst patients in Cohort 1 (19.3 [19.2]). The majority of patients received over 80% of all prescribed infusions across all cohorts, ranging from 9 (81.8%) patients in Cohort 3 to 17 (89.5%) patients each in Cohorts 4 and 5. Across all cohorts, the most commonly known reason for noncompliance was due to family dysfunction (1 patient in each cohort). The majority of patients did not discontinue Elaprase treatment. For patients who did discontinue Elaprase treatment, the most common reason was due to patients being enrolled in a clinical trial, with 2 patients in each of the Cohorts 1, 2, 4, and 5.

## Results of Generalised Linear Mixed Models

Generalised linear mixed models (GLMMs) were run on selected outcomes to analyse and evaluate the difference in the development of outcomes from Elaprase treatment initiation between younger and older sibling over time. The CIs and p-values that were calculated in the GLMM procedure could not be interpreted in the strict sense of confirmatory testing but rather served as an indicator of precision and uncertainty. However, stronger correlations, where the p value was &lt;0.05, were considered indicative of a stronger effect related to the exposure (Elaprase treatment initiation at a younger age).

Table 10 summarizes the results of the GLMM (where the coefficients and p-values were not provided, the model did not converge due to the lack of data collected).

Overall, the results of the GLMM showed a beneficial effect of early initiation of Elaprase treatment in younger siblings who initiated treatment at ≤12 months of age compared with their older siblings (Cohort 1 vs Cohort 2). This beneficial effect was observed for the following outcomes: HAZ, LVMI and presence of cardiac valve disease, joint stiffness, coarse facial features, liver size as determined by palpitation, presence of carpal tunnel syndrome, and presence of enlarged tonsils and adenoids. Similar findings were observed in the exploratory analyses that compared younger siblings who initiated treatment at ≤12 months of age with their older siblings who initiated treatment at ≥36 months (Cohort 1 vs Cohort 3) and younger siblings who initiated treatment at ≤20 months of age with their older siblings who initiated treatment at an age that was ≥12 months older than the younger sibling at treatment initiation (Cohort 4 vs Cohort 5). These results should be interpreted with caution due to the small cohort sizes.

## CHMP comments

Height and BMI increased in both the younger sibling cohort 1 and the older sibling cohort 2. From a large number of patients in both cohorts liver size and urinary GAG levels were unknown (80-90% for liver size and 50-90% for urinary GAG levels), which makes interpretation difficult. From the limited number of patients with data available, liver size was relatively normal post-Elaprase treatment (when compared to measures from Konus OL, AJR Am J Roentgenol 1998) and urinary GAG levels decreased during treatment. Also LVMI showed a general decrease during treatment. These findings do not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase.

The presence of joint stiffness, coarse facial features, enlarged tonsils, spinal abnormality were much higher among the older siblings (cohort 2) than in the younger siblings at index date. It is considered that conclusions on reduction or increase in percentage of several clinical outcomes cannot be made since the percentage of unknown/not available and missing data is high in the follow-up period. For most parameters at several timepoints data is completely missing, or the percentage of unknown/not available data is 80-90% of patients. In an exceptional case, the percentage of missing data for one timepoint for one categorical variable is somewhere between 6073%, which renders data on n=6-4 patients.

Also in regard to the 'first to event analysis' and the results of the generalized linear mixed models, the large amount of missing data prevents drawing any relevant conclusions. For the time to first event analysis it is of interest to know if and when a patient switched from 'No' to 'Yes' for a variable to generate a 'time -to-event' KM curve. From the curves it is not clear how many patients are taken along and how many events were registered. Furthermore, in all but one of the KM curves of cohort 1 and cohort 2 the lines cross each other which might suggest that one cohort has more events in the beginning of time and the other cohort has more events later (after crossing) in time (only the curves of chronic diarrhea do not cross).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In conclusion, the study design and the large amount of missing data or data that is unknown/not available preclude from drawing any conclusions on efficacy. However, the limited data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

This study concerns a multi-centre, non-interventional retrospective chart review study with the aim to study the effectiveness of Elaprase treatment in MPS II patients who started treatment before 12 months of age.

This study included MPS II male siblings, of which one sibling should have received Elaprase for at least 2 years. The younger sibling must have initiated Elaprase treatment before the age of 20 months. The younger sibling must have initiated treatment with at least a 12 month age difference compared to the older brother, in order to study the effect of an earlier start of treatment. There is no untreated control group to match the treated patients, which limits the interpretation and conclusions that can be drawn based on the data of this study.

The primary objective of is to evaluate effectiveness of Elaprase in MPS II patients who started treatment at or before the age of 12 months. Exploratory objectives are to assess the impact of treatment at an early age by comparing the effectiveness of the younger sibling to the effectiveness observed in the older sibling.

The studied outcomes are considered relevant for assessing effect of treatment on MPS-II. However as explained by the MAH, the rate of disease progression and disease severity vary widely amongst patients with MPS II, clinical manifestations are often noticed at the age of two to four years in children. Thus in this study, in these young patients, disease might not have manifested with a clinical profile, which limits the interpretation of the data.

The MAH has defined 5 cohorts, of which Cohort 1 (younger siblings who started treatment before the age of 12 months) and Cohort 2 (matching older siblings) are relevant for determining the primary objective. Data was abstracted from medical charts into an online electronic case report form (eCRF) of the CRO. In case data was not available from the charts, unknown/not available could be entered into the eCRF.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The plan was to include 25 sibling pairs (50 patients) with MPS II and in case enrolling 25 sibling pairs was not achievable, a minimum of 15 sibling pairs (30 patients) would be included. In total 38 patients (19 sibling pairs) were included in the study, of which 15 pairs were eligible for cohorts relevant for the primary objective. The MAH clarified that although the end of data collection occurred on 08 February 2023, the protocol had to be revised afterwards to take into consideration the smaller than expected sample size impacting the ranking of the study objectives. Consequently, the requirement of submitting paediatric study data within six months of completion of the trial was not met. All protocol and SAP amendments were made after the end of the data collection, which is in general not acceptable.

As could be expected, the age at MPS-II diagnosis was lower and time from diagnosis to treatment shorter in cohorts 1 and 4 (the younger sibling) than in the cohorts with the older siblings. The cohorts with the older siblings also contain a higher percentage of patients with the presence of cognitive impairment. The older siblings received a dose of 0.5 mg/kg once per week, conform SmPC. The younger siblings received a mean dose of 0.6 mg/kg once per week, which is higher than the recommended dose. A clarification was submitted that the higher mean dose is explained by the use of higher doses by one study site, to not waste medication. Although the use of off label doses in order to not waste medication is not supported, this is not considered a reason to change the product information. The fact that the younger age cohort received a different dose than the older siblings, compromises the exploratory comparison between the two groups.

The majority of patients received over 80% of all prescribed infusions. Patients died in all cohorts, with higher mortality rates in the cohorts with the older siblings. Which is to be expected since the follow-up time was longer and the older siblings had an higher age.

Height and BMI increased in both the younger sibling cohort 1 and the older sibling cohort 2. From a large number of patients in both cohorts liver size and urinary GAG levels were unknown, which makes interpretation difficult. From the limited number of patients with data available, liver size was relatively normal post-Elaprase treatment and urinary GAG levels decreased during treatment. Also LVMI showed a general decrease during treatment.

The presence of joint stiffness, coarse facial features, enlarged tonsils, spinal abnormality were much higher among the older siblings (cohort 2) than in the younger siblings at index date. It is considered that conclusions on reduction or increase in percentage of several clinical outcomes cannot be made since the percentage of unknown/not available and missing data is high in the follow-up period. For most parameters at several timepoints data is completely missing, or the percentage of unknown/not available data is 80-90% of patients. Also in regard to the 'first to event analysis' and the results of the generalized linear mixed models, the large amount of missing data prevents from drawing any relevant conclusions.

No dedicated safety assessment was performed in this study.

The study design and the large amount of missing data or data that is unknown/not available preclude from drawing any conclusions on efficacy. However, the limited data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase.

The MAH explained that this study was conducted to support the implementation of newborn screening for MPS II and for a potential label extension to younger patients. The current indication for Elaprase approved in the EU is 'Elaprase is indicated for the long-term treatment of patients with Hunter syndrome (Mucopolysaccharidosis II, MPS II)'. Thus MPS II patients, regardless of age, are covered by the current indication. The extension of the label is likely aimed at extending the label approved in the US. The MAH is requested to clarify the issue on the off-label use with a higher dose in the young patients.

Taking into account the absence of impact of the efficacy results on amendments to the SmPC, the raised issue regarding SAP and protocol amendments after data collection is not further pursued.

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

The study design and the large amount of missing data or data that is unknown/not available preclude from drawing any conclusions on efficacy. However, the limited data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase. No alterations to the product information are necessary.

## MAH responses to Request for supplementary information

Q1.a Reports of off-label use

The MAH would like to highlight that the reports the CHMP is referring to relate to an article by (Lampe et al. 2014) cited in the TAK-665-5001 CSR which was provided as a literature reference in the initial submission. Specifically, the article was referenced in Section 6.1 (page 87) of the CSR:

\"This variation…[in] dosing and off-label use has also been reported in another publication, where patients initially received a dose of 0.6 mg/kg weekly for the first eight infusions, until the infant's weight increased, and then a dose of 0.5 mg/kg was used for the ninth and tenth infusion.\"

Following the receipt of the RSI, the MAH identified another recent systematic literature review by AlHertani et al. (2024), which provides additional information regarding the use of intravenous (IV) idursulfase at doses other than 0.5 mg/kg. As noted by Al-Hertani et al., in the clinical trial publication authored by Muenzer et al. (2006), different dosages of IV idursulfase were explored, including 0.5

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

mg/kg administered every other week. In the first Phase 1/2 trial to evaluate IV idursulfase, Muenzer et al. (2006) administered dosages of 0.15, 0.5, or 1.5 mg/kg every other week. Results were pooled and showed an improvement in somatic outcomes with no clinically relevant differences across the 3 dosages (Muenzer et al. 2007).

## Q1.b Use of a higher dose in young patients

In Section 5.3.6 (page 37) of the CSR of TAK-665-5001, it is noted that \"Regarding the Elaprase dose, the variation in dosing was largely accounted for by a site effect.\" Upon looking at the data, there was only 1 site where higher initial idursulfase doses (1.2 mg/kg) were chosen in 3 younger siblings, which contributed to the higher mean dose of 0.6 mg/kg weekly in the younger sibling cohort versus the older sibling cohort. Every other site used the on-label dose of idursulfase (0.5 mg/kg).

As described in the article by Lampe et al. (2014), treating clinicians may opt to use full 6 mg vials for infusion to not waste medication. As the infants described in the article gained weight, the dose per kilogram body weight decreased until a 0.5 mg/kg dose was achieved. The clinical expert who the MAH consulted with confirmed this practice and provided additional explanation: For a very young patient (body weight &lt;6 kg), giving 1 vial of idursulfase may lead to giving a dose that is in excess of 1 mg/kg. The alternative of rounding down does not exist since this would mean giving nothing, and the option of giving a partial vial and throwing away the rest of a costly medication is not acceptable to some treaters. As noted in the article by Lampe et al., there were no adverse events related to enzyme replacement therapy among the patients treated with an initial dose greater than 0.5 mg/kg weekly.

## Assessment of the responses

The MAH has clarified that the higher mean dose in the younger siblings is explained by the administration of higher doses at one site. This was done in order not to waste medication. Although this is not understood, ERT is associated with potentially serious infusion related reactions which could be exacerbated in case of overdosing, the explanation is acknowledged and no further action is considered necessary.

Although limited, the provided case reports do not indicate safety concerns associated with the higher dose.

Issue resolved.
