## 2.3.2. Clinical study

## TAK-665-5002: Effectiveness of Treatment for MPS II: a retrospective chart review ExUS

## Description

Study TAK 665-5002 was conducted to provide real world evidence on the effectiveness of MPS II treatment with idursulfase (Elaprase) outside the US. Study TAK-665-5002 was a global, multi-centre, non-interventional retrospective chart review study to evaluate the real-world effectiveness of Elaprase in patients with MPS II.

Data will be collected from the date of MPS II diagnosis until the date of data entry, a switch to another ERT or investigational product, bone marrow transplantation (BMT), hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT), loss to follow-up, or death, whichever occurred first.

## CHMP comments

The MAH wanted to expand the knowledge on real world effectiveness of treatment of Hunter syndrome with Elaprase. This is supported.

This study concerns a multi-centre, non-interventional retrospective chart review with the aim to study the real world effectiveness of Elaprase treatment in MPS II patients. Time of index is the moment of Elaprase treatment initiation. Information from the pre-index period and post-index period of a patient is gathered from hospital charts.

It should be kept in mind that a retrospective chart study is prone to bias and limits information regarding the reason(s) to start treatment (e.g. due severity of disease or familial knowledge regarding the condition and treatment effect).

## Methods

## Study participants

## Inclusion criteria:

- The patient is male.
- The patient received a diagnosis of MPS II (defined as a documented deficiency of the enzyme ID2S) prior to the age of 6.
- The patient received Elaprase for treatment for at least 2 years OR the patient has never received any treatment for MPS II.
- The patient has at least 2 years of visit data available in the medical charts while using Elaprase.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Exclusion criteria:

- The patient received the MPS II diagnosis before the year 1997.
- The patient received treatment for MPS II with an ERT other than idursulfase (IT or IV) OR an investigational product prior to or within 2 years of initiating Elaprase treatment.
- The patient underwent BMT or HSCT prior to or within 2 years of initiating Elaprase treatment.

## CHMP comments

This study included MPS II males, which were diagnosed before age of 6. Participants should either be treated with Elaprase or never have received any treatment for MPS II. In case a patient is treated with Elaprase, at least 2 years of visit data should be available.

## Treatments

This is a non-interventional retrospective chart review study. However patients receiving Elaprase treatment for at least 2 years were included.

## Objective(s)

## Primary Objective

- Assess the clinical outcomes and survival in participants with MPS II at different treatment initiation ages and ERT status (ERT treated or ERT naïve) by:
- Evaluating clinical characteristics, progression of disease and survival, by characterizing treatment patterns and short and long-term clinical outcomes.
- Determining prevalence of cognitive impairment (CImp).
- Assess the healthcare resource utilisation (HCRU) associated with MPS II at different treatment initiation ages.

## Exploratory Objectives

- Evaluate short and long-term clinical outcomes by severity of CImp.
- Evaluate clinical outcomes by genotype.

## CHMP comments

The primary objective of the study is to evaluate real world effectiveness of Elaprase treatment in MPS II patients by assessing the impact of different initiation ages and ERT status on clinical outcomes and survival. Exploratory objectives are to evaluate impact of cognitive impairment and genotype on clinical outcomes.

The study set-up does not allow for a randomised and well controlled comparison and thus the ability to draw firm conclusions is limited.

As the exploratory objectives do not clearly relate to efficacy of Elaprase, these will not be further discussed.

## Statistical Methods

The analyses were purely descriptive as no predefined hypothesis was tested. Analyses were performed once the data from all participants were collected in the database, cleaned and final database lock had occurred (27 December 2024).

## Analysis Populations

The analysis will include all patients enrolled in the chart review study and who are eligible based on the inclusion/exclusion criteria. Analyses will be performed using subpopulations defined by exposure variables: different treatment initiation ages, ERT status, diagnosis, and genotype (severity of mutation).

The subpopulations of interest are:

Treatment Initiation Age (dependent on data availability)

The distribution of patients across different treatment initiation ages will determine if the following subpopulations are included in the final analyses.

- Cohort 1 Treated at &lt; 16 months (i.e., patients who started treatment before 16 months of age).
- Cohort 2 Treated between 16 months and 72 months of age (i.e., patients who started treatment between 16 months and 72 months of age).

The analysis for cohorts 1 and 2 will be data driven, i.e., the analysis will depend on the amount of follow-up data for specific outcomes captured at 5 years of age.

## ERT Status

- Cohort 3 ERT treated period (i.e., defined as the period when patients had received Elaprase treatment for at least 2 years).
- Cohort 4 ERT naïve period (i.e., defined as the period when patients had never received treatment for MPS II).

The ERT naïve period subpopulation (Cohort 4) will be the reference group.

Cognitive impairment diagnosis (dependent on data availability)

- Cohort 5 Ever diagnosed with CImp. (i.e., defined as a patient whose cognitive impairment was documented).
- Cohort 6 Never diagnosed with CImp.

The group never diagnosed with CImp will be the reference group.

Genotype (Severity of Mutation) (dependent on data availability)

- Cohort 7 Non-severe genetic mutation (i.e., patients with genetic mutation other than complex rearrangement/large deletion).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Cohort 8 Severe genetic mutation (i.e., patients with complex rearrangement/large deletion genetic mutation).

The group with severe mutations will be the reference group.

## General Considerations

Descriptive, time-to-event analyses and generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs) will be performed. No predefined hypothesis will be tested. Data for clinical characteristics, clinical outcomes, treatments received, and HCRU will be summarized by means, standard deviations (SD), medians, 10th and 90th percentiles, minimums, and maximums for continuous variables; and by numbers and percentages for categorical variables. All summarized data will be presented in aggregate, and stratified by characteristics of interest, such as treatment initiation age, ERT status, CImp and genotype (severity of mutation). The relevant denominator information will be provided in tables. Comprehensive listings for all gathered data will be included as appendices.

Follow-up time will be calculated in two ways. Follow-up time-1 will be defined as the time between the first and last recorded visit. The last recorded visit is defined as the last date the patients' data is abstracted from their study sites, discontinuation of care at the participating site, BMT or HSCT or initiation of an ERT or investigational product other than Elaprase, or death, whichever occurred first. Follow-up time-2 will be calculated as the time between the Index Date and last recorded visit. The last recorded visit is defined as the last date the patients' data is abstracted from their study sites, discontinuation of care at the participating site, BMT or HSCT or initiation of an ERT or investigational product other than Elaprase, or death, whichever occurred first.

For a selected list of continuous variables (anthropometric growth parameters, respiratory parameters, and 6MWT) descriptive analysis was reported at 60 months (5 years of age).

Resource use frequency was calculated by dividing the total number of resource use occurrences for a particular item by the total number of follow-up years or months. Follow-up years or months were calculated as the time between the first and last documented visit after treatment initiation.

For a selected list of continuous and categorical variables, descriptive summary statistics were reported at specific time points in 6-month intervals. 6-month intervals were selected due to the data's availability. The 6-month intervals were as follows: index date, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60, 66, and 72 up to last date of follow-up.

The KM method (Emmert-Streib and Dehmer 2019) (George et al. 2014) was used to describe cumulative incidence of \"first events\" from age at treatment initiation by cohort (per objective) for key variables. A sensitivity analysis using birth as the starting point was performed for selected variables, for example, cardiac valve disease, abnormal liver size, joint and stiffness.

The proportion of \"first events\" and its 95% confidence interval for each cohort was calculated. Corresponding p value was reported, but the interpretation of the Log-Rank test was only descriptive. No hypothesis testing applies to this data since this is a descriptive study.

## Analytical Approach

According to the primary objectives, GLMM was used to describe differences between Cohort 1 vs. Cohort 2 and Cohort 3 vs Cohort 4. For the latter, the GLMM analysis was the only statistical method/description used to analyse the treatment effect on each participant.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Cohorts 1 and 2: For a selected number of binary and continuous variables, an adjusted analysis with covariates was performed using GLMMs. Parameter coefficients were presented, and linearity assumption was checked. One model was run for each outcome variable with age at Elaprase treatment initiation (coded as binary variable: young/old) as the main exposure. The cohort who initiated Elaprase treatment at an older age was the reference cohort. Cohorts 3 and 4: To analyse the effect of treatment on each participant, each one was observed at two different periods: ERT treated period vs. ERT naïve period. Therefore, the same participant was observed twice (before and during Elaprase). An adjusted analysis with the random intercept and slope for age at measurement was performed using GLMM comparing ERT status. To avoid survival bias, the modelled data started at a common time point \"Age at MPS II diagnosis\" until of follow-up, and treatment period (ERT status) was modelled as a time varying covariate. For the treatment period, the value was 0 before the time the participant started treatment and changed to 1 after they started treatment. Thus, the coefficient of the treatment variable was indicative of the treatment effect. The choice of statistical model framework was dependent on the outcome of interest. One model was run for each outcome variable with ERT status (coded as binary variable: treated period/naïve period) as

the main exposure. The naïve period (Cohort 4) was the reference cohort. Missing Values Steps were taken to minimize the amount of missing data and optimize the integrity of the collected data, by ensuring clarity in the eCRF. In addition, the eCRF allowed for an \"unknown\" option for most questions, so information that was missing from a participant's chart was made explicit. Data fields indicated as \"unknown,\" reflecting missing data in the participant's chart, were addressed at the analytic stage. Data fields indicated as \"unknown\" or not available for a specific time point were accounted for. Descriptive statistics were restricted to the subset of participants for which the data were available, and the relevant denominator information was provided in the results tables. No imputation of missing data was conducted for this study. Missing data at \"Index Date\" For missing data at \"Index Date\", the last observation carried forward (LOCF) will be used. The cutoff point will be 12 months prior to \"Index Date\". The handling of outcomes with missing data with more than 12 months before \"Index Date\", will be decided during the analysis. This will be based on the distribution of the outcome and type of outcome. It might be decided to report a data point as missing, or the LOCF cutoff interval could be widened. Missing data at Post-index Period For the unadjusted descriptive analyses, if data was not recorded at a specific time point for: Data closest to a -90 day window to the 6 month data interval will be reported. If there is no data within the -90 day window, data closest to a +90 day window will be reported. If these two are missing, report as missing. GLMMs offer a simple alternative to handle missing data under the missing at random (MAR) assumption without requiring imputations. MAR means that given the observed data, the missingness mechanism does not depend on unobserved data; this is the missingness assumption typically used in multiple imputation. Under the MAR assumption, multiple imputation offers no additional value to GLMM analyses.

## CHMP comments

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The SAP and protocol have been amended very late in the study, just prior to data lock. See CHMP

comments on this in the recruitment section. The study set-up is prone to bias and confounding. Data was abstracted from medical charts into an online electronic case report form (eCRF) of the CRO. In case data was not available from the charts, unknown/not available could be entered into the eCRF. No explicit imputation for missing data was conducted for this study. This is understood as the disease is very heterogenous and as stated before disease progression and severity varies widely in patients, therefore predicting values might not be feasible. It should be noted that when using GLMM, imputation is implicit in the methodology and value of the \"predicted\" datapoint depends on the number of available data points. Multiple imputation would have been preferred as this might include more variables in the model next to age and exposure variable. It is noted that LOCF was used for data which was missing at the index date, which is not preferred considering the progressive nature of Hunter syndrome and the fact that the patients are children. Impact of treatment initiation age will be evaluated by comparing Cohort 1 and 2 using a descriptive analysis of selected continuous variables at 60 months of age and a generalised linear mixed model (GLMM) was used to descriptively analyse differences in the development of outcomes over time. Comparing the 2 groups at a distinct age is appreciated, however, it should be kept in mind that reason to start treatment is unknown and depends on clinician. In addition, start of treatment in Cohort 1 is likely not driven by disease as clinical manifestations are often noticed at the age of two to four years in children. Finally, at 5 years of age (60 months), treatment might not have started in all patients included in Cohort 2 (treatment start between 16 and 72 months of age). GLMM assumes a linear progression of the outcome measure, however, it is not clear that all of the outcome measures, especially the continuous variables, analysed change linearly over time. This should be kept in mind especially regarding outcomes related to disease progression as the rate of disease progression and severity varies widely amongst patients. It is not understood why patients with a treatment start &gt;72 months of age were not included in the analysis of impact of treatment initiation age in the CSR itself.

Impact of ERT treatment, i.e. treatment naïve vs treatment experienced, will be analysed in Cohort 3 and 4 using GLMM. Again this comparison is hampered by the obvious age difference in both Cohorts, as both cohorts include the same patients at different time points, which cannot be fully countered using GLMM due to potential non-linearity of the outcome measures.

No comparison can be made between cohorts for different exposure variables, as the same patients are included (e.g. a participant in Cohort 1 could also be included in Cohort 5 and 8).

## CHMP comments

The patient characteristics at index time are in line with expectations based on the subgroups.

The age at MPS-II diagnosis was substantially lower and time from diagnosis to treatment shorter in cohort 1 compared to cohort 2. It should be noted that the reason to start treatment was not recorded. As familial history was no longer recorded, no information on this was provided for Cohort 1 and 2. This is regretted as this might have given insight into time of diagnosis.

No clear differences were observed in age at MPS-II diagnosis or time from diagnosis to treatment between cohorts 5 and 6 or 7 and 8.

Patients died in all cohorts. Seeming differences in percentage of patients who died between cohort 1 and 2 and cohort 5 and 6 are potentially related to age of patients, as patients in cohort 2 and 5

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

were older compared to cohort 1 and 6 at last age at follow-up. Only in cohort 8, with the severe genetic mutations, as expected, the patients were younger and more patients died compared to cohort 7.

Almost all patients in all cohorts received a dose of 0.5 mg/kg once per week at treatment initiation, conform SmPC. During the study, only a few patients reported a change in frequency of dosing.

## CHMP comments

The comparison at month 60 between Cohort 1 and Cohort 2 was uninformative as no data was available for Cohort 1 and only a minority (13% of patients) had clinical outcomes reported at Month 60 in cohort 2.

The number of participants in cohort 1 (n=11) is very limited, while the number of participants in cohort 2 is more extensive (n=81). It is unclear how this is taken into account in the analyses.

The participants in Cohort 2 are older compared to participants in Cohort 1 and Hunter syndrome is a progressive disease. No clear conclusions can be drawn based on the GLMM analyses, as it is unclear that the measured outcomes respond in a linear fashion.

No firm conclusions can be drawn on HCRU due to low number of patients in cohort 1. In addition, the fact that reason to initiate treatment very early is unknown, further hampers assessment. It might be that participants in Cohort 1 were sicker compared to Cohort 2. Finally, age can influence a decision to visit ER and/or hospitalisation and/or test administration.

In total, 115 patients provided data for the GLMM analyses comparing Cohort 3 and 4, thus the comparison during the ERT naïve and ERT treatment period. It should be noted that &lt;20% of patients had information recorded for the measurements at the different time points, therefore interpretation of the data is hampered.

When comparing Cohort 3 and 4, GLMM analyses showed that uGAG levels reduced and slightly more measurements of \"normal liver size\" determined by abdominal ultrasound, which is expected. This indicates that in a real world setting, Elaprase still exerts beneficial effects. As Hunter Syndrome is a progressive disease, increases in counts of hearing problems, joint stiffness and coarse facial features are not considered worrying. Lack of a control group hampers any assessment whether treatment impacts disease progression.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Slight differences were seen in the information regarding CImp in Cohort 1 and 2. Slightly less patients were ever diagnosed with CImp, but this might be due to low numbers. The age of diagnosis overlaps between the 2 Cohorts. It is noteworthy that a vast majority, 83% (5/6 patients), were diagnosed using formal testing in Cohort 1, while this only occurred in 50% (29/58 patients) in Cohort 2. Slightly more patients were diagnosed with CImp in Cohort 8 compared to Cohort 7 and age of diagnosis was earlier in Cohort 8. as do severity scores. However, severity of CImp was for the majority mild in Cohort 8 and more moderate and severe CImp was seen in Cohort 7. No clear pattern with regards to CImp can be observed. Small numbers , lack of a control group with matched patients hampers the drawing of any clear conclusions on CImp.

In conclusion, the study design and the large amount of missing data or data that is unknown/not available preclude from drawing any conclusions on efficacy. However, the limited data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase.

## Safety results

No specific adverse events or reactions were reported in the medical records of these participants. There was 1 participant who discontinued Elaprase treatment due to an AE. The event was assessed as not related to Elaprase by both, the Investigator and the Sponsor.

## CHMP comments

It is regretted that no dedicated safety assessment was performed in this study. However, it is understandable since the study was not designed to gather safety data. One SAE was reported. It is agreed that this SAE is unlikely related to Elaprase treatment, due to time of onset, and other aetiologies such as medical history and underlying disease could contribute.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

This submission concerns a multi-centre, non-interventional retrospective chart review study with the aim to study the real world effectiveness of Elaprase treatment in MPS II patients based on age at treatment initiation and ERT status.

The study included male MPS II patients who were diagnosed before the age of 6. Although patients who were never treated for MPS II could be included, all patients included in the study were treated with Elaprase for at least 2 years. The lack of an untreated control group to match the treated patients, limits the interpretation and conclusions that can be drawn based on the data of this study. The relatively small number of patients in the different subgroups further restricts the evaluable information.

The primary objective of the study is to evaluate real world effectiveness of Elaprase treatment in MPS II patients by assessing the impact of different initiation ages and ERT status on clinical outcomes and survival. The studied outcomes are considered relevant for assessing effect of treatment on MPS-II.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

However as explained by the MAH, the rate of disease progression and disease severity vary widely amongst patients with MPS II and lack of an untreated control group hampers true effectiveness evaluation. In addition, the fact that the reason to start treatment is unknown is an issue as the reason could have impact on clinical outcomes. The study set-up does not allow for a randomised, well-controlled comparison and is prone to bias and confounding. This is further impacted by the fact that a protocol amendment was performed and SAP finalisation took place late in the study, when a substantial amount of information would have been available. No clear presentation of the changes and reasons why the changes were implemented was provided, therefore no assessment can be made as to whether these changes were data driven. The main assessment of impact of early treatment start and ERT status uses a generalised linear mixed model (GLMM) analysis. GLMM assumes a linear progression of the outcome measure, however, it is not clear that all of the outcome measures, especially the continuous variables, analysed change linearly over time. The limited data that is available does not contradict the known efficacy profile of Elaprase. When analysing the impact of ERT status, it was seen that after start treatment uGAG levels reduced and slightly more measurements of \"normal liver size\" as determined by abdominal ultrasound were observed, indicating that in a real world setting, Elaprase still exerts beneficial effects. However, it should be noted that limited data is available at the different time points, as &lt;20% of patients reported outcomes at any timepoint, which limits conclusions that can be drawn. The lack of a control group hampers any assessment whether treatment impacts disease progression. Analysis of the impact of treatment start is hindered by the lack of data. The comparison at month 60 between Cohort 1 and Cohort 2 was uninformative, as no data was available for Cohort 1 and only a minority (13% of patients) had clinical outcomes reported at Month 60 in Cohort 2. In addition, as it is unclear that the measured outcomes respond in a linear fashion, GLMM analyses are of limited value. Finally, as observed for Cohort 3, the amount of data available after the index date is limited and it is unclear how many patients contributed to information regarding the comparison between Cohort 1 and 2 at each time point. No firm conclusions can be drawn on HCRU due to low number of patients in cohort 1. In addition, the fact that reason to initiate treatment very early in Cohort 1 is unknown, which hampers assessment of true impact of Elaprase on HCRU. Finally, age can influence a decision to visit ER, hospitalisation and/or test administration. No dedicated safety assessment was performed in this study. In conclusion, no changes to the SmPC are recommended. Just prior to study end, major amendments to the protocol were implemented and the primary endpoint and inclusion criteria were altered. It is unknown whether any of these changes were data driven. In addition, the study design, including lack of control group, and the large amount of missing data or data that is unknown/not available preclude from drawing any conclusions on efficacy. Therefore this study, will not provide evidence that is of similar quality to the information already included in the SmPC which were obtained in randomised controlled trials. Taking into account the methodological issues and absence of impact of the on the SmPC, the raised issues are not further pursued.

The benefit-risk of Elaprase remains positive.
