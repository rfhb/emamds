## 1. Background information on the procedure

## 1.1. Submission of the dossier

The applicant Gedeon Richter Plc. submitted on 18 November 2025 an application for marketing authorisation to the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for Inijaq, through the centralised procedure under Article 3 (3) of Regulation (EC) No. 726/2004- 'Generic of a Centrally authorised product'. The eligibility to the centralised procedure was agreed upon by the EMA/CHMP on 12 December 2024.

The application concerns a generic medicinal product as defined in Article 10(2)(b) of Directive 2001/83/EC and refers to a reference product, as defined in Article 10 (2)(a) of Directive 2001/83/EC, for which a marketing authorisation is or has been granted in the Union on the basis of a complete dossier in accordance with Article 8(3) of Directive 2001/83/EC.

The applicant applied for the following indications:

## Rheumatoid arthritis

Tofacitinib in combination with methotrexate (MTX) is indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe active rheumatoid arthritis (RA) in adult patients who have responded inadequately to, or who are intolerant to one or more disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) (see section 5.1).

Tofacitinib can be given as monotherapy in case of intolerance to MTX or when treatment with MTX is inappropriate (see sections 4.4 and 4.5).

## Psoriatic arthritis

Tofacitinib in combination with MTX is indicated for the treatment of active psoriatic arthritis (PsA) in adult patients who have had an inadequate response or who have been intolerant to a prior diseasemodifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy (see section 5.1).

## Ankylosing spondylitis

Tofacitinib is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with active ankylosing spondylitis (AS) who have responded inadequately to conventional therapy.

## Ulcerative colitis

Tofacitinib is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC) who have had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to either conventional therapy or a biologic agent (see section 5.1).

## Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA)

Tofacitinib is indicated for the treatment of active polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (rheumatoid factor positive [RF+] or negative [RF-] polyarthritis and extended oligoarthritis), and juvenile psoriatic arthritis (PsA) in patients 2 years of age and older, who have responded inadequately to previous therapy with DMARDs.

Tofacitinib can be given in combination with methotrexate (MTX) or as monotherapy in case of intolerance to MTX or where continued treatment with MTX is inappropriate.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Problem statement

This application for a marketing authorisation submitted via centralised procedure concerns a generic application according to article 10(1) of Directive 2001/83/EC for Inijaq, film-coated tablet 5 mg from Gedeon Richter Plc.

The reference medicinal product is Xeljanz, 5 mg film-coated tablets (MAA No: EU/1/17/1178/003, EU/1/17/1178/004, EU/1/17/1178/0014, MAH: Pfizer Europe MA EEIG) for which marketing authorisation was granted in the European Union on 22 March 2017 on the basis of a complete dossier in accordance with Article 8(3) of Directive 2001/83/EC.

Inijaq, film-coated tablet 5 mg from Gedeon Richter Plc. is manufactured by GEDEON RICHTER POLSKA Sp. z o.o. The medicinal product contains pharmaceutically approved excipients.

Tofacitinib is a potent, selective inhibitor of the JAK family. In enzymatic assays, tofacitinib inhibits JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and to a lesser extent TyK2. In human cells, tofacitinib preferentially inhibits signalling by heterodimeric cytokine receptors that associate with JAK3 and/or JAK1 with functional selectivity over cytokine receptors that signal via pairs of JAK2. Inhibition of JAK1 and JAK3 by tofacitinib attenuates signalling of interleukins (IL-2, -4, -6, -7, -9, -15, -21) and type I and type II interferons, which will result in modulation of the immune and inflammatory response.

To support the application, the applicant has submitted a The Biopharmaceutics Classification System (BCS)-based biowaiver in line with the ICH M9 guideline on biopharmaceutics classification systembased biowaivers (EMA/CHMP/ICH/493213/2018).

The safety and efficacy profile of tofacitinib has been demonstrated in clinical trials for the reference medicinal product. In addition, there is a long-term post-marketing experience conducted by the MAH of Xeljanz contributing to the knowledge of the clinical use of this active substance.

The applicant of the generic IMA has applied for all the indications of the reference product.

The following guidelines are applicable:

- ICH M13A Guideline on bioequivalence for immediate-release solid oral dosage forms (EMA/CHMP/ICH/953493/2022).

It has a relative molecular mass of 504.5 and the following structure:

## Figure 1. Active substance structure

The chemical structure of tofacitinib citrate was elucidated by a combination of elemental analysis, infrared spectroscopy, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (1H, 13C, HMBC, COSY), UV spectroscopy and mass spectrometry.

The solid-state properties of the active substance were measured by XRPD and DSC. Polymorphism has been observed for tofacitinib citrate. The seven crystalline forms that are reported in literature were summarised along with their XRPD patterns and DSC data. The crystalline form of tofacitinib citrate does not change during storage.

Tofacitinib citrate is a white or almost white crystalline powder and it is non-hygroscopic.

Tofacitinib citrate demonstrates pH-dependent equilibrium solubility, varying from very slightly soluble to sparingly soluble in aqueous media over the pH range of 1.2-12.0.

## 2.2.2.4. Stability

Stability data from 12 batches (including 9 production scale batches) of active substance from the proposed manufacturer stored in the intended commercial package for up to 24 months under long term conditions (25 ºC / 60% RH), up to 12 months under intermediate conditions (30 °C/ 65% RH) and for up to 6 months under accelerated conditions (40 ºC / 75% RH) according to the ICH guidelines were provided.

The following parameters were tested: appearance, identification (IR, HPLC or XRPD), loss on drying, citric acid, chiral purity, related substances, assay, microbial limit test. All tested parameters were within the specifications, and no significant trends were observed under long term, intermediate and accelerated conditions.

Photostability testing following the ICH guideline Q1B was performed as part of a stress testing study applying forced degradation conditions (acid, base, oxidation, thermal/high humidity, thermal/low humidity &amp; photolytic degradation). Tofacitinib citrate is not sensitive to light. It is unstable under acid, base and oxidation degradation conditions. Polymorphism was tested by XRPD for samples under thermal stress (with high and low humidity) and photolytic stress, and results obtained demonstrated that no crystal transformation occurred. Mass balance and peak purity results confirm that the HPLC methods are stability indicating.

A commitment is made to continue the ongoing stability studies of the active substance, and thereafter one commercial batch per year will be placed on stability under long term conditions. Any confirmed out-of-specification result, or significant negative trend, should be reported to the Rapporteur and EMA.

The stability results indicate that the active substance manufactured by the proposed supplier(s) is sufficiently stable. The stability results justify the proposed retest period of 36 months in the proposed container.

## 2.2.3. Finished Medicinal Product

## 2.2.3.1. Description of the product and pharmaceutical development

The finished product is a white or almost white, round, biconvex film-coated tablet of about 8 mm in diameter. The tablet is engraved with \"5\" on one side.

## Pharmaceutical development

The pharmaceutical development of the finished product contains QbD elements. A quality target product profile (QTPP) was established with the aim to develop an immediate release oral dosage form that is bioequivalent to the reference product Xeljanz 5 mg film-coated tablets based on a BCS-based biowaiver approach, pharmaceutically equivalent with respect to the dosage form, strength, quality characteristics, and packed in a suitable container closure system to achieve the target product shelflife.

The critical quality attributes (CQAs) identified were average tablet mass, uniformity of tablet mass, dissolution, active substance content, uniformity of dosage units, chromatographic purity, identification and microbiological purity.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The physicochemical characteristics of the active substance (including solubility, polymorphic form and particle size) that could impact the CQAs, performance and manufacturing of the finished product are adequately discussed. Tofacitinib citrate is highly soluble since the highest single dose is completely dissolved in 250 ml aqueous media over the pH range 1.2-6.8. In addition, it exhibits low permeability which classifies the active substance under BCS class III. Tofacitinib citrate is obtained as a single crystal form as described in the active substance section and no polymorphic conversion has been observed in active substance stability studies, neither in development stability studies nor process optimisation tests with the finished product. Different particle size distributions have been investigated which were shown not to impact solubility (across the pH range 1.2-6.8), dissolution (&gt;85% in 15 minutes), finished product processability and CQAs. The particle size is controlled in the active substance specification. All excipients are well known pharmaceutical ingredients, and their quality is compliant with Ph. Eur. standards, except for the commercial film-coating. However, its basic components comply with Ph. Eur. standards. There are no novel excipients used in the finished product formulation. The compatibility of the active substance with the selected excipients was further confirmed with binary mixtures (4 weeks at 25 °C/60% RH, 40 °C/75% RH and 50°C, ICH Q1B conditions) and small-scale stability batches. The formulation was developed to address the requirements for a BCS-based biowaiver laid down in Appendix III of EMA guideline on investigation of bioequivalence and ICH M9 guideline on biopharmaceutics classification system-based biowaivers. The BCS-based biowaiver requirements were considered by selecting excipients that are qualitatively the same (except for the film-coating) and quantitatively similar to the reference product Xeljanz 5 mg film-coated tablets. During formulation development it was also verified whether small changes in the quantitative composition had an impact on the formulation robustness. Laboratory scale batches were prepared for this purpose which included changes in the proportion of tablet fillers, the amount of lubricant, and the amount of disintegrant. The applied deviation ranges complied with the ICH M9 criteria to demonstrate quantitative similarity for products containing BCS Class III drugs. No negative impact was observed on the finished product CQAs, and the proposed commercial formulation is shown to be robust. Furthermore, different excipient grades have been tested in several lab-scale batches to study their impact on the finished product CQAs or processability. The formulation and manufacturing process development have been evaluated through the use of risk assessment to identify the critical product quality attributes and critical process parameters. The risk analysis was performed using the failure mode effect analysis (FMEA) method to determine the active substance attributes, critical process steps and process parameters that may have an influence on the finished product quality attributes. ## 2.4.1. Exemption

## Solubility

In line with ICH M9: \"A drug substance is classified as highly soluble if the highest single therapeutic dose is completely soluble in 250 ml or less of aqueous media over the pH range of 1.2-6.8 at 37±1°C.\"

The solubility testing confirmed that the highest single therapeutic dose (of 10 mg) is dissolved in 250 ml at the 3 pH conditions, in line with ICH M9. Tofacitinib citrate can be considered a highly soluble drug substance.

Permeability In line with ICH M9: \"High permeability can be concluded when the absolute bioavailability is ≥85%. High permeability can also be concluded if ≥85% of the administered dose is recovered in urine as unchanged (parent drug), or as the sum of parent drug, Phase 1 oxidative and Phase 2 conjugative metabolites.\"

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Based on the literature provided, the absolute bioavailability of tofacitinib is 74% in healthy subjects and recovery in urine is reported as 28.8%. As high permeability cannot be demonstrated, tofacitinib is considered a low permeable drug substance.

Based on the solubility and permeability information, tofacitinib is a BCS class III substance.

## Composition and excipients

In line with ICH M9: \"For BCS Class III drugs, all of the excipients should be qualitatively the same and quantitatively similar (except for film coating or capsule shell excipients). Excipients that may affect absorption should be qualitatively the same and quantitatively similar, i.e. within ± 10% of the amount of excipient in the reference product, and the cumulative difference for these excipients should be within ± 10%.\"

The difference between the excipients amounts in the tested and reference product is within the criteria of ICH M9 for BCS class III substance. No excipient is considered to affect absorption in the quantity presented. Furthermore, the polymorphic form is stable at all conditions (refer to the Quality part).

## Comparative In vitro Dissolution

In line with ICH M9: \"To qualify for a BCS-based biowaiver for BCS Class III drug substances both the test product and reference product should display very rapid (≥85% for the mean percent dissolved in ≤15 minutes) in vitro dissolution characteristics under the defined conditions.\"

Different batches of the reference product were compared with a sufficient number of batches of the test product (all of at least of 100,000 tablet batch size) in the following media: pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8.

Under all pH conditions the in vitro release of both the test and the reference products was very rapid (more than 85% within 15 minutes). The test product thus fulfils the requirements of a BCS-based biowaiver approach as a BCS class III drug substance.

## 3. Benefit/risk assessment

This application concerns a generic version of tofacitinib citrate, film-coated tablet. The reference product Xeljanz is indicated for Rheumatoid arthritis, Psoriatic arthritis, Ankylosing spondylitis, Ulcerative colitis and Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA). See the recommendation section for the full wording of the approved indications.

No nonclinical studies have been provided for this application but an adequate summary of the available non-clinical information for the active substance was presented and considered sufficient.

From a clinical perspective, this application does not contain new data on the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics as well as the efficacy and safety of the active substance; the applicant's clinical overview on these clinical aspects based on information from published literature is considered sufficient.

The applicant has applied for a BCS-based biowaiver as a surrogate for in vivo bioequivalence.

In line with the ICH M13A Guideline on bioequivalence for immediate-release solid oral dosage forms (EMA/CHMP/ICH/953493/2022) \"The Biopharmaceutics Classification System (BCS)-based biowaiver may be used to waive in vivo BE studies for certain orally administered IR solid dosage forms\" given that \"an assumption of equivalence in in vivo performance can be justified by satisfactory in vitro data\"

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

as further delineated in the ICH M9 Guideline on biopharmaceutics classification system-based biowaivers (EMA/CHMP/ICH/493213/2018).

Accordingly, to justify the equivalent in vivo performance, the applicant first demonstrated the biopharmaceutic classification of the drug substance, and second, proved that the product qualifies for a BCS-based biowaiver as mentioned below.

The applicant has demonstrated that the high solubility and low permeability classify tofacitinib citrate as a BCS class III substance. Further to that, the excipients/composition and comparative in vitro dissolution tests between the test and reference products were presented. The excipient difference between the test and reference products are minimal (qualitatively the same and quantitatively similar) fulfilling the requirements of a BCS class III product. The comparative in vitro dissolution testing method for the BCS biowaiver demonstrated a very rapid release of both products, confirming the test product fulfils the BCS-based biowaiver requirements in line with ICH M9.

## 4. Recommendations

## Outcome

Based on the CHMP review of data on quality, safety and efficacy, the CHMP considers by majority decision that the benefit-risk balance of Inijaq is favourable in the following indications:

## Rheumatoid arthritis

Tofacitinib in combination with methotrexate (MTX) is indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe active rheumatoid arthritis (RA) in adult patients who have responded inadequately to, or who are intolerant to one or more disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) (see section 5.1).

Tofacitinib can be given as monotherapy in case of intolerance to MTX or when treatment with MTX is inappropriate (see sections 4.4 and 4.5).

## Psoriatic arthritis

Tofacitinib in combination with MTX is indicated for the treatment of active psoriatic arthritis (PsA) in adult patients who have had an inadequate response or who have been intolerant to a prior diseasemodifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy (see section 5.1).

## Ankylosing spondylitis

Tofacitinib is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with active ankylosing spondylitis (AS) who have responded inadequately to conventional therapy.

## Ulcerative colitis

Tofacitinib is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC) who have had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to either conventional therapy or a biologic agent (see section 5.1).

## Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA)

Tofacitinib is indicated for the treatment of active polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (rheumatoid factor positive [RF+] or negative [RF-] polyarthritis and extended oligoarthritis), and juvenile psoriatic arthritis (PsA) in patients 2 years of age and older, who have responded inadequately to previous therapy with DMARDs.

Tofacitinib can be given in combination with methotrexate (MTX) or as monotherapy in case of intolerance to MTX or where continued treatment with MTX is inappropriate.
