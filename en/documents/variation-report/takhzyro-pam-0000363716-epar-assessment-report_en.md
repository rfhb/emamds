## 1. Introduction

On 27 July 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for TAKHZYRO, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that \"TAK-743-4039: RESILIENT: Real-world Effectiveness of Sustained Improvements through Lanadelumab Enduring Treatment\" is a stand-alone study

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Commercial Takhzyro was used.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## Introduction

Lanadelumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody against plasma kallikrein that prevents the cleavage of high-molecular-weight kininogen and generation of bradykinin. Lanadelumab has been approved in several countries and regions for routine prevention of attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE).

TAK-743-4039 was conducted in the United States to determine the association between long-term lanadelumab use and the trends in attacks among patients with HAE who have used lanadelumab for at least 6 months. Subjects of all ages were eligible to the study.

## Assessor's comment

As this is a p46 procedure for a Phase 4 observational study that is not part of the EU RMP, only the paediatric data are assessed.

## Clinical study

## Clinical study number and title

TAK-743-4039: RESILIENT: Real-world Effectiveness of Sustained Improvements through Lanadelumab Enduring Treatment

## Description

TAK-743-4039 was a retrospective cohort study of patients with HAE who were treated with lanadelumab in the United States. The study was initiated on 09 September 2025 and completed on 06 March 2026.

## Methods

## Study participants

Patients were identified using the PIONEER-HAE database, a fully de-identified and Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act-compliant repository containing data specific to patients in care by the Consortium of Independent Immunology Clinics, a network with over 150 locations serving nearly 2 million patients residing in all 50 states in the US. Patients were selected for inclusion in the cohort if they had at least 1 drug line for lanadelumab ( \"episode\") during the selection window of 01 August 2018 to 31 May 2025. Only the first lanadelumab episode persisting for at least 6 months was used for analysis.

Diagnosis of HAE (Type I/II) in the PIONEER-HAE database was required for eligibility. No age-related criterion applied for entry.

## Treatments

TAK-743-4039 was a retrospective observational study using commercial Takhzyro according to the prescribers' ordinations.

## Objectives

The primary objective of the study was to quantify the long-term trends in attacks among patients with HAE who were on lanadelumab for at least 6 months. The exploratory objective was to describe persistence, reasons for discontinuation, and dosing interval changes of lanadelumab among patients with HAE who were on treatment for at least 6 months.

## Outcomes/endpoints

The primary endpoint was the percentage of patients who were attack free, defined as having no recorded HAE attack, during each interval.

## Sample size

All patients in the PIONEER-HAE database fulfilling the eligibility criteria could be included in the study.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Not applicable

## Statistical Methods

Means, standard deviations, medians, interquartile ranges, and 90% CI (where appropriate) were provided for continuous variables. For data related to monthly attack rates, the Wilcoxon rank- sum test was used to determine statistically significant (p-value &lt;.05) median attack rates when comparing outcome intervals to the first interval after Lanadelumab start.

Among patients with missingness of attack data, imputation of missing data was performed using MICE (Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations).

The study population included 26 patients &lt;17 years of age. One patient was &lt; 6 years of age, 8 patients were 6-11 years of age, and 17 patients were 12-17 years of age.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

TAK-743-4039 was a retrospective cohort study of patients with HAE who were treated with lanadelumab in the United States.

Twenty-six patients ≤18 years of age were included in the study. At baseline, less than half of patients in the paediatric population were attack-free. By Interval 1, 71% (ages 12-17) and 100% (ages 6-11 and 2-5) were attack-free. At Interval 4, over half remained attack-free-63% in the 12-17 group and 100% in the 6-11 group.

In the paediatric subgroup, which included patients aged 2-17 years at the start of lanadelumab treatment, nearly all days were spent attack-free during both the Baseline (97.6% and 98.6% for 1217 years and 2-11 years, respectively) and Follow-up Intervals (99.8% and 100%).

The mean (SD) baseline monthly attack rate was 0.45 +/- 0.47 attacks per month in the 12-17-year cohort and 0.31+/-0.29 in the 2-11-year cohort. Over all follow up intervals, the attack rate was 0.02+/-0.03 attacks per month in the 12-17-year cohort and 0.00 +/- 0.00 in the 2-11-year cohort.

The effectiveness of lanadelumab in the paediatric subpopulation of study TAK-743-4039 is largely in line with the previous experience of lanadelumab.

Safety data was not collected but the MAH reports no spontaneously informed safety issues.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

The effectiveness of lanadelumab in the paediatric subpopulation of study TAK-743-4039 is largely in line with the previous experience of lanadelumab. Safety data was not collected but the MAH reports no spontaneously informed safety issues.

No additional actions are considered warranted based on the data in the paediatric subpopulation of the study.

The benefit/risk ratio for Takhzyro remains unchanged.

No regulatory action required.
