## 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

VaxRabeo is indicated for pre-exposure and post-exposure prophylaxis against rabies in individuals of all age groups.

VaxRabeo should be used in accordance with official recommendations.

A booster dose consists of one dose of 0.5 mL given by intramuscular route or one dose of 0.1 mL given by intradermal route in accordance with WHO recommendations.

## Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)

PEP should be initiated as soon as possible after suspected exposure to rabies.

In all cases, proper wound care (careful washing of all bites and scratches with soap or detergent and copious amounts of water and/or virucidal agents) must be performed immediately or as soon as possible after exposure. It must be performed before administration of rabies vaccine or rabies immunoglobulins, when they are indicated.

The rabies vaccine administration must be performed strictly in accordance with the category of exposure, the patient immune status, the animal's status for rabies, and according to official recommendations (see Table 2 for WHO recommendations).

## PEP of individuals previously immunised

According to WHO recommendations, previously immunised individuals are individuals who have documented evidence of previous PrEP or already received PEP or who discontinued PEP after receiving at least two vaccinations.

These individuals should receive 1 dose of vaccine intramuscularly (0.5 mL) or intradermally (0.1 mL) on Days 0 and 3.

Rabies immunoglobulin is not indicated for previously immunised individuals.

These schedules should not apply to immunocompromised individuals.

## Interchangeability

No interchangeability studies have been conducted with VaxRabeo during a PrEP course or a PEP course.

According to WHO recommendations:

- Where continuation with the same rabies vaccine is not possible, another rabies vaccine may be used to complete the vaccination course.
- If unavoidable, the route of administration may be changed during a PEP or a PrEP course.

## Immunocompromised individuals

## PrEP

Serology testing of neutralising antibodies should be performed after completion of the primary series (see Table 1), and after each booster dose to assess the possible need for an additional dose of the vaccine. Official recommendations for immunocompromised individuals should be followed.

## PEP

Only vaccination schedules listed in Table 3 must be administered. Serology testing of neutralising antibodies may be indicated. Official recommendations for immunocompromised individuals should be followed. Rabies immunoglobulins should be given concomitantly with the vaccine for both category II &amp; III exposures in all cases, even if previously immunised (see Table 2 for category definition).

## Paediatric population

The doses and vaccination schedules for paediatric population are the same as those for adults, with the exception that the intradermal PEP IPC regimen is for paediatric individuals only.

## Method of administration

VaxRabeo is for intramuscular or intradermal use only.

## Intramuscular use

The vaccine (0.5 mL) is administered in the anterolateral region of the thigh muscle in infants and young children and in the deltoid muscle in older children and adults.

## Intradermal use

The vaccine (0.1 mL) is administered in the upper arm or the forearm, or the anterolateral thigh, or suprascapular region.

When 2 doses of rabies vaccine are administered at the same visit, they should be injected in 2 different sites/limbs.

For administration precautions, see section 4.4.

## 4.3 Contraindications

## PrEP

Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1.

## PEP

Since rabies infection almost always results in death, there are no contraindications to post-exposure vaccination (see section 4.4).

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

## Traceability

In order to improve the traceability of biological medicinal products, the name and the batch number of the administered product should be clearly recorded.

## Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis

Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been observed with vaccines. Appropriate medical treatment and supervision should always be readily available in the event of an anaphylactic reaction following administration of VaxRabeo.

In individuals who have experienced an anaphylactic reaction following a dose of VaxRabeo, the risk of developing rabies must be carefully considered in decisions regarding the discontinuation of PEP vaccination.

## Hypersensitivity to neomycin

Although antibiotics are not used in the manufacturing process, neomycin is present in the starting materials. Therefore, undetectable traces of neomycin might be present in the vaccine. Caution should be exercised when the vaccine is administered to individuals with hypersensitivity to this antibiotic.

## Anxiety-related reactions

Anxiety-related reactions, including vasovagal reactions (syncope), hyperventilation or stress-related reactions, may occur in association with vaccination as a psychogenic response to the needle injection. Procedures should be in place to avoid injury from fainting and to manage syncope.

## Concurrent illness

For PrEP, administration of VaxRabeo should be postponed in individuals suffering from moderate or severe acute illness with or without fever.

## Thrombocytopenia and coagulation disorders

As with all injectable vaccines, VaxRabeo should be administered with caution to individuals with thrombocytopenia or a bleeding disorder since bleeding may occur following an intramuscular administration.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Immunocompromised individuals

VaxRabeo has not been studied in immunocompromised individuals.

Individuals with congenital or acquired immunodeficiency may have reduced immune response to the vaccine. Therefore, it is recommended to serologically monitor RVNA (Rabies Virus Neutralising Antibodies) level in such individuals to ensure that an acceptable immune response has been induced. Additional vaccine doses should be given as necessary per official recommendations (see sections 4.2 and 4.5).

Moreover, if post-exposure vaccination is needed, only schedules of vaccination listed in Table 3 for previously non-immunised individuals should be administered. In addition, rabies immunoglobulin should be given concomitantly with the vaccine for category II &amp; III exposures in all cases, even if previously immunised (see section 4.2).

## Limitations of vaccine effectiveness

As with any vaccine, vaccination with VaxRabeo may not protect 100% of vaccinated individuals. In order to reach a sufficient level of neutralising antibodies for protection, recommendations for the use of VaxRabeo must be strictly followed (see section 4.2) as an insufficient immune response may lead to fatal cases of rabies.

Rabies PEP should not be interrupted due to any adverse reactions; serious adverse reactions are rare and need careful management (see section 4.8).

## Administration precautions

Do not inject into the gluteal area as administration in this area may result in lower neutralising antibody titres.

It is essential that intradermal administration of VaxRabeo is carried out only by medical staff trained in this technique in order to ensure that the vaccine is delivered intradermally. For the intradermal route, appropriate syringe and needle should be used.

## Excipients with known effect

## Phenylalanine

This vaccine contains 19.8 micrograms phenylalanine per dose (0.5 mL). Phenylalanine may be harmful for people with phenylketonuria (PKU), a rare genetic disorder in which phenylalanine builds up because the body cannot remove it properly.

## Proline

This vaccine contains 13.8 micrograms of proline per dose (0.5 mL). In patients suffering from hyperprolinaemia, exposure to proline should be minimised. This vaccine should be used in patients suffering from hyperprolinaemia only if strictly necessary.

## Sodium

This vaccine contains less than 1 mmol sodium (23 mg) per dose (0.5 mL), that is to say essentially 'sodium-free'.

## Sorbitol

This vaccine contains 6 mg sorbitol per dose (0.5 mL). The additive effect of concomitantly administered medicinal products containing sorbitol (or fructose) and dietary intake of sorbitol (or fructose) should be taken into account.

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

## Use with systemic immunosuppressive medicinal products

Immunosuppressive treatment, including long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy or chloroquine or medicinal products related to chloroquine, may interfere with antibody production. It is therefore advisable to perform a serological test after the last vaccine injection and administer additional doses if necessary (see section 4.2).

## Use with rabies immunoglobulin

The recommendation of administration of rabies immunoglobulin must be strictly followed (see section 4.2) since rabies immunoglobulin can interfere with development of immune response to the vaccine (see section 5.1).

## Use with other vaccines

No data are available regarding the concurrent administration of VaxRabeo with other vaccines.

Separate syringes and separate injection sites must be used in case of concomitant administration with other vaccines or other medicinal product including rabies immunoglobulin (see section 6.2).

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation

## Pregnancy

The use of VaxRabeo has not been studied in pregnant women.

Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects with respect to reproductive toxicity (see section 5.3).

## PrEP

VaxRabeo should be used in pregnant individuals only if the potential benefits to the mother outweigh the potential risks, including those to the foetus.

## PEP

Due to the severity of rabies disease, VaxRabeo can be administered during pregnancy.

## Breast-feeding

It is unknown whether VaxRabeo is excreted in human milk. No risk is anticipated for infants receiving breast milk.

## PrEP

VaxRabeo can be administered to breast-feeding women following an assessment of the risks and benefits.

## PEP

Due to the severity of rabies disease, VaxRabeo can be administered during breast-feeding.

## Fertility

No fertility data are available in humans.

A developmental toxicity study conducted in female rabbits did not show any effects on mating performance or female fertility (see section 5.3).

Non-clinical data on male fertility were not generated.

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines

VaxRabeo has no or negligible influence on the ability to drive and use machines. However, some of the adverse reactions mentioned under section 4.8 \"Undesirable effects\" (e.g. malaise, dizziness) may temporarily affect the ability to drive or use machines.

## 4.8 Undesirable effects

## Summary of the safety profile

In adults, the most frequently reported adverse reactions were injection site pain by intradermal use (44.2%) and by intramuscular use (40.5%), myalgia (32%), headache (21.4%) and malaise (16.5%).

In the paediatric population, the most frequently reported local adverse reactions were injection site tenderness/pain by intramuscular use (58.7%) and injection site swelling by intradermal use (47.3%).

In children 12 months to 23 months of age, the most frequently reported systemic adverse reactions were crying abnormal (47.4%) and irritability (47.4%), and in children 2 years of age and above, the most frequently reported systemic adverse reaction was myalgia (37.7%).

The following adverse reactions were more frequently reported in paediatric population than in adults: injection site tenderness/pain (intramuscular use) (58.7% and 40.5% respectively), injection site erythema (intradermal use) (35.7% and 0% respectively), injection site swelling (intradermal use) (47.3% and 0% respectively), fever (10.6% and 1.3% respectively).

The reported adverse reactions were mostly mild (Grade 1) or moderate (Grade 2) in intensity, occurred within 3 days after vaccination, and mostly resolved spontaneously after 1 to 3 days.

The reactogenicity profile was comparable after each of the consecutive priming doses.

The most frequently reported adverse reactions after a booster dose of VaxRabeo by intramuscular use were generally consistent with those reported during primary series: local pain, myalgia, malaise and headache. The frequency of these reactions was generally comparable to, or lower than, that observed after the primary series.

## 5. PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Rabies vaccines, ATC code: J07BG01

## Mechanism of action

VaxRabeo consists of purified inactivated rabies virus (WISTAR strain PM/WI38 1503-3M) produced in cell culture. Administration of VaxRabeo induces production of rabies-virus neutralising antibodies (RVNA) directed against the surface glycoprotein of the rabies virus.

## Clinical immunology

In the absence of clinical efficacy data, a post-vaccination RVNA titre threshold of 0.5 IU/mL has been applied as a surrogate immune marker of protection to infer efficacy of VaxRabeo, as per WHO recommendation.

Five clinical studies have been conducted to assess the immunogenicity of VaxRabeo in PrEP and PEP, by measuring the levels of RVNA.

Immunogenicity results presented for the per protocol analysis set (PPAS) are based on participants who were seronegative at baseline.

## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

No pharmacokinetic studies have been conducted.

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data

Non-clinical data revealed no special hazard for humans based on conventional studies of repeated dose toxicity.

The non-clinical developmental toxicity study was conducted with an intermediate formulation of VaxRabeo, that contained half the quantity of antigen, and lacked recombinant Human Albumin (rHA). Eight intramuscular injections of 0.5 mL (a single human dose) were administered to female rabbits: 8, 5 and 1 day(s) before mating and on days 6, 9, 12, 18 and 27 of gestation. This study did not show any effects on mating performance, female fertility, pregnancy, embryo-foetal and early postnatal development and indication of a teratogenic potential.

VaxRabeo has not been evaluated for impairment of fertility in males.

## 1. What VaxRabeo is and what it is used for

VaxRabeo is a vaccine that helps protect people of all ages against the rabies virus.

Rabies is a rare but very serious infection that affects your brain and nervous system. It is caused by a virus and is usually spread through the bite or scratch of an infected animal, such as a dog, bat, or wild mammal. It can also be transmitted if an infected animal licks a wound on your skin.

VaxRabeo helps your immune system (body's natural defences) to produce its own protection (antibodies) against the disease caused by rabies virus.

VaxRabeo can be used to prevent rabies:

- before a possible exposure, if you or your child are at risk of exposure to the rabies virus (preexposure prophylaxis)

or

- after you or your child have been exposed, or think you have been exposed, to the rabies virus (post-exposure prophylaxis).

## 2. What you need to know before you use VaxRabeo

## Do not use VaxRabeo

- if you or your child are allergic to the active substance or any of the other ingredients of this vaccine (listed in section 6), when it is being used preventatively before possible exposure to the rabies virus.

This does not apply if VaxRabeo is used after suspected or proven exposure to rabies virus, as untreated rabies infection almost always results in death.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Warnings and precautions

Talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse before using VaxRabeo if you or your child:

- have ever had a severe allergic reaction after receiving any other vaccine.
- are allergic to the antibiotic neomycin.
- are feeling nervous about getting the vaccine or have ever fainted after or even before any injection.
- have a moderate or severe acute illness with or without fever. Vaccination to protect before possible exposure to the rabies virus may need to be delayed.
- have a bleeding or bruising disorder, or if you are taking an anticoagulant medicine (to prevent blood clots) as bleeding might occur after vaccination.
- have a weakened immune system (due to a disease or use of medicine): you may not get the full benefit from VaxRabeo. In this case, your doctor may ask you to have regular blood tests to measure the amount of antibodies against rabies and may give you additional doses of VaxRabeo if needed.

As with all vaccines, VaxRabeo may not fully protect all people who are vaccinated. For the vaccine to work properly, it is important that the vaccination schedules and the recommendations for using VaxRabeo are strictly followed (see section 3) due to the severity of the disease, even if you had any side effects during or after previous vaccination.

## Other medicines and VaxRabeo

Tell your doctor or pharmacist if you or your child are taking, have recently taken or might take any other medicines.

Some medicines including long-term corticosteroids (medicines used to reduce inflammation) and chloroquine (a medicine used for certain infections or autoimmune diseases) may weaken your response to VaxRabeo.

## Pregnancy and breast-feeding

If you are pregnant or breast-feeding, think you may be pregnant or are planning to have a baby, ask your doctor or pharmacist for advice before using VaxRabeo.

Before a possible exposure to rabies virus, you may receive VaxRabeo during pregnancy or breastfeeding following an assessment of the risks and benefits by your doctor.

After you have been or may have been exposed to rabies virus, VaxRabeo can be used during pregnancy or breast-feeding.

## Driving and using machines

Some of the side effects of VaxRabeo such as feeling generally unwell (malaise) and dizziness (see section 4) may temporarily affect your ability to drive and use machines. Wait until any effects of the vaccine have worn off before you drive or use machines.

## VaxRabeo contains phenylalanine, proline, sodium and sorbitol

- This vaccine contains 19.8 micrograms phenylalanine per dose (0.5 mL). Phenylalanine may be harmful if you or your child have phenylketonuria (PKU), a rare genetic disorder in which phenylalanine builds up because the body cannot remove it properly.

- This vaccine contains 13.8 micrograms of proline per dose (0.5 mL). Proline may be harmful for patients with hyperprolinaemia, a rare genetic disorder in which proline builds up in the body. If you or your child have hyperprolinaemia, do not use this medicine unless your doctor has recommended it.

- This vaccine contains less than 1 mmol sodium (23 mg) per dose (0.5 mL), that is to say essentially 'sodium-free'.

- This vaccine contains 6 mg sorbitol per dose (0.5 mL). The additive effect of concomitantly administered medicines containing sorbitol (or fructose) and dietary intake of sorbitol (or fructose) should be taken into account.

## 4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, this medicine can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them.

## Severe allergic reactions

Serious allergic reactions have happened rarely with other vaccines like VaxRabeo. Contact your doctor or health care professional immediately or go to the nearest hospital emergency room right away if you or your child have symptoms of a serious allergic reaction. These symptoms may include rash, itching, difficulty breathing, shortness of breath, swelling of the face, lips, throat or tongue, cold, clammy skin, palpitations.

The following side effects were observed with VaxRabeo:

## Very common (may affect more than 1 in 10 people)

- headache
- feeling generally unwell (malaise)
- fever
- vomiting
- muscle pain (myalgia)
- unusual crying
- feeling irritable
- feeling drowsy
- loss of appetite
- tenderness/pain at the injection site
- swelling at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the skin
- redness (erythema) at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the skin

## Common (may affect up to 1 in 10 people)

- diarrhoea
- swelling at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the muscle
- redness at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the muscle

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Uncommon (may affect up to 1 in 100 people)

- belly (abdominal) pain
- soft faeces
- dizziness
- tiredness (fatigue)
- chills
- swollen glands in the neck, armpit or groin (lymphadenopathy)
- itching (pruritus)
- itching (pruritus) at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the muscle
- tingling or numbness (paraesthesia) at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the muscle
- blood collection under the skin (haematoma) at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the muscle
- bruising at the injection site, when the vaccine is given into the muscle

Rare (may affect up to 1 in 1 000 people)

- nausea
- rash.

The following information is intended for healthcare professionals only:

## Preparation for administration

VaxRabeo must be reconstituted only with the solvent provided in the pre-filled syringe.

VaxRabeo should be prepared using aseptic technique.

- Inspect visually the powder in vial and the solvent in pre-filled syringe to check for any foreign particles or discoloration.
- Inspect the syringe to check leakage, premature activation of the plunger or faulty tip seal.
- Attach the reconstitution sterile needle to the Luer Lock pre-filled syringe (see Instructions for use of Luer-Lock pre-filled syringe).
- Slowly inject the entire content of the pre-filled syringe of solvent into the vial of powder.
- Without removing the needle and the syringe, gently swirl the vial to obtain a homogeneous suspension. Do not shake the vial.
- The suspension should be visually inspected prior to administration. The reconstituted vaccine is a clear to opalescent colourless liquid, free from visible particles. If the physical aspect is not consistent with this description, do not administer the vaccine and discard it.

## Administration

The reconstituted vaccine must be used immediately*.

## For intramuscular use

Using the same syringe and needle, withdraw the total amount of reconstituted vaccine. Remove the reconstitution needle and discard it. Attach the sterile needle for intramuscular use. Administer the vaccine (see section 3).

## For intradermal use

With the same needle and a new sterile syringe for intradermal use, withdraw from the vial 0.1 mL of reconstituted vaccine per dose needed. Remove the reconstitution needle and discard it. Attach a new sterile needle for intradermal use. Administer the vaccine (see section 3).

The remainder of suspension can be used for another individual provided the vial is stored in a refrigerator (2 °C - 8 °C) and used within 6 hours of reconstitution. Before each withdrawal, gently swirl the vial until homogeneous suspension of the powder is obtained.

Replace the needle by a new sterile needle for intradermal use before administering the vaccine. A separate new sterile needle and a separate new sterile syringe for intradermal use must be used for administration to each individual to prevent cross infection.

*If not used immediately, chemical and physical in-use stability has been demonstrated for 6 hours after reconstitution when stored between 2 °C and 25 °C for single administration by intramuscular route or for 6 hours in a refrigerator (2 °C - 8 °C) in case of use for multiple administrations by intradermal route. Do not freeze. After 6 hours, the reconstituted vaccine must be discarded. From a microbiological point of view, if not used immediately, in-use storage times and conditions are the responsibility of the user.
