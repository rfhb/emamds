## 2. Introduction

## 2.1. Therapeutic context

Acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections (ABSSSI) represent a subset of skin and soft tissue infections (SSTI, also known as skin and skin structure infections (SSSI)) comprising cellulitis, erysipelas, major cutaneous abscess, and wound infection. Staphylococcus aureus is the primary pathogen responsible for ABSSSI in paediatric and adult patients and accounts for at least 60% of ABSSSI cases globally, with substantial contributions from both MRSA and MSSA. Another important pathogen in ABSSSI in both paediatric and adult patients is the Group A beta-haemolytic streptococcus, Streptococcus pyogenes (i.e., GAS). Other beta-haemolytic streptococcal species and anaerobic Gram-positive organisms can also be responsible for ABSSSI, with considerable dependence on anatomical location of the site of infection.

## 2.2. Aspects of development

The previous paediatric marketing application for use of tedizolid phosphate in patients &lt; 12 years of age included the results of three (2 Phase 1 and 1 Phase 3) clinical studies:

- MK-1986-013 (P013): A Phase 1, Single-Administration Pharmacokinetic and Safety Study of Oral and IV Tedizolid Phosphate in Hospitalized Subjects 2 to &lt; 12 Years Old
- MK-1986-014 (P014): A Phase 1, Single- and Multiple-Dose Safety and Pharmacokinetic Study of Oral and IV Tedizolid Phosphate (MK-1986) in Inpatients Under 2 Years Old
- MK-1986-018 (P018): A Phase 3 Randomized, Active-comparator-controlled Clinical Trial to Study the Safety and Efficacy of MK-1986 (Tedizolid Phosphate) and Comparator in Subjects From Birth to Less Than 12 Years of Age With Acute Bacterial Skin and Skin Structure Infections (ABSSSI)

The two Phase 1 studies (P013 and P014) evaluated the PK, safety, and tolerability of a single oral or IV dose of tedizolid phosphate in participants 2 to 12 years of age (P013) and from birth to &lt; 2 years of age (P014). P014 also evaluated the PK, safety, and tolerability of multiple IV doses of tedizolid phosphate in full-term and preterm neonates from birth to 28 days of age. The Phase 3 study (P018) evaluated the safety and efficacy of multiple IV and oral doses of tedizolid phosphate in participants &lt; 12 years of age.

The paediatric clinical program for tedizolid phosphate described in the previous paediatric marketing application was designed to provide:

- PK data in participants &lt; 12 years of age as a basis for extrapolation of efficacy from clinical studies in adult and adolescent populations. Modelling and simulation were used to identify dosage regimens to achieve exposures associated with a high probability of antibacterial efficacy against clinically relevant Gram-positive pathogens, as determined by E-R and PTA analyses.
- A descriptive evaluation of efficacy from P018 to support extrapolation of efficacy and safety data in the population &lt; 12 years of age to permit bridging from adolescents and adults.

The PFOS 1 multi-dose bottle formulation was used in three paediatric clinical studies (P013, P014, and P018).

## 2.3. Description of the product

Tedizolid phosphate is an antibacterial prodrug of the oxazolidinone class, rapidly converted in vivo by phosphatases into the microbiologically active molecule, tedizolid. Tedizolid acts mainly against Grampositive pathogens and exerts its effect by inhibiting protein synthesis through interaction with the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome, preventing the initiation of translation by blocking the formation of the initiation complex. Tedizolid phosphate is currently approved in intravenous (IV) and oral tablet formulations in several countries and regions, including the US, EU, and UK, for the treatment of ABSSSI (also referred to as SSTI or SSSI) in adults and paediatric patients.

The MAH submitted the results from a definitive bioequivalence study, MK-1986-044 (P044), to support an update of the currently approved labelling of tedizolid phosphate to add use of a unit-dose sachet powder for oral suspension (PFOS) formulation of tedizolid phosphate in patients with ABSSSI. The PK, efficacy, and clinical safety results for tedizolid phosphate in clinical studies in paediatric participants were presented in the previous paediatric marketing application for tedizolid phosphate.

During the paediatric clinical program, three PFOS formulations were developed.

The multi-dose bottle formulation (PFOS 1) was used in three paediatric clinical studies (P013, P014, and P018) that were included in the previous paediatric marketing application.

The proposed commercial unit-dose sachet PFOS 2B was evaluated in study P044 and demonstrated bioequivalence to PFOS 1, supporting a bridge between the formulation used in the paediatric clinical program and this intended commercial formulation. An interim formulation (PFOS 2A), used in an earlier bioequivalence study (P043), is included for historical understanding of the PFOS unit-dose sachet formulation development. The intended commercial formulation (PFOS 2B) is expected to be equally effective as the currently approved IV and tablet formulations without dose adjustment.

## 3.3. Finished medicinal product

## 3.3.1. Description of the product and pharmaceutical development

The finished product is presented as a powder for oral suspension formulation presented in a unit-dose sachet. ## 4.1.1. Other toxicity studies

Two GLP mutagenicity studies were conducted with a potential nitrosamine. These were an Enhanced Ames Test and an in vivo transgenic rodent assay.

Following the Carcinogenic Potency Categorization Approach, the potential nitrosamine was categorised into potency category 4 (Acceptable Intake (AI) limit = 1500 ng/day).

Additionally, the mutagenic potential of the potential nitrosamine was tested in an Enhanced Ames Test and in an in vivo transgenic rodent assay.

The Enhanced Ames Test employed six bacterial strains ( Salmonella typhimurium TA1535, TA1537, TA97a, TA98, and TA100, and Escherichia coli WP2 uvrA pKM101), was conducted without and with metabolic action and followed the pre-incubation method. The substance was dissolved in DMSO and tested increasing doses. The nitrosamine positive controls, which were used in addition to standard Ames test positive controls, consisted of 1-cyclopentyl-4-nitrosopiperazine (CPNP) and N-nitrosodibutylamine (NDBA), both also dissolved in DMSO. The study revealed no increase in revertants due to the potential nitrosamine.

The in vivo transgenic rodent assay was conducted in Big Blue rats with assessment of effect on mutant frequency at the cII locus in liver and duodenum. The potential nitrosamine was administered during 28 consecutive days at increasing doses. In addition, a negative control group received vehicle also during 28 days and a positive control group, N-nitroso-N-ethylurea (ENU), on Days 1, 2, 3, 12, 19, and 26. The route of administration was by oral gavage. The animals were sacrificed on day 31.

In addition to assessment for mutation induction at the cII locus in liver and duodenum, the animals were also observed/examined for general toxicity based on mortality, clinical signs, body weight, food consumption, clinical pathology (serum biochemistry) and, at necropsy, gross examination of the liver and duodenum and liver weight.

The study revealed no test article-related statistically significant increase in mutant frequency at the cII gene in liver or duodenum and there were no dose-related trends observed in either tissue. In terms of general toxicity, effects observed for the substance were limited to effects in body weight, food consumption and serum biochemistry, at the maximum tested dose.

## Phase II - Tier A

Phase II is focused on obtaining data on chronic ecotoxicological effects, biodegradation, potential for sorption to soil or sludge, and potential for partitioning to the sediment of tedizolid. Fate data can be used to assess transport within the environment and potential depletion mechanisms, and it may be used to derive predicted environmental concentrations. The more relevant methods were used, and the results were discussed.

Because tedizolid has a neutral charge and adsorption behaviour, and Kd is not pH-dependent, the log Koc of tedizolid was determined according to OECD 121 to be 2.6. The log Koc values, ranging from 1.5 to 5.0, are below the action limit of 10,000. The log Koc value reported by the OECD 121 method indicates that tedizolid has low adsorption to sludge.

The adsorption (Kd) and desorption (Kdes) coefficients of  14 C-labeled tedizolid in two sludges and four soils with diverse characteristics (including organic carbon content, pH, clay content, and soil texture) were ascertained in accordance with OECD Guideline 106. The adsorption coefficient (KOC) as a function of organic carbon content was also determined for each solid type. Additionally, the Freundlich adsorption coefficient (KFads) and desorption coefficient (KFdes) were established, along with their respective coefficients as functions of organic carbon content (KF, OCads and KF, OCdes) for both adsorption and desorption processes of  14 C-labeled tedizolid at five concentrations, utilising two sludge samples and four soil samples.

The mean percent adsorption, Kd, and KOC values indicate that it does not strongly adsorb to sludges but strongly adsorbs to soils.

Based on the OECD 301B method, tedizolid is not readily biodegradable, as no biodegradation was observed within 28 days. Therefore, according to the Guideline EMA/CHMP/SWP/44609/2010 Rev. 1, 2016, two naturally occurring aquatic sediment systems differing in pH and organic carbon content were studied to determine the fate of tedizolid in accordance with the OECD Guideline 308. The half-lives at 20°C and extrapolated to 12°C (the environmental temperature in Europe), in both aquatic systems are &lt; 2 days. Tedizolid rapidly removed from the water fraction and bound to the sediment in both sediment systems ( higher vs. lower organic carbon content ). The amount of radioactivity in the water phase remained at 0.6% in Wiehltalsperre and 1.9% in Nordhafen. Non-extractable residues were in the sediment fraction of Wiehltalsperre with &lt;5% and with 12% at Nordhafen after 100 days. All transformation products detected in both sediments were less than 10% of the total radioactivity.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Because of the lack of relevant biodegradation, the test item is assumed to accumulate in the sediment, and effects on a sediment-dwelling organism, Chironomus riparius (OECD 218), were conducted.

The effects of algae, daphnia and fish were studied in accordance with OECD test guidelines and GLP. The predicted no-effect concentration (PNEC) values derived from laboratory studies were compared to the predicted environmental concentration (PEC) values.

The risk quotient ratios (RQ=PEC/PNEC) values presented for surface water, sediment and wastewater treatment facilities are well below the action limits, indicating that the environmentally relevant residues of Tedizolid pose a low risk for these compartments. Nevertheless, the risk quotient ratio is greater than 1 for groundwater, and a PECGW-Refined is calculated in Phase II Tier B.

The PECGW-Refined value of 4.10E03 μg/L was derived from the PECSW -Refined value for tedizolid of 1.64E02 μg/L, considering the treatment regimen and conservatively assuming that 1% of the population administers tedizolid.

Results of the Phase II Tier B assessment indicate no unacceptable risk to groundwater (RQ=1.95E-02). The estimated RQ is extremely low, and this conservative assessment did not invoke any depletion mechanism, such as human metabolism to inactive metabolites, so the actual PEC is likely to be even lower.

The effects on a sediment-dwelling organism, Chironomus riparus , were assessed in accordance with the OECD 218 method. Results from the sediment toxicity test, corrected for 10% organic content, show that the NOEC is ≥ 100 mg/Kg. The risk (RQ) for sediment -dwelling organisms is based on PECsediment (derived from PECsw) and PNECsed (derived from tests with sediment-dwelling organisms). As the RQ for sediment-dwelling organisms is &lt;1, this meets the Guideline requirement for no further testing of tedizolid in sediment.

## Toxicology/ studies with the potential nitrosamine

Regarding the potential nitrosamine, following the Carcinogenic Potency Categorization Approach, this nitrosamine was categorised into potency category 4 (Acceptable Intake (AI) limit = 1500 ng/day). This is agreed by the CHMP.

Additionally, the mutagenic potential of the substance was tested in an Enhanced Ames Test and in an in vivo transgenic rodent assay. The test employed 6 bacterial strains ( Salmonella typhimurium TA1535, TA1537, TA97a, TA98, and TA100, and Escherichia coli WP2 uvrA pKM101), was conducted without and with metabolic action and followed the pre-incubation method. The potential nitrosamine was dissolved in DMSO and tested at increasing doses. The nitrosamine positive controls, which were used in addition to standard Ames test positive controls, consisted of 1-cyclopentyl-4-nitrosopiperazine (CPNP) and Nnitrosodibutylamine (NDBA), both also dissolved in DMSO.

The study revealed no increase in revertants due to the potential nitrosamine and is generally in accordance to the Enhanced Ames Test conditions for N-nitrosamines as set in appendix 3 to Questions and answers for marketing authorisation holders/MAHs on the CHMP Opinion for the Article 5(3) of Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 referral on nitrosamine impurities in human medicinal products, except for that no justification was provided to indicate that the volume of solvent does not interfere with the metabolic activation of the test N-nitrosamine. To further support the validity of the negative result of the Enhanced Ames Test, the MAH has conducted two additional studies to address the potential impact of the relatively high concentration of DMSO in the pre-incubation mixture. The new studies tested the mutagenicity of the two nitrosamine positive controls with DMSO at a range of concentrations. These studies showed that, in one test, the DMSO concentration had no effect on mutation induction by either positive control; in the other, the change in mutation frequency with increasing DMSO concentrations did not have an overall impact on detection of mutagenicity as the mutation induction remained robust in the most sensitive strain and remained positive in the least sensitive strain. The issue on the possible impact of the DMSO at this specific concentration on the results of the EAT has, therefore, been adequately addressed.

The in vivo transgenic rodent assay was conducted in Big Blue rats with assessment of effect on mutant frequency at the cII locus in liver and duodenum. The potential nitrosamine was administered during 28 consecutive days at ranging doses. In addition, a negative control group received vehicle also for 28 days and a positive control group, N-nitroso-N-ethylurea (ENU), on Days 1, 2, 3, 12, 19, and 26. The route of administration was by oral gavage. The animals were sacrificed on day 31.

In addition to assessment for mutation induction at the cII locus in liver and duodenum, the animals were also observed/examined for general toxicity based on mortality, clinical signs, body weight, food consumption, clinical pathology (serum biochemistry) and, at necropsy, gross examination of the liver and duodenum and liver weight.

The study revealed no test article-related statistically significant increase in mutant frequency at the cII gene in liver or duodenum and there were no dose-related trends observed in either tissue. Liver mutant frequency from one animal from one dose group was considered to represent a jackpot mutation, with data excluded from group mean calculation. Results for the negative and positive controls were in agreement to what expected.

In terms of general toxicity, effects observed for the potential nitrosamine were limited to effects in body weight, food consumption and serum biochemistry at the maximum tested dose:

- Body weight: in study week 1, body weight losses were observed in the maximum dose (dose 1) group and, from pre-test to the end of the study, mean body weights were increased in the maximum dose group compared to a higher increase in the vehicle control group.

The MAH took into account previous knowledge on toxicity and pharmacokinetics. In the TGR assay, animals treated with the maximum tested dose showed a reduction of body weight gain compared to that observed in negative control. ## 5.2. Clinical pharmacology

## 5.2.1. Methods

An analytical method for the determination of both tedizolid phosphate and tedizolid in human plasma was fully validated at Syneos Health Clinique (Quebec, Canada) to support sample analysis in P043 and P044. A protein precipitation technique was used to extract the analytes and stable-labelled internal standards from matrix, followed by HPLC-MS/MS analysis for separation, detection and quantification. Validation experiments were performed according to regulatory guidelines in place at the time of testing.

The analytical method measures both tedizolid phosphate and tedizolid simultaneously. However, no values were obtained for MK-1986 in all samples from the two bioequivalence studies. Samples were assayed undiluted because tedizolid phosphate concentrations were expected to be below the limit of quantitation (BLQ) of the assay due to its rapid conversion to tedizolid i n-vivo . Dilution was then performed on samples with the initial tedizolid concentrations above the upper limit of quantitation (ULOQ) for the assay, to obtain the final tedizolid results.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In study P044 a total of 16 pre-dose samples (period 2) were found with concentrations higher than the lower limit of quantitation (&lt; 5.00 ng/mL) but below 5% of Cmax. These samples were reanalysed in duplicate to obtain confirming values. The results from the repeat analysis confirmed the positive values obtained for the 16 samples. The ISR analysis also confirmed the initial results in the 96.3% of the samples. Overall, the analytical method seems appropriate.

## 5.2.2. Pharmacokinetics

## 5.2.2.1. Introduction

Tedizolid phosphate is currently approved as a 200 mg film-coated tablet and as a 200 mg vial for IV infusion. An age-appropriate tedizolid phosphate Powder for Oral Suspension (PFOS) formulation in a multi-dose bottle presentation (PFOS 1) was developed and used to support paediatric clinical studies submitted in the previous paediatric marketing application. Subsequently, the oral suspension formulation was optimised to a unit-dose sachet PFOS (PFOS 2B) with bioequivalence established to PFOS 1 in a definitive bioequivalence study P044 to support posology and commercial use. Consistent with the previous experience and approval of the current formulations (IV infusion and tablet), the proposed PFOS formulation is expected to be equally effective as the IV and/or tablet formulations, as appropriate, without dose adjustment

## 5.2.2.2. Evaluation and qualification of models

## 5.2.2.2.1. Population Pharmacokinetics

In brief, a paediatric population PK model was developed utilising PK data from three clinical studies conducted in paediatric participants birth to &lt;12 years (P013, P014, and P018) and from two clinical studies conducted in adolescents 12 to &lt;18 years (P012 and P026). No new paediatric clinical studies were conducted, and no new PK data were integrated into the dataset used to develop the paediatric population PK model. The PK characterisation, evaluation of dosing recommendations, and corresponding PTA analysis were summarised in the previous paediatric marketing application for tedizolid phosphate.

In paediatric participants (birth to &lt;18 years of age), tedizolid PK was adequately described by a 2compartment model with zero-order input for IV infusions, first-order absorption for oral suspension doses, and sigmoidal absorption for tablet doses (including sate bioavailability estimates for oral suspension and tablet formulations), and linear elimination, consistent with the PK in adults. The final paediatric population PK model included estimated exponents for allometric body weight scaling on CL (and Q) and Vc (and Vp). Each of these parameter-covariate relationships was described according to a power function, centred on the median observed body weight (34 kg) in the analysis population.

Absorption Peak plasma tedizolid concentrations are achieved within approximately 3 hours following oral administration under fasting conditions or at the end of the 1-hour intravenous infusion of tedizolid phosphate. The data obtained in the two bioequivalence studies is similar to the one reported in the past.

## 5.2.2.4. Distribution

Protein binding of tedizolid to human plasma proteins is approximately 70% to 90%. The mean steady state volume of distribution of tedizolid in healthy adults following a single intravenous dose of tedizolid phosphate 200 mg ranged from 67 to 80 L (approximately twice total body water). The PopPK model resulted in an average Vss of 62L for a 70 kg subject. Tedizolid penetrates the interstitial space fluid of adipose and skeletal muscle tissue with exposure similar to free drug exposure in plasma.

## 5.2.2.5. Metabolism

When incubated with pooled human liver microsomes, there was no degradation of tedizolid suggesting that tedizolid is not a substrate for hepatic CYP450 enzymes. Multiple SULT enzymes (SULT1A1, SULT1A2, and SULT2A1) are involved in the biotransformation of tedizolid, to form an inactive and noncirculating sulphate conjugate found in the excreta.

## 5.2.2.6. Elimination

Tedizolid phosphate is converted by endogenous plasma and tissue phosphatases to the microbiologically active moiety, tedizolid. Other than tedizolid, which accounts for approximately 95% of the total radiocarbon AUC in plasma, there are no other significant circulating metabolites in humans following a single oral dose of  14 C-labeled tedizolid. This was further confirmed in the presented bioequivalence studies, were the Tedizolid phosphate were always below the LLOQ of the analytical method.

Tedizolid is eliminated in excreta, primarily as a non-circulating and microbiologically inactive sulphate conjugate. Following single oral administration of  14 C-labeled tedizolid phosphate under fasted conditions, most of the elimination occurred via the liver with 81.5% of the radioactive dose recovered in faeces and 18% in urine, with most of the elimination (&gt;85%) occurring within 96 hours. Less than 3% of tedizolid phosphate administered dose is excreted as active tedizolid. The elimination half-life of tedizolid is approximately 12 hours and the intravenous clearance is 6 to 7 L/h.

## 5.2.2.7. Dose proportionality and time dependency

Tedizolid demonstrated linear pharmacokinetics regarding dose and time.

## 5.2.2.8. Pharmacokinetics in the target population

According to the previously assessed PopPK model, Cmax of tedizolid was achieved within approximately 3 hours after administration of the oral suspension of tedizolid phosphate in paediatric patients, similar to observations in adults following administration of the oral tablet of tedizolid phosphate. The estimated mean terminal elimination half-life was approximately 9.8 hours for patients weighing the median 34 kg. The half-life ranges from 6.9 hours to 11.1 hours from the lowest to highest observed body weights in the analysis population (1.05 to 126 kg), supporting twice-daily or once-daily dosing of tedizolid phosphate. This goes in agreement with the already described PK behaviour of tedizolid.

No dosage adjustment of tedizolid phosphate is required in renally impaired and/or hepatically impaired patients. No dosage adjustment of tedizolid phosphate is required based on gender or race of the patients nor for the elder patients.

## 5.2.3. Pharmacodynamics

Tedizolid phosphate is an oxazolidinone phosphate prodrug. The antibacterial activity of tedizolid is mediated by binding to the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome resulting in inhibition of protein synthesis. Tedizolid is primarily active against Gram-positive bacteria. Tedizolid is bacteriostatic against enterococci, staphylococci, and streptococci in vitro .

The most commonly observed mutations in staphylococci and enterococci that result in oxazolidinone resistance are in one or more copies of the 23S rRNA genes (G2576U and T2500A). Organisms resistant to oxazolidinones via mutations in chromosomal genes encoding 23S rRNA or ribosomal proteins (L3 and L4) are generally cross-resistant to tedizolid. A second resistance mechanism is encoded by a plasmidborne and transposon associated chloramphenicol-florfenicol resistance ( cfr ) gene, conferring resistance in staphylococci and enterococci to oxazolidinones, phenicols, lincosamides, pleuromutilins, streptogramin A and 16 membered macrolides. Due to a hydroxymethyl group in the C5 position, tedizolid retains activity against strains of Staphylococcus aureus that express the cfr gene in the absence of chromosomal mutations. The mechanism of action is different from that of non-oxazolidinone class antibacterial medicinal products; therefore, cross-resistance between tedizolid and other classes of antibacterial medicinal products is unlikely.

## Bioanalytical methods

An HPLC method with Tandem Mass Spectrometry Detection and Automated Extraction was used in the bioequivalence studies provided in this procedure. The method was properly validated prior to the study initiation. The analytical method measures both tedizolid phosphate and tedizolid simultaneously. However, no values were obtained for MK-1986 in all samples from the two bioequivalence studies. Samples were assayed undiluted because tedizolid phosphate concentrations were expected to be below the limit of quantitation (BLQ) of the assay due to its rapid conversion to tedizolid in-vivo. Dilution was then performed on samples with the initial tedizolid concentrations measuring above the upper limit of quantitation (ULOQ) for the assay, to obtain the final tedizolid results.

In study P044 a total of 16 pre-dose samples (period 2) were found with concentrations higher than the lower limit of quantitation (&lt; 5.00 ng/mL) but below 5% of Cmax. These samples were reanalysed in duplicate to obtain confirming values. The results from the repeat analysis confirmed the positive values obtained for the 16 samples. The ISR analysis also confirmed the initial results in the 96.3% of the samples. Overall, the analytical method seems appropriate.

## Bioequivalence

Since the PFOS formulation used in the clinical studies P013, P014, and P018, assessed in a previous application resulting in the approval of Sivextro tablets and powder for concentrate for solution for infusion for the paediatric population, was later observed to be with stability issues, the MAH changed the formulation twice. Due to that, two bioequivalence studies were presented: Study P043 for an intermediate formulation and study P044 for the final formulation. Since only the commercial formulation is of relevance, only study P044 was assessed.

Study P044 was single-dose, open-label, randomised, 2-period, 2-treatment crossover, definitive bioequivalence study under fasting conditions. Eighteen adult healthy participants receive the oral suspension of tedizolid phosphate as either a 200 mg single dose of the PFOS 1 (Treatment A; reference) or a 200 mg single dose of the PFOS 2B (Treatment B; test) on Day 1 of each period. All subjects concluded the study and were included in the PK and statistical analysis. The bioequivalence assessment was made by using the SAS PROC MIXED analysis. Since all subjects provided complete data, the observed results were similar to the ones obtained if a simple GLM approach was taken. The extrapolated AUC was always below 20% of the AUCinf. Added to that, a truncated AUC72 was used. Thus, the absorption phase is considered to be sufficiently described in the present study. Since the 90% CIs for the GMRs (PFOS 2B/PFOS 1) for plasma tedizolid AUC0-last, AUC0-inf, and Cmax are contained within the 0.80 - 1.25 acceptance interval, bioequivalence between the two formulations can be concluded.

Study P043, a single-dose, open-label, randomised, 2-period, 2-treatment crossover, definitive bioequivalence study under fasting conditions in 36 adult healthy participants, also concluded that the tedizolid phosphate interim formulation PFOS 2A was bioequivalent to a single oral dose of tedizolid phosphate PFOS 1.
