- Prevention of skeletal related events (pathological fracture, radiation to bone, spinal cord compression or surgery to bone) in adults with advanced malignancies involving bone (see section 5.1).

## 1.7. Final CHMP outcome

## 1.7.1. Final opinion

Based on the CHMP review of data on quality, safety and efficacy, the CHMP considers by consensus that the benefit-risk balance of Denosumab Ascend is favourable in the following indication(s):

- Prevention of skeletal related events (pathological fracture, radiation to bone, spinal cord compression or surgery to bone) in adults with advanced malignancies involving bone (see section 5.1).
- Treatment of adults and skeletally mature adolescents with giant cell tumour of bone that is unresectable or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity.

The CHMP, therefore, recommends the granting of the marketing authorisation subject to the conditions described in the following sections.

## 1.7.2. Conclusions on biosimilarity and benefit risk balance

Based on the review of the submitted data, Denosumab Ascend is considered biosimilar to Xgeva. Therefore, a benefit/risk balance comparable to the reference product can be concluded.

## 1.7.3. Conditions or restrictions regarding supply and use

Medicinal product subject to restricted medical prescription (See Annex I: Summary of Product Characteristics, section 4.2).

## 2. Introduction

## 2.1. Therapeutic context

Denosumab Ascend (ENZ215) contains the active substance denosumab and is being developed as a biosimilar to Xgeva. Prolia and Xgeva are marketed globally by Amgen. Prolia (denosumab, 60 mg) received a marketing authorization in the European Union (EU) and the United States (US) in 2010, while Xgeva (denosumab, 120 mg) received marketing authorization in the US and EU in 2010 and 2011, respectively.

The clinical development program is based on the demonstration of biosimilarity between Denosumab Ascend and Prolia in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis (PMO patients), since this is considered a sensitive population for demonstration of biosimilarity. Following demonstration of biosimilarity in PMO patients, the sought indication is based on a justification of extrapolation to other indications. This extrapolation is also considered valid for all indications of Xgeva based on the fact that the mechanism of action is identical across all the authorised indications of Prolia and Xgeva.

## 2.2. Aspects of development

The aim of the clinical development was to demonstrate biosimilarity between ENZ215 and the reference product in terms of clinical pharmacology, efficacy and safety.. A positive benefit/risk balance has already been established for the authorised reference product.

For this purpose, two clinical studies have been conducted as follows:

- Clinical Phase 1 study (ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1): A randomized, double-blind, three-arm, parallel-group, single-dose study to compare the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of Denosumab (ENZ215, EU-sourced Prolia, and USsourced Prolia) in healthy adult male volunteers
- Clinical Phase 3 study (ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2): A Phase 3, Randomised, Double-blind, Parallelgroup, Active-controlled Study to Compare the Efficacy, Safety, Pharmacodynamics, Pharmacokinetics and Immunogenicity of Enzene Denosumab (ENZ215) and Prolia in Postmenopausal Women with Osteoporosis.

## 2.3. Description of the product

## Active substance

Denosumab, a recombinant monoclonal antibody to RANKL, is a fully human monoclonal IgG type 2 antibody that has an approximate molecular weight of 147 kDa. It is produced in genetically engineered mammalian (Chinese hamster ovary) cells.

## Finished product

Denosumab Ascend (ENZ215) is a proposed biosimilar to the EU approved Xgeva. Enzene Denosumab Finished Product (FP) (ENZ2152) is a clear, colorless to pale yellow solution without visible particles for injection presented as 120mg/ 1.7 mL.

Enzene Denosumab DP is composed of Denosumab as an active pharmaceutical ingredient and glacial acetic acid, sorbitol, polysorbate 20, and water for injection (WFI) as excipients (sodium hydroxide is used for pH adjustment). The excipients used to develop the ENZ2152 DP formulation are qualitatively and quantitatively similar as that of RMP, Xgeva. No novel excipient is used in Denosumab ENZ2152 DP.

## Mechanism of action

Denosumab exerts its effect by binding to receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-Β ligand (RANKL), a transmembrane or soluble protein essential for the formation, function, and survival of osteoclasts, the cells responsible for bone resorption. Denosumab prevents RANKL from activating its receptor, RANK, on the surface of osteoclasts and their precursors. Prevention of the RANKL/RANK interaction inhibits osteoclast formation, function, and survival, thereby decreasing bone resorption and resulting in net increase in bone mass and strength in both cortical and trabecular bone.

The mechanism of action is identical across all the authorised indications of Prolia and Xgeva, i.e. binding to RANKL and thus preventing activation of its receptor RANK. Thus, based on the same mechanism of action, extrapolation to all indications might be allowed.

Denosumab Ascend will be available in the same formulation and strength (120 mg of denosumab in 1.7 mL of solution (70 mg/mL)) as the reference product, Xgeva. Denosumab Ascend is intended for the same indications as the ones approved in the EU for Xgeva:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Prevention of skeletal related events (pathological fracture, radiation to bone, spinal cord compression or surgery to bone) in adults with advanced malignancies involving bone (see section 5.1).
- Treatment of adults and skeletally mature adolescents with giant cell tumour of bone that is unresectable or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity.

## 4. Non-clinical aspects

## 4.1. Introduction

Denosumab Ascend is a proposed biosimilar to the EU-approved Xgeva. It is a recombinant fully human monoclonal IgG type 2 antibody inhibiting receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL) binding to its receptor (RANK) on the surface of osteoclasts and their precursors. Prevention of RANK-RANKL interaction inhibits formation, activation and survival of osteoclasts, thus reducing bone resorption resulting in increases in bone mass.

The therapeutic indications for the proposed biosimilar, Denosumab Ascend, are:

- Prevention of skeletal related events (pathological fracture, radiation to bone, spinal cord compression or surgery to bone) in adults with advanced malignancies involving bone.
- Treatment of adults and skeletally mature adolescents with giant cell tumour of bone that is unresectable or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity.

Denosumab Ascend is structurally similar to Xgeva. They share the same components and composition of active and inactive ingredients in equivalent amounts, and follow the same route of administration.

The non-clinical development programme for Denosumab Ascend focused on demonstrating similarity of Denosumab Ascend to EU-approved Xgeva with regards to in vitro (binding, cell based functional and effector function assays) characteristics.

The development programme was conducted and assessed in accordance with requirements in the EMA Guideline on similar biological medicinal products containing monoclonal antibodies - non-clinical and clinical issues (EMA/CHMP/BMWP/403543/2010). On 15th October 2020, EMA Scientific Advice (EMA/CHMP/SAWP/531236/2020) was given on the development of Denosumab Ascend. In addition of the two conducted in vivo studies, the Applicant asked for the necessity of a third one. The CHMP responded that no further in vivo studies were required and referred to EMA guidance of non-clinical and clinical aspects for the biosimilar medicinal products containing monoclonal antibodies (EMA/CHMP/BMWP/403543/2010) stating that the in vitro non-clinical studies should include relevant assays on binding to target antigen(s), binding to representative isoforms of the relevant three Fc gamma receptors, FcRn, and complement (C1q), Fab-associated functions, and Fc-associated functions. This is in line with the studies conducted and submitted by the Applicant.

As Denosumab Ascend relates to a marketing authorisation application for a biosimilar medicine there are no GLP requirements with regard to the non-clinical studies. ## Immunogenicity:

Impact of anti-denosumab antibodies and neutralising antibodies on ENZ215 pharmacokinetics

In both the clinical phase I and the clinical phase III study the incidence of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) was very high (&gt;90%) while incidence of neutralising antibodies (NAbs) was very low (≤2%). Therefore, a formal investigation into the effects of ADAs or NAbs on PK was not feasible. Effect of very high ADA titre count was investigated in the clinical phase I study (DEN1) and showed no effects on PK.

## 5.2.3. Pharmacodynamics

## 5.2.3.1. Mechanism of action

Denosumab, is a human monoclonal antibody (IgG2) that targets and binds with high affinity and specificity to RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-β ligand), a transmembrane or soluble protein essential for the formation, function, and survival of osteoclasts, the cells responsible for bone resorption. Denosumab prevents RANKL from activating its receptor. Prevention of the RANKL/RANK interaction inhibits osteoclast formation, function, and survival, thereby decreasing bone resorption and increasing bone mass and strength in both cortical and trabecular bone. RANKL is an upstream regulator of pathways linked to a broad range of bone loss related conditions. Denosumab acts as a highly targeted agent that blocks RANKL and exerts its therapeutic effect across all approved bone loss conditions. It is expected that a product highly similar to Prolia in its binding properties to RANKL will exert the same pharmacological effect regardless of any other systemic or bone related factors.

## 5.2.3.3. Pharmacodynamic interactions with other medicinal products or substances

Regarding the PK/PD study, EMA scientific advice was sought by the applicant . As a result, it was recommended recruiting non-smokers to this study due to potential effects on bone mass, despite denosumab's pharmacokinetics being unaffected by tobacco use. If recruitment proved challenging, light smokers (&lt;10 cigarettes/day) may be considered, but the need for rigorous documentation of actual tobacco consumption was emphasized. The summary of clinical pharmacology states that the study enrolled smokers, however, despite the SA recommendation, smoking was not further documented by study participants and the percentage of 'light smokers' within the groups is not shown. The issue was however not further pursued as it is not considered to have a meaningful impact on the validity or interpretability of the study results.

Immunogenicity was evaluated based on the Safety Set defined as all randomly assigned subjects who received at least 1 dose (full or partial) of either study drug for all ENZ215 clinical studies, and all subjects in the Safety Set were analysed according to the treatment they actually received.

The subjects were considered as post-treatment ADA and NAb positive if they had at least 1 \"Positive\" ADA and NAb result after the first drug exposure. Post-treatment ADA and NAb status was determined regardless of the results at pre-dose.

To estimate the magnitude of ADA positive response, a titration assay was performed with a series of 1:2 dilutions for Studies ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 and ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2. The ADA titer values were reported as the reciprocal of the last dilution resulting in the last positive response above the titer cut point.

## Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1

At each timepoint from Day 1 to Day 270, ADA and NAb incidences were generally comparable between the ENZ215, EU-Prolia and US-Prolia groups. Using a sensitive immunoassay, 100% of subjects treated with denosumab tested positive for ADA at least at one time during the study, however less than 4% of subjects tested positive for NAb. ADA and NAb incidences were generally comparable among the treatment groups. A higher ADA positivity rate was observed as compared to data reported in SmPC/IP (&lt;1%). This is almost certainly due to the very high sensitivity of the ADA assay (6.44 ng/mL) used in the current study.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

All the subjects across all treatment groups had developed posttreatment ADAs, of which 8 subjects developed NAb positive ADAs (ENZ215: 3 subjects [4.4%], EU-Prolia 3 subjects [4.3%], and US-Prolia 2 subjects [2.9%]).

There were eight patients (4 in ENZ5215 and EU-Prolia groups each) with relatively high ADA titer (&gt;900), which appeared to be outliers. Typically, titers in this study ranged between 20 and 700.

## Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2

During the double-blind treatment period, the incidences of ADA and NAb at each timepoint were similar between the ENZ215 and EU-Prolia groups (99.6% and 98.4%, respectively, had at least one positive ADA result up to Day 360), however none of the patients tested NAb positive.

The open-label period between Months 12 to 18 following switch-over did not have an impact on immunogenicity. The number of patients who showed at least one ADA positive result obtained from Month 15 post-dose up to Month 18 were fairly similar at 55 (91.7%) and 50 (83.3%) patients for the ENZ215 and EU-Prolia groups, respectively. NAb positive results after the single transition were not observed for any subject.

The mean and the median values of ADA titer were not significantly different between the treatment groups at each timepoint, and the ADA titer values were relatively low during the overall study period. While most ADA positive patients had titer results between 100 and 400, there were 4 patients (all 4 patients in double-blind treatment period ENZ215 treatment group]) who had ADA titer level of &gt;400. NAb results for all 4 patients were negative throughout the study and serum concentration levels at the time of high titer value observed were similar to the mean serum concentrations of either treatment groups. Comparison of area under the time effect curve (AUEC) of sCTX, percentage change in sP1NP and percent change from baseline in bone mineral density (BMD) showed no relevant effect of high titer development on these PD and efficacy endpoints in both treatment groups. No adverse event of related, serious, or Grade over 3 were reported for these 4 patients at any time during the study. Moreover, there was no notable impact of ADA presence on the PK and efficacy of the patients who had ADA positive results.

## 5.2.6. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical pharmacology

## 5.2.6.1. Discussion

## Bioanalytical methods

Bioanalytical methods were well-described and deemed suitable for the intended purposes.

## Pharmacokinetics

Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Assessment has focused on ENZ215 versus EU-Prolia, though no major discrepances between US and EU Prolia were found upon inspection of the data. Due to the elimination half-life of denosumab of 26 days (range: 6 to 52 days) and the potential development of immunogenicity, a parallel-group design is supported. The primary objective of the study was to demonstrate bioequivalence between ENZ215 and EU- and US-sourced Prolia.

Primary endpoints were Cmax, AUC0-t, AUC0-inf. Secondary PK endpoints were AUC0-28 days, tmax, t1/2 and CL/F. Secondary PD endpoint was AUEC for serum CTX-1 percent inhibition.

Safety and immunogenicity were evaluated by assessing the number of subjects who developed denosumab neutralizing antibodies (NAbs) and anti-drug antibodies, incidence of adverse events (AEs). Moreover, clinically significant changes in physical examination findings, safety laboratory analyses (serum chemistry, haematology, and urinalysis), vital signs, and 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) were assessed. The design of the study, including study population and the demographic characteristics, study duration and selection of the endpoints are considered acceptable.

The applicant has opted to use Prolia instead of Xgeva for the clinical study despite the previous scientific advice given and the concentration differences between Prolia (60 mg/mL) and Xgeva (70.6 mg/mL). Nonetheless, the difference in dose concentration is not expected to cause any clinical meaningful differences. The grouped concentration-time profiles are as expected from a drug with known dose-dependent non-linear elimination as denosumab is eliminated via both non- and targetmediated drug disposition (TMDD). From around D119, TMDD starts to dominate. From the concentration-time curve it is unclear whether there is a difference in the clearance of ENZ215 and EUProlia post D119. However, as the intended dosing of ENZ215 (similar to Xgeva) is every 4 weeks after three loading doses and the clinical dose is 120 mg, any potential difference during TMDD is not expected to have any meaningful clinical impact.

For the individual time-concentration curves, several subjects show multiple peaks and fluctuating drug concentrations, especially around Tmax. This is a known phenomenon for denosumab and is therefore regarded as acceptable. The PK parameters support biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Prolia/Xgeva.

## Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2

This was a Phase III, study incorporating a PK sub-study. Prolia used in this study is from EU. There is very sparse PK-sampling with only two sampling times around the expected Tmax/Cmax and three sampling times characterising the elimination phase. Concentration-time curves were very similar between ENZ215 and EU-Prolia, though the arithmetic mean of ENZ215 serum concentration is slightly higher than for EU-Prolia at D8, D15, D30 and D90. PK parameters are mostly similar between the two treatments. Very high variability makes the interpretation of certain parameters uncertain. In summary, the PK profiles from osteoporosis patients support biosimilarity of ENZ215 and Prolia/Xgeva.

## Immunogenicity:

## Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1:

One subject in the ENZ215 and one subject in the EU-Prolia were ADA positive at baseline while none were Nab positive. All participants developed ADA at some point during the treatment period making calculation of PK parameters in ADA positive vs. ADA negative participants not feasible. The total number of NAb number is very low and similar between the two treatments. There are no meaningful differences in ADA titre count between the two treatment groups. The lack of impact of immunogenicity on PK parameters supports biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Prolia/Xgeva.

## Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The number of ADA positive subjects was high in both groups. The ADA titre levels were also quite similar between the two treatments. No data showing the impact of ADA or NAb on PK parameters could be located. However, as so few subjects were NAb positive and so many ADA positive, this is accepted. The lack of impact of immunogenicity on PK results supports biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Prolia/Xgeva.

## Pharmacodynamics

The pharmacodynamics of ENZ215 and Prolia have been investigated in two studies (ALK22/ENZ215DEN1 (healthy adult men); ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 (post-menopausal women with osteoporosis)). Apart from these studies, no other clinical pharmacology studies (i.e., drug interaction studies, or studies in special populations such as hepatic or renal impairment) were performed.

ENZ215 is developed as a biosimilar product to Prolia/Xgeva. The mechanism of action is identical to the Prolia. Based on the same mechanism of action, extrapolation to all indications of Xgeva is justified, provided that similarity is shown regarding quality and that clinical data show comparability.

Area under the effect curve (AUEC) from time 0 to Day 270 for serum CTX-1 percent inhibition was a secondary endpoint in study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 and used for PD characterisation.

In study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 the area under the effect curve of percentage change from baseline in sCTX from baseline to month 6 was one of the two co-primary parameters and sP1NP was a secondary endpoint.

In study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1, the point estimate of the geometric mean ratio (ENZ215/EU-Prolia) for \"AUEC 0-9M of percent change from baseline in serum CTX concentration\" was 0.98 with the corresponding 95% CI being (0.92, 1.03) (secondary endpoint). In study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 (pivotal study for PD), the point estimate of the geometric mean ratio (ENZ215/EU-Prolia) for \"AUEC 0-6M of percent change from baseline in serum CTX concentration\" was 1.004 with the corresponding 90%CI of (0.9795, 1.0299), and 95% CI of (0.9748, 1.0349) (co-primary endpoint).

The Applicant provided the summary statistics of P1NP serum concentration for each timepoint for the PD set. The baseline levels as well as the results for all other timepoints were also similar between treatment groups. Thus, the results support the PD similarity of the test and reference product.

Immunogenicity of ENZ215 was also evaluated in healthy male subjects in Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 and postmenopausal women with osteoporosis in Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2.

In both healthy subjects and postmenopausal women with osteoporosis patients, the ADA responses were comparable between the treatment groups in terms of incidence and titer. While the incidence of ADA was higher (yet comparable between ENZ215 and Prolia) compared to that of historical studies conducted with Prolia (mostly attributed to high sensitivity of the ADA assay), the NAb positive rate was very low in both studies. Only 8 subjects in each treatment group of Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 tested NAb positive and 6 patients had a NAb positive result in Study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2. Further analyses showed there were no notable impact of ADA on PK, PD, efficacy and safety in Studies ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 and ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2.

As with all therapeutic proteins, there is a potential for immunogenicity with denosumab.

In the two studies, a significantly higher incidence of total ADA in all groups was reported compared to ADA incidence reported in the studies for MAA of Prolia. However, as aforementioned, this is likely a result of the high sensitivity of the applied assay (6.44 ng/mL cut-off). Reassuringly, the ADA incidence between the respective groups of the two studies was similar.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Concerning safety in particular, only one case of drug hypersensitivity was reported in both studies, experienced by a patient in the open-label phase, which is reassuring.

## 5.2.6.2. Conclusions

The applicant has presented PK data from a single-dose, clinical phase I study in healthy male volunteers and from a multidose, clinical phase III study in post-menopausal osteoporosis patients. The investigational product ENZ215 is a proposed biosimilar to Xgeva while all studies are conducted using EU-Prolia as comparator.

The 95%CIs of PD marker serum cross-linked C-telopeptide of type I collagen (CTX) in both studies are sufficiently close around unity to conclude on PD similarity. In addition to sCTX, the levels of PINP were comparable between the treatment groups at each timepoint, supporting PD similarity of test and reference product. No differences between groups were apparent with regards to immunogenicity.

The presented data support ENZ215 as a biosimilar of Xgeva.

## Statistical methods for estimation and sensitivity analysis on primary estimands

Primary estimand: A model of Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) will be implemented to estimate the difference of means within the pre-defined equivalence margin of ± 1.45 between the test (ENZ215) and reference (Prolia) arms in percentage change from baseline of BMD in lumbar spine at month 12.

The observed %CfB in lumbar spine BMD at month 12 will be the dependent variable while the followings will be used as covariates:

- Treatment Arm (ENZ215 and Prolia, as planned (randomised) treatment)
- Stratification factors at randomisation:

-Age (≥ 55 to &lt; 70 years and ≥ 70 to ≤ 85 years)

-Previous use of bisphosphonates (yes/no)

- Baseline BMD value in lumbar spine

As result from ANCOVA model, the effect of the binary variable Treatment Arm will be estimated as regression coefficients. Wald's two-sided 95% Confidence Interval (CI) will be derived for the parameter estimate.

Missing data without experiencing ICE2 will be assumed to be MCAR and will not be imputed.

Assessments of the primary endpoints observed after occurrence of ICE2 will be disregarded, i.e., artificially set as missing, and will be replaced with MI techniques.

Different assumptions will be made for handling of ICE2 in each of the two arms. Under Prolia group, data artificially set as missing after ICE2 occurrence will be assumed to be MAR and imputed assuming they would have behaved like subjects in the same arm had they not taken prohibited medication. Under the ENZ215 group, Month 12 data artificially set as missing after ICE2 occurrence will be imputed using MNAR method 'Under the Null': after ICE2 primary efficacy data are assumed to worsen from \"MAR\" by an amount of equivalence margin \"delta\".

The equivalence margin of -1.45% will be used as the \"delta\" for the ICE2 in the ENZ215 group. MI will be performed through SAS PROC MI, variables used to impute Month 12 missing values will be the dependent variables from ANCOVA model defined above; additionally, Month 6 data will be used as well in the SAS PROC MI as a post-randomization predictive variable; in that contest the post-ICE2 Moth 6 BMD will be imputed as well within the SAS PROC MI itself, with MAR approach for both the treatment arms.

Sensitivity analysis: Missing primary efficacy post baseline data will be imputed as a sensitivity estimation. As sensitivity analysis, missing data will be imputed: if the proportion of non-complete cases is below 5%, sensitivity analysis will be not conducted.

Missing data will be assumed to be MAR and imputed using fully conditional specification (FCS) method via SAS PROC MI. Additionally, likewise for the primary endpoint primary estimand analysis as outlined in section, assessments of the primary endpoints observed after occurrence of ICE2 will be disregarded, i.e., artificially set as missing, and will be replaced with MI techniques.

Different assumptions will be made for handling of ICE2 in each of the two arms. Under Prolia group, data artificially set as missing after ICE2 occurrence will be assumed to be MAR and imputed assuming they would have behaved like subjects in the same arm had they not taken prohibited medication. Under the ENZ215 group, Month 12 data artificially set as missing after ICE2 occurrence will be imputed using MNAR method 'Under the Null': after ICE2 primary efficacy data are assumed to worsen from \"MAR\" by an amount of equivalence margin \"delta\". The equivalence margin of -1.45% will be used as the \"delta\" for the ENZ215 group. MI will be performed through SAS PROC MI, variables used to impute Month 12 missing values will be the dependent variables from ANCOVA model. Additionally, Month 6 data will be used as well in the SAS PROC MI as a post-randomization predictive variable; in that contest the missing Month 6 (originally or post-ICE2 assessments) will be imputed as well within the SAS PROC MI itself, with MAR approach for both the treatment arms. Supplementary analysis: The supplementary estimand of BMD %CfB at Month 12 will utilize a hypothetical ICE handling strategy as if the ICE did not occur, as a further, sensitive, investigation into whether differences in outcomes would emerge if the whole study population were fully compliant with treatment. Data points captured after the ICE will be left out from the ITT analysis. The same ICEs will be applied as for the primary and secondary estimands. ## Medical History and Concurrent Illnesses

## Prior and Concomitant Therapy

Prior medications: A total of 398 participants (79.0%) had at least one prior medication. Most were vitamin A, D, calcium and viral vaccines.

Drugs affecting bone structure and mineralisation (87 participants overall [17.3%]: 45 participants [17.8%] in the ENZ215 group; 42 participants [16.7%] in the Prolia group). All these participants had discontinued the medication before study entry per study protocol requirements.

## Concomitant medications

Double-blind treatment period: A total of 282 participants overall (56.0%) had at least one concomitant medication. The most common (≥ 5% of participants overall) concomitant medications by ATC level 3 were:

- Anti-inflammatory and anti-rheumatic products, non-steroids (77 participants overall [15.3%]: 46 participants [18.2%] in the ENZ215 group; 31 participants [12.4%] in the Prolia group)
- Other analgesics and antipyretics (71 participants overall [14.1%]: 36 participants [14.2%] in the ENZ215 group; 35 participants [13.9%] in the Prolia group)
- Beta-lactam antibacterials, penicillins (38 participants [7.5%]: 19 participants [7.5%] in the ENZ215 group; 19 participants [7.6%] in the Prolia group)
- Drugs for peptic ulcer and gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (37 participants [7.3%]: 23 participants [9.1%] in the ENZ215 group; 14 participants [5.6%] in the Prolia group)
- Macrolides, lincosamides and streptogramins (37 participants [7.3%]: 18 participants [7.1%] in the ENZ215 group; 19 participants [7.6%] in the Prolia group)

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Expectorants, excl. combinations with cough suppressants (33 participants [6.5%]: 17 participants [6.7%] in the ENZ215 group; 16 participants [6.4%] in the Prolia group)
- Viral vaccines (33 participants [6.5%]: 17 participants [6.7%] in the ENZ215 group; 16 participants [6.4%] in the Prolia group)
- Other antibacterials (28 participants [5.6%]: 14 participants [5.5%] in the ENZ215 group; 14 participants [5.6%] in the Prolia group)
- Other beta-lactam antibacterials (28 participants [5.6%]: 16 participants [6.3%] in the ENZ215 group; 12 participants [4.8%] in the Prolia group)

Major protocol deviations related to disallowed medications were reported in nine participants overall (1.8%): six participants (2.4%) in the ENZ215 group and three participants (1.2%) in the Prolia group. In the ENZ215 group, disallowed medications included enoxaparin sodium, trazodone hydrochloride, sertraline, citalopram, tiapride and warfarin (one participant each). All participants were excluded from both the PP and PD Sets, except for the participants who took enoxaparin sodium and warfarin who were only excluded from the PP Set. In the Prolia group, disallowed medications included enoxaparin sodium (two participants), pioglitazone and prednisone (one participant). The participant taking pioglitazone and prednisone was excluded from the PP set; the two participants taking enoxaparin sodium were excluded from both the PP and PD Sets.

Overall, minor protocol deviations related to disallowed medications were reported in 14 participants (2.8%) during the double-blind period.

Open-label extension period: A total of 39 participants overall (32.5%) had at least one concomitant medication. The most common (≥ 5% of participants overall) concomitant medications by ATC level 3 were other analgesics and antipyretics (11 participants overall [9.2%]: five participants [8.3%] in the ENZ215 group; 6 participants [10.0%] in the Prolia group).

There were no major protocol deviations related to disallowed medications during the open-label period. Overall, minor protocol deviations related to disallowed medications were reported in five participants (4.2%).

## 5.3.10. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical efficacy

Denosumab Ascend is being developed as a biosimilar to Xgeva. The clinical development program is based on the demonstration of biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Prolia (this is acceptable as Xgeva and Prolia are considered the same medicinal product?). Accordingly, efficacy biosimilarity was evaluated in study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2, a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, activecontrolled study to compare the efficacy, safety, PD, PK, and immunogenicity of ENZ215 with Prolia in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis (PMO), which is considered a sensitive population for demonstration of biosimilarity between the test and reference denosumab.

Study design: the design of the study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 is acceptable and has been discussed in EMA Scientific Advice procedures (EMA/CHMP/SAWP/531236/2020; EMEA/H/SA/4580/1/2020/III; EMA/SA/0000060480).

Study population: postmenopausal women with osteoporosis aged ≥ 55 and ≤ 85 years were enrolled. Additional inclusion criteria were a body weight between 50 and 90 kg and an absolute BMD consistent with T-scores of ≤ -2.5 and ≥ - 4.0 at the lumbar spine (L1-L4 region) as measured by dual-energy X ray absorptiometry (DXA) at screening. These inclusion criteria are considered acceptable and in line with previous similar studies. In general, the inclusion and exclusion criteria are considered acceptable and capable of ensuring a sufficiently homogenous population. The requirement for patients to have a T-score ≤ -2.5 and ≥ -4.0 at the lumbar spine as measured by dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), is in line with the definition of osteoporosis, and using DXA is the gold standard for assessing bone mineral density (BMD). The exclusion of patients with a T-score below -4.0 is acceptable and could reduce inter-subject variability of the patients. The weight and age limits may also enhance population homogeneity. The exclusion criteria ensure a population without previous exposure to denosumab and little exposure to other osteoporosis medication and bone active drugs.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Randomisation and blinding: Patient allocation was stratified by age (≥ 55 to &lt; 70 years and ≥ 70 to ≤ 85 years) and based on prior use of bisphosphonate. According to the CHMP SA

(EMEA/H/SA/4580/1/2020/III), prior use of bisphosphonate was the most important stratification factor. The applicant was advised to further consider the importance of stratification by age, weight, and geographic area. As such, the advice from CHMP was partly followed. Weight ended up balanced across arms and geographic area was not included since only European centers enrolled patients.

The study was double-blind until Month 12. Patient-level data were frozen before transition to the open-label extension, as detailed in the Data Management Plan.

The process of randomization and blinding is adequately described and is considered acceptable.

Trial intervention: In the double-blind period of the trial, the participants received two subcutaneous doses of study drug (ENZ215 or Prolia) at 6-month intervals (60 mg on day 1 and after 6 months). After EOS, 120 participants randomised to Prolia were offered to enrol in the open-label, switch-over extension period, in which they received a third injection of either Prolia or ENZ215 (60 mg) SC at Month 12 (1:1 randomisation). These participants completed the study at Month 18. From study enrolment to EOS, all patients were administered calcium and vitamin D supplementation.

The selected dose, frequency and route of administration of ENZ215 and Prolia were in line with the SmPC of Prolia and therefore acceptable.

Concomitant and rescue therapies: A number of medications were prohibited during the study, including prior denosumab, drugs affecting bone metabolism, and other treatments against osteoporosis. The list of prohibited medications is considered reasonable and acceptable. No rescue therapies are mentioned in the study protocol, which is acceptable due to the nature of the applied product, the study purpose and overall study design.

Study assessments: The efficacy assessment comprised BMD assessments of the lumbar spine, total hip, and femoral neck performed by DXA scan at screening and after 6 and 12 months of treatment. This scheme is acceptable. The results were evaluated by a central assessor. The same DXA machine was used for all study procedures for a particular patient and the results were centrally assessed, this is endorsed.

Primary objectives, endpoints, estimands: The primary analysis of the primary estimand, defined as the treatment policy estimand, was conducted using an ANCOVA model including treatment, age strata, previous treatment with bisphosphonate and baseline BMD value as covariates. Intercurrent event 1, defined as a delay in BMD assessment of more than 35 days at Month 12, was handled using a treatment policy strategy, and all observed data were included in line with the ITT principle, which was considered appropriate. Intercurrent event 2, defined as the receipt of prohibited medication, was handled using a composite strategy in which post-event values were imputed under the null hypothesis. This approach did not target a clearly defined treatment effect and would be more appropriate as a sensitivity analysis than as the main analysis of the primary estimand. However, given the extremely low frequency of intercurrent events, the choice of imputation strategy was considered to have negligible impact on the treatment effect estimate and had little chance of influencing the overall benefit-risk conclusion.

The incidence of intercurrent events was very low, with six participants in each treatment group experiencing intercurrent event 1 (2.4% overall) and six participants in the ENZ215 group and three in the Prolia group experiencing intercurrent event 2 (1.8% overall). In addition, 20 patients in the ENZ215 arm and 15 in the Prolia arm discontinued treatment, mostly due to participant withdrawal, with very few related to adverse events. These discontinuations were handled under the treatment policy strategy and patients remained included in the analysis where data were available. Results across the ITT, mITT and PP sets were consistent. The adjusted least squares mean difference between ENZ215 and Prolia in percentage change from baseline to Month 12 in lumbar spine BMD was -0.195 with a 95% CI of -0.9167 to 0.5259, fully contained within the pre-specified equivalence margin of ±1.45%. A sensitivity tipping-point analysis based on the principal stratum estimand confirmed the robustness of the results, although the graphical display was not informative since no missing data were observed in the principal stratum.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Secondary objectives, endpoints: The secondary efficacy objectives were to compare the change in serum P1NP concentrations from baseline to Month 6, the change in lumbar spine BMD from baseline to Month 6, and the change in BMD at the total hip and femoral neck from baseline to Months 6 and 12. Serum concentrations of P1NP were summarized descriptively by treatment group at each scheduled sampling time, and percentage change from baseline was presented in tabular and graphical format using the PD population. Secondary BMD endpoints were analysed descriptively in the ITT population, with sensitivity analyses performed in the mITT and PP populations. No formal estimand was clearly defined for the secondary endpoints.

Statistical methods: The ITT set consisted of all randomized patients who received at least one dose of study intervention, which in this trial included the full population of 504 participants, with 253 in the ENZ215 arm and 251 in the Prolia arm. The modified ITT set included patients with both baseline and at least one post-baseline LS-BMD assessment, comprising 241 patients in the ENZ215 arm (95.3%) and 246 in the Prolia arm (98.0%). The per-protocol set was defined as ITT patients with baseline and Month 12 LS-BMD data, who received both scheduled doses and had no major protocol deviations, including 230 patients in the ENZ215 arm (90.9%) and 233 in the Prolia arm (92.8%). The ITT set was used as the primary analysis set for BMD efficacy endpoints, with supportive analyses in the mITT and PP sets. The utilization of the various population sets is endorsed.

The primary analysis of the treatment policy estimand was carried out using an ANCOVA model including treatment, age strata, prior bisphosphonate use and baseline BMD as covariates, which was considered appropriate and with assumptions reasonably fulfilled. Missing data arising from delayed BMD assessments were handled under a treatment policy strategy consistent with the ITT principle, whereas data after prohibited medication were imputed differently between treatment arms, with MAR applied in the Prolia arm and MNAR under the null in the ENZ215 arm. A broad range of sensitivity analyses, including mITT, PP, principal stratum and MMRM under a hypothetical strategy, consistently confirmed the robustness of the results, while the tipping point analysis added little due to absence of missing data in the principal stratum. Discontinuations were limited in number, balanced between treatment groups and mostly related to participant withdrawal, with very few due to adverse events, and were therefore not considered to influence the primary efficacy conclusions.

The secondary efficacy endpoints included sP1NP and changes in BMD at lumbar spine, total hip and femoral neck. sP1NP concentrations decreased similarly in both groups, with peak reductions at Month 3 and no between-group differences. For BMD, ANCOVA analyses showed no significant differences at lumbar spine or total hip at Months 6 or 12, and no difference at femoral neck at Month 6. A statistically significant difference in favour of Prolia was observed at femoral neck at Month 12, but this was considered a chance finding due to high variability, with no supporting evidence from PK, immunogenicity or quality data. Sensitivity and MMRM analyses confirmed the overall robustness of results. As multiplicity was not controlled, the secondary endpoints are considered exploratory.

Data quality assurance: Data quality assurance was ensured through careful planning, delegation to qualified personnel, and continuous review by the Investigator. The Sponsor or designee acted as the statistical and data coordinating center, overseeing data management, quality review, analysis, and reporting, as well as monitoring sites to confirm authenticity, accuracy, and compliance with GCP and regulatory requirements. Data were collected via eCRF within a secure EDC system, which maintained a full audit trail. Investigators verified completeness and accuracy of entries, while monitors performed source data verification. Data cleaning included systematic checks for missing values, ranges, consistency, sequence, and protocol adherence. Once verified, the database was locked, and all data queries were resolved in a timely manner. The procedures ensuring data quality are endorsed.

Changes in planned study conduct: During the conduct of the study, the protocol was amended to version 3.0 (Amendment 3, 16 Feb 2023). The changes concerned several sections of the protocol and apart from one they are sufficiently justified. Of note, the confidence interval used in the assessment of equivalence for the co-primary PD analysis (change in AUEC sCTX from baseline to month 6) was changed from a 95% CI to 90% CI. This change is not supported. However, the 95% CI is also provided in the results section and therefore no concern is raised.

Overall, in line with the applicant position, the protocol amendments are regarded acceptable and not of importance with regards to the data integrity or the safety of the participants.

After Month 12 database lock date, a few data point discrepancies were identified. These discrepancies are considered of no importance for the primary/secondary outcome data set.

## Primary efficacy analysis:

The analysis of the primary endpoint (using a treatment policy estimand in the ITT set) showed equivalence of ENZ215 and Prolia for the percentage change in BMD at lumbar spine (L1-L4 region) measured by DXA from baseline to Month 12 (LS mean difference (95% CI) of -0.183% (-0.9044; 0.5380) (the second co-primary endpoint is assessed in the PD section). Since the 95% CI is within the pre-defined equivalence margin of ±1.45%, the pre-specified biosimilarity criterion is fulfilled.

An analysis of data from the PP set (excluding participants with ICEs) provided similar results to those observed in the ITT Set (LS mean difference (95% CI) of -0.190% (-0.9149; 0.5342)). The number of patients with intercurrent events is considered low with 12 patients (6 patients in each arm) with ICE1 (BMD assessment delays for more than 35 days) and 9 patients (6 patients in ENZ215 arm and 3 patients in Prolia arm) with ICE2 (prohibited medication). Accordingly, the size of the per protocol population does not differ much from the ITT population.

A sensitivity analysis using multiple imputation (treatment policy estimand) in the ITT Set also showed equivalence in percentage change in BMD between the treatment groups (LS mean difference (95% CI) of -0.195 (-0.9167; 0.5259)).

A supplementary analysis using mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) in the ITT set showed no statistically significant differences between the two treatment groups in the percentage change in BMD at the L1-L4 region measured by DXA from baseline to Month 12 (LS mean difference (95% CI) of - 0.220 (-0.9317; 0.5427)).

In conclusion, the provided results have demonstrated equivalence in percentage change in BMD at lumbar spine (L1-L4 region) measured by DXA from baseline to Month 12 between ENZ215 and the reference product Prolia.

Secondary efficacy analysis: In the ITT set, the percentage change in BMD at lumbar spine region (L1L4) from baseline to Month 6 between ENZ215 and Prolia was -0.389% (95% CI: -0.994; 0.215). Thus, bioequivalence was demonstrated. A similar result was obtained in a supplementary analysis using MMRM in the ITT set.

In addition to the lumbar spine BMD, the percent change from baseline in BMD was also measured in total hip and femoral neck at Months 6 and 12. At Month 6, the LS Mean Difference for total hip was - 0.105%, 95%CI [-0.5727%; 0.3629%] and for femoral neck 0.012%, 95%CI [-0.5520%; 0.5765%].

At Month 12, the LS Mean Difference for total hip was -0.401% (95%CI: -0.8691%; 0.0665%) and for femoral neck -0.763% (95%CI: -1.4285; -0.0970).

The applicant has provided a comparison of baseline characteristics and efficacy outcomes with the results obtained in the original PMO study (FREEDOM) with Prolia. In the FREEDOM study, participants were 6 years older, in average, and had slightly more vertebral fractures at baseline. The %CfB in BMD after 52 weeks of treatment measured at the lumbar spine, total hip, and femoral neck was comparable between the studies, indicating that the study design and results are robust.

## 5.3.10.1. Conclusions on the clinical efficacy

Denosumab Ascend is being developed as a biosimilar to Xgeva. The clinical development program is based on the demonstration of biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Prolia in PMO patients, since this is considered a sensitive population for demonstration of biosimilarity. Following demonstration of biosimilarity in PMO patients, extrapolation to the sought indications is justified as the mechanism of action, i.e. binding to RANKL and thus preventing activation of its receptor RANK, is identical across all the authorised indications of Prolia and Xgeva.

The co-primary efficacy endpoint in the PMO study was percentage change in BMD at Lumbar Spine (L1-L4 Region) measured by DXA from baseline to Month 12, and the provided results demonstrated equivalence in this endpoint. The primary analysis was further supported by the results from analyses on the principal stratum estimand, sensitivity and subgroup analyses, and secondary efficacy endpoints.

From an efficacy point of view, biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Xgeva is considered established.

## 5.4. Clinical safety

The clinical safety data presented in this module derives from two biosimilarity trials of ENZ215 as follows:

ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 was a Phase 1 pharmacokinetic (PK) trials in healthy, male subjects and ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 was a Phase 3 comparative efficacy/safety trial in women with post-menopausal osteoporosis (PMO).

Please refer to the table of studies in section 5.3.2 for further details.

For the purpose of this document, the following definitions apply:

'Adverse event - AE' means any untoward medical occurrence in a subject to whom a medicinal product is administered and which does not necessarily have a causal relationship with this treatment.

'Serious adverse event - SAE' means any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose requires inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity, results in a congenital anomaly or birth defect, is life-threatening, or results in death. The definition (in line with ICH E2A) includes important medical events that may not be immediately life-threatening or result in death or hospitalisation but may jeopardise the patient or may require intervention to prevent one of the other outcomes listed in the definition above.

'Adverse drug reaction - ADR' means any untoward and unintended response to a medicinal product related to any dose administered, for which, after a thorough assessment, a causal relationship between the medicinal product and the adverse event is at least a reasonable possibility, based for example, on their comparative incidence in clinical trials, or findings from epidemiological studies and/or on an evaluation of causality from individual case reports.

## 5.4.11. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical safety

## 5.4.11.1. Discussion

## 5.4.11.1.1. Overall assessment of available safety data

Comparability of safety and immunogenicity between the biosimilar candidate ENZ215 and the reference medicinal product Prolia was investigated in two pivotal studies:

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1, a Phase 1, randomized (1:1:1), double-blind, three-arm, parallelgroup, single-dose study to compare the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of ENZ215, EU-Prolia, and US-Prolia in healthy adult male volunteers for a period of 270 days.

· ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2, a Phase 3, randomized (1:1), double-blind, parallel-group, activecontrolled study to compare the efficacy, safety, PD, PK and immunogenicity of ENZ215 with EUProlia in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis (PMO) for a period of 12 months (360 days). Patients initially randomized to EU-Prolia and completed the double-blind period were re-randomized 1:1 to either receive ENZ215 or EU-Prolia for additional 6 months and 1 dose.

The design of both studies is considered acceptable for assessment of comparability between the respective treatments.

Safety data are reported separately for each study, no pooling was possible. Collection of safety data was reliable.

The safety data set of each study consisted of all subjects who received at least one dose of the study drug.

One year double-blinded safety comparability data between ENZ215 and EU-Prolia are available from the Phase 3 study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 with PMO patients, which is considered sufficient. Also number of subjects and extent of exposure is adequate.

In the Phase 1 study with healthy male subjects, most TEAEs were reported to be mild (83 [40.3%]) or moderate (50 [24.3%] subjects); severe TEAEs were only reported in 2 (1.0%) subjects. Incidence of total TEAEs was slightly higher in the EU-Prolia group than in the ENZ215 and US-Prolia groups, with 39 (56.5%), 28 (41.2%), 32 (46.4%) of the subjects, respectively. the reported incidence of TEAEs by SOC and PT was similar between groups.

In the double-blinded phase of the Phase 3 study with PMO patients, most TEAEs were reported to be mild or moderate in severity. One fatal (i.e., leading to death) SUSAR (suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction) and one fatal AE/TEAE were reported, both in the Prolia group, please see section SAEs below.

The number of TEAEs was higher in the ENZ215 group than in the Prolia group (441 events vs. 393 events) but affected a similar number and percentage of patients as those were reported in 164 (64.8%) patients of the ENZ215 group and in 157 (62.5%) patients of Prolia group. No significant differences were observed in incidence of severe TEAEs or incidence of TEAEs by SOC and PT.

In order to assess the impact of treatment on number of events, relatedness with respect to study treatment and non-study treatment was assessed. Any TEAE related to study treatment were observed in 7.5% (19) patients in ENZ215 group versus 9.2% (23) patients in Prolia group. Overall treatment related AEs were observed in 8.3% patients. Any TEAE related to non-study treatment were observed in 3.6% patients in ENZ215 group verses 5.2% patients in Prolia group. Overall treatment related AEs were observed in 4.4% patients.

In the open-label phase of the Phase 3 study with PMO patients, all of the TEAEs were mild or moderate in severity. There were no SUSAR, SAE, fatal AE, or AE leading to treatment withdrawal reported during the open-label extension period (see section SAEs below).

Similar to the double-blind phase, the number of TEAEs was higher in the ENZ215 group than in the Prolia group (36 events vs. 30 events) but affected a similar number and percentage of patients as those were reported in 23 (38.3%) patients of the ENZ215 group and in 23 (38.3%) patients of Prolia group. TEAEs considered related by the investigator were only reported in the ENZ215 group (3 events in 2 [3.3) patients), however, this low number and percentage does not raise concerns.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Overall the safety profile was as to be expected from the known safety profile of the substance and no concerning differences between groups were observed.

## Deaths

There were no deaths in the healthy subject study. For the phase III study, one death occurred in a patient in the Prolia group (that was not enrolled in the open label phase) on study day 385, caused by a TESEA of Covid-19. The investigator assessed that as not related to study treatment.

## Serious adverse events

In the phase I study in healthy males one subject in the EU-Prolia group experienced a ligament rupture that resulted in hospitalization. This event was considered as not related to study treatment by the investigator.

In the phase III study in postmenopausal women all SAEs were treatment-emergent with 40 events in 31 (6.2%) of patients. Most of those (i.e., TESAEs) were in the SOC 'injury, poisoning and procedural complications' with 17 events in 14 (2.8%) patients, of which the majority were fractures.

No significant differences were reported as regards the incidence of TESAEs (ENZ215: 23 events reported in 16 [6.3%], Prolia: 17 events reported in 15 [6.0%] patients, respectively). An TEAE of cystitis of a patient in the Prolia group (0.4%) was assessed as related by the investigator and thus considered a SUSAR.

No SAE or SUSAR was reported during the open-label extension period.

## AESI

Adverse events of special interest (AESI) were not defined in the CSR or study protocol of the phase I study.

The incidence of hypocalcaemia, which is defined as AESI in the Phase 3 study with PMO patients and also in other denosumab procedures, due to being a potential risk with denosumab treatment (listed in 4.4 of the denosumab SmPC), was similar across the ENZ215, US-Prolia, and EU-Prolia groups, in 3 (4.4%), 2 (2.9%), and 3 (4.3%) of subjects, respectively. All these events were mild or moderate in intensity and were resolved.

In the phase III study AESIs were defined as hypocalcaemia, osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ), atypical femoral fracture, fracture healing complications, severe infection (including skin infection) not leading to hospitalisation, hypersensitivity leading to emergency room visit and potential Hy's law.

All AESIs were treatment-emerging and were reported with similar incidence between the two groups with 8 events in 8 patients (3.2%) in the ENZ215 group and 7 events in 7 patients (2.8%) in the Prolia group, respectively. The majority of AESIs were hypocalcaemia with 7 events in 7 patients (2.8%) in ENZ215 group and 6 events in 6 patients (2.4%) in the Prolia group, respectively, all assessed as related to study drug except one in the Prolia group.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

All events of hypocalcaemia were non-serious and mild in severity, except for one event that was moderate in severity. Two other AESIs were reported: One fracture displacement (moderate severity) in the ENZ215 group was assessed as not related to study treatment by the investigator. The other AESI was a non-serious TEAE of pneumonia (graded severe) in the Prolia group, assessed as not related to study treatment by the investigator.

All the AESIs were recovered except for one AESI of hypocalcaemia in the ENZ215 group that was ongoing on Day 364. All AESIs occurred during the double-blind treatment period, no AESI was reported during the open-label extension period.

## Discontinuation

No TEAE leading to discontinuation of study treatment was reported in the phase I study in healthy males or the open label extension of the phase III study in postmenopausal women.

In the double-blind period of the latter, one participant (0.4%) in the ENZ215 group and two participants (0.8%) in the Prolia group experienced TEAEs leading to treatment and study withdrawal. All the TEAEs were unrelated to study treatment.

Despite most thorough reporting of laboratory and other findings, in neither the healthy male nor the postmenopausal female population any trend was seen.

## Immunogenicity

In the healthy male study the proportion of subjects who had developed ADA were similar across the treatment groups.

On Day 28, 98.5% in ENZ 215, 100% in EU-sourced Prolia, and 100% in US-sourced Prolia had developed ADA. The number of subjects with ADA decreased gradually, achieving on day 270, 10.3%, 4.3%, respectively in ENZ215 and both comparator groups.

The higher ADA incidence in ENZ215 at Day 270 is likely multifactorial, driven by assay sensitivity, low residual drug levels, rather than a clinically meaningful immunogenicity concern. Moreover, the absolute difference was small and not statistically significant. No neutralizing antibodies were detected.

## 9. Biosimilarity assessment

## 9.1. Comparability exercise and indications claimed

Denosumab Ascend is being developed as a biosimilar to Xgeva. The clinical development program is based on the demonstration of biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Prolia in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis (PMO patients), since this is considered a sensitive population for demonstration of biosimilarity. Following demonstration of biosimilarity in PMO patients, extrapolation to the sought indications is supported by an acceptable justification.

Denosumab Ascend will be available in the same formulation and strength (120 mg of denosumab in 1.7 mL of solution (70 mg/mL)) as the intended reference product, Xgeva. Denosumab Ascend is intended for the same indications as the ones approved in the EU for Xgeva:

- Prevention of skeletal related events (pathological fracture, radiation to bone, spinal cord compression or surgery to bone) in adults with advanced malignancies involving bone (see section 5.1).
- Treatment of adults and skeletally mature adolescents with giant cell tumour of bone that is unresectable or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity.

For this MAA, the Applicant claims all of the indications of the reference product, Xgeva.

## Summary of Quality data

At the quality level the Applicant compared ENZ2152 finished product with reference medicinal product Xgeva by investigating descriptive quality attributes of the denosumab molecule with stateof-the-art methodology. In order to support the biosimilarity of ENZ2152 to Xgeva the analytical comparability between ENZ2151 finished product and Prolia and between Xgeva and Prolia was studied. An extensive summary on the analytical biosimilarity assessment is provided under 4.3.5. In conclusion, ENZ2152 can be regarded biosimilar to Xgeva.

## Non-clinical aspects

The non-clinical program aimed at proving similarity between Denosumab Ascend and the EUapproved Xgeva primarily based on in vitro pharmacology studies in accordance with requirements presented in the EMA \"Guideline on similar biological medicinal products containing monoclonal antibodies - non-clinical and clinical issues\" (EMA/CHMP/BMWP/403543/2010).

Additionally, two 4-week repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and rabbits assessing safety profile after s.c. injection of denosumab for Denosumab Ascend and Xgeva were conducted. However, in vivo animal studies are not deemed necessary for biosimilarity assessment of monoclonal antibody analogues in accordance to guideline. Hence, the study is only considered for information purposes and the non-clinical package is deemed acceptable.

## Summary of Pharmacokinetics

The applicant has presented PK data from a single-dose, clinical phase I study in healthy male volunteers and from a multidose, clinical phase III study in post-menopausal osteoporosis patients. The investigational product ENZ215 is a proposed biosimilar to Xgeva while all studies are conducted using EU-Prolia as comparator. This is acceptable as Prolia and Xgeva have the same active substance (denosumab) and the mechanism of action is identical in all indications for Prolia and Xgeva. The presented data support ENZ215 as a biosimilar of Xgeva. An extensive summary on the pharmacokinetic assessment is provided under 5.2.6.

## Summary of Clinical data

Denosumab Ascend is being developed as a biosimilar to Xgeva. However, the clinical development program is based on the demonstration of biosimilarity between ENZ215 and Prolia in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis, followed by an extrapolation to all the other indications of Xgeva.

The clinical development program of ENZ215 included two clinical studies to demonstrate similarity in PK, PD, efficacy, safety and immunogenicity between ENZ215 and Prolia:

ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 was a randomized, double-blind, three-arm, parallel-group, single-dose study to compare the PK, PD, safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of Denosumab (ENZ215, EU-sourced Prolia, and US-sourced Prolia) in healthy adult male volunteers. The study was conducted at three investigational sites in three countries (Bulgaria, Poland, and UK). Subjects were randomised in a 1:1:1 ratio to one of the three treatments groups without stratification. The study had a duration of 270 days (EOS), plus a screening period of up to 28 days prior to IP administration on Day 1. Overall, the design of study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN1 is acceptable and is generally in agreement with previous Scientific Advice received from EMA (EMA/CHMP/SAWP/531236/2020, EMEA/H/SA/4580/1/2020/III, EMA/SA/0000060480).

ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 was a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, active-controlled study to compare the efficacy, safety, PD, PK, and Immunogenicity of ENZ215 and EU-Prolia in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis. The study was conducted in 44 sites that enrolled participants across the EU. The study was divided into three periods: Screening period: up to 35 days; Double-blind treatment period of 12 months; and Open-label, switch-over period of six months. Subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio for the main, double-blind treatment period. A subset of patients of the Prolia arm were offered to enroll in the open-label, switch-over extension period, where patients were re-randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either another dose of Prolia or switch to ENZ215. In total, patients received two s.c. denosumab doses during the double-blind period (on Day 1 and Month 6) and another dose during the open-label period (at Month 12). Overall, the design of study ALK22/ENZ215-DEN2 is acceptable and is generally in agreement with previous Scientific Advice received from EMA (EMA/CHMP/SAWP/531236/2020, EMEA/H/SA/4580/1/2020/III, EMA/SA/0000060480).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The mechanism of action of ENZ215 is identical across all the authorised indications of Prolia and Xgeva (the reference product). The monoclonal antibody denosumab targets and binds to RANKL, thus preventing interaction of RANKL with RANK. Blocking of interaction of RANKL with RANK leads to reduced osteoclast formation and function. Thus, bone resorption and cancer induced bone destruction are decreased. Therefore, based on the same mechanism of action, extrapolation to all indications might be allowed.

The extrapolation is further supported by the fact that the known PK, safety and immunogenicity profile of denosumab (as summarized in the product information for Xgeva) is comparable across the approved indications and patient populations.

Additionally, the clinical data were derived from healthy male volunteers and female osteoporosis patients. These are regarded as sensitive populations for the evaluation of biosimilarity between ENZ215 and the reference product.

In conclusion, the safety and efficacy profile of ENZ215 as assessed in the PMO indication can be extrapolated to all indications approved for Xgeva and ENZ215 is considered biosimilar to Xgeva.

## Safety

In the Phase 1 study with healthy male subjects, incidence of total TEAEs was slightly higher in the EU-Prolia group than in the ENZ215 and US-Prolia groups, with 39 (56.5%), 28 (41.2%), 32 (46.4%) of the subjects, respectively. No healthy subject experienced a fatal event. One healthy subject in the EU-Prolia group experienced an SAE of a ligament rupture that resulted in hospitalization, considered as not related to study treatment. No TEAE leading to discontinuation of study treatment was reported in the healthy subject study.

As regards the postmenopausal patients with osteoporosis, the number of TEAEs in the double-blind phase of the study was higher in the ENZ215 group than in the Prolia group (441 events vs. 393 events) but affected a similar number and percentage of patients as those were reported in 164 (64.8%) patients of the ENZ215 group and in 157 (62.5%) patients of Prolia group. Additionally, no significant differences were observed when comparing incidence of severe TEAEs, and TEAEs assessed related to study treatment by the investigator (ENZ215: 25 events reported in 19 [7.5%], Prolia: 36 events reported in 23 [9.2%] patients, respectively). No significant differences were reported as regards the incidence of TESAEs (ENZ215: 23 events reported in 16 [6.3%], Prolia: 17 events reported in 15 [6.0%] patients, respectively). One death occurred in a patient in the Prolia group (that was not enrolled in the open label phase) on study day 385, caused by a TESEA of Covid19, assessed that as not related to study treatment. Seven TEAEs graded severe were reported in the ENZ215 group in 5 patients (2.0%), whereas five were reported in the Prolia group in 5 patients (2.0%). TEAEs leading to treatment and study withdrawal were rare and with similar incidence between the two groups. TEAEs leading to treatment interruption were also rare and reported with similar incidence between the two groups. In the open-label phase, the number of TEAEs was higher in the ENZ215 group than in the Prolia group (36 events vs. 30 events) but affected a similar number and percentage of patients as those were reported in 23 (38.3%) patients of the ENZ215 group and in 23 (38.3%) patients of Prolia group; TEAEs considered related by the investigator were only reported in the ENZ215 group (3 events in 2 [3.3) patients). Similarly, to the healthy subjects, among the postmenopausal patients with osteoporosis, significant differences in ADA incidence between the groups were not observed for neither phase. Also, alike healthy subjects, incidence of NAbs was extremely low compared to total ADA incidence, e.g., only one subject in the Prolia group (0.4%) at Visit 5 (Day 30). Additionally, there were no significant differences in NAb incidence observable between the groups for neither phase and no patients had NAbs in either treatment group throughout the open-label extension period.
