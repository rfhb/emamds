## 1. NAME OF THE MEDICINAL PRODUCT

Zeydovio 10 mg solution for injection in pre-filled pen

## 2. QUALITATIVE AND QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITION

Each pre-filled pen contains 10 mg glepaglutide (as acetate) in 0.5 mL solution.

## 3. PHARMACEUTICAL FORM

Solution for injection (injection)

## 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

Zeydovio is indicated for the treatment of adults with Short Bowel Syndrome (SBS). Patients should be stable following a period of intestinal adaptation after surgery.

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration

Treatment should be initiated under the supervision of a medical professional with experience in the treatment of SBS.

Zeydovio treatment should not be initiated until it is reasonable to assume that the patient is stable following a period of intestinal adaptation and until the patient's parenteral fluid and nutrition support is optimised. In addition, a colonoscopy of the entire colon and an upper gastro-intestinal endoscopy (or alternative imaging) with removal of polyps as appropriate (see section 4.4) should be performed prior to start of treatment.

Posology

Adults

The recommended dose of glepaglutide is 10 mg administered twice weekly.

Treatment effect should be evaluated after 6 months. If no overall improvement is achieved after these 6 months, it should be considered to switch to an alternative treatment or discontinue GLP-2 analogue treatment as a whole.

Continued treatment is recommended for patients who have weaned off parenteral nutrition.

## Missed dose

If a dose is missed, Zeydovio should be injected as soon as possible, and the next dose should be taken on the regularly scheduled day. Two doses must not be taken on the same day.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Monitoring of treatment

Clinical assessment should consider individual treatment objectives and patient preferences. Efficacy and safety in all patients should be closely monitored on an ongoing basis according to clinical treatment guidelines. Patient's weight and nutritional status should be closely monitored during treatment with glepaglutide to ensure appropriate nutritional support.

A follow-up colonoscopy and upper endoscopy (or alternative imaging) are recommended once a year during the first 2 years after starting treatment, and then at a minimum of 5-year intervals (see section 4.4).

## Discontinuation of treatment

Discontinuation of glepaglutide may result in fluid and electrolyte imbalance. It is recommended to monitor fluid and electrolyte status in patients who discontinue glepaglutide (see section 4.4). Glepaglutide treatment should be discontinued if no overall improvement of the patient condition is achieved.

## Elderly

No dose adjustment is necessary in patients above 65 years (see section 5.2).

## Renal impairment

No dose adjustment is necessary for patients with renal impairment (see section 5.2).

## Hepatic impairment

No dose adjustment is necessary for patients with mild and moderate hepatic impairment (limited data are available in patients with moderate hepatic impairment). No data are available in patients with severe hepatic impairment. Caution should be exercised if treating these patients with glepaglutide (see section 5.2).

## Intestinal insufficiency

No dose adjustment is necessary for patients achieving enteral autonomy during treatment with glepaglutide. Limited clinical data are available related to initiation of glepaglutide in SBS patients with intestinal insufficiency.

## Paediatric population

The safety and efficacy of glepaglutide in children and adolescents below 18 years of age have not yet been established. No data are available.

## Method of administration

For subcutaneous use only.

Zeydovio should be administered twice weekly as a subcutaneous injection with an interval of 3-4 days between doses using the single-dose pre-filled pen.

Zeydovio is to be injected subcutaneously in the abdomen or thigh by using the pre-filled pen. It is recommended to alternate the injection sites within the chosen area of administration (thigh or abdomen).

For information on handling before administration, see section 6.6.

For instructions for use of the medicinal product, please refer to the 'Instructions for Use' at the end of the package leaflet.

## 4.3 Contraindications

Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Active or suspected malignancy.

Patients with a history of malignancies in the gastrointestinal tract, including the hepatobiliary system and pancreas within the last five years.

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

## Colorectal neoplasia

A colonoscopy with removal of polyps should be performed at the time of starting treatment with glepaglutide. It is recommended to perform a colonoscopy once a year during the first 2 years after starting treatment, and then at a minimum of 5-year intervals. An individual assessment whether increased frequency of surveillance is necessary should be performed based on the patient characteristics and comorbidities. See also section 5.1. If a polyp is detected, adherence to current polyp follow-up guidelines is recommended. In case of malignancy, glepaglutide treatment must be discontinued (see section 4.3).

## Gastric and small bowel neoplasia

Gastric and small intestinal polyps have been reported in clinical studies with glucagon-like peptide-2 (GLP-2) analogues (see section 4.8). Therefore, upper gastro-intestinal endoscopy or other imaging is recommended at the time of starting treatment with glepaglutide, once a year during the first 2 years of treatment and then at a minimum of 5-year intervals. If a polyp is detected, it should be removed. In case of gastrointestinal malignancy, glepaglutide treatment must be discontinued (see section 4.3).

## Intestinal stenosis and/or obstruction

Intestinal stenosis and/or obstruction has been reported in clinical studies with glepaglutide (see section 4.8). In patients who develop intestinal stenosis and/or obstruction, temporarily discontinuation of glepaglutide is recommended. Glepaglutide may be restarted when the obstructive presentation resolves, if clinically indicated. In case of recurrent intestinal stenosis and/or stomal obstruction, the need for continued treatment with glepaglutide should be reassessed.

## Gallbladder and biliary tract disease

Cholecystitis, cholangitis (see section 4.8), and cholelithiasis have been reported in clinical studies with GLP-2 analogues. Monitoring of liver and gall bladder functions is recommended. If clinically meaningful changes are seen, further evaluation including imaging of the gallbladder and/or biliary tract is recommended and the need for continued glepaglutide treatment should be reassessed.

## Pancreatic disease

Pancreatitis has been reported in clinical studies with glepaglutide (see section 4.8). In case of pancreatitis, the need for continued treatment should be reassessed.

## Fluid and electrolyte imbalance

To avoid fluid overload or dehydration, careful adjustment of parenteral support is required in patients taking glepaglutide. Electrolyte balance and fluid status should be carefully monitored throughout treatment especially during initial treatment and after discontinuation of treatment.

Fluid overload has been reported in clinical studies with glepaglutide (see section 4.8). Patients, particularly those with cardiovascular disease (such as cardiac insufficiency and hypertension), should be monitored for fluid overload and advised to contact their physician in case of sudden weight gain, face swelling, swollen ankles, and/or dyspnoea. If clinically relevant fluid overload occurs, parenteral support should be adjusted.

## Dehydration

Patients with SBS are susceptible to dehydration that may lead to acute renal failure. In patients receiving glepaglutide, parenteral support should be reduced carefully and should not be discontinued abruptly. The patient's fluid status should be evaluated following parenteral support reduction and corresponding adjustment performed, as needed.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Discontinuation of treatment

Discontinuation of treatment with glepaglutide may result in fluid and electrolyte imbalance. It is recommended to monitor fluid and electrolyte status in patients who discontinue treatment with glepaglutide.

## Concomitant medicinal products

Patients receiving oral concomitant medicinal products requiring titration or with a narrow therapeutic index should be monitored closely due to potentially increased absorption (see section 4.5).

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

No clinical interaction studies have been performed.

Based upon the pharmacodynamic effect of glepaglutide there is a potential for increased absorption of orally concomitantly administered medicinal products. It is recommended to increase clinical and/or laboratory monitoring of medicinal products which have a narrow therapeutic index or require clinical monitoring.

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation

## Pregnancy

There are no data from the use of glepaglutide in pregnant women.

Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects with respect to reproduction toxicity (see section 5.3).

As a precautionary measure, it is preferable to avoid the use of glepaglutide during pregnancy.

## Breast-feeding

It is unknown whether glepaglutide/metabolites are excreted in human milk.

Glepaglutide or its metabolites were not detected in the blood of 4-day-old pups of female rats receiving glepaglutide.

As a precautionary measure, it is preferable to avoid the use of Zeydovio during breast-feeding.

## Fertility

There are no data on the effects of glepaglutide on human fertility. Animal data do not indicate any impairment of fertility.

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines

Zeydovio has no or negligible influence on the ability to drive and use machines.

## Description of selected adverse reactions

## Hypersensitivity

Adverse reactions such as hypersensitivity, injection site rash, injection site urticaria, and rash erythematous have been reported in 9.8% of patients in clinical studies with glepaglutide. Most reactions were mild, localised and of short duration. Three events were possibly suggestive of a generalised hypersensitivity reaction to glepaglutide, but none were indicative of anaphylaxis or SCAR (severe cutaneous adverse reaction).

## Fluid retention/fluid overload

Adverse reactions relate to fluid retention or fluid overload occurred in 17.1% of the patients in the clinical studies. These reactions included oedema peripheral, hypervolaemia, fluid retention, and generalised oedema.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Intestinal stenosis and/or obstruction

Adverse reactions relating to intestinal stenosis and/or obstruction occurred in 8.9% of the patients. The events included intestinal obstruction, intestinal stenosis, small intestinal obstruction, and subileus.

## Injection site reactions

Injection site reactions were reported in 61 % of patients receiving glepaglutide. The reactions included symptoms like injection site induration, injection site bruising or heamatoma, injection site erythema, injection site pain, injection site paraesthesia, injection site swelling, injection site rash and injection site pruritus. The injection site reactions were generally mild.

## Stoma complications

Stoma complications were reported in 32.3 % of glepaglutide-treated patients with a stoma. In approximately 14% of events this led to surgical intervention. The most frequently reported stoma site complications was mild to moderate oedema/enlargement of the stoma nipple. Other stoma site complications include erythema, haemorrhage, ulceration, infection, irritation and pain.

## Immunogenicity

Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) were detected in a total of 88 % of patients treated with glepaglutide. The ADA (incidence and titers) peaked after 12-24 weeks of treatment followed by a decreasing trend with continued glepaglutide treatment. Similarly, ADA cross-reacting with human GLP-2 found in 26 % of the patients were observed to disappear over time with continued glepaglutide treatment.

There was no identified clinically significant effect of ADA on pharmacokinetics, effectiveness, or safety of glepaglutide over the treatment duration of up to 4.5 years.

## Reporting of suspected adverse reactions

Reporting suspected adverse reactions after authorisation of the medicinal product is important. It allows continued monitoring of the benefit/risk balance of the medicinal product. Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions via the national reporting system listed in Appendix V.

## 4.9 Overdose

The maximum dose of glepaglutide studied during clinical development was 96 mg in a single subcutaneous dose administered to healthy subjects. The maximum dose of glepaglutide in patients was 20 mg daily injected subcutaneously for 14 days. No unexpected systemic adverse reactions were seen.

## 5. PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Other alimentary tract and metabolism products, various alimentary tract and metabolism products, ATC code: A16AX26

## Mechanism of action

Glepaglutide is a long-acting analogue of GLP-2. Glepaglutide consists of 39 amino acids. When glepaglutide is injected subcutaneously, it forms a depot in the subcutaneous tissue, from which glepaglutide and its two main metabolites (both pharmacological active GLP-2 agonists) are released.

GLP-2 is a peptide secreted by L-cells predominantly located in the distal ileum and colon. GLP-2 possesses intestinotrophic properties and is known to increase intestinal and portal blood flow, inhibit gastric acid secretion, and intestinal motility. In non-clinical studies, glepaglutide has been shown to promote growth leading to increased mucosal thickness and length of the intestine. In a clinical phase 2 study in SBS patients, glepaglutide showed increased intestinal epithelium height and crypt depth.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Pharmacodynamic effects

In functional in vitro assays, glepaglutide is a high-affinity GLP-2 receptor agonist, with receptor activity in the picomolar range. The agonistic activity of glepaglutide is supported by in vivo data showing a significant increase in small intestinal mass and length across various animal species and dosing regimens.

Based on the mechanism of action with the trophic effects on intestinal mucosa, there appears to be a risk for the promotion of small intestinal and/or colonic neoplasia (see section 4.4).

## Intestinal absorption

The ability of glepaglutide to improve intestinal absorption was studied in 18 adult patients with SBS (13 with intestinal failure and 5 with intestinal insufficiency) using daily doses of 0.1 mg, 1 mg, 10 mg (N=12 per dose group) in a 21-day, cross-over, double-blind, single-center, dose-ranging study.

From a second study (EASE-4), effect of glepaglutide on intestinal absorption was evaluated after 24 weeks of once-weekly dosing of glepaglutide 10 mg in 10 patients with SBS (8 with intestinal failure and 2 with intestinal insufficiency).

Both studies showed a clinically relevant increase in intestinal absorptive capacity (including wet weight absorption, energy and electrolytes) for the 1 mg once daily (OD), 10 mg OD and 10 mg once weekly (OW) doses. For EASE-4 wet weight absorption, the mean standard deviation (SD) change from baseline to week 24 was 398.4 (581.6) g/day and 1 037.7 (1 182.2) kJ/day for energy absorption. The intestinal absorptive capacity parameters were increased in a similar manner in both studies.

## Intestinal epithelium

Plasma citrulline levels, a biomarker of GLP-2 receptor activation and enterocyte mass were measured in EASE-1 study. A dose-dependent increase from baseline in plasma citrulline levels was observed for glepaglutide dose groups versus placebo after 24 weeks of treatment.

## Cardiac electrophysiology

At a dose 10 times the recommended dose, glepaglutide did not prolong the QT interval to any clinically relevant extent.

## Clinical efficacy and safety

## EASE-1 study

The efficacy and safety of twice weekly (TW) or once weekly (OW) glepaglutide 10 mg was evaluated in a 24-week double blinded, randomised, multi-centre, placebo-controlled three arm study (EASE-1 study) in 106 SBS patients (randomised 1:1:1), dependent and stable on parenteral support (PS) and requiring PS at least 3 days per week.

The patient population had a mean age of 55 years (range 20 to 82 years), 25 patients were above 65 years, 4 patients were above 75 years, and 53.8 % were women. Stoma was present in 55 % (58/106) of the patients. The PS volume generally decreased over time in all three treatment groups showing a robust dose-response relationship with greater reductions achieved with more frequent glepaglutide dosing. For glepaglutide 10 mg TW there was a statistically significant reduction from baseline in PS volume compared to placebo after 24 weeks (Table 2 and Figure 1).

At week 24, a total of 9 patients were weaned off, 5 patients (14 %) in the TW group and 4 patients (11 %) in the OW group versus no patients in the placebo group.

## EASE-2 study

Long-term clinical safety and efficacy are evaluated in 114 SBS patients (96 completers of EASE-1 study and 18 naive patients randomised directly into EASE-2 study).

The results from EASE-1 study were confirmed with a reduction in PS volume change from baseline of -5.4 L/week for glepaglutide 10 mg TW at 6 months. The reductions were sustained up to 2 years with a mean reduction in PS volume of 6.4 L/week in the 28 patients who have completed 2 years of treatment with glepaglutide 10 mg TW.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

A reduction in PS ≥ 1 day/week was seen in 61.7 % of patients treated with glepaglutide 10 mg TW for 6 months, in 60.0 % after 12 months and in 67.9 % after 24 months.

A reduction from baseline of at least 20 % in weekly PS volume was found in 74.5 % of the patients, in 85.7 %, and in 92.9 % after 6, 12, and 24 months, respectively in the glepaglutide 10 mg TW treatment group.

## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

## Absorption

After subcutaneous injection of glepaglutide the time to peak exposure ranged from 4-36 h and 4-24 h for OW and TW administration, respectively. Steady state is reached after 2-3 weeks of treatment with glepaglutide.

The peak exposure in SBS patients following long-term treatment with 10 mg glepaglutide TW for up to 4.5 years was 4 731 pmol/L (geometric mean of Cmax with range [1 755; 13 510]), whereas the maximum dose of 96 mg glepaglutide administered as a single dose in a healthy subject resulted in peak exposure of 73 677 pmol/L (geometric mean of Cmax with range: [42 934; 155 908]). Higher dose levels and exposures have not been investigated.

Glepaglutide is extensively metabolised in the subcutaneous depot, resulting in a low (0.5 %) absolute bioavailability of the parent drug after subcutaneous injection. Substantial amounts of the active metabolites M1 and M2 are released from the depot and, once steady state is reached, weekly cumulative exposure (AUC0-168h) to M1 and M2 exceeds that of the parent compound by 1.8-fold and 21-fold, respectively with TW dosing. This results in a considerably higher pharmacologically relevant bioavailability of glepaglutide compared to the absolute bioavailability of the parent compound.

The accumulation ratio for total glepaglutide is approximately1.7 with TW dosing. Anti-glepaglutide antibodies have a negligible effect on this ratio.

## Distribution

Following intravenous injection, the parent drug has a volume of distribution (VSS) at steady state of 14.0 L. Similar low volumes of distribution following intravenous injections of the parent drug, M1 and M2 were seen indicating distribution to the extracellular space (based on animal data).

## Biotransformation

After subcutaneous injection of glepaglutide, active metabolites are formed by carboxy terminal proteolytic degradation. Total glepaglutide covers parent, M1 and M2. All metabolites are potent GLP-2 agonists in vitro.

Glepaglutide, M1, and M2 are further metabolised into small peptides by catabolic pathways and these are further metabolised to free amino acids.

## Elimination

The main route of elimination is via proteolytic metabolism.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The estimated effective half-life of glepaglutide 10 mg administered subcutaneously TW to SBS patients is approximately 70 hours.

Following IV dosing of healthy subjects, glepaglutide parent drug had a clearance of 5.72 L/h.

## Linearity/non-linearity

In healthy participants, total exposure (AUC) to glepaglutide increased in a dose-proportional manner following single doses up to 24 mg and at steady state following once-weekly dosing over the range of 5 to 10 mg.

## Pharmacokinetics in subpopulations

Based on observed data and population pharmacokinetic (PK) analysis age (studied range 18-82 years), gender, body weight (studied range 42.4-107 kg), race, and renal impairment (mild, moderate, severe, impairment) do not have a clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics.

## Renal impairment

In a phase 1 study, the effect of renal impairment on the pharmacokinetics of glepaglutide following subcutaneous administration of 10 mg glepaglutide was investigated. Severe renal impairment/endstage renal disease had no clinically relevant impact on the pharmacokinetics of glepaglutide. See section 4.2.

## Hepatic impairment

Based on population PK analysis, there is no clinically relvant change in the pharmacokinetics of glepaglutide in patients with mild hepatic impairment. Limited data are available in patients with moderate hepatic impairment. No data are available in patients with severe hepatic impairment. See section 4.2.

## Drug-drug interactions

In vitro studies have shown no potential for glepaglutide to inhibit or induce CYP450 enzymes, and no potential to inhibit human drug transporter proteins.

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data

Non-clinical data reveal no special hazard for humans based on conventional studies of safety pharmacology, repeated dose toxicity, genotoxicity, and toxicity to reproduction and development.

Carcinogenicity studies were performed in rats and mice. In mice, glepaglutide did not induce neoplastic growth in the gastrointestinal tract. In rats, adenoma and adenocarcinoma were seen in duodenum from the lowest dose of 0.2 mg/kg/day corresponding to exposure levels of at least 4 times the human exposure.

Fibrosarcomas were found at injection sites at all dose levels in male rats and mice and are considered a response to chronic tissue irritation and inflammation in rodents. These tumours were not seen in female animals and likely have limited relevance for humans.

## 1. What Zeydovio is and what it is used for

Zeydovio contains the active substance glepaglutide. It is used to treat adults with short bowel syndrome (SBS). SBS is a condition that occurs when someone has lost a significant part of their small intestine (gut), due to surgery or disease. Because the small intestine is responsible for absorbing nutrients, fluids, and vitamins from food, having less of it means the body cannot take in enough of what it needs.

The active substance in Zeydovio, glepaglutide, is designed to mimic a hormone in the body known as glucagon-like peptide-2 (GLP-2), which helps maintain the health of the intestines. This helps the intestines to absorb nutrients and fluids from food and drinks.

## 2. What you need to know before you use Zeydovio

## Do not use Zeydovio

- if you are allergic to glepaglutide or any of the other ingredients of this medicine (listed in section 6).
- if you have cancer or you are suspected of having cancer.
- if you have had cancer in the gastrointestinal tract (a series of organs that digests food, absorbs nutrients and removes waste from the body) in the past 5 years. This includes cancer affecting the liver, gallbladder or bile ducts or the pancreas.

Do not use Zeydovio if any of the above apply to you. If you are not sure, talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse before using Zeydovio.

## Warnings and precautions

Talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse before using Zeydovio

- if you have severely decreased liver function.
- if you have a condition affecting your heart or blood vessels (cardiovascular disease) such as high blood pressure (hypertension) or a weak heart (cardiac insufficiency). Signs of these can include sudden weight gain, swollen face or ankles, shortness of breath (dyspnoea).

If any of the above apply to you, or you are not sure, talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse before using Zeydovio. Your doctor will consider these factors when prescribing this medicine.

## Medical checks before and during treatment with Zeydovio

Polyps can grow on the inside of the stomach, the small intestine and the large intestine (colon). These are checked for by doing an endoscopy of the stomach and intestines to see if you have any polyps.

Before you start using Zeydovio, your doctor will have your stomach, small intestine and large intestine (colon) checked for polyps. If polyps are found, these will be removed.

It is recommended that your doctor will perform these examinations once a year during the first two years after starting treatment with Zeydovio, and then at a minimum of five-year interval.

If cancer is found in a polyp, your doctor would ask you to stop using the medicine.

Bowel narrowing and/or blockage: Bowel narrowing and/or blockage has been reported with glepaglutide. If you develop a bowel narrowing or blockage, your doctor will ask you to stop taking Zeydovio for a while. You may be able to start taking it again once the problem has cleared up, if your doctor thinks it is appropriate. If bowel narrowing or blockage keeps coming back, your doctor will need to consider whether you should continue treatment with Zeydovio.

Gallbladder and bile duct disease: Gallbladder inflammation, bile duct inflammation, and gallstones have been reported in patients taking medicines in the same medicine class as Zeydovio. Your doctor will monitor how your liver and gallbladder are working. If any concerning changes are found, your doctor may arrange a scan of your gallbladder and/or bile ducts, and will consider whether you should continue treatment with Zeydovio.

Inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis): Cases of inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis) have been reported in patients treated with Zeydovio. If you develop pancreatitis, your doctor will decide whether you should continue treatment with Zeydovio.

## Body fluids and electrolytes

The doctor will monitor your body fluids and electrolytes particularly when you start or stop treatment with Zeydovio. This is because an imbalance may cause fluid overload (when the body has more fluid than it can manage) or dehydration (when the body does not have enough fluid).

If you have a heart condition (such as heart failure or high blood pressure), your doctor will monitor you for signs of fluid build-up in your body. Contact your doctor straight away if you notice sudden weight gain, swelling in your face or ankles, and/or difficulty breathing. Your drip feed (intravenous support) may need to be adjusted if fluid build-up becomes a problem.

Your drip feed (intravenous support) should be reduced gradually and should not be stopped suddenly, as this can increase the risk of dehydration. Dehydration can lead to acute kidney problems. Contact your doctor if you notice symptoms of dehydration including feeling very thirsty, dizzy or lightheaded, having a dry mouth, or passing less or darker urine than usual. Your drip feed may need to be adjusted.

## 3. How to use Zeydovio

Always use this medicine exactly as your doctor has told you. Check with your doctor, pharmacist or nurse if you are not sure. Your treatment will be started by a doctor who is experienced in the treatment of short bowel syndrome.

## 4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, this medicine can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them.

## Serious side effects

Seek medical help immediately if you notice any of the following side effects:

Common (may affect up to 1 in 10 people)

- Allergic reactions (hypersensitivity). Contact your doctor or the emergency unit immediately if you experience any of the following symptoms: sudden difficulty breathing or shortness of breath; swelling (of the face, lips, tongue or throat); tightness in the throat or trouble swallowing; severe rash, hives, or itching, especially if widespread, dizziness; fainting or collapse, rapid or irregular heartbeat. These symptoms can be life-threatening and require immediate treatment.
- Intestinal stenosis and/or obstruction (narrowing and/or blockage of the bowel). Contact your doctor or the emergency unit immediately if you experience symptoms including severe stomach pain, vomiting, cramps, swelling of the abdomen or around your stoma and constipation.
- Inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis). Contact your doctor or the emergency unit immediately if you experience severe pain in the upper abdomen, nausea, vomiting, pain that worsens after eating and fever.
- Reduced flow of bile from the gallbladder and/or inflammation of the gallbladder (cholecystitis). Contact your doctor or the emergency unit if you experience symptoms including yellowing of the skin and the whitesof the eyes, itching, dark coloured urine and light-coloured stools or pain in the upper right side or middle of the abdomen.

Uncommon (may affect up to 1 in 100 people)

- Infection or inflammation of the bile duct (cholangitis). Contact your doctor or the emergency unit if you experience fever and chills, pain in the upper right side of the abdomen, yellowing of the skin or whites of the eyes, dark urine and pale or clay-colored stool, nausea or vomiting, feeling very weak or unwell.

- Fluid retention/fluid overload

- Belly (abdominal) pain

Common (may affect up to 1 in 10 people)

- Polyps (small abnormal growths) in the large bowel (colorectal)
- Polyps in the stomach (gastric)
- Constipation

Uncommon (may affect up to 1 in 100 people)

- Polyps in the first part of the small bowel (duodenal)
