## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that trial TAK-771-3005, entitled 'A Phase 3, Open-label, Non-controlled, Extension Study to Evaluate the Long-term Safety of TAK-771 in Japanese Patients with Primary Immunodeficiency Disease (PID)', is a stand-alone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

There is no paediatric formulation for HyQvia. The dose is calculated based on patient weight and the clinical response. There is no dose adjustment for the paediatric population. Please refer to the currently approved Summary of Product Characteristics. HyQvia is IGI, 10% with rHuPH20:

## Investigational product: TAK-771

## Immune globulin infusion 10 % (IGI, 10%) component of HyQvia

A ready-for-use, sterile, liquid preparation of highly purified and concentrated IgG antibodies. The distribution of the IgG subclasses was similar to that of normal plasma. The fragment crystallisable region and the antigen-binding fragment functions were maintained in the primary component. Prekallikrein activator activity was not detectable. The IGI, 10% contained 100 mg/mL protein. At least 98% of the protein was IgG. The IGI, 10%, contained a broad spectrum of IgG antibodies against bacterial and viral agents. Glycine (0.25 M) served as a stabilising and buffering agent. There were no added sugar, sodium, or preservatives. The pH was 4.6 to 5.1. The IGI, 10% was manufactured from large pools of human plasma. IgG preparations were purified from plasma pools using a modified Cohn-Oncley cold ethanol fractionation process and cation- and anion-exchange chromatography.

The solution was administered at a volume of up to 600 mL per infusion site for participants ≥40 kg or up to 300 mL per infusion site for participants &lt;40 kg, as tolerated. On a given infusion day, the maximum infusion volume was not allowed to exceed 1200 mL for participants weighing ≥40 kg or 600 mL for participants weighing &lt;40 kg. The HyQvia dose was to be administered over multiple days as divided doses with 48 to 72 hours between doses only if a participant's total IgG dose on a given day exceeded the maximum infusion volume or exceeded the SC maximum infusion volume the participant was able to tolerate.

## Recombinant human hyaluronidase (rHuPH20) component

The drug product was supplied as a sterile, clear, colorless, and ready-for-use solution. Each vial contained 160 U/mL rHUPH20 with the following excipients: sodium chloride, sodium phosphate dibasic dihydrate, human serum albumin, edentate disodium dihydrate, calcium chloride dihydrate, sodium hydroxide, and hydrochloric acid, added for pH adjustment. rHuPH20 solution contains 0.1% human serum albumin with an approximate pH of 7.4. The rHuPH20 solution was administered at a dose ratio of 80 U/g IgG before the infusion of IGI, 10%. The infusion rate previously tolerated in Trial 3004, and recommended by the investigator, was used in this extension trial. The infusion rate did not exceed 300 mL/h/site.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## Introduction

The MAH submitted a final report for:

- TAK-771-3005, entitled 'A Phase 3, Open-label, Non-controlled, Extension Study to Evaluate the Long-term Safety of TAK-771 in Japanese Patients with Primary Immunodeficiency Disease (PID)'

## Clinical study

A Phase 3, Open-label, Non-controlled, Extension Study to Evaluate the Long-term Safety of TAK-771 in Japanese Patients with Primary Immunodeficiency Disease (PID) (TAK-771-3005)

## Description

This was a phase 3, prospective, multicenter, open-label, non-controlled, single-arm extension study to evaluate the long-term safety of TAK-771 in Japanese patients with PID. This study enrolled 15 patients with PID who successfully completed Study TAK-771-3004 (hereinafter referred to as \"Study 3004\") and wished to continue to receive TAK-771; subjects receive TAK-771 at the same dose and frequency. This study included 5 paediatric patients &lt; 18 years of age and was carried out solely in Japan between September 2022 and October 2025.

## Methods

## Study participants

Trial 3005 was conducted at 9 centres in Japan. 16 participants completed Trial 3004. One participant decided not to continue to receive HyQvia and did not consent to participate in Trial 3005. The remaining 15 participants rolled over to Trial 3005. All 15 participants were included in the efficacy analysis set for extension study (EXFAS) and in the safety analysis set for extension study (EXSAS). Inclusion Criteria:

Each subject had to meet all of the following criteria to be eligible for the study.

1. Subject had completed or was about to complete Study 3004.
2. Written and/or electronic informed consent was obtained from either the subject or the subject's legally authorized representative prior to any study-related procedures and study product administration. If a subject was &lt;18 years of age, written and/or electronic informed consent was also to be obtained from the subject's legally authorized representative in addition to written informed assent by the subject if appropriate.
3. Subject was willing and able to comply with the requirements of the protocol.

Exclusion Criteria:

Subjects who met any of the following criteria were excluded from the study.

1. Subject had developed a new serious medical condition during Study 3004 such that the subject's safety or medical care would be impacted by participation in the study.
2. Subject was willing to participate in other clinical trials.
3. Women of childbearing potential who met any one of the following criteria:
- a. Subject presented with a positive pregnancy test.
- b. Subject did not agree to employ adequate birth-control measures (for example, intrauterine device) throughout the course of the study.

## Objective(s)

The primary objective of the trial was:

- To evaluate the long-term safety of HyQvia in Japanese participants with PID.

The exploratory objectives of this trial were:

- To evaluate the efficacy of HyQvia in Japanese participants with PID.
- To assess serum trough IgG concentrations following administration of HyQvia in 3- or 4-week intervals in Japanese participants with PID.
- To evaluate the tolerability of HyQvia in Japanese participants with PID.
- To assess the treatment preference of Japanese participants with PID.
- To evaluate the actual status of infusion in Japanese participants with PID.

## Other Observations Related to Efficacy

## Treatment Preference

Treatment preference was assessed at the end of trial (EOT) or early termination (ET) visit in the age groups of 2 to 13 years (observer: parent) and ≥14 years (observer: participant) separately. 5 participants were in the age group of 2 to 13 years and 10 participants were in the age group ≥14 years. ## Key Efficacy Findings

The Poisson estimates of the annual rate of VASBIs were 0.03, which are less than the Poisson estimates of an annual rate of 1.0 VASBIs per year (the threshold of substantial evidence of efficacy provided by the FDA Guidance for Industry: US Department of Health and Human Services, Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research, Safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetic studies to support marketing of immune globulin intravenous [human] as replacement therapy for primary humoral immunodeficiency, June 2008; and EMA, CHMP, Guideline on the clinical investigation of human normal immunoglobulin for intravenous administration [IVIg] - EMA/CHMP/BPWP/94033/2007 rev. 4, December 2021). Together with the ≥5 g/L IgG protective trough levels observed predominantly, the results demonstrated that HyQvia is effective in terms of protection against VASBIs. Only 1 VASBI was observed in 1 participant in the age group of ≥18 years, and its Poisson estimate of the annual rate was comparable with those observed in global phase 3 studies. The efficacy of HyQvia in long-term treatment was further demonstrated by the Poisson estimates of the annual rate of all infections, which were consistent between Trial 3005 (2.03 [95% CI: 1.37, 2.87]) and the Overall Period (2.18 [95% CI: 1.48, 3.09]). Poisson estimates for days on antibiotics, number of days not able to attend school or work or to perform normal daily activities due to illness or infection, number of hospitalizations due to illness or infection and length of stay, and number of acute physician visits due to illness or infection were similar between Trial 3005 and the Overall Period. In Trial 3005, 12 participants (80.0%) performed self-administration, and 10 participants (66.7%) received home administration. Compared with Trial 3004 over the first 6 months of treatment initiation, in which the corresponding numbers were 10 participants (62.5%) and 9 participants (56.3%) (Kanegane et al. 2026), these results suggest a trend toward greater familiarity with the administration procedure and a preference for home self-administration. ## Discussion on clinical aspects

The applicant submitted final study results from study TAK-771-3005, a Phase 3, multicenter, openlabel, uncontrolled extension study which evaluated the long-term safety, PK and efficacy of HyQvia (TAK-771) in adult and paediatric Japanese patients with PID. Patients were enrolled from their predecessor study TAK-771-3004. Overall, 15 participants rolled over to Trial 3005, 10 participants were adults, and 5 participants were paediatric study subjects aged ≤ 17 years. The dosage regimen administered in the prior study was maintained. The primary objective of the trial was to evaluate the long-term safety of HyQvia in Japanese participants with PID. Further objectives were, amongst others, the evaluation of the efficacy of HyQvia and the assessment of serum trough IgG concentrations following administration of HyQvia in 3- or 4-week intervals in Japanese participants with PID. All 15 participants were included in the EXFAS and in the EXSAS. PK analysis demonstrated that the measured serum trough levels of total IgG were stable and maintained above the clinically relevant, protective threshold of 5 g/L. This finding is consistent with the efficacy outcomes, i.e. efficacy results demonstrated that HyQvia was effective in preventing validated acute serious bacterial infections (VASBIs) with a Poisson estimate of 0.03, which is below the threshold of evidence of efficacy. Only one VASBI was observed in one adult participant that resolved during the trial without any further consequences regarding the safety of the participant or the study participation. The annual rate of all infections was consistent between the study and the overall period, i.e. efficacy analysis based on the FAS for the pooled analysis of Trials 3004 and 3005. Following HyQvia administration, frequency of acute physician visits and absenteeism from work and school were comparable than those of the general population. The hospitalisations and number of days on antibiotics were higher compared to the general population, however, similar compared to the comparator population on immunoglobulin replacement therapy. The trial showed that the administration of HyQvia was safe and well-tolerated. Serious TEAE (SAEs) and severe TEAEs were rarely reported thoughout the study, and none of them were considered to be treatment-related. The overall TEAE rates and TEAE rates per patient-year in study 3005 were lower than in the Overall Period, indicating that participants experienced fewer TEAEs with long-term treatment. The majority of TEAEs were mild and not related to HyQvia, and most resolved. Especially, no severe or serious infusion-associated TEAEs were reported, and most infusion-associated TEAEs were mild and resolved. The rates of infusion-associated TEAEs per participant year also decreased with long-term administration. Most common non-related TEAEs by PT were nasopharyngitis, pyrexia, and COVID-19. Apart from one event, the infection-associated events were mild and resolved. Most treatment-related TEAEs were infusion site erythema, pyrexia and injection site pain. Among the 5 paediatric patients, 2 experienced SAEs and 4 reported TEAEs related to the study intervention. The most common TEAEs by PT were nasopharyngitis and influenza. All related TEAEs by PT were observed in 1 participant (injection site reactions). Neither the severe TEAEs nor the SAEs were related to the treatment.

The majority of participants were able to self-administer the treatment at home, and most preferred to continue receiving HyQvia.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

Based on the study results it can be concluded that HyQvia is safe and effective in Japanese adult and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older with PID, with a very low incidence of VASBIs, annual rate of infections, a low incidence of TEAEs and a high rate of self-administration at home. No new safety concerns were identified. The results support the use of HyQvia as a treatment option for patients with PID.
