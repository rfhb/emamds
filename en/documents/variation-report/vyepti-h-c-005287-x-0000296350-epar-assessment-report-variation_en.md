## 2. Introduction

## 2.1. Therapeutic Context

In the EU, eptinezumab (Vyepti®) was approved in January 2022 for the indication prophylaxis of migraine in adults who have at least 4 migraine days per month.

Migraine is a chronic neurological disease characterized by severe headache attacks with associated hypersensitivity to environmental stimuli, as well as gastrointestinal, cognitive, and vestibular symptoms that can be severe and disabling (Buse et al. 2009). Typically, the headaches affect one half of the head, are pulsating in nature, and last from 4 to 72 hours without treatment. The disease is associated with higher frequencies of depression, anxiety disorders, sleep disturbances, cardiovascular risk, chronic pain syndromes, and suicide attempts. The therapeutic goal is to reduce the frequency and severity of migraine attacks, as well as the need for acute headache medication use.

## 2.2. Aspects of development

Eptinezumab for clinical development and commercial distribution, where approved, is currently manufactured using Pichia. As part of the ongoing product development and life cycle management to secure a continued commercial supply, H. Lundbeck A/S is in the process of changing the expression system for producing eptinezumab from Pichia to CHO. The change of the expression system is accompanied by a change in DS manufacturing site and scale. Currently, there are no plans to change the DP formulation, manufacturing site, or scale.

## 2.3. Description of the product

Eptinezumab is a humanized anti-calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) monoclonal antibody that has been developed as an intravenous (IV) formulation for the preventive treatment of migraine with an early onset of effect.

The currently approved indication in the EU is as follows: \"VYEPTI is indicated for the prophylaxis of migraine in adults who have at least 4 migraine days per month.\"

## 3. Quality aspects

## Introduction

The aim of this line extension is to change the expression system for the manufacturing of eptinezumab active substance (AS) from a yeast-based Pichia pastoris system to a Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell-based system. The change of expression system is accompanied by a change in the active substance manufacturing site and scale. There were no changes to the finished product formulation, manufacturing site, or scale.

The finished product (FP) is presented as concentrate for solution for infusion containing 100 mg or 300 mg of eptinezumab as active substance. Other ingredients are: sorbitol (E420), L-histidine, L-histidine hydrochloride monohydrate, polysorbate 80 and water for injections.

The product is available in 4 ml type I glass vial with chlorobutyl rubber stopper.

## 3.1. Active substance

## 3.1.1. General information

Eptinezumab is a genetically engineered humanized monoclonal antibody directed against the human α-calcitonin gene-related peptide (α-CGRP) and is based on a human immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) framework containing heavy chain VH and light chain VL subgroup sequences.

The recombinant antibody is produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells and consists of two heavy chains and two light chains.

## 3.1.2. Manufacture, characterisation, and process controls

## 3.1.2.1. Description of manufacturing process and process controls

## Manufacturers

Eptinezumab using the newly implemented CHO-expression system is manufactured at Samsung Biologics Co. Ltd. (300 Songdo Bio-Daero, Yeonsu-gu, Incheon, 21987, Republic of Korea).

Valid GMP compliance documentation has been provided for all sites involved in the manufacture of the active substance.

## Manufacturing process and process controls

Eptinezumab is produced in a fed-batch process using a suspension-adapted CHO cell line. The source is the Working Cell Bank (WCB), which is derived from the Master Cell Bank (MCB).

The upstream manufacturing process of eptinezumab active substance is carried out across four successive stages: Thaw and Inoculum expansion; Seed bioreactors; Production bioreactor; Harvest.

Expansion of the cell culture at several scales is to accumulate sufficient cell mass.

The production bioreactor is operated in fed-batch mode. Process parameters are controlled by the equipment control system to provide optimal conditions for cell culture growth and production.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Critical process parameters (CPP) and critical in-process controls (CIPCs) have been defined for the production bioreactor, bulk AS and harvest steps with specified conditions.

The downstream process consists of purification, followed by further purification of eptinezumab accomplished by the use of chromatography steps. The resulting solution undergoes to formulate and concentrate the final AS, followed by filtration, filling, and storage.

The steps are described with sufficient detail. Adequate in-process controls (IPCs) and PPs have been defined and are indicated. The batch numbers are assigned to the individual batch records. The information provided is sufficient and acceptable.

## Control of materials

The raw materials used in the manufacture of eptinezumab active substance are purchased from qualified and approved vendors. Upon receipt, raw materials are stored according to predetermined procedures. In accordance with the respective manufacturing site's standard operating procedures, raw materials are inspected, sampled, and tested under appropriate conditions and according to the respective acceptance criteria. The responsible unit compiles and reviews all test results and vendor certificates. Finally, all raw materials must be approved before they can be used in the manufacturing process. All the non-compendial raw materials are tested and released for eptinezumab manufacturing based on the in-house specifications.

The expression system for eptinezumab was transferred from Pichia pastoris to CHO cells. The DNA-encoded mature heavy and light chain polypeptide sequences. The description of the expression vector development is considered sufficient.

Master Cell Bank (MCB) and Working Cell Bank (WCB) were manufactured and characterized and tested according to the ICH Q5A guideline. Future WCB will be manufactured according to a pre-defined protocol.

The in vitro cell age was determined by limit-of-in-vitro-cell-age (LIVCA) analyses. Genetic stability was verified.

## Control of critical steps and intermediates

Critical manufacturing steps and intermediates are adequately defined and sufficiently controlled. Deviation from CPP and Critical In-Process control (cIPC) acceptance criteria leads to an investigation and possible batch rejection. Exceeding Critical In-Process Test (cIPT) limits leads to batch rejection. This is considered acceptable.

## Process validation

The active substance manufacturing process has been validated adequately. Chromatography resin and membrane lifetime studies were performed using small-scale and manufacturing-scale runs. Relevant performance indicators, carry-over and microbial controls were evaluated. The overall resin and membrane lifetime validation strategy was considered acceptable.

## 3.1.2.2. Manufacturing process development

The manufacturing process has been updated and a new expression system (CHO) established. The former process (Pichia pastoris) has been termed process A and the new process B. Several major changes have been incorporated due to the change in the expression system. The changes have been described in sufficient detail.

Site- and scale-independent process characterization (PC) studies were performed using scale-down models (SDMs) to support the classification of parameters as critical, those which impact critical quality attributes (CQAs), and non-critical, those which impact key performance indicators (KPIs). Critical input parameters were designated as critical process parameters (CPPs) whereas non-critical input parameters were designated as process parameters (PPs). The results were also used to define the target ranges (TRs) and/or acceptable ranges (ARs) of the upstream process parameters.

A pre-process characterization (pre-PC) risk assessment was performed to identify potential CPPs to be included in process characterisation studies. Process parameters given were evaluated on the severity of their potential impact. The risk evaluation considered both the severity ranking and the manufacturing control capability to rank and select the higher risk parameters to include in PC.

The pre-PC risk assessment incorporated development data, platform knowledge, scientific principles, a process failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA), manufacturing history, and subject matter expertise (SME) from process development, manufacturing, analytical development, quality control, and quality assurance as inputs.

Studies were used for cell culture process characterization.

The studies were performed using qualified scale down models (SDM). Following completion of the comparison, all SDMs were deemed to be representative of their respective manufacturing-scale steps and are considered qualified SDMs.

The final criticality assignment of process parameters for each manufacturing step including their set acceptance criteria is considered acceptable.

The manufacturing process was validated using consecutive batches. All CQAs, and process parameter limits were met for all batches. Therefore, the process is considered sufficiently validated. Parameters were independently investigated.

Hold times for media and buffers were validated based on studies, which is considered adequate.

The impact of process steps was investigated using small-scale studies.

An ICH Q5E compliant comparability study has been performed using Process A and B material. Due to the major change of the expression system differences were identified, as expected. It was concluded that the differences are unlikely to increase the risk of immunogenicity, which is supported. The results further showed no increase in immunogenic risk with Process B in comparison to Process A material.

The ICH Q5E comparability study included Process B batches which were compared to historical results from Process A active substance batches. Batch release data extended characterization, and stability data were investigated.

There were differences in both Process A and B materials. These can be attributed to the change in the expression system. The in-depth analyses showed no differences which are expected to affect the safety or efficacy of the product. Therefore, the Process A and B materials show overall comparable results.

Extended characterization data were provided as part of the ICH Q5E package. The primary sequence was confirmed for both materials.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Therefore, taking also the overall low levels and data from functional potency assays into account the difference is not considered to have an effect on the safety or efficacy of the product. Data showed some differences in band patterns which could be attributed to differences in degradation products or differences in disulphide linkages. Different assays showed comparable results between both materials. Forced degradation studies were provided as part of the ICH Q5E Guideline package.

In conclusion, Process A and B materials are considered comparable.

## 3.1.4. Stability

The Eptinezumab CHO active substance (CHO-AS) stability program includes three CHO batches as primary, the PPQ batch as well as three Phase III clinical trial batches and the engineering batch manufactured at Samsung as supportive stability data.

In line with ICH Q5C Guideline, the batches were tested under long-term and accelerated conditions. Furthermore, forced degradation studies were performed. All acceptance criteria were met and only under accelerated conditions changes were detected. This is expected for mAbs under these conditions. Based on the stability data provided the claimed shelf-life for the active substance under long-term conditions is acceptable.

Stability under stress conditions was evaluated during the manufacturing process comparability exercise (described in section S.2.6 of the dossier).

Taken together, it can be concluded that the used analytical methods are sufficiently stability indicating. An adequate post-approval stability protocol has been provided and it has been committed that a minimum of one batch of eptinezumab BDS will be put on long-term stability at the recommended storage condition every year that manufacturing of such batches occurs. Finally, a sufficient post-approval stability protocol is in place.

## 3.2.1. Description of the product and pharmaceutical development

## 3.2.1.2. Pharmaceutical development

The eptinezumab active substance is manufactured in either P. pastoris or CHO cells. The physicochemical and biological properties of the active substance have been adequately discussed, specified and controlled.

## Virus safety

The safety strategy for adventitious viruses includes the use of the host cell line, establishment of a twotiered cell bank system, testing for potential virus contaminants in the cell banks, testing of production cultures for potential viral contaminants, development and use of a chemically-defined cell culture medium, employment of a purification process including dedicated virus reduction steps, a rational evaluation of the overall ability of the purification process to remove/inactivate viruses, and a formulation that is devoid of human- or animal-derived materials. The eptinezumab cell banks (MCB, WCB, and LIVCA cells) were screened sufficiently for endogenous and adventitious viruses and found to be negative with the exception of A-type and C-type retrovirus-like particles (RVLP).

Each antibody batch will be routinely screened at the stage of unprocessed bulk harvest for adventitious viruses by an in vitro assay using suitable indicator cell lines and read outs. The methods have been validated and the validation report for the in vitro assay has been provided. ## 5.2.3. Pharmacodynamics

## 5.2.3.1. Mechanism of action

Eptinezumab is a humanized anti-CGRP IgG1 monoclonal antibody that has been developed as IV formulation for the preventive treatment of migraine. CGRP is a member of the calcitonin family of peptides; eptinezumab inhibits both α-CGRP and β-CGRP from binding to its receptor.

## 5.2.3.2. Primary and secondary pharmacology

## Target engagement

The efficacy of eptinezumab is mediated through its binding to CGRP. To address if eptinezumab produced using Pichia or CHO is comparable with respect to clinical performance, a post-hoc assessment was conducted to measure the eptinezumab-CGRP complex in serum samples from participants in Trial 19889A. For this purpose, an exploratory ECLIA S-PLEX assay, originally developed for plasma-based detection, was adapted for serum sample analysis.

## 5.2.4. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical pharmacology

## 5.2.4.1. Discussion

In the EU, eptinezumab (Vyepti®) is approved since January 2022 for the indication prophylaxis of migraine in adults who have at least 4 migraine days per month. Currently, eptinezumab for clinical development and commercial distribution is manufactured using the yeast Pichia pastoris as expression system. As part of the ongoing product development and life cycle management to secure a continued commercial supply, the Applicant is in the process of changing the expression system for producing eptinezumab from Pichia to CHO. The clinical data provided with this submission are intended to support this switch of the expression system.

Trial 19889A was designed and conducted to investigate the comparability of eptinezumab produced using Pichia (reference) and eptinezumab produced using CHO (test) with respect to PK, safety and tolerability after administration of a single IV dose of eptinezumab 300 mg.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## PK assay

For the quantitative determination of eptinezumab in Trial 19889A, the same ELISA procedure was used as applied in previous clinical trials (method WIL-997005). Since then, two partial validations had been conducted for this ELISA method. First, the assay was partially validated for the quantification of eptinezumab in human plasma using frozen calibration standards and frozen quality controls. Subsequently, the assay was partially validated for the quantification of CHO-derived eptinezumab. In both partial validation exercises, it was shown that the assay continuously performs with adequate accuracy and precision. Sample stability of eptinezumab PK samples produced using CHO is however expected to be similar to what was demonstrated for eptinezumab produced using Pichia.

## Study design (Trial 19889A)

Trial 19889A was conducted in the US and was a randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, single-dose comparative PK trial in healthy participants.

The study duration was 20 weeks (140 days), which is considered sufficient to adequately cover the time of eptinzumab exposure after a single administration, both in terms of an appropriate description of the PK parameters and sufficiently long follow-up for safety.

The primary PK endpoint of Trial 19889A was AUC(0-inf). Secondary PK endpoints comprised AUC0-inf (AUC%extrap), Cmax, AUC0-t, t½, CL, and Vz. The selected primary and secondary PK endpoints are acceptable.

For demonstration of comparative PK, the equivalence margin was pre-specified to be 75 - 133% and thus wider than the margin that is usually recommended (= 80 - 125%, see EMA guideline on the investigation of bioequivalence)..

The highest (300 mg) eptinezumab dose approved was administered in Trial 19889A. This is in line with the EMA guidance on bioequivalence, where it is described that in case of linear PK, the bioequivalence study should generally be conducted at the highest strength.

PK samples were collected pre-dose on Day 1, at the end of infusion, and 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12 hours post Day 1 administration. Further PK samples were collected on Day 2, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42, 56, 70, 84, 112, and 140. This schedule is considered sufficiently dense to allow for an adequate characterization of the full eptinezumab PK profile.

## Participants and demographics

In total, 84 healthy participants were enrolled and randomized 1:1 to receive either 300 mg eptinezumab produced using Pichia (reference) or 300 mg eptinezumab produced using CHO (test).

Baseline characteristics of participants were overall balanced, except for a higher proportion of male participants enrolled in the IV CHO test arm as compared to the IV Pichia reference arm (57.1% vs. 42.9%). Likely as a result, there was also a slight difference in body weight between the IV CHO test and the IV Pichia reference arm (74.3 kg vs. 71.2 kg). Given that both male and female participants were allowed to enroll in Trial 19889A, the study would have benefited from gender stratification to avoid differences in gender and weight between the treatment arms. The slightly higher body weight in the test arm may have contributed to the observed lower exposure of eptinezumab in the IV CHO test arm.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Results

PK results were provided both for the PKSB and PKSB excluding 2 participants with atypical PK profiles. In general, as outlined in the EMA bioequivalence guideline, exclusion of data cannot be accepted on the basis of pharmacokinetic reasons alone. However, the explanation provided by the Applicant, who assumes that unintentional extra vasal infusion occurred in these participants, is considered reasonable. The exclusion of the 2 participants with atypical PK profiles can therefore be agreed. Moreover, it should be noted that PK results formally confirmed equivalence of AUC(0-inf) also in the analysis where the 2 participants had not been excluded.

Overall, the results of the statistical analysis of the primary endpoint in the PKSB revealed that AUC(0-inf) was comparable between eptinezumab produced using CHO (test) and eptinezumab produced using Pichia (reference), with the 90% CI for the eptinezumab AUC0-inf geometric mean ratio within the pre-defined comparability range of 75 - 133%. Of note, the 90% CI for the eptinezumab AUC0-inf geometric mean ratio was also contained within the conventional 80 - 125% equivalence range. It is however noted that the ratios of PK parameters comparing eptinezumab produced using CHO and eptinezumab produced using Pichia were consistently below 100%, with the upper bound of the 90% CI also lower than or only marginally above 100%. Nevertheless, PK equivalence was formally demonstrated, and as discussed above, results may also have been biased by the slight imbalance observed in baseline body weight, which was higher in the treatment arm receiving eptinezumab produced using CHO as compared to the treatment arm receiving eptinezumab produced using Pichia. Since eptinezumab was administered as fixed dose of 300 mg, participants with higher body weight are expected to exhibit lower eptinezumab exposure.

Importantly, AUC%extrap was low and thus, the primary parameter AUC0-inf can be considered reliable.

Comparability in the PKSB was further demonstrated for the secondary endpoint AUC0-t.

When applying the wider margin of 75 - 133%, equivalence in the PKSB was also shown for Cmax . The conventional equivalence margin of 80 - 125% was however only met when Cmax results were adjusted for body weight and when the 2 participants with atypical PK profiles were excluded . Accounting for the fact that Cmax is usually not a key PK parameter for demonstration of bioequivalence of IV administered drugs, this issue is not considered of significant concern.

No major differences were observed in the PK parameters such as tmax, t1/2, CL and Vz investigated as secondary endpoints in Trial 19889A.

## Pharmacodynamics

The efficacy of eptinezumab is mediated through its binding to CGRP. In further support of the assumption that the change of the expression system does not negatively impact the efficacy of eptinezumab, a posthoc assessment of target engagement in Trial 19889A was conducted. For this purpose, eptinezumab-CGRP complex concentrations were determined by an ECLIA S-PLEX assay. This assay method was originally developed for plasma-based detection, but was adapted and subsequently validated for serum sample analysis. The data are solely considered supportive/exploratory, given that less strict criteria as compared to the ICH M10 guideline were applied for validation and not all parameters met pre-defined requirements.

Eptinezumab-CGRP complex concentrations were assessed on Day 28, Day 56, Day 84, Day 112, and Day 140. The mean concentrations of the complex measured at the respective timepoints were overall comparable. In conclusion, it was shown that eptinezumab-CGRP complex concentrations were largely comparable between the test and the reference arm. Based on these data, no significant impact of the changed expression system on clinical performance of eptinezumab may be expected.

Immunogenicity There were no changes to the ADA assay format that was applied for analysis of samples from the pivotal registrational studies (described in the original ISI). Nevertheless, the former, fully validated ADA and NAb assays were meanwhile partially validated, e.g. for detection of antibodies against both Pichia- and CHOderived eptinezumab. Partial validation results demonstrate that the assay overall performed with acceptable precision, except in case of some QC samples during NAb assay validation. However, the higher variability observed within the partial validation is not suggested to significantly impact the ability of the assay to detect positive NAb samples using eptinezumab from either CHO or Pichia.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

While the drug tolerance level of the ADA assay (determined to be higher in partial validation exercise: 125 µg/mL; initially: 25 µg/mL) is considered sufficient for reliable detection of anti-eptinezumab antibodies, drug tolerance of the NAb assay was found to be far below the concentrations determined for eptinezumab in Trials 19889A, at least at the early ADA sampling timepoints. It is therefore possible that NAb rates in Trial 19889A were underestimated. However, considering that ADA or NAb positivity in general did not result in diminished efficacy or any safety concerns, issues with regard to the NAb assay are not further pursued at this point.

ADA samples in Trial 19889A were collected on Day 1 (pre-dose), Day 28, Day 56, Day 84, Day 112, and Day 140. Two additional assessments 12 and 24 weeks after the Follow-Up Visit were conducted for those participants that were found to be ADA-positive on Day 140. The incidence of ADA (all post-dose timepoints) was slightly lower in participants treated with eptinezumab produced using CHO as compared to participants treated with eptinezumab produced using Pichia. Similarly, the incidence of NAbs against eptinezumab was lower in the eptinezumab CHO arm. Accounting for the low number of participants in each treatment arm, no valid conclusions may be drawn from this data. Lower immunogenicity observed in equivalence trials is not considered an issue precluding an approval.

The impact of ADA-positivity on PK and safety was also investigated in Trial 19889A. Eptinezumab exposure appears to be generally decreased in ADA-positive as compared to ADA-negative participants. However, this effect seems to be less pronounced in participants treated with eptinezumab produced using CHO. Of note, a marginal impact of ADA on eptinezumab exposure has already been observed in the pivotal trials supporting the marketing authorization of Vyepti, while no relevant impact on efficacy or safety had been demonstrated. Taken together, the change of the expression system is not considered to (negatively) alter previous conclusions drawn on ADA impact on PK, efficacy, or safety.

Noteworthy, referring to the overall immunogenicity results (AR Table 6), ADA/NAb results from participants treated with eptinezumab produced using Pichia in Trial 19889A seem to be comparable to the results obtained in previous clinical trials.

## 5.2.4.2. Conclusions

Overall, results from Trial 19889A demonstrated that PK is comparable between eptinezumab produced using Pichia or eptinezumab produced using CHO. No clinically relevant differences in immunogenicity (incidence of ADA or NAbs and impact on PK or safety) were detected. Likewise, target engagement results revealed comparable concentrations of eptinezumab-CGRP complex between eptinezumab produced using Pichia or CHO and thus, no relevant difference in clinical performance is expected.

## 5.4.6. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical safety

## 5.4.6.1. Discussion

All 84 participants in Trial 19889A were monitored for the occurrence of adverse events after administration of the single 300 mg eptinezumab dose until Day 140. The number of participants in the treatment arms is however too low to draw any reliable conclusions on safety. The above presented results are therefore solely considered supportive.

Overall, 24 subjects in the eptinezumab CHO (test) group and 27 subjects in the eptinezumab Pichia (reference) group reported at least 1 adverse event, with similar numbers of adverse events being reported for both treatment groups (43 and 45, respectively). There were no SAEs, and 1 subject had an adverse event leading to withdrawal (pregnancy).

In the eptinezumab CHO (test) group, 39 adverse events in 22 subjects were mild, and 4 adverse events in 4 subjects were moderate. In the eptinezumab Pichia (reference) group, 42 adverse events in 26 subjects were mild, and 3 adverse events in 3 subjects were moderate.

No subjects reported any severe adverse events.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In the eptinezumab CHO (test) group, 34 adverse events in 21 subjects were not related to the IMP, and 9 adverse events reported by 8 subjects were possibly or probably related to the IMP. In the eptinezumab Pichia (reference) group, 40 adverse events in 27 subjects were not related to the IMP, and 5 adverse events in 5 subjects were possibly related to the IMP.

In both treatment groups, the most commonly reported adverse events were those related to COVID-19 (preferred terms; COVID-19 and SARS-CoV-2 test positive) and upper respiratory tract infections (preferred terms; upper respiratory tract infection, pharyngitis, and viral upper respiratory tract infection).

There were no apparent trends in the mean or individual clinical safety laboratory data and no apparent trends in vital signs. Individual values outside of the reference ranges were identified but these generally occurred at isolated timepoints.

No clinically significant abnormal ECG results were reported.

Referring to the totality of safety data provided, no signal of relevant differences in the occurrence of adverse events was identified between the treatment arms (eptinezumab CHO vs. eptinezumab Pichia).

## 5.4.6.2. Conclusions on clinical safety

Due to the low sample size, safety data from Trial 19889A are solely considered as supportive evidence. There is no signal of relevant differences in the occurrence of adverse events between eptinezumab produced using CHO vs. eptinezumab produced using Picha. However, no final or valid conclusions can be drawn from the data.

## Therapeutic context

## 6.1.1. Disease or condition, therapeutic indication

In the EU, eptinezumab (Vyepti®) was approved in January 2022 for the indication prophylaxis of migraine in adults who have at least 4 migraine days per month.

Migraine is a chronic neurological disease characterized by severe headache attacks with associated hypersensitivity to environmental stimuli, as well as gastrointestinal, cognitive, and vestibular symptoms that can be severe and disabling (Buse et al. 2009). Typically, the headaches affect one half of the head, are pulsating in nature, and last from 4 to 72 hours without treatment. The disease is associated with higher frequencies of depression, anxiety disorders, sleep disturbances, cardiovascular risk, chronic pain syndromes, and suicide attempts. The therapeutic goal is to reduce the frequency and severity of migraine attacks, as well as the need for acute headache medication use.

## 6.1.2. Available therapies

In case of infrequent migraine attacks (less than 2 times per month), treatment is limited to acute medications that include: triptans, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, combination of analgesics, opioids, and ergots.

In case of frequent migraine attacks (2-6 times or more per month) prophylactic drugs are introduced on a daily basis, including antihypertensive, anti-epileptic, or antidepressant drugs. Most of the commonly used prophylactic drugs have a registered indication, however others are used off-label with limited evidence of efficacy. The safety profile of these drugs is not optimal with neurological AEs including dizziness, vertigo, nausea, anorexia, fatigue, memory problems, paraesthesia, often requiring dose titration, and carrying contraindications and warnings.

As a new therapeutic class, anti-CGRP mAb therapies have recently been authorised. The rapid onset of treatment effect and the less frequent administration scheme, in conjunction with a good tolerability and safety profile are meaningful advances of these new therapies.

Eptinezumab was the first anti-CGRP treatment developed for the intravenous route of administration. The approved dosing regimen is 100 mg eptinezumab administered by 30-minute IV infusion every 12 weeks, while some patients may receive a dosage of 300 mg administered by 30-minute IV infusion every 12 weeks.

## 6.2. Main clinical studies

Trial 19889A was conducted to investigate the comparability of eptinezumab produced using Pichia (reference) and eptinezumab produced using CHO (test) with respect to PK properties and to evaluate the safety and tolerability of a single IV dose of eptinezumab 300 mg. The trial was designed as a randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, single-dose trial in healthy participants.

For a detailed description of the main clinical study supporting this application, please refer to section 6.2.2 of this document.

## 6.3. Favourable effects

The data from the comparability study between eptinezumab using P.pastoris or CHO as expression system do not raise concerns regarding the safety or efficacy of the drug product. As shown in the characterization studies eptinezumab expressed in CHO cells show differences in post-translational modifications. A risk assessment was performed for the differences and in vitro immunogenicity assays (MHC-associated Peptide Proteomics (MAPPS) and Cytokine Release Assay (CRA)) were performed. The data do not raise issue regarding differences on safety or efficacy.

The results of the PK comparability assessment in Trial 19889A show that eptinezumab produced using Pichia (reference) or CHO (test) was comparable with respect to the primary endpoint AUC(0-inf) when administered at 300 mg IV to healthy participants.

## 6.3.1. Uncertainties and limitations about favourable effects

None

## 6.4. Unfavourable effects

All participants in Trial 19889A were monitored for the occurrence of adverse events after administration of a single 300 mg eptinezumab dose until Day 140.

Based on the limited safety data provided, no signal of relevant differences in the occurrence of adverse events was identified between the treatment arms (eptinezumab CHO vs. eptinezumab Pichia).

In Trial 19889A, immunogenicity was slightly lower in participants treated with eptinezumab produced using CHO as compared to participants treated with eptinezumab produced using Pichia (ADA incidence: 9.5% vs. 16.7%). Similarly, the incidence of NAbs against eptinezumab was lower in the eptinezumab CHO arm (4.8% vs. 11.9%).

## 6.4.1. Uncertainties and limitations about unfavourable effects

The number of participants in Trial 19889A (N=84, 42 participants in each treatment arm) is too low to draw any reliable conclusions on safety and immunogenicity. Besides, only a single dose of eptinezumab 300 mg was administered.

## 6.5. Benefit-risk assessment and discussion

## 6.5.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

The comparability of eptinezumab expressed in Pichia and CHO cells did not raise concerns regarding safety or efficacy of the drug product.

Comparability in clinical PK between eptinezumab produced using Pichia and eptinezumab produced using CHO has been adequately demonstrated in Trial 19889A, with the geometric mean ratio and 90% CI of the primary endpoint AUC(0-inf) ranging within the standard bioequivalence margin of 80 - 125%. Secondary endpoints supported PK similarity.

Safety and immunogenicity data are inconclusive due to the low sample size and single administration of eptinezumab. However, no clinically relevant differences in safety are indicated, and immunogenicity data suggest that eptinezumab produced using CHO could be even less immunogenic than eptinezumab produced using Pichia. Ultimately, comparability of safety and immunogenicity between eptinezumab produced using Pichia and eptinezumab produced using CHO needs to be mainly inferred from the analytical comparability exercise and similar PK profiles.

## 6.5.2. Balance of benefits and risks

From a quality perspective eptinezumab expressed in Pichia and CHO cells is considered comparable. Some differences were detected, which can be attributed to the difference in expression systems. However, all differences were sufficiently addressed and justified to not affect efficacy or safety of the drug product.

In the clinical trial 19889A, it was demonstrated that eptinezumab produced using CHO is comparable to eptinezumab produced using Pichia with respect to PK characteristics and no negative or clinically relevant impact of the manufacturing change on safety and immunogenicity was apparent.

The benefit-risk of eptinezumab produced using CHO in the approved indications can therefore be considered equally positive as previously assessed for eptinezumab produced using Pichia.

## 6.6. Benefit-risk conclusions

Based on the available data, the benefit-risk of eptinezumab produced using CHO as compared to eptinezumab produced using Pichia is considered equally positive.

## 6.6.1. Final CHMP conclusions

Based on the available data, the benefit-risk of eptinezumab produced using CHO as compared to eptinezumab produced using Pichia is considered equally positive.
