## 1. Introduction

On 14 April 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Bexsero, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that 217043 (MENACWY=MEN7B-003) is part of a clinical development program. The present study was designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of 2 MenACWY-7B vaccine formulations and of the MenABCWY vaccine in healthy infants 2 MoA at enrollment. The comparators MenB (Bexsero) and Nimenrix (meningococcal groups A, C, W-135 and Y TT-conjugated vaccine, hereafter referred to as MenACWY-TT) were administered as control vaccines.

The aim of this study was to provide safety and immunogenicity data in infant population to support the selection of the MenACWY-7B vaccine formulation to be tested in Phase 3 clinical studies. In the 217043 study, Bexsero was used as one of the active comparator vaccines.

The study has not been conducted according to an agreed Paediatric Investigation Plan (PIP) for Bexsero; however, it was conducted according to the agreed PIP for MenACWY-7B.

## Brief licensing history:

The vaccine is currently approved in over 60 countries and is indicated for active immunisation of individuals from 2 months of age and older with some differences in the age indication depending on the country where the vaccine is registered.

In January 2013, a centralised marketing authorisation in the European Union (EU) granted Bexsero approval for use in individuals from 2 months of age and older against invasive meningococcal disease (IMD) caused by Neisseria meningitidis ( N. meningitidis ) serogroup B.

Bexsero has been approved in the United States (US) since 2015 for active immunisation to prevent IMD caused by N. meningitidis serogroup B in adolescents and young adults 10 through 25 years of age.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

The current vaccine formulation of Bexsero is based on 3 proteins: factor H binding protein (fHbp), Neisseria adhesin A (NadA), and Neisseria heparin binding antigen (NHBA) or 287. The fHbp protein is fused with the accessory protein GNA2091 (936) and the 287 protein is fused with GNA1030 (953), resulting in 2 well - characterised fusion proteins included in the vaccine formulation. In addition, the vaccine contains OMV (with porin A [porA] antigen) derived from the NZ epidemic strain.

In addition, GSK is developing the investigational MenACWY-7B vaccine, composed of the same MenACWY-CRM glycoconjugates, in the same amounts as the licensed Menveo vaccine, and 7 serogroup B (Men7B) antigens which include the 4 MenB antigens present in Bexsero (NadA, NHBA953, 936-fHbp, Outer Membrane Vesicles [OMV]) and 3 additional antigens. A First-Time in Human Phase I/II study is currently ongoing in healthy adults and adolescents and is designed to assess safety, tolerability, effectiveness and immunogenicity of 2 formulations of the investigational MenACWY-7B vaccine.

## Introduction

## Clinical study

## 217043 (MENACWY=MEN7B-003)

## Description

The MENACWY=MEN7B-003 was a Phase 2, randomised, partially blinded, multi-country, selfcontained dose-finding study in healthy infants comprising of 2 parts designed to ensure the safety of participants (Figure 1).

## Part I

This part of the study was designed to include 4 study groups (2 groups in Step 1 and 2 groups in Step 2, randomised 3:1 in both steps) enrolled with a staggered approach (20 participants in Step 1, followed by 20 participants in Step 2) for a total of 40 participants.

The administration of the second vaccine dose in Step 1 was initially planned to happen after the positive outcome of the review of the safety data from the 20 participants receiving the first dose in Step 1. However, the study did not meet the planned enrollment of 20 participants in Step 1 by the scheduled date. To reduce potential impact on the study sites in Spain and the UK, the following strategy was adopted: in an ad-hoc meeting, IDMC reviewed the safety data for 15 participants and issued recommendations regarding the second dose administration for participants already enrolled. Enrollment in Step 1 continued in the UK and Poland until the target of 20 participants was achieved. The IDMC reviewed the safety data for these 20 participants as originally intended, and provided guidance on the second dose administration for newly enrolled participants as well as on dose escalation for the start of Step 2 in Part I.

## Step 1

- ACWY-7B low-dose group: Participants received 3 doses of the investigational MenACWY-7B low-dose vaccine at 2, 4 and 12 MoA ('low dose').
- MenB+MenACWY-TT group: Participants received 3 doses of both comparators, MenB and MenACWY-TT vaccines, at 2, 4 and 12 MoA as control for the ACWY-7B low-dose group.

## Step 2

- ACWY-7B high-dose group: Participants received 3 doses of the investigational MenACWY-7B high-dose vaccine at 2, 4 and 12 MoA ('high dose').
- MenB+MenACWY-TT group: Participants received 3 doses of both comparators, MenB and MenACWY-TT vaccines, at 2, 4 and 12 MoA as control for the ACWY-7B high-dose group.

## Methods

## Study participants

This study was conducted in 33 sites that enrolled 726 participants in the Dominican Republic, Finland, Germany, Honduras, Poland, South Africa, Spain, and the United Kingdom (UK).

## Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Participants were eligible to be included in the study only if all of the following criteria applied:

- A male or female between, and including, 55 and 89 days of age (approximately 2 MoA) at the time of the first study vaccination.
- Healthy participants as established by medical history and clinical examination before entering into the study.
- Born after a gestation period of ≥ 37 weeks, with a birth weight ≥ 2.5 kg.
- Participants' parent(s)/LAR(s) who, in the opinion of the investigator, could and would comply with the requirements of the protocol (e.g. completion of the eDiaries, return for follow-up visits and were available for telephone calls).
- Written or witnessed/thumb printed informed consent obtained from the parent(s)/LAR(s) of the participant prior to performance of any study specific procedure.

Participants were excluded from the study if any of the following criteria applied:

- Current or previous, confirmed or suspected disease caused by N. meningitidis .
- Household contact with and/or intimate exposure to an individual with laboratory confirmed N. meningitidis infection from birth.
- Progressive, unstable or uncontrolled clinical conditions.
- Clinical conditions representing a contraindication to intramuscular vaccination and blood draws.
- Any neuroinflammatory disorders (including but not limited to: demyelinating disorders, encephalitis or myelitis of any origin), congenital and peripartum neurological conditions, encephalopathies, seizures (including all subtypes such as: absence seizures, generalised tonic-clonic seizures, partial complex seizures, partial simple seizures or febrile convulsions).

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

## Participant identification

Participant identification numbers was assigned sequentially to the individuals who had consented to participate in the study. Each study centre was allocated a range of participant identification numbers.

## Randomisation to study intervention

The randomisation of supplies within blocks was performed at GSK.

## Intervention allocation to the participant

The randomisation algorithm used a minimisation procedure accounting for study.

Once a participant identification number was allocated, the randomisation system determined study group and provided the study intervention number to be used for the first dose. The study intervention number(s) that was used for subsequent dosing was provided by the same automated Internet-based system (SBIR). Concomitantly administered non-study vaccines was allocated via SBIR.

## Blinding and unblinding

In Part 1, data was collected in an MenACWY-7B low-dose, Men ACWY-7B high-dose open label manner.

In Part 2, data was collected in an observer-blind manner, only for the MenACWY-7B low-dose, Men ACWY-7B high-dose and MenABCWY arms. To do so, study interventions was prepared and administered by qualified study personnel who did not participate in data collection, evaluation, review or the entry of any study endpoint (i.e. reactogenicity, safety, efficacy).

The central laboratory in charge of sample testing was blinded to the study intervention assignment as well as to the subject number. There was no link between the study intervention groups and the identity of the participant. In addition, for each sample, a different randomly selected participant code was used at each timepoint.

This participant coding prevented the testing laboratory personnel from linking the consecutive timepoints to a specific participant.

## Emergency unblinding

Unblinding a participant's individual study intervention number should occur ONLY in case of a medical emergency when this information is essential for the clinical management or welfare of the participant.

## Unblinding prior to regulatory reporting of SAEs

GSK policy required unblinding of any unexpected SAE which is attributable/suspected to be attributable to the study interventions, prior to regulatory reporting. Vaccines Clinical Safety and Pharmacovigilance (VCSP) was responsible for unblinding the study intervention assignment within the timeframes defined for expedited reporting of SAEs.

## Sample size

The sample size determination was done to provide 90% CIs for the GMTs ratio between ACWY-7B low dose/ACWY-7B high-dose and MenB+MenACWY-TT at 1 month post second dose, that have enough power to be greater than 0.5 for the endpoints related to serogroups A, C, W and Y and serogroup B indicator strains and greater than 1 for the endpoint related to serogroup B indicator strains for additional fHbp antigens.

## Recruitment

The study vaccination and recruitment were voluntarily put on hold globally on 23 May 2022 after a fatal event in Spain, followed by a second event in Poland. Other study procedures (safety follow-up) continued as planned. Investigations concluded that both deaths were unrelated to the investigational vaccines (MenACWY-7B and MenABCWY, respectively). The IDMC reviewed the safety data and recommended continuing the study. GSK assessed the benefit/risk as favourable and resumed the trial in most countries; however, the coordinating investigator in Finland judged the benefit/risk as unfavourable, so the study was not restarted there. Participants already enrolled in Finland remained in safety follow-up, which ended on 17 October 2023.

## Safety results

All safety results are descriptive. Analyses of safety data were performed on the ES.

## Safety summary

Solicited AEs (7-day post-vaccination period): During the 7-day post-vaccination period following each and any vaccination, the percentage of participants with any solicited AE was similar across the 4 groups (

Table 19).

During the 7-day post-vaccination period after any vaccination, the percentage of participants reporting solicited administration site events in the 2 ACWY-7B groups and the ABCWY group was comparable to the MenB vaccine in the control group and was higher than the MenACWY-TT vaccine in the control group.

During the 7-day post-vaccination period after any vaccination, the percentage of participants reporting solicited systemic events in the 2 ACWY-7B groups and the ABCWY group was comparable to the MenB+MenACWY-TT control group.

Most of the solicited (administration site or systemic) AEs were mild or moderate in intensity.

The percentage of participants reporting fever after any vaccination was similar across all groups; 1 event of severe fever was reported in 1 participant in the ACWY-7B low-dose group after the first vaccine dose.

The administered investigational vaccines including the 2 MenACWY-7B vaccine formulations (low-dose and high-dose) in ACWY-7B groups and MenABCWY vaccine in the ABCWY group were well tolerated in the study population. No significant difference in reactogenicity was observed between the 2 MenACWY-7B vaccine formulations (low-dose and high-dose). Overall, the reactogenicity profile in the 2 ACWY-7B groups and ABCWY group was comparable to that of MenB vaccine in the control group.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

The report submitted for study 217043 concerns primarily the safety, tolerability and immunogenicity of investigational vaccine MenACWY-7B vaccine. Bexsero, concomitantly administered with Nimenrix, was used as the comparator. No statistical hypothesis testing was done.

The safety and reactogenicity profile of Bexsero in this study was consistent with its known safety profile. No safety concerns were identified. None of the serious AEs (SAEs) reported during the study were assessed by the investigator as related to Bexsero. These included 1 case of staphylococcal sepsis with fatal outcome 5 days after receiving the first dose of the control vaccines in the control (Bexsero/Nimenrix) group, concomitantly with routine vaccines. The case was assessed by the investigator and GSK as not related to the study interventions.

With regard to immunogenicity, after 2 and 3 vaccine doses, the number and percentages of participants with human serum bactericidal assay (hSBA) titre ≥ lower limit of quantitation (LLOQ) were generally comparable between the control (Bexsero/Nimenrix) group and the 2 ACWY-7B (high dose and low dose) groups. The GMT values for all serogroups were also comparable between the control (Bexsero/Nimenrix) group and the 2 ACWY-7B (high dose and low dose) groups after 2 and 3 doses, except for NadA, for which higher titres were observed in the control (Bexsero/Nimenrix) group.

It is noted that the assay used for Bexsero was the qualified agar overlay assay rather than the manual tilt assay used for Bexsero licensure, and the strains assessed also differed from those used for licensure. These differences limit the comparability of the results with previously generated Bexsero data. However, the immunogenicity results generally reflect what would be expected from Bexsero.

The MAH has concluded that the results of Bexsero obtained in study 217043 are in line with the approved product information for Bexsero in the EU. Therefore, no changes to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for Bexsero are considered necessary. The Rapporteur agrees with this assessment.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

Bexsero, concomitantly administered with Nimenrix, was used as the comparator in this clinical trial. The safety and reactogenicity profile of Bexsero was consistent with its known safety profile. The immunogenicity results generally reflect what would be expected from Bexsero, noting the difference assay and strains used in this clinical trial compared to the ones used in Bexsero authorisation. No changes to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for Bexsero are considered necessary.
