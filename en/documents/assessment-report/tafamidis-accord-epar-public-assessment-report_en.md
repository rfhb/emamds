## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Introduction

This marketing authorisation application is a generic application according to article 10(1) of Directive 2001/83/EC for Tafamidis Accord 61 mg soft capsules.

The originator/reference product is Vyndaqel (Tafamidis) of Pfizer Europe MA EEIG, Belgium, authorized in the European Economic Area (EEA) on 16 November 2011 (Vyndaqel 20 mg Capsules, marketing authorisation number EU/1/11/717/001-002)). The data protection period is expired in the EU, and reference can be made to the non-clinical and clinical documentation of the originator. Vyndaqel 20mg is indicated for the treatment of transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with stage 1 symptomatic polyneuropathy to delay peripheral neurologic impairment.

Vyndaqel 61 mg Capsules was authorised in the European Economic Area on 17 February 2020 (CHMP opinion on 12/12/2019) as an extension of Vyndaqel 20 mg Capsules (marketing authorisation number EU/1/11/717/003-004). Vyndaqel 61 mg is indicated for the treatment of wild-type or hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM).

The applicant applied only for the indication \"Treatment of hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with cardiomyopathy (ATTRh-CM)\", which does not violate the existing orphan market exclusivity of the reference product Vyndaqel for senile systemic amyloidosis.

To support the marketing authorisation application, the applicant conducted one bioequivalence study in healthy adult human subjects under fasting condition, between the test product Tafamidis Capsules 61 mg and Vyndaqel (Tafamidis) Capsules 61 mg of Pfizer Service Company BV, Belgium (open-label, balanced, randomized, two treatment, two sequence, two period, two way cross-over, single dose bioequivalence study).

The use of tafamidis is well established. Human pharmacology, efficacy and safety of tafamidis are well known. The applicant has submitted a clinical overview describing the pharmacodynamics (PD) and pharmacokinetics (PK) of tafamidis, as well as efficacy and safety data, based on published literature.

## 2.2. Quality aspects

## 2.2.1. Introduction

The finished product is presented as soft capsules containing 61 mg of tafamidis as active substance.

Other ingredients are:

Capsule shell: gelatine (E 441), glycerol (E 422), sorbitol, liquid partially dehydrated (E 420), iron oxide red (E 172), and purified water.

Capsule contents: macrogol 400 (E 1521), polysorbate 20 (E 432), povidone, butylhydroxytoluene (E 321).

## · Study design

The BE Study was an open-label, balanced, randomized, two treatment, two sequence, two period, two way cross-over, single dose bioequivalence study of Tafamidis Capsules 61 mg of and Vyndaqel (Tafamidis) Capsules 61 mg of Pfizer Service Company BV, Belgium, in normal, healthy, adult, human subjects under fasting condition.

## Study Investigators and Study Sites

The study investigators and the clinical, bioanalytical, and statistical facilities are presented

## Study Dates

The duration of the subject participation was 33 days including washout period of 21 days between consecutive dosing. The study was conducted between 03.02.2024 and 27.05.2024.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Wash-out period

Subjects were randomized into two treatment sequence groups and the study periods were separated by 21 days between consecutive dose administrations.

## Method of Assigning Subjects to Treatment Groups

As per the study design, two sequenced (AB and BA) randomization schedule was generated by a biostatistician by PROC PLAN using Statistical Analysis Software SAS Version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., U.S.A.).

Subjects were allocated sequential numbers starting from 01 on the day of check-in for period I and were assigned randomization sequence as per their number stated in randomization schedule.

## Dosing Procedure:

Following an overnight fast of at least 10.00 hours, subjects were orally administered one capsule of either test or reference product with 240 ± 2 mL of drinking water at ambient temperature, in a sitting upright position as per randomization schedule.

Investigational product (IP) administration to the subjects during each study period was performed in three groups (stations) with a two-minute gap between consecutive dose administrations at each dosing station to maintain scheduled collection time for subsequent blood samples.

## Blood Sample Collection:

A total of 24 blood samples (5.0 mL each) were collected during each study period, at 0 (within 2 hours prior to dosing), 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 16, 24, 36, 48, 72, 120, 168 and 240 hours post-dose in pre-labeled vacutainers containing K3EDTA as an anticoagulant. Prior to each sample collection, 0.5 mL of normal saline mixed blood was discarded and cannula patency was maintained by flushing with 0.5 mL of normal saline after each sample collection. In case of any difficulty in blood sample draw, or cannula blockage, samples were drawn by either recannulation or direct venipuncture.

In cases, when meals, vital signs measurements and blood sample collection time coincided, sample collections were given the priority over vital signs measurements and then meals.

In-house samples were collected within +2 minutes of scheduled sampling time. Any delay in sample collection from the scheduled sampling time was reported as PK sampling deviations as per in-house SOP. Reason for delay was documented (in eCRF) for blood samples collected beyond +2 minutes. Blood sample(s) were drawn at 72, 120, 168 and 240 hours post-dose in each study period were on an ambulatory basis. Ambulatory blood samples were permitted to collect within +6 hours of the scheduled time. Any delay in sample collection from the scheduled sampling time was reported as PK sampling deviations as per in-house SOP. Reason for delay was documented (in eCRF) for blood samples collected beyond +2 hours.

Actual sampling time was taken into consideration for PK calculations.

Blood samples were centrifuged at 4000 RPM for 10 minutes (± 1 minute) at 5°C ± 3°C to separate the plasma. The plasma samples were segregated equally in duplicate (analytical and replicate) prelabeled polypropylene tubes and stored in the deep freezer maintained at -20°C (-15°C to -25°C) as soon as possible.

At the end of clinical phase, both analytical and replicate plasma samples aliquots were transported to the bioanalytical facility (A-242) and stored in deep freezer maintained at -20°C (-15°C to -25°C) until analysis.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

All the samples were kept upright throughout the storage at clinical and bioanalytical facilities and during shipment.

Plasma concentrations of tafamidis were measured by a validated LC-MS/MS method in accordance with EMA guidance and in-house SOPs of the bioanalytical laboratory.

As per the protocol, samples of 44 subjects were analyzed for determination of plasma concentrations and were considered for PK and statistical analysis.

## Housing:

Subjects were housed at least 10 hours prior to dosing and confined until 48 hours post-dose during each study period.

The subjects were checked out after 48 hours post-dose during each study period and they reported to the clinical facility for 72, 120, 168 and 240 hours ambulatory blood sample collection.

Drinking water was allowed during the IP administration (240 ± 2 mL) and as desired except for one hour before and after dosing.

## Study sample analysis

Plasma samples were analysed for tafamidis (bioanalysis) using a validated method in accordance with EMA guidelines and in-house SOPs of bioanalytical laboratory. All concentration values Below the Limit of Quantification (BLQ) were reported in the bioanalytical report and considered as zero for all PK and statistical evaluations. <div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

During subject sample analysis, slope, y-intercept and correlation coefficient of calibration curve standards were evaluated and calculated by linear regression weighted (1/x2) analysis. The concentration of the unknown samples was calculated using peak area ratio method from following equation:

## y = mx + b

(where y = peak area ratio of Drug to IS, m = slope of the calibration curve standards, x = concentration of Drug in ng/mL, b = y-axis intercept of the calibration curve standards).

Quantitation of tafamidis in K3EDTA based human plasma samples was done using a sensitive and selective LC-MS/MS method with Tafamidis-13C6 as an internal standard. In this LC-MS/MS method, the Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM) mode was used for the analysis. This MRM mode of analysis is very specific to the molecule and it analyzes the compound on the basis of fragmentation pattern i.e., parent ion (Q1) to daughter ion (Q3).

## · Statistical methods

## Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed on PK parameters using Statistical Analysis Software - SAS (SAS Institute Inc., USA) Version 9.4. Descriptive statistics of all the pharmacokinetic parameters were computed and reported for tafamidis. The summary statistics (for relevant pharmacokinetic parameters) were computed and reported for both test and reference products.

The observed plasma concentrations were tabulated for each subject - product combination. Descriptive statistics included number of observations, arithmetic mean, standard deviation, range (minimum - maximum), median and coefficient of variance for untransformed data of all PK parameters, and geometric least square means and Intra CV(%) for log (natural) transformed data (primary PK parameters) for test and reference products. Concentration data and pharmacokinetic parameters from dropouts, withdrawn and terminated subjects were presented in the individual listings, but were not included in the summary statistics. The mean and individual subject's plasma concentrations, for tafamidis versus time profiles for each product was presented graphically as linear and semi-log plots, respectively.

The ratio of untransformed data of test and reference product for primary PK parameters were reported. PROC GLM was used for the estimation of LSM differences of test and reference products on the log-transformed primary PK parameters for Tafamidis along with the computation of corresponding standard errors. The 90% CIs was constructed for the LSM differences of log-transformed primary PK parameters. The antilog (or exponential) of the limits obtained from the log-transformed data gave the 90% CI for the ratio of geometric least square means of test and reference products.

The statistical analysis included: a) Two one-sided test; b) Power analysis; c) Ratio analysis d) Acceptance criteria for bioequivalence.

Consistent with Schuirmann's two one-sided tests procedure for bioequivalence, ANOVA at 5% level of significance was performed on Ln-transformed PK parameters Cmax, AUC0-t and AUC0-inf to determine whether the average values for these PK measures determined after administration of the test and reference products are comparable. This involved the calculation of 90% CI for the ratio of the averages (population geometric least square means) of the measures for test and reference products. The ANOVA model included sequence, subjects nested within sequence, period and treatment as fixed effects factors. The significance of the sequence effect was tested using the subjects' nested sequences as the error term. Each ANOVA testing also included calculation of LSM, adjusted differences between formulation means and the standard error associated with these differences. Geometric least square mean values were reported for log-transformed primary PK parameters of tafamidis on which CI is based. Ratios of means are expressed as a percentage of the LSM for the test and reference products.

## Criteria for Bioequivalence

The test product was considered bioequivalent to the reference product, if 90% confidence intervals for ratio (test/reference) of geometric least square means based on Ln-transformed primary PK parameters Cmax and AUC0-t fall within acceptable BE limits of 80.00% to 125.00% for Tafamidis.

## Treatment Effect Justification

In this study, the result of ANOVA for PK parameters Cmax indicates a statistically significant treatment effect between the Test and Reference product for the log transformed data at 5% level of significance. However, the decision of equivalence is based on Schuirmann test and the 90% confidence interval data, which in this case is within the equivalence boundaries of 80.00% to 125.00%. In this study the bioequivalence criteria is met for the Test and Reference product for the Cmax pharmacokinetic parameter, despite having statistically significant difference in treatment effect for ANOVA for the same.

## Period Effect Justification

A statistically significant period effect was detected for AUC0-t at 5% level of significance. In this randomized crossover study, equal numbers of subjects were administered the Test product (N=44) in a given period as were administered the Reference product (N=44). As a result, any true period effect influences the results of both products similarly. In addition, least-squares treatment means were adjusted for any imbalances in subject numbers. Also, since there was no detectable pre-dose concentration at any of the study periods and there was no sequence effect, there was no indication of unequal carryover effect. Accordingly, the observation of a statistically significant period effect does not alter the validity of the bioequivalence evaluation based upon the Test and Reference least-squares means.

The results of in vitro dissolution tests at three different buffers (normally pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8), obtained with the batches of test product used in the BE study and one batch of the reference product, have been provided. The relative standard deviation (RSD) in these tests was more than 20% for the first point of batch 50683521E and more than 10% for the second point of batch 58423161E. According to the BE guideline, the ƒ2 statistic was not suitable to determine dissolution similarity and, as recommended in EMA Q&amp;A 3.13 MWP Q&amp;A on In vitro Dissolution Profile Comparison for Bioequivalence Inference, the applicant determined dissolution similarity using Bootf2bca bootstrapping software. The bootstrapping results, together with the dissolution graphs and histograms were provided. Similarity between the batches at pH 6.8 has been shown since an averaged bootstrapped f2 result of 60 was obtained.

## 2.4.4. Discussion on clinical aspects

This marketing authorisation application is a generic application according to article 10(1) of Directive 2001/83/EC for Tafamidis Accord 61 mg soft capsules.

The reference product is Vyndaqel (Tafamidis) MAH Pfizer Europe MA EEIG, Belgium, authorised in the European Economic Area (EEA) on 16 November 2011 (VYNDAQEL 20 mg soft capsules, marketing authorisation number EU/1/11/717/001-002)). VYNDAQEL 61 mg soft capsules was authorised in the European Economic Area on 17 February 2020 as an extension application of VYNDAQEL 20 mg soft capsules (marketing authorisation number EU/1/11/717/003-004).

The applicant applied only for part of the indication of the reference medicinal product, namely \"Treatment of hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis in adult patients with cardiomyopathy (ATTRhCM)\".

The use of tafamidis is well established. Human pharmacology, efficacy and safety of tafamidis are well known. In line with the requirements for generic products, no new clinical data has been provided. The applicant has submitted a clinical overview describing the pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics of tafamidis, as well as efficacy and safety data based on published literature. It is considered appropriate.

To support the marketing authorisation application the applicant conducted one bioequivalence study in healthy adult human subjects under fasting condition.

A routine GCP inspection for the BE study was conducted in November 2025. The general conclusion of the inspection report was that the data reported and submitted to the Agency in the context of the present application are acceptable.

Tafamidis Accord 61 mg soft capsules is not considered a high-risk formulation, either in terms of formulation or manufacturing process (as per ICH M13A guideline). Thus, a BE study only in fasted state is considered adequate for the demonstration of bioequivalence with the reference product.

Overall the design of the BE study is acceptable according with the EU guidelines and in line with pharmacokinetic properties of tafamidis.

44 subjects were enrolled in the study, which is considered sufficient, and all of them completed the study. The population was chosen according to the BE guideline.

The bioequivalence study was conducted under standardised conditions. The sampling period was sufficient, the sampling time schedule and wash-out period were adequate taking into account the tmax and elimination half-life of tafamidis. The sampling schedule reached up to 240 hours. The residual AUC was evaluated and found to be lower than 20% for all subjects indicating that the duration of sampling was sufficient.

The bioanalytical method had satisfactory performance and was adequately validated. The pharmacokinetic and statistical method applied were appropriate for a single dose study. The 90% confidence intervals for ln-transformed pharmacokinetic variables Cmax and AUC0-t were within the conventional bioequivalence range of 80.00% to 125.00%. Analytical and statistical method were in line with the BE guideline and pre-specified in study's protocol.

There were no withdrawals/dropouts in the BE study and no protocol or SOP deviations reported.

The test (T) and reference (R) products were well tolerated by the study subjects. There was no serious adverse event in the study.

The test product Tafamidis Accord 61mg capsules when compared with the reference product Vyndaqel 61mg capsules meets the bioequivalence criteria in terms of rate and extent of absorption after administration of single doses as set in the protocol of the BE study under fasting conditions.

## Conditions or restrictions with regard to the safe and effective use of the medicinal product to be implemented at national level when the product is marketed

- In line with the originator regarding the follow-up pregnancy questionnaire.

The applicant must ensure systematic follow-up of all pregnancy cases using the follow-up questionnaire on \"reproductive and developmental toxicity and lactation\".

- In line with the originator, to keep the information material for HCP.

The HCP Guide, is proposed as a one-time distribution to all potential prescribers, including physicians knowledgeable in the management of patients with amyloidosis and/or cardiomyopathy, in each Member State where Tafamidis 61 mg is marketed for ATTRh-CM.
