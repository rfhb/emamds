This medicinal product is subject to additional monitoring. This will allow quick identification of new safety information. Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions. See section 4.8 for how to report adverse reactions.

## 1. NAME OF THE MEDICINAL PRODUCT

ICOTYDE 200 mg film-coated tablets

## 2. QUALITATIVE AND QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITION

Each film-coated tablet contains icotrokinra hydrochloride equivalent to 200 mg icotrokinra.

For the full list of excipients, see section 6.1.

## 3. PHARMACEUTICAL FORM

Film-coated tablet (tablet).

Yellowish orange to yellowish brown, oval film-coated tablet, approximately 19 mm in length and 9 mm in width debossed with '200' on one side and 'JNJ' on the other side.

## 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

ICOTYDE is indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in adults, and adolescents 12 years of age and older and weighing at least 40 kg, who are candidates for systemic therapy.

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration

Treatment should be initiated under the guidance and supervision of a physician experienced in the diagnosis and treatment of plaque psoriasis.

Adults and adolescents weighing at least 40 kg

The recommended dose of ICOTYDE is 200 mg orally once daily on an empty stomach (see Method of administration).

If a patient shows no evidence of therapeutic benefit after 24 weeks, treatment discontinuation should be considered. The patient's response to treatment should be evaluated on a regular basis. Some plaque psoriasis patients with initial partial response may subsequently improve with continued treatment beyond 24 weeks.

## Missed dose

If a dose is missed, patients should be instructed to take the dose on the same day as soon as possible and then resume normal dosing schedule the following day.

## Special populations

## Elderly

No dose adjustment is required in elderly patients aged 65 years and older (see section 5.2).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Renal impairment

No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with mild, moderate or severe renal impairment. ICOTYDE has not been studied in patients with end-stage renal disease and should only be used in these patients if the benefit outweighs the possible risk (see section 5.2).

## Hepatic impairment

No dose adjustment is recommended in patients with mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment (see section 5.2).

## Paediatric population

In children aged 12 years to less than 18 years and weighing at least 40 kg, the recommended dose is the same as in adults (see above).

The safety and efficacy of ICOTYDE in children younger than 12 years of age or children weighing less than 40 kg have not yet been established. No data are available.

## Method of administration

For oral use.

ICOTYDE film-coated tablets should be taken upon waking on an empty stomach with water at least 30 min before eating food (see section 5.2). Film-coated tablet must be swallowed whole.

## Alternative method of administration

For patients who have difficulty swallowing tablets whole, ICOTYDE 200 mg film-coated tablet can be dispersed in water.

Patients should place one ICOTYDE 200 mg film-coated tablet in a cup containing at least 120 mL of water. It may take a few minutes for the film-coated tablet to disperse. The film-coated tablet may not completely disperse. The mixture may look yellow, milky, or cloudy and small pieces may be seen in the water which are safe to swallow. The cup should be gently swirled before drinking. The full mixture should be swallowed.

Patients should fill the cup with at least an additional 120 mL of water to make sure the whole dose is taken and should swallow contents completely. ICOTYDE film-coated tablet should be taken as soon as possible after dispersion in water.

## 4.3 Contraindications

Hypersensitivity to the active substance or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1.

Clinically important active infections (e.g. active tuberculosis, see section 4.4).

## 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

## Immunisations

No data on the response to immunisations under icotrokinra treatment are available. As a precautionary measure, prior to treatment initiation, completion of all age-appropriate immunisations according to the current immunisation guidelines should be considered. Administration of live vaccines is not recommended during treatment with icotrokinra and within 3 days after discontinuation of icotrokinra.

For recommendations on administration of live vaccines in newborns potentially exposed during pregnancy, see section 4.6.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Infections

Medicines that interact with the immune system may increase the risk of infection. Treatment should not be initiated in patients with any clinically important active infection until the infection resolves or is adequately treated. Patients should be instructed to seek medical advice if signs or symptoms suggestive of a clinically relevant chronic or acute infection occur. If a patient develops such an infection and/or is not responding to standard therapy, monitor the patient closely and discontinue icotrokinra until the infection resolves.

## 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

## Live vaccines/therapeutic infectious agents

Live vaccines should not be administered to patients during treatment with icotrokinra and within 3 days after discontinuation of icotrokinra (see sections 4.4 and 4.6).

## Interaction with other medicinal products

No clinical drug-drug interaction studies with icotrokinra have been performed.

Based on in vitro studies, icotrokinra is not a CYP enzyme substrate, inhibitor, or inducer. Icotrokinra is not a substrate or inhibitor of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) or other major drug transporters. Therefore, no drug interactions are expected.

## 4.6 Fertility, pregnancy and lactation

## Pregnancy

There are no or limited amount of data (less than 300 pregnancy outcomes) from the use of icotrokinra in pregnant women. Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects with respect to reproductive toxicity (see section 5.3).

As a precautionary measure, it is preferable to avoid the use of ICOTYDE during pregnancy.

It is unknown if icotrokinra crosses the placenta. As a precautionary measure, administration of live vaccines to newborns potentially exposed to icotrokinra during pregnancy within the first 3 days after birth is not recommended. If there is a clear clinical benefit for the individual infant, administration of a live vaccine might be considered (see sections 4.4 and 4.5).

## Breast-feeding

It is unknown whether icotrokinra/metabolites are excreted in human milk. Available pharmacokinetic data in animals have shown the presence of icotrokinra in the plasma of nursing rat pups (see section 5.3). A risk to the newborn/infant cannot be excluded. A decision must be made whether to discontinue breast-feeding or discontinue/abstain from ICOTYDE therapy taking into account the benefit of breast-feeding for the child and the benefit of therapy for the woman.

## Fertility

The effect of icotrokinra on human fertility has not been evaluated. Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects with respect to fertility (see section 5.3).

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines

ICOTYDE has no or negligible influence on the ability to drive and use machines.

## Paediatric patients

A total of 72 paediatric patients 12 to less than 18 years of age have been treated with icotrokinra 200 mg orally once daily in two clinical studies in moderate to severe plaque psoriasis. Of these, 71 patients were exposed to icotrokinra for at least 6 months and 67 patients were exposed to icotrokinra for at least one year. The safety profile in adolescents was consistent with the safety profile observed in the overall study population.

## Reporting of suspected adverse reactions

Reporting suspected adverse reactions after authorisation of the medicinal product is important. It allows continued monitoring of the benefit/risk balance of the medicinal product. Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions via the national reporting system listed in Appendix V.

## 4.9 Overdose

In clinical studies, single oral icotrokinra doses up to 1 000 mg have been administered to healthy subjects without adverse reactions.

There is no specific treatment in the event of an overdose. In the event of an overdose, administer appropriate symptomatic treatment.

## 5. PHARMACOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Immunosuppressants, interleukin inhibitors, ATC code: L04AC28.

## Mechanism of action

Icotrokinra is a peptide that binds selectively to the IL-23 receptor (IL-23R) with high affinity and competitively antagonizes the binding of IL-23. IL-23 is a naturally occurring cytokine that is involved in inflammatory and immune responses, but when produced in excess contributes to chronic inflammation associated with plaque psoriasis. Icotrokinra inhibits the IL-23/IL-23R-dependent release of proinflammatory cytokines.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Pharmacodynamic effects

In evaluated patients with plaque psoriasis serum levels of IL-17A, IL-17F, IL-22, β-defensin-2 and IL-19 were reduced after treatment with icotrokinra. Gene expression levels of IL-17A, IL-17F, IL-22, DEFB4A and IL-19 in psoriatic skin, were normalised to non-lesional skin. Reductions in epidermal thickness and densities of T cells, including CD8+ tissue resident memory T cells, were also observed.

## Immunogenicity

Consistent with the potential immunogenic properties of medicinal products containing proteins or peptides, patients may develop antibodies following treatment with icotrokinra.

In four Phase III clinical studies through Week 52, 12.2% (291/2 387) of patients treated with icotrokinra developed antibodies to icotrokinra. Of these patients who developed antibodies to icotrokinra, none had antibodies that were classified as neutralizing antibodies.

In two Phase III clinical studies (ICONIC-LEAD and ICONIC-TOTAL) through Week 52, 32.4% (23/71) of adolescent patients treated with icotrokinra developed antibodies to icotrokinra.

There was no identified clinically significant effect of anti-drug antibodies on pharmacokinetics, safety or effectiveness of icotrokinra through Week 52.

## Clinical efficacy

## Plaque psoriasis

The efficacy of icotrokinra 200 mg orally once daily was evaluated in 2 492 patients (2 420 adult patients and 72 adolescent patients 12 years to less than 18 years of age and weighing at least 40 kg) with plaque psoriasis who were eligible for systemic therapy or phototherapy in four Phase III randomised, multi-centre, double-blind, placebo and/or active comparator-controlled studies ICONICLEAD, ICONIC-TOTAL, ICONIC-ADVANCE 1, and ICONIC-ADVANCE 2.

## Adult and adolescent patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis

ICONIC-LEAD enrolled 684 patients (618 adult and 66 adolescent patients 12 years of age and older) with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis. Patients were randomised to either icotrokinra 200 mg orally once daily or placebo for 16 weeks.

ICONIC-ADVANCE 1 and ICONIC-ADVANCE 2 evaluated 1 497 adult patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis. Patients were randomised to either icotrokinra 200 mg orally once daily, deucravacitinib 6 mg orally once daily or placebo.

In these three studies moderate to severe plaque psoriasis was defined as IGA score (Investigator's Global Assessment) ≥ 3, PASI score (Psoriasis Area and Severity Index) ≥ 12 and BSA (body surface area involvement) ≥ 10%.

Across the three studies, 67% of patients were male and 76% of the patients were White. The mean age was 45 (range: 12 to 86) years, the mean baseline weight was 88 (range: 39 to 211) kg for adults and was 73 (range: 41 to 130) kg for adolescents, 3% were 12 years to less than 18 years of age and 10% were ≥ 65 years. At baseline, the patients had a median affected BSA of 20.5%, a median PASI score of 18, and 14% of adults and 4.5% of adolescents had a history of psoriatic arthritis. The proportions of patients with a baseline IGA score of 3 [moderate] and 4 [severe] were 78% and 22%, respectively. At baseline, 72% had prior systemic treatment, 32% of patients had received prior phototherapy, and 28% had received prior biologic therapy (anti-TNF agents: 13%, IL-12/23 inhibitors: 8%, IL-23 inhibitors: 9% and IL-17 inhibitors: 9%).

The efficacy of icotrokinra was evaluated with respect to overall skin disease, specific body locations (scalp, genitalia, nails, and hand/foot), patient reported outcomes and impact on quality of life. The co-primary endpoints were the proportion of patients who achieved (1) an IGA cleared or minimal (IGA 0/1) with at least two grade improvement from baseline and (2) PASI 90 response at Week 16 versus placebo. Adults and adolescents with plaque psoriasis involving special areas (scalp, genitalia and hand/foot)

ICONIC-TOTAL enrolled adult and adolescent patients with plaque psoriasis who had failed to respond to at least one topical therapy for the treatment of plaque psoriasis. Additionally, patients needed to have at least moderate scalp, genital, or hand/foot psoriasis.

A total of 311 patients (305 adult and 6 adolescent patients) were randomised to either icotrokinra 200 mg orally once daily or placebo for 16 weeks; 64% were male, and 78% were White. The mean age was 45 (range: 12 to 87) years, the mean baseline weight was 86 (range: 40 to 246) kg, 1.9% were 12 years to less than 18 years of age, and 8% were ≥ 65 years. The proportion of patients with affected BSA 1% to less than 10% was 36% with a median affected BSA of 12%. The proportion of patients with a baseline IGA score of 2 [mild], 3 [moderate] and 4 [severe] were 5%, 73%, and 22%, respectively. The proportion of patients with a baseline scalp-specific Investigator's Global Assessment (ss-IGA) score of 3 or greater was 81%. The proportion of patients with Static Physician's Global Assessment of Genitalia (sPGA-G) score of 3 or greater was 45%. The proportion of patients with Physician's Global Assessment of Hands and Feet (hf-PGA) score of 3 or greater was 23%. Scalp, genital and hand/foot subpopulations are not mutually exclusive. At baseline, 73% had received prior systemic therapy, 39% of patients had received prior phototherapy and 33% had received prior biologic therapy (anti-TNF agents: 14%, IL-12/23 inhibitors: 9%, IL-23 inhibitors: 12% and IL-17 inhibitors: 9%).

The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients who achieved an IGA score of 0 or 1 and a ≥ 2-grade improvement from baseline at Week 16. Main clinical outcomes are presented in Table 4.

## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

## Absorption

Icotrokinra is a compound with low solubility and low permeability.

The median (range) Tmax of icotrokinra after oral administration is 2.00 (0.25-8.00) hours in healthy subjects. The mean Cmax and mean AUC∞ of icotrokinra were 3.62 ng/mL and 44.8 ng.h/mL, respectively.

After repeat once daily dosing of icotrokinra, steady-state icotrokinra is expected within 3 days with minimal accumulation, consistent with its terminal half-life.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Inter-subject variability of Cmax and AUC∞ was estimated to be 26.7% and 19.6%, respectively. Intra-subject variability of Cmax and AUC∞ was estimated to be 35.6% and 20.7%, respectively.

Based on nonclinical data, the absolute bioavailability of icotrokinra was &lt; 1%.

## Food effect

In healthy subjects, co-administration of a single icotrokinra 200 mg dose orally with a high-calorie, high-fat meal decreased icotrokinra AUC∞ and Cmax by 43% and 59%, respectively, relative to fasted state. Icotrokinra should be taken upon waking at least 30 minutes before eating food.

Oral administration of a single icotrokinra 200 mg tablet with caffeine resulted in no difference in exposure.

Co-administration of a single icotrokinra 200 mg tablet orally with whole milk decreased Cmax by 33% and AUC∞ by 24% compared with co-administration with water.

## Distribution

Icotrokinra is 52% bound to plasma protein. Blood to plasma partition ratio was 0.53. The volume of distribution (V/F) at steady state is 92 800 L.

## Biotransformation

Icotrokinra is a peptide, and intact active substance is the main medicine-related species in plasma. Icotrokinra is metabolised by peptide catabolism into smaller peptides and is not metabolised by CYP enzymes.

## Elimination

The median terminal half-life (t1/2) of icotrokinra is 12 hours. Clearance following oral dosing (Cl/F) is 6 550 L/h.

The majority of the active substance is not absorbed with 37% to 81% of the intact medicine recovered unchanged in faeces up to 24 hours after oral dosing of 10 to 1 000 mg. Less than 0.001% of administered dose was recovered as intact medicine in urine.

Renal clearance is the main route of elimination for the absorbed medicine.

## Linearity

Following oral administration, icotrokinra Cmax and AUC increased in an almost dose-proportional manner over a dose range of 10 to 1 000 mg (approximately 0.05 to 5 times the recommended dose) in healthy subjects.

## Immunogenicity

Among icotrokinra-treated patients who developed anti-drug antibodies, population pharmacokinetic analysis using pooled data from the four Phase III clinical studies through Week 52 showed that Cmax and AUC increased by 1.2- and 1.3-fold respectively, compared with patients who did not develop anti-drug antibodies.

There was no identified clinically significant effect of anti-drug antibodies on pharmacokinetics, safety or effectiveness of icotrokinra through Week 52.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Special populations

Based on population pharmacokinetic analysis, age (12-87 years), sex, race, ethnicity, do not have a clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics of icotrokinra.

## Body weight

Clearance and volume of distribution of icotrokinra after oral dosing increased with increasing body weight (39-211 kg). These differences are not clinically meaningful, and no dose adjustment is warranted.

## Paediatric population (12 years to less than 18 years of age)

No meaningful difference in the systemic exposure of icotrokinra was observed in paediatric patients with plaque psoriasis 12 years of age and older weighing at least 40 kg compared to adults.

## Elderly

In the population pharmacokinetic analysis, age did not have a significant impact on the pharmacokinetics of icotrokinra.

## Renal impairment

Icotrokinra AUC∞ increased 2.47-fold and 2.78-fold in patients with moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 30 to &lt; 60 mL/min) and severe renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 15 to &lt; 30 mL/min), respectively, compared to patients with normal renal function (eGFR ≥ 90 mL/min). Mild renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 60 to &lt; 90 mL/min) had no effect on icotrokinra exposure based on population pharmacokinetic analysis. Patients with end-stage renal disease (eGFR &lt; 15 mL/min) or patients on dialysis were not studied (see section 4.2).

Icotrokinra Cmax increased 1.79-fold and 1.27-fold in patients with moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 30 to &lt; 60 mL/min) and severe renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 15 to &lt; 30 mL/min), respectively, compared to patients with normal renal function (eGFR ≥ 90 mL/min).

## Hepatic impairment

No formal pharmacokinetic studies with icotrokinra were conducted in patients with hepatic impairment. Changes in hepatic function are unlikely to have any effect on the elimination of icotrokinra as icotrokinra is not metabolised through hepatic pathways.

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data

Nonclinical data reveal no special hazard for humans based on conventional studies of safety pharmacology, repeated dose toxicity, genotoxicity, carcinogenicity, and toxicity to reproduction and development.

Icotrokinra was neither embryo-lethal nor teratogenic in rats or rabbits at maternal exposures at least 95 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD), respectively based on unbound AUC. In rabbits, maternal body weight loss, low food consumption, and pregnancy losses were observed at maternal exposures 95 times the MRHD based on unbound AUC.

Icotrokinra had no effects on prenatal and post-natal development in rats at maternal exposures 127 times the MRHD based on total AUC. Although not directly measured in rat milk, icotrokinra was detected in the plasma of nursing rat pups. On post-natal day 12, the mean pup-to-maternal plasma ratio for icotrokinra was 0.042 to 0.29.

## 1. What ICOTYDE is and what it is used for

## What ICOTYDE is

ICOTYDE contains the active substance icotrokinra.

This medicine helps to control the inflammation associated with psoriasis by blocking a protein called IL-23R, which is increased in people with psoriasis.

ICOTYDE is used to treat moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis (a disease causing red, scaly patches on the skin). It is used in adults, and adolescents aged 12 years and older with a body weight of at least 40 kg.

ICOTYDE can improve the condition of the skin and appearance of nails. ICOTYDE can reduce signs such as scaling, redness and skin thickness and improve symptoms such as itching, pain, stinging, burning and skin tightness. ICOTYDE can also improve the quality of life in patients with psoriasis.

## 2. What you need to know before you take ICOTYDE

## Do not take ICOTYDE

- if you are allergic to icotrokinra or any of the other ingredients of this medicine (listed in section 6).
- if you have an active infection, including active tuberculosis, which your doctor thinks is important.

## Warnings and precautions

Talk to your doctor or pharmacist before and during the use of ICOTYDE:

- if you have recently received or are scheduled to receive an immunisation (vaccine):

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- you should avoid receiving live vaccines during treatment with ICOTYDE and within 3 days after stopping treatment with ICOTYDE.
- if you currently have an infection, a long-term infection, or if you have an infection that keeps coming back.

## Children and adolescents

ICOTYDE is not recommended for children and adolescents under 12 years of age or with a body weight below 40 kg because it has not been studied in this group.

## Other medicines and ICOTYDE

Tell your doctor or pharmacist if you are taking, have recently taken or might take any other medicines.

## Pregnancy, breast-feeding and fertility

If you are pregnant, think you may be pregnant or are planning to have a baby, ask your doctor for advice before using this medicine. This is because it is not known how this medicine will affect the unborn baby.

If you took ICOTYDE when you were pregnant:

- tell your baby's doctor and other health care professionals before your baby has any vaccines.
- live vaccines are not recommended for your baby in the first 3 days after birth unless your baby's doctor recommends otherwise.

It is unknown whether ICOTYDE passes into human breast milk. Talk to your doctor if you are breast-feeding or are planning to breast-feed. You and your doctor should decide if you will breast-feed or use ICOTYDE.

## Driving and using machines

ICOTYDE is unlikely to influence your ability to drive and use machines.

## 3. How to take ICOTYDE

Always take this medicine exactly as your doctor or pharmacist has told you. Check with your doctor or pharmacist if you are not sure.

## ICOTYDE is available as a tablet which is taken by mouth.

The recommended dose is 200 mg every day.

- Take on an empty stomach after you wake up.
- Swallow the tablet whole with water.
- After taking the ICOTYDE tablet, wait at least 30 minutes before eating food.

## If you have trouble swallowing whole tablets

- Place one tablet in a cup with at least 120 mL of water.
- Wait a few minutes until the tablet breaks up and spreads out. The mixture may look yellow, milky, or cloudy. Small pieces may be seen in the water - these are safe to swallow.
- Swirl the mixture in the cup before drinking and swallow the full mixture straight away.
- Fill the cup with at least another 120 mL of water and drink straight away to make sure that you take the full dose.

## If you take more ICOTYDE than you should

If you take more ICOTYDE than you should, talk to your doctor straight away.

## If you forget to take ICOTYDE

If you forget to take a dose, take the missed dose on the same day as soon as possible. Take your next dose at the usual time on the next day.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Do not take a double dose to make up for a forgotten dose.

## If you stop taking ICOTYDE

Do not stop using this medicine without talking to your doctor. If you stop treatment, symptoms of psoriasis may come back.

If you have any further questions on the use of this medicine, ask your doctor or pharmacist.

## 4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, this medicine can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them.

Tell your doctor, pharmacist or nurse if you get any of the following side effects

Common (may affect up to 1 in 10 people)

- fungal infection

Uncommon (may affect up to 1 in 100 people)

- inflamed lower part of the gut (diverticulitis)

## Reporting of side effects

If you get any side effects, talk to your doctor or pharmacist. This includes any possible side effects not listed in this leaflet. You can also report side effects directly via the national reporting system listed in Appendix V. By reporting side effects you can help provide more information on the safety of this medicine.

## 5. How to store ICOTYDE

Keep this medicine out of the sight and reach of children.

Do not use this medicine after the expiry date which is stated on the blister, bottle and carton after 'EXP'. The expiry date refers to the last day of that month.

This medicine does not require any special temperature storage conditions.

Blister: Store in the original package in order to protect from moisture.

Bottle: Store in the original package and keep the bottle tightly closed in order to protect from moisture.

Do not use this medicine if you notice the tablets are damaged or there are signs of tampering with the medicine packaging.

Do not throw away any medicines via wastewater or household waste. Ask your pharmacist how to throw away medicines you no longer use. These measures will help protect the environment.
