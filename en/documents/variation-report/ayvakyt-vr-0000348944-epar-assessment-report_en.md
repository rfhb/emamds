## 2. Overall conclusion and impact on the benefit/risk balance

Avapritinib was granted a Marketing Authorisation (MA) in the EU in September 2020 as monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GIST) harbouring the platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRA) D842V mutation (Procedure number EMEA/H/C/005208/0000)

In March 2022, avapritinib was approved in the EU for the treatment of advanced systemic mastocytosis (AdvSM), including patients with aggressive systemic mastocytosis (ASM), systemic mastocytosis with associated haematological neoplasm (SM-AHN) and mast cell leukaemia (MCL), after at least one systemic therapy, based on the results of the BLU-285-2101 and BLU-285-2202 studies (EMEA/H/C/005208/X/0004/G).

In November 2023, the CHMP adopted a positive opinion for the extension of indication for the treatment of adults with indolent systemic mastocytosis (ISM) based on results from the pivotal part of study BLU285-2203 (PIONEER) [EMEA/H/C/005208/II/0023: Ayvakyt; INN-avapritinib].

In accordance with Article 46 of the regulation (EC) No. 1901/2006, on 17 June 2026, the MAH submitted the final clinical study report dated 28 April 2026 for the paediatric Study BLU-285-3101 (ROVER) which is part of an agreed PIP (EMEA-002358-PIP02-18-M04) for avapritinib, through the current variation.

Study BLU-285-3101 (ROVER) is a phase 1/2 single-arm study to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity of avapritinib in paediatric patients with solid tumours dependent on KIT or PDGFRA signalling.

The pharmacokinetic evaluation should be considered exploratory due to the limited sample size collected, without the possibility of establishing subsequent inferences in younger paediatric sub-groups of patients. The 80% BSA-adjusted dose showed better exposure matching with adults receiving 300 mg, whereas 100% BSA-adjusted doses resulted in slightly higher exposures. However, it is not possible to determine whether similar exposure in the paediatric population compared to adults would ensure a benefit-risk balance similar to that observed in adults.

The primary endpoint of the study (ORR) did not meet as none of the patients had a best response of confirmed CR o PR per Investigator assessment according to RANO criteria. As a result, participant enrolment was stopped. The efficacy results submitted were however very scarce. Therefore, avapritinib

## 4. Introduction

Avapritinib is a type I tyrosine kinase inhibitor designed to potently and selectively inhibit oncogenic KIT and PDGFRA mutants by targeting the kinase active conformation.

Avapritinib is approved for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) harbouring the platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRA) D842V mutation and for the treatment of systemic mastocytosis (advanced systemic mastocytosis and indolent systemic mastocytosis).

Paediatric GIST represents a rare and biologically distinct disease entity compared with adult GIST. Because most paediatric GIST do not harbour activating KIT or PDGFRA mutations, avapritinib is not expected to demonstrate clinical activity in these tumours. Consistent with the mechanism of action of avapritinib, the paediatric study focused on advanced solid tumours harbouring KIT or PDGFRa mutations. Accordingly, BLU-285-3101 was initiated to evaluate avapritinib in paediatric patients with advanced solid tumours, particularly CNS tumours, in which these mutations are more commonly observed.

This type II variation is to submit the final Clinical Study Report (CSR) of BLU-285-3101 (ROVER) a phase 1/2 single-arm study to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity of avapritinib in paediatric patients with solid tumours dependent on KIT or PDGFRA signalling (Table 1).

## 5. Clinical Pharmacology aspects

Study BLU-285-3101 was a Phase 1/2, multicenter, open-label, single-arm study to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and anti-tumour activity of avapritinib in paediatric patients with solid tumours dependent on KIT or PDGFRA signalling.

Objectives: This study had as a secondary objective the characterization of the PK profile and determine the PK parameters for avapritinib and its metabolites (BLU111207 and BLU111208) using noncompartmental (NCA) methods in paediatric patients.

Study design: The study consisted of two parts: Part 1 (dose determination, safety, and PK) and Part 2 (efficacy, safety, and PK at the recommended Part 2 dose).

- Part 1: The starting dose was 80% of the adult dose (300 mg), normalized for BSA, and was sequentially administered to adolescents (12-&lt;18 years) and children (6-&lt;12 years) with CNS tumours in cohorts of 3 patients each. If tolerated, dose escalation to 100% of the adult dose (normalized for BSA, maximum 300 mg) was permitted. The recommended part 2 dose (RP2D) was determined independently for each age group.
- Part 2: The starting dose for patients aged 2 to &lt;6 years was determined using a PBPK model incorporating PK data from older patients (6-&lt;18 years) from Part 1 after the RP2D was established.

Demographics: The study enrolled paediatric patients aged 2 to &lt;18 years with advanced relapsed/refractory solid tumours (including CNS tumours and DMG-H3K27a) driven by PDGFRA and/or KIT mutations/amplifications, with no available curative treatment options.

The final PK population comprised 29 patients: 16 children (4-11 years, body weight 19.0-48.9 kg, BSA 0.79-1.42 m²) and 13 adolescent (12-17 years, body weight 37.1-79.0 kg, BSA 1.20-1.92 m²) stratified by BSA-adjusted dose (80% or 100% of adult dose). Two paediatric patients were excluded from PK analysis due to insufficient sampling, and two patients experienced dose modifications during the study.

Study Treatment: Avapritinib was administered orally once daily in the morning in the fasted state (2 hours before to 1 hour after a meal) in continuous 28-day cycles. Dosing was based on age and BSA, with doses rounded to the nearest 25 mg for tablets or 10 mg for minitablets.

PK sampling:

Part 1: All patients underwent intensive PK sampling at:

- Cycle 1 Days 1 and 15: Pre-dose (±2 h), 1 h (±5 min), 2 h (±5 min), 4 h (±10 min), 8 h (±10 min) post-dose
- Cycle 1 Days 2 and 16: Pre-dose (±2 h) approximately 24 h post-dose
- Cycles 2-4 Day 1: Pre-dose (±2 h)

Part 2: 7 patients (including ≥2 patients aged 2-&lt;6 years) were enrolled in Part 2 and underwent intensive PK sampling at:

- Cycle 1 Days 1 and Cycle 2 Day 1: Pre-dose (±2 h), 1 h (±5 min), 2 h (±5 min), 4 h (±10 min), 8 h (±10 min) post-dose
- Cycle 1 Day 2 and Cycle 2 Day 2: Pre-dose (±2 h) approximately 24 h post-dose
- Cycle 1 Day 15: Pre-dose (±2 h)
- Cycles 3-7 Day 1: Pre-dose (±2 h)

Newly enrolled Part 2 patients followed sparse PK sampling (pre-dose only) at Cycle 1 Day 15 and Cycles 2-7 Day 1 after sufficient intensive data was collected.

## 5.1. Methods

Avapritinib and its metabolites (BLU111207 and BLU111208) were quantified in study BLU-2085-2101 to characterise their PK profile and determine their PK parameters.

Bioanalysis: No validation report of the analytical method employed neither bioanalytical report was submitted.

Pharmacokinetic Methods: PK parameters were calculated by NCA methods from the concentration-time data using Phoenix® WinNonlin® (Version 8.6, Certara USA, Inc., Princeton, NJ).

Additional Analysis: An additional analysis was conducted to account for dose capping effects, where patients were reclassified based on their actual received doses due to the 300 mg maximum dose cap, to assess the impact of dose capping on pharmacokinetic parameters.

## 5.3. Discussion

Study BLU-2085-3101 was a Phase 1/2, single-arm study to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity of avapritinib in paediatric patients with solid tumours dependent on KIT or PDGFRA signalling. This study consisted of two parts, in which PK of avapritinib and its metabolites BLU111207 and BLU111208 was characterised. Considering the relevance of pharmacokinetics to this procedure, although the MAH submitted PK parameters for avapritinib and its metabolites, only the data for avapritinib was assessed and included in this assessment.

The NCA is based on a bioanalytical method whose bioanalytical report for the study sample analysis has not been submitted. However, taking into account that no regulatory decision is taken based on these plasma concentrations, compliance of the bioanalysis with ICH M10 is not considered essential.

Dosing regimen in Part 1 is based on a dose escalation to reach the 100% of the adult dose (normalised for BSA, maximum 300 mg). In Part 2, the starting dose of patients aged 2 to &lt;6 years was determined using PBPK modelling based on PK data obtained from patients aged 6 to &lt;18 years in Part 1, but that PBPK model development, verification and application has not been submitted. Therefore, it is not addressed.

Given the small sample size in Part 2, the pharmacokinetic conclusions are primarily based on Part 1 findings. Overall, due to the limited sample size collected, the pharmacokinetic evaluation should be considered exploratory, without the possibility of establishing any subsequent inferences in younger paediatric sub-groups of patients.

NCA shows that steady-state exposures (Cmax,ss and AUC0-τ) of avapritinib 80% BSA-adjusted dose in children (6-&lt;12 years) and adolescent patients showed better exposure-matching agreement to those observed with 300 mg dosing in adults with GIST. The 100% BSA-adjusted doses across age groups resulted in slightly higher exposures compared to 300 mg dosing in adults with GIST. However, it is not possible to determine whether similar exposure in the paediatric population compared to adults would ensure a benefit-risk balance similar to that observed in adults.

In Part 1 on Cycle 1 Day 15, children and adolescent exposures appeared to be comparable to adults when considering the observed untransformed data (0.8-1.2-fold), following either 80% or 100% BSAadjusted doses. However, when these exposures were normalized by dose or BSA, apparent discrepancies emerged with normalized values exceeding 1.5-fold of adult values. This pattern is expected and reflects fundamental differences in body size between age groups. For example, children patients receiving 80%

## 6. Clinical Efficacy aspects

Study BLU-285-3101 is a multicentre, open-label, single arm, phase 1/2 study to evaluate safety, pharmacokinetics and anti-tumour activity of avapritinib in paediatric patients from 2 to less than 18 years of age with a relapsed/refractory (R/R) solid tumour, including CNS tumours, that harboured a mutation (including nonsynonymous point mutations, insertions, and deletions) in KIT or PDGFR-alpha, KIT or PDGFR-alpha amplifications, or a H3K27M mutant glioma and no available alternative treatment options.

The study consisted of 2 parts: dose determination, safety, and PK (Part 1) and initial efficacy, safety, and PK at the RP2D (Part 2). Efficacy was assessed in all participants across Part 1 and Part 2. Both parts included a Screening period (up to 28 days), treatment period (≥ 1 28-day cycle), EOT visit (within 14 days after permanently discontinuing treatment with avapritinib), Safety follow-up (at least 30 days after last dose of study avapritinib), and Survival follow-up (every 3 months until death, withdrawal of consent, or lost to follow-up).

The dose (normalized for BSA) used in Part 2 was determined based on observed safety, PK, and preliminary efficacy in Part 1. Different RP2Ds for different age groups were permitted.

## Treatments

Avapritinib was administered orally as 25 mg and 100 mg film-coated tablets and 50 mg sprinkle capsules (containing five 10 mg minitablets), QD, and in continuous 28-day cycles.

The specific dose (in m g/m2) administered to each participant was based on age and BSA. The calculated doses were rounded to the nearest 25 mg (for participants taking tablets) or 10 mg (for participants taking minitablets) and the highest allowable dose was 300 mg QD. The BSA was recalculated every cycle and the dose adjusted, as needed.

Avapritinib doses were administered with a glass of water in a fasted state, with no food intake from 2 hours before until 1 hour after avapritinib administration. For paediatric participants unable to fast for this time period, care was taken to provide the least amount of food required for the participants during the time surrounding the avapritinib dose. Each dose was administered at approximately the same time each day, ideally in the morning. If the participant forgot to take their daily dose, they took the avapritinib dose at least 8 hours before the next dose. If the dose had not been taken 8 hours before the next dose, that dose was omitted, and the participant resumed treatment with the next scheduled dose.

For participants able to swallow tablets, participants were instructed to swallow tablets whole and to not chew the tablets. The sprinkle capsules were available only in Part 2. A 50 mg sprinkle capsule contained five 10 mg minitablets. The sprinkle capsules were opened and the appropriate number of 10 mg minitablets based on the assigned dose were administered with 15 mL (1 tablespoon) of yogurt, chocolate pudding, or banana. The minitablets were also allowed to be swallowed directly after opening the sprinkle capsules (the capsule should not have been swallowed).

In Part 1, the starting dose was 80% of the adult dose (300 mg), normalized for BSA, and was first administered in 3 adolescent participants (12 to &lt; 18 years old) with CNS tumours, followed by 3 children (6 to &lt; 12 years old) with CNS tumours. If 100% of the adult BSA-normalized dose was not considered the RP2D, and the 80% starting dose was tolerated, the 80% of the adult BSA-normalized dose was to be selected as the RP2D or the Sponsor evaluated alternative dosing schedules (e.g., same total daily dose but different frequency), in consultation with the DSMC. If a lower dose and/or alternative schedule was evaluated, 3 participants were enrolled. If ≤1 DLT was observed, 3 additional participants were treated at the same lower dose and/or alternative schedule. The DLT assessment window started post dose on C1D1 and ended prior to the C2D1 dose. Participants had to receive 75% of their BSA-normalized doses of avapritinib in C1 or experience a DLT to be considered evaluable. Participants who had not experienced a DLT continued on avapritinib until the RP2D had been confirmed, after which participants either continued to be treated with the RP2D (if that was the dose the participant started on) or titrated to the RP2D (if the participant was on a dose not confirmed to be the RP2D). ## Part 2

The dose (normalized for BSA) used in Part 2 was determined based on observed safety, PK and preliminary efficacy in Part 1. Different age groups could have different RP2Ds.

The RP2D was determined by the DSMC in collaboration with the Sponsor. Decisions to select the RP2D occurred after all participants in Part 1 had completed C1 and was based on evaluation of all relevant, available data, and not solely on DLT information. If neither the 100% nor the 80% adult-normalized dose was considered tolerable and selected as the RP2D, the next dose to be tested was to be based on the PBPK model, which was to have been updated using the data generated in participants enrolled in this study. An additional group of 6 evaluable participants would be enrolled in an age group (adolescents and/or children) and treated at the new dose or alternative schedule. If a lower dose and/or alternative schedule was evaluated, 3 participants would be enrolled. If ≤ 1 DLT had been observed, 3 additional participants would be treated at the same lower dose and/or alternative schedule.

The starting doses for the paediatric participants 2 to &lt; 6 years of age were informed by PBPK modelling incorporating the observed PK obtained from participants 6 to &lt; 18 years old in Part 1 after the RP2D had been determined for the older participants. Based on these simulations, 191 mg/m 2 and 152 mg/m 2 doses in participants 2 to &lt; 6 years of age were predicted to yield exposures equivalent to those of 100% (300 mg QD) and 80% (240 mg QD) of the adult dose in patients with GIST, respectively.

## Statistical Methods

All analyses are presented by treatment arms as 'Children 80%', 'Children 100%', 'Adolescent 80%', 'Adolescent 100%', and 'Total'.

Hypothesis and Hypothesis Testing Procedure

Null hypothesis (H0): ORR ≤ 10% vs. Alternative hypothesis (Ha): ORR ≥ 30%

Across Part 1 and Part 2, a sample size of at least 31 patients will be needed. This will allow testing the hypothesis with 85% power assuming a 2-sided type I error of 0.10, using the exact binomial testing method. ORR will be analysed in the ITT population, response rate and the associated exact 95% binomial confidence interval will be presented.

## Determination of Sample Size

The total number of patients to be enrolled in Part 1 of the trial was dependent on the observed safety profile in patients enrolled in Part 1, which determined whether 1 or more than 1 cohort would be required to confirm the Part 2 recommended dose for paediatric patients with solid tumours and CNS tumours. At least 12 evaluable patients were to be enrolled in Part 1.

Overall, across Part 1 and Part 2, a sample size of at least 31 patients was needed to allow testing of the null hypothesis of ORR ≤ 10% versus the alternative hypothesis of ORR ≥ 30% with 85% power assuming a 2-sided type I error of 0.10, using the exact binomial testing method.

An ORR of ≥ 25.8% in 31 patients (at least 8 confirmed responders) will result in a binomial 95% CI with a lower bound &gt; 10%, which is clinically meaningful, and exceeds the ORR expected with available therapies in paediatric patients with relapsed and refractory disease.

## Objective Response Rate by Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology

ORR by RANO criteria was the primary endpoint in Part 2 and a secondary endpoint for Part 1.

ORR was defined as the proportion of participants in the ITT population with confirmed CR or PR as best response per RANO. Confirmation of an initial CR or PR response was required to be at least 4 weeks apart. A PR or CR without confirmation was considered as the best response of SD. ORR was presented with the associated exact 95% binomial CIs. Hypothesis testing against a null ORR of 10% was performed using exact binomial testing method. The p-value was reported.

In addition, the best response rate by RANO (RANO-HGG or RANO-LGG) was summarized including CR, PR, MR, SD, PD, and NE, for the ITT population, and separately for the ITT population from Part 1 and Part 2 of the study. Best response was defined as the best overall response observed during the study for all participants with CNS tumours according to the RANO criteria.

## Duration of Response by RANO

DOR was defined as time from first documentation of CR or PR by RANO to PD or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first. Participants who were still in response at time of database lock were censored at their last valid assessment. DOR was analysed using KM method. Median DOR and the estimated DOR rate at 6 and 12 months (Day 183 and Day 365) were presented. If there were &lt; 5 CR or PR, DOR was presented in listing only.

## Progression-free Survival by RANO

PFS was defined as time from first dose to PD or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first. Participants who were still in response at time of database lock were censored at their last valid assessment. PFS was analysed using KM method. Median PFS and the estimated PFS rate at 6 and 12 months (Day 183 and Day 365) were presented.

## Disease Control Rate at 24 Weeks by RANO

DCR at 24 weeks was defined as the proportion of participants with RANO CR, PR, MR, or SD at the end of Week 24 (Day 169).

## Time to Treatment Response by RANO

TTR was defined as time from first dose to first documentation of CR or PR by RANO with RANO confirmed best response of CR or PR. TTR was summarized descriptively. If there were &lt; 5 CR or PR, TTR was presented in listing only.

## Objective Response Rate by RAPNO

For participants with DMG-H3K27a, anti-tumour activity was assessed per the DIPG RAPNO criteria. ORR was defined as the proportion of participants with confirmed CR or PR as best response per DIPG RAPNO.

For participants with HGG/DMG-H3K27a, anti-tumour activity was assessed by the HGG RAPNO criteria. ORR was defined as the proportion of participants with confirmed CR or PR as best response per HGG RAPNO.

## Objective Response Rate (ORR) by Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology

The ORR per Investigator assessment per RANO criteria (RANO HGG or RANO LGG) was 0.0% (95% CI: 0.0%, 11.9%). No participant had a best response of confirmed CR or PR per Investigator assessment (Table 11). Best response of minor response was reported for 1 participant (3.4%), SD was reported for 7 participants (24.1%), and PD was reported for 18 participants (62.1%); disease was not evaluable for 3 participants (10.3%).

The protocol required that there be at least 8 confirmed responses out of 31 participants (across Part 1 and Part 2) to meet the primary endpoint of the study. In Part 1, there were no confirmed responses in 19/22 participants who had postbaseline tumour assessments and in Part 2, none of the 5 participants who completed the study had a response. Therefore, the primary endpoint of the study was not met and the decision was made to stop participant enrolment.

TEAEs were defined as AEs that occurred during or after administration of the first dose of avapritinib through 30 days after the last dose. The Applicant presented in-text treatment-emergent AEs (referred to as \"AEs\") for the safety population, overall and according to age and dose level groups (children 80%, children 100%, adolescent 80%, and adolescent 100%) for Parts 1 and 2 combined.

Seventeen patients (58.6%) experienced SAEs, with 5 patients (17.2%) experiencing SAEs related to avapritinib. Seven patients (24.1%) experienced AESIs, with 4 patients (13.8%) experiencing AESIs related to avapritinib.

A total of 12 patients (41.4%) reported a dose interruption because of AEs and 7 patients (24.1%) required a dose reduction of avapritinib because of AEs. Three patients (10.3%) reported an AE leading to treatment discontinuation. One patient (3.4%) experienced an AE that resulted in death, assessed not related to treatment with avapritinib.

## Serious adverse events

Overall, 17 patients (58.6%) experienced ≥ 1 SAE. The most common SAEs (reported in ≥ 5% of patients) were intracranial tumour haemorrhage (27.6%), seizure (10.3%), followed by blood bilirubin increased, depressed level of consciousness, haemorrhage intracranial, headache, and hydrocephalus (6.9% each). Treatment-related SAEs were blood bilirubin increased and haemorrhage intracranial (6.9% each) and intracranial tumour haemorrhage (3.4%).

· Intracranial tumour haemorrhage and tumour haemorrhage were confined to bleeding within the intracranial tumour. Considering the risk associated with the underlying disease, the majority were Grade 1 identified through imaging required per the protocol; these were not assessed to be consistent with the known intracranial bleedings observed with avapritinib in other indications such as GIST and AdvSM.

Eight patients (27.6%) reported intracranial tumour haemorrhage and 1 patient (3.4%) reported tumour haemorrhage (of CNS origin). The Grade 5 event resulted in death due to brain herniation secondary to increased intracranial pressure in the setting of disease progression and haemorrhage. Most events occurred in the setting of disease progression (7 patients). One patient who experienced a Grade 1 intracranial tumour haemorrhage was determined to benefit from treatment with avapritinib and continued treatment; the event subsequently resolved.

· Intracranial haemorrhage events occurred both within and outside the intracranial tumour and considering the known safety profile of avapritinib in the other indications these were classified as events of special interest.

Three patients (10.3%) experienced Grade 4 SAEs: tumour haemorrhage, depressed level of consciousness, hydrocephalus, and seizure (3.4% each).

## Discussion on clinical efficacy and safety

With this submission the MAH presents via a Type II variation the final CSR for Study BLU-285-3101, in order to fulfil the remaining obligation under Article 22 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006. Study BLU-2853101 is included in the agreed PIP for avapritinib EMEA-002358-PIP02-18-M04 (Study 2) with EMA Decision P/0363/2024.

Study BLU-285-3101 (ROVER) is a multicentre, open-label, single arm study to evaluate safety, pharmacokinetics (part 1) and antitumour activity (part 2) of avapritinib in paediatric patients from 2 to less than 18 years of age with a relapsed / refractory solid tumour harbouring a mutation in KIT or PDGFR-alpha, KIT or PDGFR-alpha amplifications, or a H3K27M mutant glioma and no available alternative treatment options.

Consequently, the aim of this variation application is the update of the SmPC (sections 4.2 and 5.1) of Ayvakyt in order to incorporate the paediatric information based on the assessment of the final results of the clinical Study BLU-285-3101.

Avapritinib is approved as a single agent for the treatment of adult patients in the following indications: unresectable or metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) harbouring the PDGFRA D842V mutation; advanced systemic mastocytosis (AdvSM); and indolent systemic mastocytosis (ISM).

The paediatric study BLU-285-3101 focused on advanced solid tumours harbouring KIT or PDGFRa mutations, KIT or PDGFR-alpha amplifications, or a H3K27M mutant glioma; particularly CNS tumours, in

Avapritinib was administered orally daily supplied as 25 mg and 100 mg film-coated tablets (for patients able to swallow the tablets) and 50 mg sprinkle capsules (each capsule contains five 10 mg minitablets), and in continuous 28-day cycles.

The dose (in m g/m 2 ) administered to each patient was based on age and BSA. The BSA was recalculated every cycle and the dose adjusted, as needed.

The primary objective of the study in Part 1 was the determination of the Part 2 recommended dose and the assessment of the safety and tolerability of avapritinib in paediatric patients. In Part 2, the primary objective was to assess the anti-tumour activity of avapritinib (ORR) by RECISTv1.1 or RANO. ORR in Part 1 was a secondary endpoint. Other secondary endpoints in both Part 1 and Part 2 included the assessment of ORR by RAPNO in patients with DMG and HGG, DOR, PFS, TTR and DCR. OS was an exploratory endpoint.

## Statistical methods

Regarding sample size, across part 1 and part 2 at least 31 participants were planned to be enrolled to allow testing of the null hypothesis of ORR ≤ 10% versus the alternative hypothesis of ORR ≥30% with 85% power assuming a 2-sided type I error of 0.10, using the exact binomial test.

Of note, the sample size was modified with protocol amendment 7 (initially at least 37 patients were planned to be enrolled: 12 in Part 1 and 25 in Part 2).

There was a total of 11 amendments of the original protocol dated on 16 December 2020.

All efficacy analyses were performed in the ITT population, or in the applicable subset of the ITT population. In addition, all participants enrolled in the study had CNS tumours, therefore the response related efficacy was evaluated by RANO (RANO-HGG or RANO-LGG) or RAPNO criteria.

Interim analysis or imputation missing data were not planned for this study as well as multiplicity adjustment for the secondary efficacy endpoints.

## Efficacy results

Regarding the efficacy results, the ROVER study did not meet its primary endpoint. Out of the 29 participants in Part 1 or Part 2, none of the participants had a best response of confirmed CR or PR per Investigator assessment according to RANO criteria (RANO-HGG or RANO-LGG). No confirmed responses were observed among the 29 participants included in the analysis. The treatment with avapritinib did not demonstrate anti-tumour activity in paediatric participants with CNS tumours with H3K27a and/or PDGFRA mutated or amplified and therefore the enrolment in the study was stopped.

## Safety results

All the subjects from the Study BLU-285-3101 who received at least 1 dose of avapritinib were included in the safety analysis set (n=29). The MAH presented treatment-emergent AEs according to age and dose level groups (children 80%, children 100%, adolescent 80%, and adolescent 100%) for Parts 1 and 2 combined. However, the MAH has not performed an analysis based on paediatric age groups (adolescents, children aged 6 to &lt;12 years, and children aged 2 to &lt;6 years) although the number of patients included in the study is limited.

The median duration of exposure was very limited, 1.4 months (Part 1 and Part 2).

DLTs (Part 1) were experienced by 4 patients (18.2%) out of the 22 DLT-evaluable patients. However, one of these 4 patients which DLT event was a Grade 3 bilirubin increase that was not considered DLTevaluable because it received &gt; 130% of the BSA-adjusted dose due to rounding up to the full adult dose in a participant with a BSA &gt; 1.2 m 2 in the dosing table.

In the DD population (all patients in the safety population for Part 1 who received at least 21 of the 28 doses of avapritinib in Cycle 1, or experienced DLT in C1 regardless of how much exposure they had), 3 patients (17.6%), 2 in the children 100% group and 1 in the adolescent 100% group, experienced 4 DLTs, including 3 Grade 3 blood bilirubin increased and 1 Grade 2 ALT increased.

All 29 patients (100.0%) experienced at least one AE and 28 patients (96.6%) experienced ≥ 1 AE related to avapritinib. The most common treatment-related AEs (reported in ≥ 20% of participants) were blood bilirubin increased (48.3%), hair colour changes (41.4%), vomiting (34.5%), anaemia, nausea, and white blood cell count decreased (31.0% each), lymphocyte count decreased and neutrophil count decreased (27.6% each), and constipation and fatigue (24.1% each).

Section 5.1: Pharmacodynamic properties

## Paediatric population

The European Medicines Agency has deferred the obligation to submit the results of studies with AYVAKYT in one or more subsets of the paediatric population with a relapsed/refractory solid tumour harbouring mutations in either KIT or PDGFRA (see section 4.2 for information on paediatric use).

The safety, pharmacokinetics and antitumour activity of avapritinib were evaluated in ROVER (BLU 2853101), a Phase 1/2, open label, single-arm study in 29 paediatric patients aged from 2 to &lt; 18 years with relapsed or refractory CNS tumours, dependent on KIT or PDGFRA signalling.

The median duration of treatment was 1.4 months and AYVAKYT did not demonstrate efficacy in paediatric patients with CNS tumours (ORR 0.0%). Due to the short duration of treatment, the safety could not be established in this patient population.

Section 5.2: Pharmacokinetic properties

## Paediatric population

The pharmacokinetics of avapritinib was evaluated in study BLU-285-3101; however, the pharmacokinetic data obtained were limited and insufficient to reliably characterise exposures in paediatrics. Therefore, no conclusions can be drawn regarding pharmacokinetics behaviour of avapritinib in this population.
