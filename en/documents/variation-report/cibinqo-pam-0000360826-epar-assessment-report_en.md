## 1. Introduction

On 6 July 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for Cibinqo, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

The MAH does not propose an update of the product information.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

Cibinqo which contains the JAK1-inhibitor abrocitinib as active substance, was approved in EU on 09 December 2021 for the treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) in adults who are candidates for systemic therapy. A type II Variation to expand the indication to include adolescents with moderate-to-severe AD who are candidates for systemic therapy was submitted to EMA in May 2023. The MAH received a positive CHMP Opinion on February 22, 2024, and a EC Decision on March 21, 2024.

According to the line listing of all the studies included in the development program submitted by the MAH, Study B7451134 titled \"Description of Real-World Abrocitinib Dose Patterns: A Retrospective Observational US Claims Database Study\" was not part of the development program.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

The abrocitinib formulation currently marketed in the US and used in the study is an oral tablet formulation: 50 mg, 100 mg and 200 mg film-coated tablet. This is also the authorised formulation for treatment of AD in the EU.

## Description

Study B7451134 was a non-interventional, retrospective cohort study of adolescent and adult patients with AD treated with abrocitinib in the US utilising de-identified data from the Komodo Health claims database from January 1, 2021 to April 30, 2025. The study aimed to describe real-world treatment patterns of abrocitinib, including dosing regimens, among adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (age ≥18) patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib. ## Methods

## Study participants

The study population included adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (age ≥18) patients with AD treated with abrocitinib in the US, utilising de-identified data from the Komodo Health claims database.

The adolescent abrocitinib cohort included patients with ≥1 pharmacy fill (paid claim) for abrocitinib (100 mg or 200 mg dose only) between February 10, 2023 and October 31, 2024; ≥1 inpatient or ≥2 outpatient medical claims at least 30 days apart for a diagnosis of AD (ICD-10-CM: L20.x), with at least 1 inpatient or 1 outpatient claim on or before the index date; aged 12-17 years old on the index date; ≥12 months of continuous enrollment prior to the index date; and ≥6 months of continuous enrollment post-index date.

The adult abrocitinib cohort included patients with ≥1 fill (paid claim) for abrocitinib (100 mg or 200 mg dose only) between January 14, 2022 and October 31, 2024; ≥1 inpatient claim or ≥2 outpatient claims at least 30 days apart for a diagnosis of AD (ICD-10-CM: L20.x), with at least 1 on or before the index date; aged ≥18 years old on the index date; ≥12 months of continuous enrollment prior to the index date; and ≥6 months of continuous enrollment post-index date.

Patients in the adolescent or adult abrocitinib cohort were excluded if they had ≥1 pharmacy fill for abrocitinib any time prior to the index date, were pregnant or breastfeeding in the pre index period, had any form of active or latent tuberculosis in the pre-index period, or had severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease any time prior to the index date.

## Treatments

No study drug was administered as part of the study. Patients were identified based on ≥1 pharmacy fill (paid claim) for abrocitinib 100 mg or 200 mg dose only during the identification period. The date of the first fill was defined as the index date.

## Objectives

The primary objective was to describe real-world treatment patterns of abrocitinib, including dosing regimens, among adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (age ≥18) AD patients who initiated abrocitinib.

Secondary objectives were to describe real-world dosing regimens of abrocitinib among adolescent and adult AD patients who initiated abrocitinib, stratified by subgroups of interest, including LOT of index abrocitinib, race/ethnicity, sex, provider type prescribing abrocitinib, and prior AD treatment history; and to describe the baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of adolescent and adult AD patients who initiated abrocitinib by treatment pattern.

## Outcomes/endpoints

This study was descriptive in nature, and no outcome data were collected or assessed.

## Sample size

As this study was descriptive without a pre-specified hypothesis, a sample size calculation was not applicable. A total of 2,528 patients met the inclusion criteria: 233 (9.2%) adolescents and 2,295 (90.8%) adults.

## Statistical Methods

This study conducted descriptive analyses, providing an overview of the data and summarising key characteristics of patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib in a real-world clinical setting. The analysis was conducted separately for adolescent and adult populations.

Baseline patient characteristics were summarised using descriptive statistics by adolescent and adult populations. These characteristics included demographics, clinical characteristics, and treatment history. Dosing and treatment patterns were also summarised using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables were summarised by mean, standard deviation, median, interquartile range, minimum, and maximum. Missing data were reported as a separate missing category. Categorical variables were summarised using frequency counts and percentages.

Real-world dosing and treatment patterns of abrocitinib were evaluated among adolescent and adult AD patients who initiated abrocitinib. Analyses included dosing and treatment pattern outcomes, such as dose modifications, treatment switch, and discontinuation. All treatment pattern outcomes were derived from prescription claims data and summarised using descriptive statistics. Dose adjustment was determined by changes in dose strength between consecutive paid pharmacy prescription claims for abrocitinib during the follow-up period.

All categorical and continuous variables, such as dosing adjustments and treatment switch, were evaluated during the 6-month period following the index date to ensure that estimates were calculated within a consistent and clinically meaningful time frame. For time-to-event outcomes, patients were followed until the occurrence of the event or a censoring event; no 6-month restriction was applied to these analyses.

Time-to-event outcomes, including time to dose adjustment, treatment discontinuation, or switch, were summarised descriptively. KM analyses were conducted to estimate survival distributions related to dosing and treatment pattern outcomes. Patients were censored at the earliest occurrence of end of continuous enrollment, end of data availability, or end of follow-up. A Sankey plot was developed to characterise treatment trajectories among patients initiating abrocitinib.

## Other Analyses

A sensitivity analysis was conducted requiring patients to have at least 12 months of continuous medical and pharmacy enrollment. When extending the post-index continuous enrollment criteria from a minimum of 6 months post-index to a minimum of 12 months post-index, there were 201 patients with AD treated with abrocitinib in the adolescent cohort and 2,009 patients with AD treated with abrocitinib in the adult cohort.

In the sensitivity analysis, 97.5% of the adolescent cohort initiated abrocitinib at a starting dose of 100 mg. Within 12 months of index date, 22.4% of adolescent patients adjusted their index dose of abrocitinib, with 19.9% of adolescent patients increasing their index dose and 2.5% decreasing their index dose. Within 12 months of index date, 31.8% (n=64) of adolescents switched to non-abrocitinib therapy and among those that switched, 21.9% switched from their first non-abrocitinib therapy to a different non-abrocitinib therapy. Approximately 47.8% of adolescent patients changed their index treatment (dose adjustment or treatment switch) within 12 months of index treatment. A little over a quarter (28.9%) of adolescent patients discontinued AD therapy within 12 months of index date.

The 12-month sensitivity analyses by subgroup were generally consistent with the 6-month main analysis. Among the 201 patients with AD treated with abrocitinib in the adolescent cohort with at least 12-months of available follow-up, 60 patients (29.8%) received abrocitinib as 1L, and 141 patients (70.1%) received abrocitinib as 2L+ of advanced treatment. Within 12 months of index date, 13.3% of patients with 1L index and 26.2% of patients with 2L+ index adjusted their index dose of abrocitinib. Within 12 months of index date, 28.3% of 1L and 33.3% of 2L+ index abrocitinib patients switched to non-abrocitinib therapy. Approximately 40.0% of 1L and 51.1% of 2L+ subgroup of adolescent patients changed their index treatment within 12 months of index treatment.

## Safety results

Safety was not examined in this study.

## The MAH's conclusions

This was a non-interventional, retrospective cohort study describing real-world dosing and treatment patterns of abrocitinib in adolescent and adult patients with AD in the US using de-identified claims data. The study was descriptive in nature, and no outcome data were collected or assessed; therefore, no efficacy conclusions can be drawn.

A total of 2,528 patients met the inclusion criteria, including 233 (9.2%) adolescents and 2,295 (90.8%) adults. Abrocitinib was primarily initiated at a starting dose of 100 mg in both adolescents (97.0%) and adults (90.1%).

Within 6 months of index, dose adjustments were observed in 14.2% of adolescents and 11.4% of adults, primarily representing dose increases. Treatment switching from abrocitinib occurred in 16.7% of adolescents and 18.1% of adults, and approximately 28.8% of adolescents and 28.5% of adults experienced a treatment change (dose adjustment or switch) within 6 months of index treatment. Treatment discontinuation within 6 months was observed in 25.8% of adolescents and 21.8% of adults. Phototherapy augmentation was rare (&lt;1%) in both groups, or not well captured.

Across both adolescent and adult populations, abrocitinib was most commonly used as 2L treatment, with a substantial proportion of patients receiving abrocitinib as 1L treatment. Dose modifications and treatment changes were more frequent among patients receiving abrocitinib in 2L or later settings compared with 1L.

No new efficacy or safety findings were identified, and the study does not change the understanding of abrocitinib use in adolescent or adult patients with AD.

## The MAH's benefits/risks

This study was observational and descriptive in design and did not evaluate PK, PD, efficacy outcomes, or safety. Therefore, this study does not provide new efficacy or safety evidence for abrocitinib in the paediatric population.

In the therapeutic context of AD, adolescent patients with moderate-to-severe disease may require systemic treatment when topical therapies are inadequate. Abrocitinib is an oral JAK1 inhibitor approved for use in adolescents aged 12 to &lt;18 years with moderate-to-severe AD. In this study, abrocitinib was used in real-world clinical practice across 1L, 2L, and 3L+ settings, predominantly initiated at 100 mg, with relatively low rates of early dose adjustment and treatment change.

The descriptive findings indicate that most patients initiated abrocitinib at the recommended starting dose and that dose modifications and treatment changes occurred in a minority of patients within the first 6 months. These observations are consistent with flexible dosing in clinical practice to achieve disease control while maintaining treatment tolerability.

The interpretation of these findings is limited by the use of administrative claims data, which lack clinical information on disease severity. As no new safety signals or efficacy outcomes were assessed, the study does not identify new risks or demonstrate additional benefits in the paediatric population.

Overall, the results of Study B7451134 do not change the established benefit/risk profile of abrocitinib for the paediatric population in the approved indication. No changes to the EU marketing authorisation, including the currently approved SmPC, are warranted.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

The MAH submitted the results for study B7451134 in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006.

Study B7451134 was a non-interventional, retrospective cohort study conducted in the US using deidentified medical and pharmacy closed claims data from the Komodo Health claims database from January 1, 2021 to April 30, 2025. The aim of the study was to describe real-world treatment patterns of abrocitinib, including dosing regimens, among adolescent (ages 12-&lt;18) and adult (age ≥18) patients with AD who initiated abrocitinib.

In the study results, real-world dosing and treatment patterns of adolescents and adults treated with abrocitinib as well as dosing regimens of adolescents and adults initiating abrocitinib, stratified by subgroups of interest including line of treatment, was reported. As already stated by the MAH, data interpretation is constrained by the descriptive study design, the use of administrative claims data and thereby lack of clinical information on disease severity.

Safety was not examined in this study.

The benefit-risk of abrocitinib is unchanged and no update to the product information has been proposed based on these data, which is endorsed.
