## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Problem statement

## 2.1.1. Disease or condition

The current application is intended to support registration of a scored D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10 mg film-coated FDC oral tablet for once-daily use for use in HIV 1 infected children aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg, who are either ART naïve or ART -experienced.

The following wording is proposed by the Applicant:

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications

Symtuza is indicated for the treatment of human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV 1) infection in adults and paediatric patients (aged 6 years and older with body weight at least 25 kg).

Genotypic testing should guide the use of Symtuza (see sections 4.2, 4.4, and 5.1).

## 2.1.2. Epidemiology

HIV continues to be a major global public health issue, having claimed more than 40 million lives so far. In 2023, 630 000 people died from HIV-related causes globally. There were approximately 39.9 million people living with HIV at the end of 2023, of whom 1.4 million were children 0-14 years old. Since 2010, the number of people acquiring HIV has been reduced, but there were still 1.3 million people becoming newly infected in 2023 globally, 120.000 being children 0-14 years old. (WHO FACT sheet and UNAIDS Gloval HIV &amp; AIDS statistics Fact sheet).

There is no cure for HIV infection. However, with access to effective HIV prevention, diagnosis, treatment and care, including for opportunistic infections, HIV infection has become a manageable chronic health condition, enabling people living with HIV to lead long and healthy lives.

## 2.1.3. Biologic features

HIV-1 infection results in chronic activation of the immune system and a subsequent gradual loss of CD4+ T cells, eventually leading to a state of acquired immunodeficiency (AIDS). One of the predictors for HIV-1 disease progression is the level of HIV-1 RNA in the blood (i.e. viral load). The aim of treatment of HIV-1 infection is therefore to suppress, and subsequently maintain, the HIV-1 viral load to levels that are at least below the limit of detection of most commonly used assays (50 copies/mL of blood).

HIV-1 is a rapidly replicating virus, with an error-prone Reverse Transcriptase (RT). Mutations in the viral genome occur randomly when the virus replicates, with an estimated mutation rate of approximately one nucleotide mutation per replicative cycle. Many HIV variants are simultaneously present in each infected individual, which is also described as 'quasispecies'. Resistance-associated mutations (RAMs) can rapidly be selected when there is selection pressure due to e.g. too low concentrations of antiviral drugs. Often, resistance to drugs in a certain ARV class results in crossresistance to other drugs in that same class.

## 2.1.4. Clinical presentation, diagnosis

The symptoms of HIV vary depending on the stage of infection. Though people living with HIV tend to be most infectious in the first few months after being infected, many are unaware of their status until the later stages. In the first few weeks after initial infection people may experience no symptoms or an influenza-like illness including fever, headache, rash, or sore throat. As the infection progressively weakens the immune system, they can develop other signs and symptoms, such as swollen lymph nodes, weight loss, fever, diarrhoea, and cough. Without treatment, they could also develop severe illnesses such as tuberculosis, cryptococcal meningitis, severe bacterial infections, and cancers such as lymphomas and Kaposi's sarcoma.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Children may have more rapid disease progression and accelerated damage of the developing immune system compared to adults, with higher viral loads and less effective immunological responses to HIV infection than adults.

## 2.1.5. Management

Standard of care for the treatment of HIV-1 requires use of combination ART to suppress viral replication to below detectable limits, increase CD4 cell counts, and stop disease progression. The choice of the combination regimen depends on the status of the patient, particularly in terms of plasma HIV viral load, CD4 cell counts, any previous treatments and prior treatment failure/intolerance.

Commonly used guidelines are those developed by the WHO, the European AIDS Clinical Society (EACS), the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) in the USA and the Paediatric European Network for Treatment of AIDS (PENTA), for use in children and adolescents.

Treatment options in children are more limited compared to adults. The recommended (collaborative Penta/EACS HIV treatment guideline, 2022) initial treatment of HIV-1 infection for paediatric patients is therapy with two NRTIs in combination with a drug from a different class (third agent). DTG is the preferred third agent in all children over 4 weeks of age and 3 kg. Whilst 'preferred options' are recommended, 'alternative options' are acceptable and remain important choices in settings where ART availability is limited or in individuals at particular risk of specific toxicity or DDIs.

## 2.2. About the product

The D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10 mg (EU: Symtuza®) FDC film-coated tablet is indicated in the EU, for once daily use, for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in adults and adolescents aged ≥ 12 years and weighing ≥ 40 kg, who are either ARTnaïve or ART -experienced. The D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10 mg FDC film-coated tablet was developed by Janssen in collaboration with Gilead Sciences, Inc. (Gilead).

In addition, a scored D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10 mg film-coated FDC tablet for once-daily use was developed by Janssen in collaboration with Gilead for use in HIV-1 infected paediatric patients aged ≥ 6 years and weighing ≥25 kg to &lt;40 kg, who are either ART - naïve or ART -experienced.

The proposed indication is:

Symtuza is indicated for the treatment of human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV 1) infection in adults and paediatric patients (aged 6 years and older with body weight at least 25 kg).

Genotypic testing should guide the use of Symtuza (see sections 4.2, 4.4, and 5.1).

The proposed posology for paediatric patients aged 6 years and older weighing at least 25 kg to less than 40 kg is:

## ART naïve paediatric patients

The recommended dose regimen is one 675 mg darunavir/150 mg cobicistat/200 mg emtricitabine/10 mg tenofovir alafenamide tablet once daily taken with food.

ART experienced paediatric patients One 675 mg darunavir/150 mg cobicistat/200 mg emtricitabine/10 mg tenofovir alafenamide tablet once daily taken with food may be used in patients with prior exposure to antiretroviral medicinal products but without DRV-RAMs* and who have plasma HIV-1 RNA &lt; 100 000 copies/mL and CD4+ cell count ≥ 100 cells x 106/L (see section 5.1).

## 2.4. Quality aspects

## 2.4.1. Introduction

Symtuza 800/150/200/10 mg film-coated tablets are already authorised for the treatment of adults and adolescents (aged 12 years and older, weighing at least 40 kg). The current application is intended for registration of a new lower strength for once-daily use in HIV 1 infected children aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg .

The finished product is presented as a scored film-coated tablet containing 675/150/200/10 mg of darunavir/cobicistat/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide respectively as active substances. The product contains darunavir as the ethanolate solvate, cobicistat adsorbed on silicon dioxide and tenofovir alafenamide as the fumarate salt.

Other ingredients are:

- tablet core: colloidal anhydrous silica (E551), croscarmellose sodium (E468), magnesium stearate (E572), and cellulose microcrystalline (E460(i));
- tablet coating: polyethylene glycol (macrogol 3350), polyvinyl alcohol (partially hydrolysed), titanium dioxide (E171), talc (E553b), and iron oxide black (E172).

The product is available in white, high density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles with a silica gel desiccants (contained in a separate sachet or canister) fitted with polypropylene (PP) child resistant closures with induction seals.

## 2.4.2. Active Substance

The active substances are not described in the Ph. Eur. They are already approved for commercial use in Symtuza 800/150/200/10 mg, film coated tablets. The new proposed strength 675/150/200/10mg contains the same active substances, from the same sources, as the commercially available strength. No changes regarding the active substances compared to the currently approved strength are proposed; this is acceptable.

The development of the product has been described, the choice of excipients is justified, and their functions have been explained. Development of the proposed strength was based on previous experience with the formulation and manufacturing development of the 800/150/200/10 mg strength (G001). The choices of the container closure system and manufacturing process are justified. The quantitative formulation designated as G002 was adjusted during development to improve manufacturability and resulted in the commercial formulation G004.

To facilitate the administration to patients unable to swallow the tablet whole, the proposed filmcoated tablets bear a score line allowing them to be broken in half. Each piece should be consumed immediately after splitting to ensure the entire dose is administered. The acceptability/swallowability for the target population either as whole or split tablets, was demonstrated in a phase 1 study (TMC114FD2HTX1006). While administration of the proposed product using food or drink vehicles has been referenced in the clinical acceptability study, all study participants chose to swallow the whole or split tablet with water, and therefore alternative administration using a food/drink vehicle, and the associated compatibility studies, are not considered necessary.

A bioequivalence study was performed where the proposed product was compared to the four active substances as single agents or combined tablets (i.e., darunavir 600 mg and 75 mg tablets, emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide 200/10 mg tablets, and cobicistat 150 mg tablets.

The bioequivalence study was performed with formulation G002, which was slightly different to the commercial formulation G004. The two formulations were bridged through comparative dissolution studies in four different media (pH 1.2, 4.5, 6.8, and the QC media). The bridging was not initially acceptable as the dissolution profiles were not demonstrated to be similar due to excessive variability for different active substances in different media resulting in a Major Objection (MO1). In response, the applicant submitted a bootstrapping analysis - the profiles were not similar if the 5 minute timepoint was included but profiles were similar when that timepoint was omitted. It was argued that the timepoint is not relevant since tablets are to be taken with food which results in an extended residence time in the stomach, with dissolution occurring over a longer period. This was accepted and the MO1 was considered resolved.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The CHMP raised another MO requesting the applicant to provide additional data to confirm the representativeness of the test biobatch in relation to typical commercial batches of test product and the acceptability of the test biobatch (e.g. its assay versus the reference biobatch) in line with the EMA guideline on the investigation of bioequivalence.

The applicant confirmed that the size of the batch used in the bioequivalence studies was considered full commercial size at its time of manufacture. Furthermore, the operating principles and control strategy are the same for both the batch used in the bioequivalence studies (CBXXB) and the proposed commercial product, the equipment only varying in size. Comparative batch data according to the release specifications and IPC results were provided, demonstrating the representativeness of the biobatch. Furthermore, comparative dissolution studies were conducted and profiles considered to be similar, as per MO1. Overall, it is concluded that the test and reference medicinal product batches used in the bioequivalence batches adhere to the requirements of the EMA guideline on the investigation of bioequivalence and MO2 was thus resolved.

As with the authorised strength, two dissolution methods are used to control the finished product, due to different solubility profiles of the four active substances. The proposed dissolution methods are the same as those used for the already approved product, and the applicant has shown the suitability of these conditions for the proposed commercial formulation (G004). The parameters are in line with Ph. Eur. 2.9.3, and the higher rotation speed has been justified in relation to coning observed at a lower speed and minimising of artefacts. The selection of media and the use of a surfactant (for the darunavir dissolution method) has been discussed in terms of sink conditions and pH dependency, and this justification is considered applicable for the proposed paediatric formulation.

The container closure system is a white, high density polyethylene (HDPE) bottle containing 30 tablets, fitted with polypropylene (PP) child resistant closure with induction seal. The packaging is common for this type of dosage form, and the container closure system is similar to that used for the already approved strength. The container closure system materials comply with the current European Regulation for materials intended to come into contact with food EC 1935/2004, including Regulation (EU) No 10/2011 on Plastic Materials and Articles Intended to Come into Contact with Food. The suitability of these materials is further demonstrated by the results obtained from stability studies.

## 2.4.3.2. Manufacture of the product and process controls

The finished product manufacturing and batch release sites are stated in the dossier. Satisfactory evidence of GMP compliance has been provided.

The manufacturing process consists of 8 main steps: initial blending, 2 nd  blending, granulation, final blending, compression, preparation of coating suspension, film coating and packaging. The product is manufactured using conventional manufacturing techniques. However, since the amount of tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) is &lt;2%, it is considered a non -standard manufacturing process.

A flow chart and written narrative of the manufacturing process have been provided, and both are sufficiently detailed.

Process validation was conducted on three full scale batches in line with the EMA guideline on process validation for finished products. Ranges for critical and non-critical process parameters were explored, and critical and non-critical process controls were adequately recorded. For each step in the process, suitable in-process controls and process parameters/ranges were defined based on development and process validation data. In addition, blend uniformity was investigated and found to be adequate, which is especially relevant to the distribution of TAF since it is present at &lt;2%.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The proposed holding time of 12 months for the bulk drug product is considered acceptable based on the stability data provided.

It has been demonstrated that the manufacturing process is capable of producing the finished product of  intended  quality  in  a  reproducible  manner.  The  in-process  controls  are  adequate  for  this  type  of manufacturing process and pharmaceutical form.

The finished product release and shelf life specifications include appropriate tests for this kind of dosage form including appearance (visual), tablet dimension, identification (UHPLC, UV), assay (UHPLC), chromatographic purity related to darunavir (UHPLC), chromatographic purity related to cobicistat  (UHPLC), chromatographic purity related to emtricitabine (UHPLC), chromatographic purity related to tenofovir alafenamide (UHPLC), N -nitroso-2,4-thiazoleamine (LC-MS), uniformity of dosage units (Ph. The proposed analytical procedures and corresponding specification limits are adequate for routine control of the finished product at release and shelf life. The proposed limits are generally in line with ICH guidelines and Ph. Eur., and batch release and stability data. The wider limits for some parameters at shelf-life are justified based on the behaviour observed during stability studies.

The potential presence of elemental impurities in the finished product was assessed following a riskbased approach in line with the ICH Q3D Guideline for Elemental Impurities. Based on the risk assessment it can be concluded that it is not necessary to include any elemental impurity controls in the finished product specification. The information on the control of elemental impurities is satisfactory.

A risk assessment concerning the potential presence of nitrosamine impurities in the finished product has been performed considering all suspected and actual root causes in line with the 'Questions and answers for marketing authorisation holders/applicants on the CHMP Opinion for the Article 5(3) of Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 referral on nitrosamine impurities in human medicinal products' (EMA/409815/2020) and the 'Assessment report- Procedure under Article 5(3) of Regulation EC (No) 726/2004- Nitrosamine impurities in human medicinal products' (EMA/369136/2020).

During the procedure, the applicant reported elevated levels of N -nitroso-2,4-thiazole amine (NNTA) in already approved Symtuza 800/150/200/10 mg tablets (ranging from 30% to 100% of the Maximum Acceptable Limit (MAL) of 12 ppb with respect to the finished product based on an.(With regard to the authorised product the MAH has followed the foreseen process as per the 'European Medicines Regulatory Network approach for the implementation of the CHMP Opinion pursuant to Article 5(3) of Regulation (EC) No 726/2004 for nitrosamine impurities in human medicines' (EMA/425645/2020), and the relevant regulatory actions have been followed) acceptable intake (AI) of 18 ng NNTA/day and a maximum daily dose (MDD) of 1.4571 g finished product per day.

In the light of the above, the applicant provided an updated nitrosamine risk assessment, and presented confirmatory  testing  data  for  NNTA  from  three  validation  batches  of  the  new  strength  at  a  single timepoint (20 months) stored under long-term conditions.

However, no trend data, nor any information on impurity behaviour in-use was available. Furthermore, the system suitability acceptance criteria for the applied analytical method were considered too wide. Therefore, the CHMP raised a MO requesting the applicant for further confirmatory testing for NNTA up to the end of shelf-life and also under accelerated, and when relevant, intermediate conditions, to tighten the NNTA limit in the release specifications, to restrict storage conditions in line with available stability data, to provide in-use stability data, and to tighten the system suitability criteria.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In response, the applicant presented additional confirmatory testing data for NNTA on three validation batches as well as on three registration batches. The validation batches were manufactured using microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) with low nitrite content, whereas nitrite was significantly higher in the MCC used to manufacture the registration batches. The validation batches were stored for 11 months under long-term, accelerated as well as intermediate storage conditions. These batches were 23 months old (from date of manufacture) at time of testing and the NNTA levels measured at all storage conditions were below the acceptable intake limit for all three batches, although levels were higher (up to ~50% MAL) under accelerated conditions . The registration batches were also tested for NNTA after storage for 54 months under both long-term and intermediate storage conditions. These batches were 60 months old at the time of testing (from date of manufacture), i.e. exceeding the proposed shelf life of 36 months. The NNTA levels were below the acceptable intake limit (5494% MAL) for all batches except for one which was found to be above the acceptable intake limit (106%) when stored at 30 ° C/75% RH. The registration batches are considered worst case since testing for NNTA was conducted when the batches far exceeded the proposed shelf-life. NNTA also increased above the AI limit in the in-use studies, conducted with the registration batches, more so at higher temperature and humidity.

The applicant also clarified that, in order to mitigate the formation of NNTA, future commercial batches will be manufactured with MCC with low nitrite content, an acceptable limit for which is included in its specification. Finally, the system suitability criteria for the analytical method were tightened as requested. However, since confirmatory results covering only one timepoint for the validation and registration batches are insufficient to fully understand the increase of NNTA during storage and considering that the available confirmatory results on both registration and validation batches show higher levels of NNTA when the batches were stored at higher temperature and humidity, the proposed storage condition and the in-use storage recommendation in the SmPC (section 6.4) was restricted to 'Store below 25 °C' for the unopened bottle as packaged for sale as well as during the in-use storage period. The major objection was therefore considered resolved and the nitrosamines control strategy is acceptable. Nonetheless, the applicant is recommended to generate stability data for NNTA according to the ICH guidelines over the approved shelf-life of the product and re-evaluate the release specifications, storage conditions, and shelf-life (including in use) as appropriate (REC). The analytical procedures used have been adequately described and non-compendial methods appropriately validated in accordance with the ICH guidelines. Satisfactory information regarding the reference standards used for testing has been presented. were provided. The results were within the specifications and confirm consistency of the manufacturing

## 2.4.3.4. Stability of the product

Stability data were provided from three commercial scale batches stored at 5 °C (up to 3 months), 25 °C / 60% RH (up to 36 months), 30 °C / 75% RH (up to 36 months) and 40 °C / 75% RH (up to 6 months). The conditions are generally in line with ICH Q1A, except for intermediate conditions, but the use of the harsher 30 °C / 75% RH condition is acceptable.

Samples were tested for appearance, assay, chromatographic purity, dissolution, and microbial purity.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Changes were mainly seen in tenofovir alafenamide, and to a lesser extend in cobicistat, where the assay decreases and impurities increase, however all parameters remained within the proposed acceptance criteria. The proposed shelf-life of 3 years is justified by the data, including the confirmatory testing data on NNTA. In view of this data, the temperature storage conditions have been restricted to 'Store below 25 °C' as stability for NNTA has only been fully demonstrated under longterm storage conditions (i.e. 25 °C / 60% RH).

The forced degradation studies were assessed for the already approved strength and are considered applicable for the new strength. The storage precaution 'store in original package' to protect from moisture is justified as the active substance tenofovir alafenamide is susceptible to degradation in presence of water. A photostability study was conducted in line with ICH Q1B and the product is not sensitive to light.

The in-use stability period of 6 weeks is justified by the in-use stability data of batches stored at 25 °C / 60% RH. The increase in NNTA observed during the in -use period is considered significant, in particular at 30 °C / 75% and the available data justify the recommendation to store the bottle below 25 °C during the in-use period.

Overall, the stability results justify the proposed shelf life of 3 years, the proposed in-use shelf life and the recommended storage conditions in the proposed container, as stated in the SmPC sections 6.3 and 6.4.

## 2.6.1.1. Pharmacokinetics

This assessment focusses only on the new paediatric data received from study GS-US-216-0128 for patients aged between 6 and 12 years and weighing more than 25 kg and on the new bioequivalence study comparing the FDC of DRV/COBI 675/150 mg and the separate agents.

## Bioanalytical methods

Validated analytical methods for DRV, COBI, FTC, TAF and TFV have been applied, showing that the methods are accurate and precise. In study validation during study sample analysis showed acceptable accuracy and precision. Incurred sample reanalysis were within normal criteria.

## Population PK modeling for DRV

The previously developed PopPK model in adults for DRV in combination with COBI was updated to describe the data from the current analysis dataset, for example, by re-estimating selected parameters and updating the covariate model. The reestimated parameter estimates had RSEs &lt;20%. Shrinkage of the random effects was &lt;30% for CL/F and Vp/F, and over 30% for the other parameters. These shrinkage values were expected because the IIVs were kept fixed to the estimates from the previously developed PopPK model. pcVPCs stratified by body weight sufficiently captured the central tendency and variability of the data across all 3 weight bands. The updated PopPK model is considered fit-forpurpose, as was previous concluded. COBI, FTC, TAF, and TFV are already approved in children weighing ≥ 25 kg at the proposed doses. No simulations were performed, as doses were already accepted, but popPK analysis were used for covariate analysis. The PopPK simulations show that for paediatric patients in the weight band ≥ 25 to &lt;40 kg a DRV/COBI regimen of 675/150 mg qd results in comparable or slightly higher (up to 34% higher at 25 kg) DRV AUC024h and comparable or slightly lower (up to 33% lower at 39 kg) DRV C0h with respect to adults. For paediatric patients weighing 25 to &lt;40 kg receiving DRV/COBI 675/150 mg qd , DRV C0h below 55 ng/ml (protein-binding adjusted EC50 for wildtype virus) is estimated to occur in &lt;1% of paediatric patients. Distribution plots of DRV AUC0-24h and C0h in paediatric patients compared to adults (study FD2HTX3001) were provided, however, the plots did not contain observed data. On request, the applicant provided plots with observed data, showing that the model predictions and observed data are aligned. In study GS-US-216-0128, based upon non-compartmental data, for COBI, FTC, TAF and TFV, a higher exposure was observed in paediatric subjects compared to adults. For COBI this was a known observation based upon previous data and considered not of safety concern. For FTC, popPK analysis including 6 Phase 2/3 studies (GS-US-292-0106, GS-US-292-1515, GS-US-236-0112, GS-US-3111269, GS-US-380-1474 and GS-US-216-0128) in paediatric participants infected with HIV-1, the observed higher FTC exposure is mainly driven by the lower bodyweight, however, exposures overlap with dose in adolescents and adults, and the 200 mg FTC dose is approved for use in adults and paediatric patients weighing at least 25 kg, in combination with other antiretrovirals. For TAF, the main difference in the observed exposures is due to the difference in TAF dose, i.e., 25 mg in study GS-US216-0128. In addition, comparison with adult data showed an overlap in exposures and comparison with other data in children &gt;25 kg receiving 25 mg TAF (study GS-US-380-1474 receiving B/F/TAF) and study GS-US-292-0106 receiving E/C/F/TAF) showed comparable exposures. TAF 10 mg is recommended for use with agents requiring a PK enhancer, such as DRV. For TFV the main difference in the observed exposures is due to the difference in TAF dose (25 mg vs. 10 mg, respectively). Taking into account 2.5-fold difference in TAF dose, the TFV values would be in the upper quartile of adult data, but still overlapping. Moreover, the 10 mg dose in children &gt;25 kg is in line with that for instance indicated for Genvoya in this patient group. Bioequivalence (study TMC114IFD1007) The single dose, randomized, open-label, 2-treatment, 2-sequence, 4-period replicate crossover study, evaluated the bioequivalence of the 675/150/200/10 mg FDC tablet vs. the separate co-administered tablets, i.e., 600 mg + 75 mg DRV + 150 mg COB + 200/10 mg FTC/TAF. ## 2.6.1.2. Pharmacodynamics

## Mechanism of action

DRV is an inhibitor of the dimerisation and of the catalytic activity of the HIV-1 protease (KD of 4.5 x 10-12M). It selectively inhibits the cleavage of HIV encoded Gag-Pol polyproteins in virus infected cells, thereby preventing the formation of mature infectious virus particles.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

COBI is a mechanism-based inhibitor of cytochromes P450 of the CYP3A subfamily. Inhibition of CYP3A-mediated metabolism by cobicistat enhances the systemic exposure of CYP3A substrates, such as DRV, where bioavailability is limited and half-life is shortened due to CYP3A-dependent metabolism.

FTC is a nucleoside analogue of 2'-deoxycytidine. FTC is phosphorylated by cellular enzymes to form FTC triphosphate. FTC triphosphate inhibits HIV replication through incorporation into viral DNA by the HIV reverse transcriptase (RT), which results in DNA chain-termination.

TAF is a phosphonoamidate prodrug of tenofovir (2'-deoxyadenosine monophosphate analogue). TAF is permeable into cells and due to increased plasma stability and intracellular activation through hydrolysis by cathepsin A, TAF is more efficient than tenofovir disoproxil in concentrating tenofovir in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) (including lymphocytes and other HIV target cells) and macrophages. Intracellular tenofovir is subsequently phosphorylated to the pharmacologically active metabolite tenofovir diphosphate. Tenofovir diphosphate inhibits HIV replication through incorporation into viral DNA by the HIV RT, which results in DNA chain-termination.

## Primary and Secondary pharmacology

No PK/PD analyses for efficacy or safety were performed in Study 216-0128.

Exposure-response analysis for safety was conducted for the TAF (and TFV) component by Gilead using pooled data from children ≥2 to &lt;12 years of age living with HIV-1 administered F/TAF-containing regimens from 4 paediatric studies (292-0106, 311-1269, 380-1474, and 216-0128) with 10- and 25mg doses of TAF to evaluate the relationship between TAF and TFV exposures and bone and renal safety endpoints. No clinically meaningful exposure-response relationship was observed between TAF or TFV exposures and changes in bone and renal safety endpoints in children ≥ 6 to &lt;12 years with body weight ≥25 to &lt;40 kg.

## 2.6.2. Discussion on clinical pharmacology

The scored DRV/COBI/FTC/TAF 675/150/200/10-mg FDC film-coated tablet is administered according to the same once-daily dose regimen as the separate commercially available tablet formulations. The development program for the scored DRV/COBI 675/150/200/10-mg FDC film-coated tablet is based on the clinical development programs of the single agents of DRV, COBI, FTC and TAF and the PK bridging of the separate commercially available tablets with the FDC tablet.

The aim of the drug product development was to develop a scored DRV/COBI/FTC/TAF 675/150-mg FDC film-coated tablet that is bioequivalent to the single agents of DRV, COBI, FTC/TAF administered concomitantly as the commercially available tablet formulations (DRV 1x600-mg and 1x75-mg tablet + COBI 1x150-mg tablet + FTC/TAF 1 x 200/10 mg). Excipient selection and manufacturing process development of the drug product was executed based on prior knowledge gained during the development of the commercially approved SYMTUZA (D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10-mg) FDC filmcoated tablet.

In study IFD1004, the scored DRV/COBI 675/150-mg FDC film-coated tablet was shown to be bioequivalent to the single agents of DRV and COBI administered concomitantly as the commercially available tablet formulations (DRV 1x600-mg and 1x75-mg tablet; and COBI 1x150-mg tablet) under fed conditions in healthy adults, which confirms the suitability of the proposed formulation for use in children.

The PK results showed that DRV, COBI, FTC and TAF administration as a scored FDC tablet (DRV/COBI/TFC/TAF 675/150/200/10 mg) was bioequivalent to the coadministration as the separate available tablet formulations (DRV 1×600 mg + 1×75 -mg tablet +COBI 1×150 mg tablet + TFC/TAF 1 x 200/10 mg tablet), under fed conditions. ## 2.6.3. Conclusions on clinical pharmacology

Overall, the available PK data and PopPK modelling, indicate that PK exposures for the components of the D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10mg FDC in children (aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg) are comparable to those observed for the D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10-mg FDC in adults and adolescents (aged ≥12 years and weighing ≥40 kg) . ## 2.6.4.1. Dose-response studies

No dose response studies were performed.

The proposed DRV dose of 675 mg is aligned with the approved DRV dose in combination with 100 mg rtv for patients weighing ≥30 to &lt;40 kg. COBI 150 mg qd was shown to be generally safe and welltolerated throughout its development and increased DRV exposures to levels that were comparable to the boosting effect of co-administered rtv 100 mg. The proposed DRV dose of 675 mg was extended down to a body weight of 25 kg supported by PopPK modelling for dosing weight-bands simplification and to align with the approved paediatric dose for COBI in E/C/F/TAF 150/150/200/10-mg FDC (Genvoya SmPC 2022), ie, for use in patients weighing ≥ 25 kg.

F/TAF 200/10 mg and F/TAF 200/25 mg are approved for treatment of HIV-1 infection in the EU in combination with other ARV agents for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in adults and adolescents aged ≥12 years, with a weight ≥35 kg (Descovy SmPC 2023). This f ormulation contains the same dose of FTC as in the approved Truvada FDC (FTC/TDF 200/300 mg) for use in the same indication (Truvada SmPC 2024) and as Emtriva, approved for use in adults and children from 4 months and older (Emtriva SmPC 2023). TAF 25 mg is recommended for use with an ART not requiring a PK enhancer, and TAF 10 mg for use with an ART requiring a PK enhancer, such as DRV (Descovy SmPC 2023). TAF 10 mg is also approved as part of the E/C/F/TAF 150/150/200/10 mg FDC for use in adults and paediatric patients weighing ≥25 kg (Genvoya SmPC 2022).

## Statistical methods

Analysis sets and their definitions are as follows:

All Enrolled Analysis Set included all participants who were enrolled into the study. This is the primary analysis set for by-participant listings.

The Full Analysis Set (FAS) included all participants who took at least 1 dose of study drug. This is the primary analysis set for efficacy analyses.

The Safety Analysis Set included all participants who took at least 1 dose of study drug. All the data collected up to 30 days after participants permanently discontinued their study drug was included in the safety summaries. This is the primary analysis set for safety analyses.

The Intensive PK (IPK) Analysis Set was defined for each analyte of interest and included all participants who received at least 1 dose of study drug and for whom steady-state PK profiles at IPK visits were evaluable for the analyte of interest. The IPK Analysis Set was used for IPK analyses.

The Pharmacokinetic (PK) Analysis Set included all participants who received at least 1 dose of study drug and for whom at least 1 observed concentration data of any analyte of interest was available. This is the primary analysis set for all PK analyses. The PK analysis was used for the trough blood concentration listing.

The PBMC PK Analysis Set included all participants who (1) are enrolled into the study, (2) have received at least 1 dose of study drug and (3) have at least 1 nonmissing concentration data of TFVDP. The PBMC PK Analysis Set will be used for PK analyses of TFV-DP.

The Spine DXA Analysis Set included all participants who (1) were enrolled in the study, (2) had received at least 1 dose of study drug, and (3) had non-missing spine BMD value for the Day 1 visit.

The Total Body Less Head (TBLH) DXA Analysis Set included all participants who (1) were enrolled in the study, (2) had received at least 1 dose of study drug, and (3) had non-missing TBLH BMD value for the Day 1 visit.

The FAS is the primary analysis set for efficacy endpoints. No formal statistical testing was planned.

All HIV-1 RNA data collected on treatment (ie, data collected up to 1 day after the last dose date) was used in the US FDA-defined snapshot algorithm. For the snapshot algorithm, the numbers and percentages of participants with HIV1 RNA &lt; 50 copies/mL, HIV - 1 RNA ≥ 50 copie s/mL, and reasons for no virological data at Week 24/48 were summarized by cohort. The 95% CI for the percentage of participants with HIV1 RNA &lt; 50 copies/mL was constructed using the Clopper -Pearson exact method. The Week 24 virologic outcomes for the US FDA-defined snapshot algorithm were listed.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The proportion of participants with HIV1 RNA &lt; 50 copies/mL was also analysed by cohort and by visit using the following 2 methods for imputing missing HIV-1 RNA values:

- missing = failure (M = F)

In this approach, all missing data were treated as HIV1 RNA ≥ 50 copies/mL. The denominator for the percentage was the number of participants in the FAS, excluding ongoing participants who had both missing HIV-1 RNA at a visit and had not reached the upper limit of that analysis window for the corresponding visit.

- missing = excluded (M = E)

In this approach, all missing data were excluded in the computation of the percentages (ie, missing data points were excluded from both the numerator and denominator in the computation). The denominator for percentages at a visit is the number of participants in the FAS with non-missing HIV-1 RNA value at that visit.

For both M = F and M = E analyses, the number and percentage of participants with HIV-1 RNA in the following categories were summarized: &lt; 50 copies/mL (&lt; 20 copies/mL [&lt; 20 copies/mL not detectable, &lt; 20 copies/mL detectable], 20 to &lt; 50 copies/mL), 50 to &lt; 200 copies/mL, 200 to &lt; 400 copies/mL, 400 to &lt; 1000 copies/mL, ≥ 1000 copies/mL, and missing (only applicable to M = F analysis).

The CD4 cell count and CD4% data were summarized using observed, on-treatment data (ie, data collected up to 1 day after permanent discontinuation of study drug or all available data for participants who were still on study drug). Absolute values and changes from baseline in CD4 cell count (cells/μL) and CD4% at each visit were summarized b y cohort and by visit descriptively (sample size, mean, SD, median, Q1, Q3, minimum, and maximum) and also included the 95% CI based on the tdistribution. The mean and 95% CI of change from baseline over time by cohort was plotted.

Adverse event and clinical laboratory data were summarized using descriptive statistics. Adverse events were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) Version 26.0. Tanner stage assessments were used to evaluate the onset and progression of pubertal changes. Body weight, body weight Z-score, height, and height Z-score were summarized.

## 2.6.4.7. Supportive study(ies)

Study TMC114FD2HTX1006, A Study to Assess the Acceptability of Scored Film-coated darunavir/cobicistat/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide (D/C/F/TAF) Fixed-dose Combination (FDC) Tablets in HIV-1 Infected Paediatric Participants Aged ≥ 6 to &lt;12 years, Using Matching Placebo Tablets.

Study design: Phase 1, open-label, randomised, single-dose, cross-over, international, multicenter study to evaluate the acceptability of a scored film-coated D/C/F/TAF FDC tablet administered as a matching placebo tablet, one taken as a whole and one as a split tablet. Treatments: The placebo tablets were 21 mm x 11 mm x 7 mm in size, and participants could indicate their preferred method for tablet swallowing (ie , taken with 1 tablespoon [~15 mL] of semi solid food [ie, apple sauce, yogurt, or pudding] followed by drinking up to 150 mL of liquid [ie, water, milk, apple juice, or cranberry juice], or taken only with up to 150 mL of liquid).

Participants: The study population was planned to include 24 male or female participants ≥ 6 to &lt;12 years of age, weighing at least 25 kg and less than 40 kg with documented chronic HIV-1 infection, who were on a stable ARV regimen for at least 3 months prior to screening and had documented plasma HIV1 ribonucleic acid (RNA) &lt;400 copies/mL with in 6 months prior to screening, with the aim of including at least 7 participants in the age group from ≥ 6 to &lt;9 years. Objectives : The primary objective was to assess the ability to swallow the placebo-matched scored film-coated D/C/F/TAF FDC (fixed-dose combination) tablet, irrespective of the mode of intake. The main secondary objectives were: 1) to assess the acceptability of swallowing the placebo-matched scored film-coated D/C/F/TAF FDC tablet, taken as a whole tablet and as a split tablet, 2) to assess the ease of splitting the placebo-matched scored film-coated D/C/F/TAF FDC tablet.

After each intake of a placebo tablet, the participant had to complete an acceptability questionnaire (one questionnaire for the whole tablet and one for the split tablet). Similarly, after each intake of a placebo tablet by the participant, an acceptability questionnaire (one questionnaire for the whole tablet and one for the split tablet) had to be completed by the caregiver and by the observer based on observation of the child. In addition, the observer had to record in the observer questionnaire whether the tablet was hard, OK, or easy to break by hand (based on observation of the caregiver).

## 2.6.5. Discussion on clinical efficacy

In support of the current variation, data from Interim Analysis 3 of Study 216-0128 (Cohort 2) was submitted. Study 216-0128 is an ongoing open-label, multicenter, multicohort, two-part study (Part A and B) evaluating the PK, safety, efficacy, and antiviral activity of ATV/co+F/TAF or DRV/co+F/TAF administered in HIV-1 infected treatment-experienced, virologically suppressed paediatric subjects. Only data from Cohort 2 receiving DRV/co + F/TAF is taken into consideration. A total of 9 participants were enrolled and received at least 1 dose of study drug. Further, study TMC114FD2HTX1006 was submitted to support the acceptability/swallowability of the scored DRV/COBI 675/150-mg FDC filmcoated tablet for children aged ≥ 6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg.

The proposed DRV dose of 675 mg is aligned with the approved DRV dose in combination with 100 mg rtv for patients weighing ≥30 to &lt;40 kg. COBI 150 mg qd showed to be generally safe and well tolerated throughout its development, and boosted DRV exposures to levels that were comparable to the boosting effect of co-administered rtv 100 mg. No clinical data has been generated in patients &lt;30kg, as patients with a body weight between 25 and 30 kg received 600mg DRV in Study 216 -0128 Cohort 2. The proposed DRV dose of 675 mg was extended down to a body weight of 25 kg supported by PopPK modelling for dosing weight-bands simplification and to align with the approved paediatric dose for COBI in Genvoya (E/C/F/TAF 150/150/200/10-mg FDC), ie, for use in patients weighing ≥ 25 kg. Based on the PopPK simulations, a DRV/COBI 675/150-mg qd dosing regimen for the weight-band ≥25 to &lt;40 kg in p aediatric patients would result in similar or slightly higher DRV AUC0-24h,ss and similar or slightly lower DRV C0h,ss compared to adults. The 200/10 mg F/TAF regimen is already approved for use in paediatric patients 6-12 years of age as part of the Genvoya fixed dose combination regimen. Overall, a D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10-mg qd dosing regimen for the weightband ≥25 to &lt;40 kg in paediatric patients could be acceptable. However, some concerns regarding higher exposures should be adequately addressed (see PK section).

The design of Study 216-0128 Cohort 2, and the in- and exclusion criteria are fit for purpose. As specific demonstration of antiviral efficacy in paediatric patients is not required for an EoI (rather, efficacy and safety data from adults and adolescents can be extrapolated to children provided that comparable exposures are reached), a non-randomised, non-comparative study can be accepted. The study is part of the agreed PIP.

The efficacy outcomes that have been generated are supportive of the use of Symtuza in HIV-1 infected, virologically suppressed paediatric subjects. All 9 participants in Cohort 2 remained virologically suppressed and none experienced virologic failure. The changes in CD4+ cell count are not clinically relevant and may have been due to chance/small sample size of the enrolled population.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Data generated in Study HTX1006 suggest that acceptability and swallowability of the tablet is acceptable for the target population, though results have not been presented by age group. Although not considered of consequence in this assessment, it should be noted that the participants enrolled in this study were all HIV-1 infected, ART-experienced patients, and do not represent treatmentnaïve paediatric patients who are also part of the proposed target population for the current EoI application.

## 2.6.6. Conclusions on clinical efficacy

The available efficacy data from Study 216-0128 Cohort 2 are supportive of the use of D/C/F/TAF in HIV-1infected, virologically suppressed paediatric patients aged ≥6 to &lt;12 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg. All 9 participants remained virologically suppr essed and no virologic failures were reported.

Taking into account the established efficacy of D/C/F/TAF in adults and adolescents, and provided that the PK assessment confirms no clinically relevant differences in exposure between the components of the D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10 mg FDC in adults/adolescents and the scored D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10 mg FDC in children, a similar efficacy profile is expected in the proposed paediatric population.

Overall, no efficacy concerns have been identified based on the data submitted in the current application

## 2.6.8. Discussion on clinical safety

In support of the current variation, safety data from Interim Analysis 3 of Study 216-0128 (Cohort 2) was submitted. Study 216-0128 is an ongoing open-label, multicenter, multicohort, two-part study (Part A and B) evaluating the PK, safety, efficacy, and antiviral activity of ATV/co +F/TAF or DRV/co +F/TAF administered in HIV-1 infected treatment-experienced, virologically suppressed paediatric subjects. A total of 9 participants were enrolled and received at least 1 dose of study drug. At the data cutoff date (30 January 2024), 3 (33.3%) participants had completed the main (48-week treatment) phase and had entered the extension phase. At the data cutoff date, all participants who had completed the main phase were continuing to receive study drug in the extension phase. The median (Q1, Q3) duration of exposure to DRV/COBI + F/TAF FDC in Cohort 2 was 45.1 (26.3; 131.1) weeks.

As such, exposure is too limited to reliably assess the safety profile in paediatric patients 6 to 12 years of age. Rather, safety is inferred based on similar exposures as observed in adults and adolescents.

The submitted clinical data did not raise concerns. The observed AEs were consistent with those expected in the study population as well as with those observed in the already approved population. No deaths, AEs leading to premature study drug discontinuation, SAEs, or Grade ≥ 3 AEs were reported. AEs considered related to study drug were reported in 3 participants (Grade 2 vomiting [1 participant]; Grade 1 vomiting [1 participant]; and Grade 1 headache [1 participant]). Vomiting and headache are already listed as adverse reactions in section 4.8 of the SmPC. Overall, no new ADRs were identified.

No AEs related to either bone or renal safety endpoints were observed in this small group of paediatric patients. Both spine and TBLH BMD increased relative to baseline through Week 120. Shifts in spine and TBLH height-age BMD Zscores from &gt;-2 at baseline to ≤-2 at some point postbaseline were observed in 1 participant. There was an initial small increase in serum creatinine, which stabilised with median values remaining within the normal range. A decrease from baseline in eGFRSchwartz was observed from Week 4 onwards, which did not lead to substantial adverse events or renal laboratory measurements out of reference range. No exposure-response relationship with F/TAF and bone or renal events was observed. However, the data available for this analysis was limited and no firm conclusions can be drawn. Of note, the same C/F/TAF dose has been approved for another HIV FDC (E/C/F/TAF) in paediatric patients weighing at least 25 kg, with similar exposures observed as for DRV/COBI + F/TAF.

## 2.6.9. Conclusions on clinical safety

Overall, no clinically relevant differences in PK exposures of the components of the D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10 mg FDC filmcoated tablet (in adults and in adolescents aged ≥12 to &lt;18 years and weighing ≥40 kg) and the components of the scored D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10 -mg FDC film-coated tablet (in children aged ≥6 to &lt;12 years and weighing ≥25 kg ) were observed, therefore a similar safety and efficacy profile is expected.

## 3. Benefit risk assessment

## 3.1. Therapeutic Context

## 3.1.1. Disease or condition

The most recent UNAIDS report estimates that of the 39.9 million people globally living with HIV in 2023, 1.4 million (4.4%) were children aged 0 to 14 years (UNAIDS 2023).

The current application is intended to support registration of a scored DRV/COBI 675/150-mg FDC film-coated oral tablet for once-daily use for use in HIV 1 infected children aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg, who are either ART naïve or ART -experienced.

The following wording is proposed by the MAH:

Symtuza is indicated, in combination with other antiretroviral medicinal products, for the treatment of human immunodeficiency virus-1 (HIV-1) infection in adults and paediatric patients (aged 6 years and older, weighing at least 25 kg).

Genotypic testing should guide the use of Symtuza (see sections 4.2, 4.4 and 5.1).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 3.1.2. Available therapies and unmet medical need

As for adults, HIV infection is a life-threatening condition in the paediatric population. Lifelong treatment is needed, and although treatment options have markedly improved the last decades, there remains a need to expand the available treatment options for children by a.o. additional ageappropriate formulations.

The recommended (collaborative Penta/EACS HIV treatment guideline, 2022) initial treatment of HIV-1 infection for paediatric patients is therapy with two NRTIs in combination with a drug from a different class (third agent). DTG is the preferred third agent in all children over 4 weeks of age and 3 kg. Whilst 'preferred options' are recommended, 'alternative options' are acceptable and remain important choices in settings where ART availability is limited or in individuals at particular risk of specific toxicity or DDIs.

The choice of HIV-1 therapy depends on various factors including the availability of age-appropriate formulation, ease of use, age/developmental stage of the patient, prior exposure (maternal or prevention), adherence, and in adolescent girls, the risk of pregnancy.

The goal of combination ART in paediatric patients is to achieve and sustain HIV-1 virologic suppression, preserve/restore immune function, minimize drug toxicity, prevent drug resistance, and ultimately lead to normal growth and neurocognitive development.

People living with HIV-1 who are aware of their status, take ART as prescribed, and get and keep an undetectable viral load can live healthy lives.

## 3.1.3. Main clinical studies

The proposed EoI is based on Study GS-US-216-0128 (hereafter referred to as 216-0128), which is an ongoing, Phase 2/3, multicenter, open-label, multicohort study evaluating PK, safety, and efficacy of COBI-boosted ATV, COBI-boosted DRV, and F/TAF in HIV-1 infected children and adolescents aged ≥4 weeks to &lt;18 years.

For this application, the PK, efficacy, and safety of the scored D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10 mg FDC film coated tablet in HIV1 infected children (aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg) are supported by data on COBI-boosted DRV and F/TAF FDC from Cohort 2 (N=9) of study 216-0128, combining DRV (dose based on body weight, once daily), COBI (150 mg, once daily), and F/TAF (200/25 mg tablet, once daily)  in HIV-1 infected, ARTexperienced, virologically suppressed children aged ≥6 to &lt;12 years and weighing ≥25 kg.

Further, Study TMC114FD2HTX1006 (also referred to as FD2HTX1006) investigated the acceptability/swallowability the FDC filmcoated tablet for use in children aged ≥6 to &lt;12 years using matching placebo tablets, which support the acceptability/swallowability of the scored DRV/COBI 675/150 mg FDC filmcoated tablet for children aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg.

## 3.2. Favourable effects

The efficacy of Symtuza has been established in adult and adolescent patients 12 years of age and older. The efficacy demonstration in children is based on a PK bridge, inferred through similar exposure as in adults.

Overall, the available PK data and PopPK modelling indicate no clinically relevant differences in PK exposures between the components of the D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10 mg FDC in children (aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg) and those of the D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10 mg FDC in adults and adole scents (aged ≥12 years and weighing ≥40 kg). Together with the demonstrated bioequivalence of the 675/150/200/10 mg FDC formulation to the individual components (Study FD2HTX1007), this supports the extrapolation of established clinical safety data from adults and adolescents to the younger paediatric population.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

All 9 participants in Cohort 2 remained virologically suppressed and non-experienced virologic failure.

The acceptability and swallowability of the film-coated tablet were considered adequate.

## 3.3. Uncertainties and limitations about favourable effects

A DRV dose of 675 mg is proposed for children aged ≥6 to &lt;12 years and weighing ≥25 kg . This dose is aligned with the approved DRV dose in combination with 100 mg rtv for patients weighing ≥ 30 to &lt;40 kg. The down extension to a body weight of 25 kg is supported by PopPK modelling, but no clinical data with the 675 mg DRV dose has been generated in patients &lt;30kg . However, three patients with a body weight between 25 and 30 kg received the 600 mg dose according to the protocol.

Data generated in Study HTX1006 suggest that acceptability and swallowability of the tablet is acceptable for the target population. Participants enrolled in this study were all HIV-1 infected, ARTexperienced patients, and do not represent treatmentnaïve paediatric patients who are also part of the proposed target population for the current EoI application.

## 3.4. Unfavourable effects

The safety profile of Symtuza is well-established in adults and adolescents, and given that exposure is comparable, it is not expected to be substantially different in a paediatric setting. The safety assessment of study 216-0128 Cohort 2 (n=9) did not give rise to new safety concerns.

AEs considered related to study drug were reported in 3 participants (Grade 2 vomiting [1 participant]; and Grade 1 vomiting [1 participant] and Grade 1 headache [1 participant]). Overall, no new ADRs were identified.

## 3.5. Uncertainties and limitations about unfavourable effects

The paediatric study programme is not powered to generate a comprehensive safety database in children and in the data currently presented, no control arm is available for comparison. Rather, exposure of the components are shown to be comparable to either adults, adolescents or licensed products for this age group. Safety will therefore be extrapolated which is in line with EMA guidance.

Exposure is too limited to reliably assess the safety profile in paediatric patients ≥3 years of age. Rather, safety is inferred based on similar exposures as observed in adults and adolescents.

The Applicant explained that, based on all available data, the observed differences in DRV/COBI exposure for children weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg in Study GS -US-216-0128 are not considered clinically relevant. This is based on the following rational:

- Population PK derived DRV exposures in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were within the range of those previously observed in adult and paediatric patients, especially with bid DRV dosing, and which were found to be safe and efficacious.
- COBI exposures in Cohort 3 of Study GS-US-216-0128 were in line with those previously observed in the E/C/F/TAF paediatric Study GS-US-292-0106 (Cohort 2 and Cohort 3).
- Based on this, COBI 90 mg qd (as part of the E/C/F/TAF FDC) was approved for the same paediatric population as is the basis of this submission, i.e. children weighing ≥ 15 to &lt;25 kg.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Throughout the development of DRV (boosted with either COBI or rtv), there have been no indications for an exposure-response relationship for any AEs.
- Similarly, COBI Phase 2 (GS US-216-0105) and Phase 3 (GS-US-216-0114) studies using population PK model-derived COBI exposures versus safety parameters revealed no relevant exposure-AE trends.
- Juvenile toxicity studies showed that the toxicological profile of DRV and COBI was comparable to that in adult rats.
- The higher F exposure is mainly driven by the lower body-weight, however, exposures overlap with those in adolescents and adults, and the 200 mg F dose is approved for use in adults and paediatric patients weighing at least 25 kg, in combination with other antiretrovirals.

The rational and conclusions of the Applicant can be followed. Although DRV AUC0-24h was higher in Cohort 3 vs other studies, the values were still in the same range as those included in the exposuresafety analysis which was submitted as part of the original MAA for Rezolsta. As there was no exposure-safety relation observed, it can be agreed that the increased exposures seen in paediatric patients &gt;=14 to &lt;25 kg are not likely to be clinically relevant. However, as it cannot be excluded that there are safety aspects associated with this higher exposure that are specific to the paediatric population, i.e. it is uncertain whether the exposure-safety relationship is the same for paediatrics and adults, the Applicant should further follow this post approval (via routine pharmacovigilance activities).

Similarly, COBI exposure (mainly Cmax and Ctrough) was higher in study 0128 Cohort 3 vs E/C/F/TAF Cohort 3. Although there is no direct concern based on the information provided by the Applicant and the absence of an exposure-safety correlation in adults, also here this may be different in paediatrics and close attention of any safety signals is warranted.

## 3.7. Benefit-risk assessment and discussion

## 3.7.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

The extrapolation of efficacy and safety from adults to paediatric patients is in accordance with regulatory practice in the field of HIV. The PD target is expected to respond in the same manner to similar plasma concentrations of the HIV antivirals in both adult and paediatric patients.

Overall, the available PK data and PopPK modeling, indicate that there are no clinically relevant differences in PK exposures of the components of the D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10-mg FDC in children (aged ≥6 years and weighing ≥25 to &lt;40 kg) compared to the components of the D/C/F/TAF 800/150/200/10-mg FDC i n adults and adolescents (aged ≥12 years and weighing ≥40 kg), together with the bioequivalence of the D/C/F/TAF 675/150/200/10-mg FDC formulation versus single components (Study FD2HTX1007), allow for the extrapolation of the established clinical safety data from adults and adolescents to the younger paediatric population. The safety profile is well-established in adults and adolescents. The safety assessment of study 216-0128 Cohort 2 is limited but does not give rise to new safety concerns. No cases of virologic failure have been seen. The development of resistance mutations is important, as these may also have an impact on future ARV treatment options. Moreover, as the target population are growing children, potential effects on growth or (pubertal) development are also of importance.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Of note, the same C/F/TAF dose has been approved for another HIV FDC (E/C/F/TAF) in paediatric patients weighing at least 25 kg, with similar exposures observed as for DRV/COBI + F/TAF. Similarly, the DRV dose is aligned with the approved DRV dose in combination with 100 mg rtv.

## 3.7.2. Balance of benefits and risks

The overall data presented support the efficacy and safety for an extension of the therapeutic indication of Symtuza.
