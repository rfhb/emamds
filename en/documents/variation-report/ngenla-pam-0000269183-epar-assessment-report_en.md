## 1. Introduction

On 30 April 2025, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study, Study CP-4-004 (C0311006) LTOLE, for Ngenla, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

Study CP-4-004 was part of the MAH's clinical development program for MOD-4023 (somatrogon).

Study CP-4-004 was a Phase 2, open label, randomized, multi-centre, dose finding, and safety study of different somatrogon dose levels in pre-pubertal growth hormone deficient (GHD) pediatric subjects with yearly extension until marketing approval. The study consisted of a 6-week screening period, 2 active-controlled treatment periods, referred to as the main study, and 3 OLE periods. The main study (Periods I and II) was completed at the time of the initial marketing authorisation application (MAA) and the results were presented in the primary CSR, dated 15 May 2017. Although the LT-OLE study was on-going at the time of the initial MAA, data from Periods III, IV, and the earlier part of Period V (data cut-off of 21 st  December 2020) were evaluated as part of that procedure.

The MAH is submitting the final CSR for the long-term exposure data for Study CP-4-004 LT-OLE in this procedure. This CSR summarises long-term exposure data for the OLE Study, Period III through Period V.

The MAH states that Study CP-4-004 is a standalone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

In Study CP-4-004, somatrogon was provided in single use vials as a solution for injection containing 20 or 40 mg protein/ml. In the LT-OLE-PEN period (Period V), somatrogon was provided as a solution for injection in 20 or 50 mg/mL strengths for a single patient use, multi-dose, disposable pre-filled PEN.

## Methods

This was a Phase 2, open label, randomized, multi-centre, dose finding, and safety study of different somatrogon dose levels in pre-pubertal GHD pediatric subjects with yearly extension until marketing approval. The study consisted of a 6-week screening period, 2 active-controlled treatment periods, and 3 OLE periods as presented in Figure 1 and described below.

Figure 1. Study Diagram for CP-4-004 OLE and PEN Periods

<!-- image -->

## SOURCE: CSR04-004-OLE

The main study (Periods I and II) assessed treatment with 3 dose levels of somatrogon (0.25, 0.48, or 0.66 mg/kg/week) administered weekly via subcutaneous (SC) injection compared to the active control, daily SC injection of Genotropin (0.034 mg/kg/day) for up to 12 months.

Patients who completed 12 months of treatment in the main study period, remained eligible for inclusion in the study, and consented to participate in the OLE study continued with open label treatment until marketing approval. There were 3 defined OLE study periods:

- OLE period (Period III [Year 1 OLE]): This period lasted for 12 months post-completion of the main study. Patients continued dosing with the 3 originally assigned dose levels of somatrogon (0.25, 0.48, and 0.66 mg/kg/week). Patients originally assigned to daily Genotropin in the main study were randomly re-assigned to 1 of the 3 somatrogon dose levels.
- LT-OLE period (Period IV [Years 2-4 OLE]): This LT-OLE period was planned to follow the 12 months in Period III (ie, to start from second year of OLE and third year of the overall study). All eligible patients were transitioned to receive somatrogon at a dose of 0.66 mg/kg/week.
- LT-OLE-PEN period (Period V [PEN Years 1-5]): Patients were transitioned to somatrogon 0.66 mg/kg/week SC administration using a single patient, multi-dose, disposable pre-filled PEN device and formulation. The Period V (PEN) duration was until marketing approval.

## Study participants

Patients who completed 12 months of treatment in the main study were eligible to continue treatment with somatrogon in the 12-month OLE period (Period III), and subsequent LT-OLE period (Period IV) and PEN period (Period V) until marketing approval in their country.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Treatments

During the OLE (Period III) and LT-OLE period (Period IV), somatrogon was provided in single use vials as a solution for SC injection via a needle and syringe containing 20 or 40 mg/mL somatrogon.

During the PEN period (Period V), somatrogon was provided as a refrigerated solution for SC injection containing 20 or 50 mg/mL somatrogon in a multi-dose, single subject use PEN.

In all study periods, somatrogon was administered preferably in the morning hours once weekly, using the needle and syringe (or PEN during Period V) into the upper arms, buttocks, thighs, or abdomen (8 locations). It was recommended that all 8 injection sites were used successively, using a different injection site at each subsequent injection.

## Objective(s)

Safety objective: The primary objective of this analysis was to evaluate the safety of somatrogon in extended treatment after the Periods I and II (main study), including the transition to PEN (Periods IIIV).

Growth objective: The secondary objective was to evaluate the growth outcome with treatment with somatrogon in long-term treatment beyond the initial 12 months of the main study, including the transition to PEN.

## Outcomes/endpoints

Safety Endpoints:

- Incidence of Adverse Events (AEs)
- Incidence of anti-drug antibody (ADA) formation (including characterization of the Abs and neutralizing properties)
- Local injection site assessment
- Insulin-like growth factor (IGF-1) levels and IGF-1 standard deviation score (SDS)
- Insulin-like growth factor-binding protein-3 (IGFBP-3) levels
- Parameters of glucose metabolism: blood glucose, fasting insulin level, HbA1c
- Thyroid status (FT4, T3, and TSH)
- Lipid parameters
- Cortisol levels
- All other haematology and biochemical parameters
- Physical examination
- Vital signs
- ECG

Growth Endpoints:

- Annual height velocity (cm/year)
- Change in height SDS

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Annual bone maturation

## Sample size

A sample size of 12 patients per treatment cohort (in Period I of the main study) was planned to be chosen in this pilot investigation (14 patients were planned to be enrolled to get 12 per protocol evaluable patients in each treatment cohort).

A total of 48 patients entered Period III (OLE Year 1).

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

In Period I of the main study, eligible patients were to be randomised in a 1:1:1:1 ratio to one of three somatrogon cohorts (0.25, 0.48, or 0.66 mg/kg/week), or to a somatropin cohort (0.034 mg/kg/day). Eligible subjects were kept on the originally allocated dose/dose level in Period II. Entering the OLE period (Period III), eligible subjects originally assigned to daily somatropin in the main study were randomly re-assigned to 1 of the 3 somatrogon dose levels. Entering the LT-OLE period (Period IV), a ll eligible subjects were transitioned to receive somatrogon at a dose of 0.66 mg/kg/week.

This was an open-label study throughout.

## Statistical Methods

This was an open label long-term extension study. No formal hypothesis testing was performed. Descriptive statistics, with mean, standard deviation, range, count and confidence intervals were used to present the results.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

In accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, the MAH has submitted the final CSR for Study CP-4-004 (C0311006). Data from this phase 2, open label, randomized, multi-centre, dose finding, and safety study were submitted in support of the initial MAA for somatrogon. Although the LT- OLE study was on-going at the time of the initial MAA, safety and efficacy data from Periods III, IV, and Period V (data cut-off of 21 st  December 2020) were evaluated as part of that procedure. This submission provided the final summary of long-term exposure data for the completed OLE Study. The MAH states that Study CP-4-004 is a standalone study.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The primary objective of Study CP-4-004 LT-OLE was to provide long-term safety data on somatrogon use as eligible patients transitioned from the use of single use vials for SC injection (in OLE Periods IIIIV; Years 1-4) to the single-patient multi-dose disposable pre-filled PEN device (in OLE Period VI; PEN Year 1-5), the currently authorised presentation. Growth outcomes were evaluated as secondary objectives.

The numbers enrolled in the study were low, with 48 subjects entering Period III, the first OLE phase of the trial (OLE Year 1), 40 subjects continuing to the first year of Period V (PEN Year 1), and 21 subjects continuing to PEN Year 5. Overall, completion rates for each year of the OLE were high, with year-to-year retention of subjects ranging from 80.8% to 97.7% throughout the treatment periods. The incidence of withdrawal from the study due to an Adverse Event was low throughout the study.

## Efficacy

From an efficacy perspective, subjects treated in CP-4-004 OLE (vial or PEN) demonstrated sustained improvement in clinical parameters of growth including annualized HV, change in height SDS and height SDS. Height SDS showed a trend towards zero during the OLE study (Periods III - V), indicating that mean height for age and gender was achieved following treatment with somatrogon.

Mean annualized HV at the end of Period III (OLE Year 1) was similar for the 0.25 mg/kg/week and 0.48 mg/kg/week somatrogon treatment groups but was higher in the 0.66 mg/kg/week somatrogon treatment group. These results were consistent with results reported for the main study in the CP-4004 primary CSR. Mean annualized HV at the end of Periods IV to V (OLE Years 2-3 and PEN Years 15) showed sustained growth response in treated subjects, which was independent of initial cohort assignment. This included subjects initially randomized to somatropin treatment during the main study. The mean annualized HV after transition to the PEN device remained similar.

The mean change in height SDS and height SDS at the end of Period III (OLE Year 1) were similar across treatment groups. These results were consistent with results reported at 12 months in the main study.

Progressive gains in change in height SDS and height SDS were observed at the end of Periods IV to V (OLE Years 2-3 and PEN Years 1-5) and were independent of initial cohort assignment. The mean height SDS and change in height SDS after transition to using the PEN device remained similar.

Similar increases in mean annual bone maturation were observed across all treatment groups in Period III (OLE Year 1) and in Periods IV to V (OLE Years 2-3 and PEN Years 1-5). Bone maturation with weekly somatrogon did not advance discordantly relative to advancement in chronological age.

No clinically meaningful differences were observed in annualised HV and other growth parameters between ADA-positive and ADA-negative patients throughout Periods III to V.

## Safety

In Study CP-4-004, 40 patients were exposed to weekly somatrogon treatments (vial or PEN) for longer than 60 months, with over 50% of all study participants (n=27) exposed to weekly treatments (vial or PEN) for longer than 90 months. Of those participants who entered Period V (PEN), 50% (n=20) were exposed to greater than 48 months of weekly SC injections with the PEN device.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Anomalies with respect to safety reporting for Study CP-4-004-LT-OLE were observed between data presented in the final CSR and those summarized in the clinical overview submitted in support of this procedure. These include discrepancies in the overall incidence of any TEAE [42 subjects (87.5%) versus 39 patients (81.3%) reporting any TEAE] and SAE [4 unique subjects (8.3%) versus 3 subjects (6.3%) reporting any SAE] reported in the final CSR and clinical overview, respectively. The MAH is requested to clarify these discrepancies and confirm that all data presented in the clinical overview are in accordance with source data and correct. Any revision to the clinical overview should be submitted in tracked format for review. (OC)

Notwithstanding the discrepancies above, the overall incidence and types of TEAEs and treatmentrelated TEAEs were broadly consistent with results reported for the main study in the primary CSR. Whilst the highest incidence of any reported TEAE [23 subjects (57.5%)] was observed in PEN Year 1, further analysis of the annual incidence of treatment-related TEAEs by SOC and PT does not give rise to concerns that the higher incidence was associated with the transition from vial to PEN use.

Most reported TEAEs were mild or moderate in intensity, and most were considered unrelated to study treatment. The most commonly reported TEAEs, considered by the investigator to be possibly or probably treatment-related, were reported under the SOC Musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders with Scoliosis being the most commonly reported by PT (3 subjects reported 4 events). One event of Scoliosis resulted in study withdrawal. These AEs were reported after long-term treatment with somatrogon (from OLE Year 3). Scoliosis is most commonly diagnosed in adolescence and may have been exacerbated during rapid bone growth with GH treatment. Precautionary wording pertaining to Scoliosis is included in section 4.4 of the SmPC.

There were no deaths reported in Study CP-4-004 and the incidence of SAEs was low, with no notable trends or concerns raised by SAE-reporting in Periods III through V. One event of Schwannoma was reported under Neoplasms benign, malignant and unspecified. It resulted in patient withdrawal from the study and was not considered treatment-related by the investigator. No new concerns were raised with respect to the reporting of TEAEs of special interest. No new safety concerns were identified.

From the evaluation of ISR data, no safety concerns were identified for patients transitioning from vial to PEN use. Both presentations appeared to be well tolerated.

Clinical laboratory evaluation results were similar to results reported for the main study. More specifically, measurements of glucose metabolism, thyroid status, cortisol levels, lipid parameters, and haematology/chemistry remained within normal limits. Although the observed mean blood glucose values and HbA1c remained within normal limits, mean blood glucose values did tend to increase during the PEN phase. Observed mean IGF-1 SDS values also increased over the duration of the study but remained within normal range. The proportion of subjects with IGF-1 SDS values &gt;2 and IGF-1 SDS values &gt;2 that led to dose reductions tended to increase with time and were observed to be highest in Period V. Concerns pertaining to the long-term safety implications of increased IGF-1 levels with long-term somatrogon exposure, including the potential for neoplasia (benign and malignant) and impaired glucose tolerance, are under active surveillance as part of the non-interventional Post Authorisation Safety Study (PASS), ' An Active Surveillance Study to Monitor the Real-World Long-Term Safety of Somatrogon Among Paediatric Patients in Europe', which was imposed as an Annex IID condition to the marketing authorisation for Ngenla.

Immunogenicity data did not give rise to any new concerns. The percentage of subjects that tested positive for ADA in the OLE study (Periods III - V) was 39.6%, of which most were also positive for hGH. NAbs were not detected in any subject across Periods III to V. Development of ADAs did not appear to affect IGF-1 response or reported AEs.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Overall, the submission of final data for this LT-OLE study did not give rise to any new concerns with respect to the long-term safety and efficacy of somatrogon in the treatment of children and adolescents from 3 years of age with growth disturbance due to insufficient secretion of growth hormone. Although numbers enrolled in the study were small, somatrogon, administered weekly SC, either via the single-use vial or pre-filled PEN presentation, appeared to be well tolerated and sustained improvement in clinical parameters of growth was evident.

Longer-term safety (incidence rates of neoplasm, diabetes type 2, immunogenicity, medication errors) is subject to ongoing evaluation as part of the non-interventional PASS. The evaluation of long-term efficacy (by measuring IGF-1 levels and height in paediatric patients treated with somatrogon) is listed as a secondary objective of this PASS for which the first interim study report, dated 04 November 2024, has been reviewed by PRAC (Reference: EMEA/H/C/005633/MEA/001.3).

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

Based on data presented in the final CSR, no new safety or efficacy concerns have been identified from the completion of Study CP-04-004-LT-OLE. In response to RSI (as per Section 4 below), the MAH provided a satisfactory clarification of discrepancies noted in the submitted dataset.

The CHMP considers that the benefit-risk profile of Ngenla in the treatment of children and adolescents from 3 years of age with growth disturbance due to insufficient secretion of growth hormone remains positive.

The MAH has not proposed any updates to product information. The CHMP agrees that no updates are required based on the data submitted in this procedure.
