## Description

Inclisiran was first approved in the EU on 09-Dec-2020 under the proprietary name of Leqvio. The currently approved EU indication is:

Leqvio is indicated in adults with primary hypercholesterolemia (heterozygous familial and nonfamilial) or mixed dyslipidemia, as an adjunct to diet:

- in combination with a statin or statin with other lipid-lowering therapies in patients unable to reach LDL-C goals with the maximum tolerated dose of a statin, or
- alone or in combination with other lipid-lowering therapies in patients who are statin-intolerant, or for whom a statin is contraindicated.

Study ORION-16 evaluated safety, tolerability, and efficacy of inclisiran in adolescents from 12 to less than 18 years of age with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) and elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C). It is part of the inclisiran paediatric program as agreed with European Medicines Agency (EMA) in the paediatric investigation plan (PIP; EMEA-002214-PIP01-17-M02, P/0322/2023) and intended to support the registration of inclisiran in adolescents with HeFH. Study ORION-16 also included pharmacokinetic (PK) and pharmacogenetic assessments; PK assessment was based on sparse PK sampling (i.e. 3 samples at the Day 1 visit; pre-dose immediately before the first administration of study drug, 4-, and 24-hours after first dose).

The study was conducted in two-parts, a double-blind, inclisiran versus placebo part (Year 1) followed by an open-label inclisiran part (Year 2). It was a randomised, multicentre Phase III study in adolescents aged 12 to &lt;18 years with HeFH and elevated LDL-C (&gt;130 mg/dL; 3.4 mmol/L) on stable, individualised, optimal standard of care (SoC) background lipid-lowering therapy including maximally tolerated statin treatment, at the Investigator's discretion.

According to the data provided, study ORION-16 was conducted in compliance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP). The study design is summarised in the following table.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Table 1: Summary of study design

The two-part, randomised, multicentre, international Phase III study had two sequential parts:

- Part 1/Year 1: 12 months double-blind, parallel group period, in which participants were randomised in a 2:1 ratio to receive either inclisiran sodium 300 mg s.c. or placebo. The primary endpoint was assessed at Day 330.
- Part 2 / Year 2: 12 months single-arm, open-label follow-up period, with all participants receiving inclisiran sodium 300 mg s.c.

## Assessor's comment

The completed paediatric study Orion-16 (CKJX839C12301) is part of post-authorisation measures for Leqvio (inclisiran) and was intended to investigate the use of inclisiran in adolescents from 12 to less than 18 years of age with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) and elevated LDLcholesterol. ## Methods

## Study participants

The study population consisted of adolescents (male or female; 12 to &lt;18 years) with a genetic or clinical diagnosis of HeFH and LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L) and being on a maximally tolerated dose of statin (at investigator's discretion) with or without other lipid lowering therapy (e.g., ezetimibe).

## Key inclusion criteria were:

- HeFH diagnosed either by genetic testing or on phenotypic criteria (according to the EAS Consensus Panel),
- fasting LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L) at screening,

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- on maximally tolerated dose of statin (Investigator's discretion) with or without other lipidlowering therapy (e.g. ezetimibe),
- participants on lipid-lowering therapies (such as statin and/or e.g. ezetimibe) should have been on a stable dose for ≥ 30 days before screening with no planned medication or dose changes during study participation,
- fasting triglycerides &lt;400 mg/dL (4.5 mmol/L) at screening, and
- eGFR &gt;30 mL/min/1.73 m2 at screening.

Key exclusion criteria were:

- previous treatment (within 90 days of screening) with monoclonal antibodies directed towards PCSK9,
- active liver disease or unexplained, confirmed ALT, AST elevation &gt;3x ULN, or total bilirubin elevation &gt;2x ULN (except patients with Gilbert's syndrome),
- pregnant or nursing females,
- HoFH,
- recent and/or planned use of other investigational medicinal products or devices,
- secondary hypercholesterolemia, e.g. hypothyroidism or nephrotic syndrome, and
- major adverse cardiovascular events within 3 months prior to randomization.

## Assessor's comment

The study included male and female adolescents from 12 to less than 18 years of age with a genetic or clinical diagnosis of HeFH and an LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL who were on a maximally tolerated dose of an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) with or without other lipid lowering therapy (excluding PCSK9 inhibitors).

Patients with homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) or secondary hypercholesterolemia were excluded.

For the primary efficacy assessment reflexive LDL-C was used for analysis, i.e., Friedewald formula to calculate LDL-C levels in a first step, and in those cases where the formula is not valid (i.e., triglycerides &gt;400 mg/dL, calculated LDL &lt;40 mg/dL), in a second step, ultracentrifugation to measure LDL-C directly.

Participants were in a fasted state for all efficacy laboratory assessments. From randomisation (Day 1) until Day 360, the results were blinded to participants, investigator staff, persons performing the assessments, and the clinical trial team, except for bioanalytical analysis related to PK which was conducted in an unblinded manner (see Randomisation and blinding (masking) page 18 below).

In addition, carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT) was measured locally by ultrasonography and was exploratory / hypothesis-generating in nature.

All participants were invited to optional pharmacogenetic analyses of the underlying causal mutation(s) of their HeFH. Samples were analysed for potential mutations in the LDL receptor (LDLR), ApoB, PCSK9, and LDLR adaptor protein 1 (LDLRAP1) genes.

The key safety assessments consisted of monitoring and recording of all adverse events (AEs), laboratory evaluations (haematology, biochemistry, urinalysis, pregnancy tests), anti-drug antibody (ADA) measurement, vital signs, growth (height, weight, body mass index [BMI]), and pubertal development (sex hormones, Tanner staging). Other safety evaluations were physical examinations, neurological evaluations, and electrocardiograms (ECGs).

## Assessor's comment

The primary endpoint was the percentage change in LDL-C from baseline to the end of the double-blind treatment period at day 330 (year 1).

Secondary endpoints included absolute change in LDL-C and other lipid parameters during Part 1 as well as assessments from baseline to the end of the open-label Part 2, i.e. day 720.

Exploratory endpoints included individual responsiveness of participants, changes in LDL-C by type of underlying causal mutation, change in carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT), and evaluation of inclisiran pharmacokinetics (PK) based on sparse data.

The endpoints are considered to be in line with previous adult inclisiran studies.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Part 1 of the study was double-blind and placebo-controlled. Participants, investigator staff, persons performing the assessments, and the clinical trial team were blinded to the identity of the treatment from the time of randomisation until at least database lock for all Part 1 (year 1) data. Exceptions were the independent statistician and programmer to prepare interim analysis reports for the DMC, and the PK analyst to avoid unnecessary analysis of placebo samples; in addition, a PK interim analysis (IA) team had access to unblinded individual-level PK/baseline data in order to update a PK extrapolation to paediatrics before the Part 1 database lock to support early planning of a dose selection strategy for paediatric studies in younger children. Part 2 of the study had a single-arm, open-label design.

After baseline visit (Day 1), participants were randomised by interactive response technology in a 2:1 ratio to receive double-blind s.c. injections of inclisiran sodium 300 mg or placebo in Part 1 (year 1) of the study (days 1, 90, 270). On day 360 (\"switch day\"), participants randomised to placebo in Part 1 received inclisiran and participants randomised to inclisiran in Part 1 received placebo. This dose remained blinded in order to maintain the blind for Part 1 of the study. Subsequently, all participants received open-label s.c. injections of inclisiran sodium 300 mg in Part 2 (year 2).

Investigational and control drugs were supplied by the MAH as single-use pre-filled syringes for s.c. injection. ## Statistical Methods

Efficacy and safety analyses of Part 1 data were performed after all participants completed the Day 360 visit or discontinued prior to the Day 360 visit; analyses of Part 2 data were performed after all participants completed the Day 720 (EoS) visit or discontinued prior to the Day 720 visit. In cases where the dosing time at Day 360 did not allow to determine whether data belonged to Part 1 or 2, the data were considered to belong to Part 1.

The following data sets were analysed. The Screened Set (SCR) included all participants who provided study informed consent. The Randomised Set (RAN) consisted of all participants who received a randomisation number, regardless of whether double-blind treatment was received or not. The Full Analysis Set (FAS) consisted of all randomised participants with the exception of those mis-randomised participants who did not receive study drug; mis-randomised participants were defined as those who were not qualified for randomization but were inadvertently randomised into the study. According to the intent to treat principle, participants were analysed according to the treatment they were assigned to during the randomisation procedure. The FAS was used in analyses for the primary, secondary, and exploratory efficacy objectives in Part 1 of the study. The Safety Set (SAF) included all participants who received at least 1 dose of study treatment. Participants were analysed according to the study treatment received. This was the primary population for the safety analyses in Part 1. The SAF allowed the inclusion of nonrandomised participants who received double-blind treatment in error. The PK analysis Set included all randomised participants who had at least 1 PK measurement. Participants were analysed according to the treatment they received. This was the primary population for the PK analyses. The Open-label Phase Analysis Set (OAS) included all participants who entered Part 2 of the study and received at least 1 dose of study treatment in Part 2. The OAS was used to analyse data from participants who entered Part 2. For the primary endpoint percentage change in LDL-C from pre-dose baseline measurement to Day 330, the primary null hypothesis (H0) was the difference (inclisiran minus placebo) in the LS mean percentage change from baseline to the Day 330 visit in LDL-C= 0 and the alternative hypothesis (Ha) the difference in the LS mean percentage change from baseline to the Day 330 visit in LDL-C &lt; 0. The primary objective would be achieved if the null hypothesis is rejected at a significance level of 0.025 (one-sided test). The primary endpoint was analysed using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model, in which the response variable was the LDL-C percentage change from baseline to Day 330. The model included the fixed effects of treatment group and baseline age group (≥12-&lt;15 or ≥15-&lt;18 years of age), with baseline LDL-C as a covariate. No remaining intercurrent events were expected. Multiple imputation was used to impute missing data based on a washout model which is a modified controlbased pattern-mixture-model. The wash-out model imputed the missing values for participants in the inclisiran group with data from participants with similar background characteristics in the placebo group, under the assumption of missing not at random (MNAR) unless the participants with missing values completed the first 3 doses and had data available after Day 330. For participants who completed the first 3 doses of inclisiran and had data available after Day 330, and participants in the placebo group (regardless of how many doses taken), the missing values were imputed under the assumption of missing at random (MAR). A total of 100 imputed datasets were created. The ANCOVA model was fit to each imputed dataset, and results were combined using Rubin's combination rules. For sensitivity analyses of the primary endpoint a linear mixed-effect model for repeated measures (MMRM) analysis without multiple imputation (under an implicit assumption of MAR), a MMRM analysis on the imputed datasets, and multiple imputation will be based on a control-based pattern mixture model (under the assumption of MNAR), and an ANCOVA using the washout model including the following fixed effects: baseline concomitant lipid-modifying therapies, baseline age group, region, and a treatment-by region interaction were performed. Supplementary analyses included LDL-C percent change from baseline to Day 330 by subgroups gender, age group (≥12 to &lt;15, ≥15 to &lt;18 years of age), race, ethnicity, BMI, region, baseline statin use, baseline LDL-C, and baseline PCSK9. The MMRM model was used to evaluate the difference between inclisiran and placebo groups in each subgroup. The key secondary endpoints time-adjusted percentage change in LDL-C from baseline after Day 90 and up to Day 330 (year 1), defined as the average of percent changes from baseline to Days 150, 270, and 330 and absolute change in LDL-C and percentage change in Apo B, Lp(a), non-HDL-C, and total cholesterol from baseline to Day 330 (year 1) were analysed using the same ANCOVA with multiple imputation using wash-out model, except the time-adjusted percent change in LDL-C from baseline after Day 90 and up to Day 330; the latter was analysed using a MMRM model with a controlbased pattern mixture model for missing value imputation. A hierarchical testing strategy was applied to control the family-wise type I error rate at the one-sided significance level of alpha = 0.025 to assess the superiority of inclisiran over placebo; hypotheses were tested in the order listed above. For sensitivity analysis a MMRM analysis without multiple imputation that assumed missing data are MAR was performed and MMRM analysis using control-based pattern mixture model for missing value imputation was performed for other key secondary endpoints. Supplementary analyses of the key secondary endpoints were performed in subgroups as specified for the primary analysis. The MMRM model (using control-based pattern mixture model for missing value imputation) was used to evaluate the difference between inclisiran and placebo groups in each subgroup. Another supplementary analysis for Lp(a) was applied in log-transformed scale using the same MMRM without multiple imputation specified above. Other secondary efficacy endpoints included the percent change and absolute change from baseline throughout the time course up to Day 720 (year 2) of LDL-C, Apo B, Lp(a), non-HDL-C, total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL-C, VLDL-C, Apo A1, and PCSK9. Descriptive summaries by treatment group were provided for these endpoints. In addition, reductions of ≥20% and ≥30% LDL-C from baseline were summarised at Day 720 (year 2) as supportive analyses. Furthermore, values of cIMT were summasrised at each time point by treatment group. Change in cIMT from baseline to Day 330 (year 1) and Day 720 (year 2) were summarised by treatment group. As regards PK analyses, inclisiran plasma concentration data were listed by treatment, participant, and sampling time point. ## Assessor's comment

Overall, interpretation of the provided data is limited by the low number of participants included in study Orion 16 (141 participants, 93 inclisiran, 48 placebo) as well as by the open-label uncontrolled design of Part 2.

Study drug exposure was comparable between groups in both periods of study ORION-16; over the entire study duration, 97.9% (138/141) of participants were administered all 6 scheduled doses of study drug.

The safety data of inclisiran in adolescents with HeFH gathered from both parts of the study are considered to be in line with the known safety profile in adult patients and no new or unexpected safety signals have been observed. No participant died and the percentage of participants experiencing serious AEs was low and balanced between groups. Only 1 participant in Part 1 in the inclisiran group discontinued study drug due to a mild AEs of vertigo and nausea. Study-drug related AEs were primarily injection site reactions, an identified ADR of inclisiran. In the double-blind, controlled Part 1, musculoskeletal events and hypersensitivity events were balanced between the inclisiran and the placebo group; hepatic AEs were slight, transient elevations in liver function tests (LFTs) and 1 case of mild hepatic steatosis. In the open-label Part 2, there were also 2 hepatic AEs of slight, transient elevations in LFTs and 1 participant with a family history of diabetes was diagnosed with type 1 diabetes.

There were no clinically meaningful changes or between-group differences in safety laboratory parameters of haematology or biochemistry in Part 1 and there were also no clinically meaningful changes in safety laboratory parameters of haematology or biochemistry during Part 2.

No adverse effects regarding growth, pubertal development, or hormonal status were seen with inclisiran treatment in either period of the study.

No participants developed anti-drug antibodies during Study Part 1. During Part 2, 8/139 participants (5.8%) showed a positive ADA response (5.5% inclisiran-inclisiran, 6.3% placebo-inclisiran). However, conclusions on immunogenicity are considered preliminary at present as relevant documentation has not been submitted: As mentioned in the method section, partial method validation report 3002412 (DMPK R2100159-ig-01) claimed to cover also the cross-validation of the ADA method used for Study Part 2 to the ADA method used for Study Part 1 still needs to be provided (RSI).

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

Inclisiran was first approved in the EU on 09 December 2020 under the proprietary name of Leqvio. Currently, inclisiran is only licensed in adults, including patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HeFH) from 18 years of age onwards.

Study ORION-16 (CKJX839C12301) evaluated efficacy and safety of inclisiran in adolescents from 12 to less than 18 years of age with HeFH. It is part of the inclisiran paediatric program (EMEA-002214PIP01-17-M02, P/0322/2023) and intended to support the registration of inclisiran in adolescents with HeFH.

The randomised, multicentre Phase III study was conducted in two-parts; Part 1 (year 1) investigated inclisiran versus placebo in a double-blind setting, while in Part 2 (year2) all participants received open-label inclisiran. The study included male and female adolescents ≥12 to &lt;18 years of age with a genetic or clinical diagnosis of HeFH and an LDL-C &gt;130 mg/dL and who were on a maximally tolerated dose of an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin). Patients with homozygous familial (HoFH) or secondary hypercholesterolaemia were excluded.

Study ORION-16 included 141 participants and 139 of them completed Part 1 and Part 2. The mean age was 15.1 years with a range of 12 to 17 years. The majority of participants had a genetic diagnosis of HeFH.

Study ORION-16 met the primary endpoint of superiority of inclisiran compared to placebo in reducing LDL-C in adolescents with HeFH on maximally tolerated statin treatment. The between-group difference (95% CI) of -28.54% (-35.81, -21.27; p&lt;0.0001) between inclisiran and placebo is considered clinically relevant. Results of sensitivity and subgroup analyses were consistent with the primary analysis.

Furthermore, in Part 1 relevant reductions were seen for key secondary efficacy endpoints (timeadjusted percent change in LDL-C from baseline after Day 90 up to Day 330 between-group difference mean percent change -29.30% [-36.24, -22.36; p&lt;0.0001]; absolute change in LDL-C, percent change in Apo B, non-HDL-C, total cholesterol all p&lt;0.0001). Results for Lp(a)were not statistically significant. Results of pre-specified sensitivity and subgroup analyses were consistent with the primary analysis and during Part 2, consistent reductions in LDL-C were seen. For the exploratory endpoints concerning individual responsiveness of participants, relevant effects of inclisiran versus placebo were seen in the percentage of participants reaching an LDL-C &lt;130 mg/dL and &lt;100 mg/dL as well as a ≥50% reduction in LDL-C. Consistent LDL-C lowering was seen with inclisiran across the included genetic variant groups in Part 1. No clinically relevant effect of inclisiran on carotid intima media thickness (cIMT) was seen. The study included an evaluation of inclisiran pharmacokinetics (PK) based on sparse data and of changes in LDL-C by type of underlying causal mutation as exploratory endpoints. No final conclusions regarding plasma concentrations / PK are possible at present stage. This pertains to the fact that relevant documentation such as reports on method validation, cross validation as well as population PK were not submitted so far and are requested (see below). The safety data for inclisiran in adolescents with HeFH gathered from both parts of study Orion 16 are considered to be in line with the known safety profile in adult patients and no new or unexpected safety signals have been observed. No participant died and the percentage of participants experiencing serious AEs was low and balanced between groups. Study-drug related AEs were primarily injection site reactions, an identified ADR of inclisiran. No adverse effects regarding growth, pubertal development, or hormonal status were seen with inclisiran treatment in either period of the study. Conclusions on immunogenicity (ADA measurements) are considered preliminary at present. As mentioned in the method section, partial method validation report 3002412 (DMPK R2100159-ig-01) claimed to cover also the cross-validation of the ADA method used for Study Part 2 to the ADA method used for Study Part 1 could not be located and needs to be provided (see below). In summary, although effects on LDL-C appear to be somewhat less pronounced than in studies in the adult population, efficacy results in adolescents ≥12 to &lt;18 years of age with HeFH are considered to be in line with previous results in the adult population; data for a direct comparison are not available. The data indicate a consistent and clinically relevant reduction in LDL-C with inclisiran, including sustained efficacy over 2 years of treatment. The LDL-C lowering effect with inclisiran was accompanied by relevant improvements in other lipid parameters. The safety results are considered to be in line with the known safety profile in the adult population with no new or unexpected safety signals and no adverse effects regarding growth, pubertal development, or hormonal status observed. However, interpretation of the provided data is limited by the low number of participants included in study Orion 16 (141 participants, 93 inclisiran, 48 placebo) as well as by the open-label uncontrolled design of Part 2. It was stated in the dossier (eCTD sequence: 0058) that the MAH intends to submit a type II variation application to support the expansion of the indication to paediatric patients aged 12 to less than 18 years with HeFH and the 'Post Authorisation Measure Submission Form' (Appendix 1) states that \"If the MAH has identified amendments to be introduced to the Product Information as a result of the P46 Study, a variation (e.g. category C.1.4 or C.1.6) should be submitted containing said study. Indeed, the MAH has in the meantime submitted a grouping of two Type II variation C.I.6.a - Addition of new therapeutic indication (eCTD sequence: 0061). Therefore, the request for supplementary information (RSI) as raised in the preliminary p46 AR and included in the following will be pursued and assessed as part of this grouped variation procedure (see section 3 below).
