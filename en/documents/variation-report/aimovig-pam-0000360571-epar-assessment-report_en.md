## Pharmacokinetics results

Erenumab was administered once monthly at doses of 35, 70, or 140 mg to participants with CM in children (6 to &lt; 12 years) and adolescents (12 to &lt; 18 years). The PK analysis dataset included 1566 serum erenumab samples collected from 274 participants (N = 128 for placebo; N = 7 for 35 mg QM less than 40 kg; N = 6 for 70 mg QM less than 40 kg; N = 128 for 70 mg QM for ≥ 40 kg; N = 131 for 140 mg QM for ≥ 40 kg).

At Dose Level 1, mean trough concentration (Ctrough) following the third dose (Week 12) was higher in participants weighing ≥ 40 kg who received 70 mg QM compared with those weighing &lt; 40 kg who received 35 mg QM, although the ranges overlapped. At Week 12, mean (SD) Ctrough were 5670 (451) ng/mL and 7320 (2,670) ng/mL for the 35 mg and 70 mg dose groups, respectively.

At Dose Level 2, mean Ctrough after the third dose (Week 12) was higher in subjects weighing ≥ 40 kg who received 140 mg QM compared with those weighing &lt; 40 kg who received 70 mg QM, although the ranges overlapped. At Week 12, mean (SD) Ctrough were 15 700 (8,380) ng/mL and 17 200 (6,230) ng/mL for the 70 mg and 140 mg dose groups, respectively.

These results should be interpreted with caution due to the small number of participants in the groups weighing &lt; 40 kg.

## Safety results

The 3 most common TEAEs were nasopharyngitis (13 (9.8%) for erenumab, 12 (9.3%) for placebo); COVID-19 (9 (6.8%) for erenumab, 10 (7.8%) for placebo); and upper respiratory tract infection (8 (6.1%) for erenumab, 3 (2.3%) for placebo).

For events of interest (EOI), a higher number of participants with events in the erenumab treatment group were reported for injection site reactions (17 (12.9%) for erenumab vs 8 (6.2%) for placebo) and constipation (6 (4.5%) for erenumab vs 2 (1.6%) for placebo). The rest of the EOI reporting rates were comparable between the treatment groups.

No safety findings were observed related to growth and development, electrocardiographic changes, and serum bone metabolism markers.

Suicidal ideation was of low frequency and balanced between the erenumab group and placebo group in adolescent participants. No suicidal behavior was reported in either treatment group.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

The MAH has conducted a paediatric study in chronic migraine (Study 20160354) in line with the PIP. The study did not show any favourable effects on chronic migraine. The safety in paediatric patients with chronic migraine was in line with the safety profile in adults. No changes to the SmPC are proposed at this stage, which is agreed by the Rapporteur.

However, the MAH has informed that they plan to submit a single Type II variation by Q1 2028, incorporating results from both paediatric migraine studies: Study 20160354 (chronic migraine) and Study 20150125 (episodic migraine). While the primary analysis of Study 20150125 (Randomized, double-blind, placebo- controlled, parallel group study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of erenumab in paediatric subjects from 6 to less than 18 years old with Episodic Migraine) has been completed, its extension phase remains ongoing. The MAH has informed that since neither study met its primary endpoint, the application is expected to seek only update of the SmPC to reflect the study results and will not include a request for a paediatric indication extension. Unless new safety findings from Study 20150125 require an earlier label update, submission of both studies in a single procedure will enable a comprehensive review and consolidated SmPC update.
