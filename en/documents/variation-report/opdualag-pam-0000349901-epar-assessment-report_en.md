A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that study CA224069 (RELATIVITY-069) is a stand alone study.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Relatlimib and nivolumab were administered as intraveneous infusion. In the flat dosing cohort relatlimab 160 mg + nivolumab 480 mg IV Q4W was administered. ## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## 2.3.1. Introduction

The MAH submitted a final report for:

- CA224069 (RELATIVITY-069): A Phase I/II study of the safety tolerability, pharmacokinetics and preliminary efficacy of relatlimab plus nivolumab in pediatric and young adult participants with recurrent or refractory classical Hodgkin Lymphoma and Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma

## 2.3.2. Clinical study

## CA224069 (RELATIVITY-069): A Phase I/II study of the safety tolerability, pharmacokinetics and preliminary efficacy of relatlimab plus nivolumab in pediatric and young adults

## Description

Study CA224069 was a Phase 1/2 pediatric study of relatlimab + nivolumab to evaluate safety and tolerability in R/R cHL and NHL. The study was planned to be conducted in 2 parts: Part A (safety evaluation and dose determination) and Part B (cohort expansion). The study design is shown in Figure 1. Only Part A of the study was initiated prior to early study termination by the Sponsor; Part B was not initiated. The study was terminated early by the Sponsor due to the following reasons:

- Recent biomarker data indicating limited relevance of LAG-3 inhibition in lymphoma, particularly in the R/R setting.
- Enrollment feasibility challenges due to changes in treatment landscape for cHL. As a result of the addition of more targeted therapies into first-line treatment (anti-PD-1 therapy) there have been a decreased number of pediatric patients progressing to R/R disease.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- Limited interest from Investigators to enroll subjects, due to concurrent competing clinical trials and the expanding availability of alternative treatment options.

## Treatments

Part A consisted of two cohorts (Figure 2 and Figure 3):

- Flat dosing (AF) cohort included subjects ≥ 12 years of age who weighed ≥ 40 kg:
- o D = Relatlimab 160 mg + nivolumab 480 mg IV Q4W (A1F); if the starting dose was found to be not tolerated, the relatlimab dose was to be decreased to D-1
- o D-1 = Relatlimab 80 mg + nivolumab 480 mg IV Q4W (A2F)
- Age/weight based dosing (AW) cohort included subjects ≥ 12 years of age who weighed &lt; 40 kg and/or subjects &lt; 12 years of age irrespective of weight:
- o D = Relatlimab 2 mg/kg (max 160 mg) + nivolumab 6 mg/kg (max 480 mg) Q4W (A1W); if the starting dose was found to be not tolerated, then the relatlimab dose was to be decreased to D-1
- o D-1 = Relatlimab 1 mg/kg (max 80 mg) with nivolumab 6 mg/kg (max 480 mg) Q4W (A2W)

Enrollment in the 2 dosing cohorts (A1F and A1W) was initiated in parallel. Up to 6 subjects for both age/weight cohorts were planned to be assessed for safety and PK. The observation period for DLTs (DLT-evaluation period) was 1 cycle, defined as 28 days. Subjects were monitored for safety using CTCAE v.5.0 criteria. If DLT with CTCAE v5.0 criteria of Grade 3 or higher severity was observed in 2 or more subjects, the dose was to be de-escalated to D-1 (A2F and A2W) and an additional 6 subjects were planned to be evaluated for toxicity and exposure, for another ≥ 2 DLTs. If no DLTs were observed, a minimum of 6 subjects were to be enrolled in A1F and A1W, respectively. Both AF and AW dosing schemas were independent of each other and were evaluated for DLTs separately. Blood samples were drawn on days of infusion for all pre-dose and end of infusion.

The proposed treatment duration was until metabolic progression or toxicity up to a maximum of 2 years.

No intra-subject dose reductions or escalations for immunotherapy were permitted. Dose delay criteria applied for all drug-related AEs for both relatlimab and nivolumab.

## Sample size

The sample size of Part A was not based on statistical considerations and there was no statistical hypothesis tested. Though it was expected to treat up to 6 subjects in each age/weight dose level, the actual number treated in Part A depended on the accepted toxicity profile of each dose being assessed.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Treatment assignment to subjects was performed using interactive response technology. There was no randomization or blinding.

## Statistical Methods

Since the treated population number was limited to 5 subjects, by-subject listings were provided for the safety analyses.

PK parameters were summarized descriptively by doses of relatlimab/nivolumab. Mean, geometric mean, and CV were presented for Cmax, Ctrough, and AUC(TAU). Medians and ranges were presented for Tmax. Means and SD were provided for the other PK parameters such as Ceoi and Ctrough. The PK of relatlimab and nivolumab was derived from serum concentration versus time measurements.

## Results

## Participant flow

Seven pediatric subjects were enrolled and 5 of them received study treatment. Among the treated subjects, 4 subjects were assigned to the AF cohort and received treatment with relatlimab 160 mg or 80 mg + nivolumab 480 mg Q4W. One subject received treatment with relatlimab 2 mg/kg or 1 mg/kg (max 160 mg) + nivolumab 6 mg/kg (max 480 mg) Q4W in the AW cohort.

All 4 subjects treated in the AF cohort discontinued study treatment (2 due to disease progression, 1 due to achieving maximum clinical benefit, and 1 due to other reason). All 4 subjects subsequently discontinued the study due to 'other' reasons.

The subject treated in the AW cohort discontinued study treatment due to disease progression and discontinued from the study due to death.

## Safety results

## Exposure

AF cohort: In the AF cohort, all 4 subjects received 5-8 cycles (4-week period/cycle) of relatlimab 160 mg and nivolumab 480 mg. There were no dose delays, interruptions, or rate reductions reported.

AW cohort: The subject in the AW cohort received 2 cycles of weight-based relatlimab and nivolumab treatment. There were no dose delays, interruptions, or rate reductions reported.

## Adverse events

## AF cohort:

All-causality AEs (any grade) were reported by all subjects (100%) in the AF cohort, with the majority of AEs reported as single events (Table 6). The most frequently reported AE within 30 days of the last study dose was lymphopenia, reported in 2 subjects.

At least 1 drug-related AE was reported in each subject in the AF cohort within 30 days of the last study dose. The events included ALT increased, anaemia, AST increased, blood thyroid stimulating hormone increased, hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism, lymphopenia, non-cardiac chest pain, and thrombocytopenia.

No Grade 3-5 drug-related AEs were reported within 30 days of the last study dose.

## AW cohort:

The subject in the AW cohort reported Grade 2 all-causality AEs of toothache, otitis media, and pyrexia within 30 days of the last study dose (Table 6). None of these AEs were considered related to the study treatment by the investigator.

The subject in this cohort reported 1 Grade 3 SAE (facial pain) and 1 Grade 4 SAE (spinal cord compression) during the treatment period. During the follow-up period, the subject was reported with a fatal (Grade 5) AE of malignant neoplasm progression. The event was considered as an SAE.

## 2.3.4. Discussion on clinical aspects

CA224069 (RELATIVITY-069) was a Phase I/II study of the safety tolerability, pharmacokinetics and preliminary efficacy of relatlimab plus nivolumab in paediatric and young adults.

## Methods

The study included two parts aimed at dose finding (part A) and cohort expansion (part B). The dose finding was further subdivided in a flat dosing cohort for subjects ≥ 12 years of age who weighed ≥ 40 kg and an age/weight-based dosing cohort for subjects &lt; 40 kg and/or subjects &lt; 12 years of age. The aim was to include 6 subjects for both age/weight cohorts without any formal sample size calculation. The primary aim was to assess the safety and pharmacokinetics.

## Study termination

The study was terminated early by the Sponsor; only part A of the study was initiated. The reasons for early study termination were recent biomarker data indicating limited relevance of LAG-3 inhibition in lymphoma, particularly in the R/R setting and feasibility issues due to changes in treatment landscape for cHL and competing clinical trials. The MAH provided the biomarker report for study CA224022 in support. Study CA224-022 was a Phase 1/2a, open-label study of relatlimab administered as monotherapy or in combination with nivolumab to subjects with advanced relapsed or refractory B-cell malignancies. Data from study CA224022 were compared to two other cohorts consisting of newly diagnosed patients. The biomarker report described that cellular LAG-3 was highest in the immune tumor microenvironment of patients with newly diagnosed cHL, and significantly lower in biopsies from patients with relapsed disease post-chemotherapy, post-nivolumab and post-nivolumab+relatlimab. Nivolumab was recently approved in newly diagnosed patients with cHL aged 12 years and older and in children 5 years of age and older, adolescents and adults up to 30 years of age with relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma after one prior line of therapy (SmPC Opdivo). Therefore, the rationale for study termination can be followed in light of the potential lower relevance of LAG-3 inhibition in the R/R cHL setting in combination the feasibility issues due to the recent approvals of Opdivo for newly diagnosed and R/R cHL.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## Study population

In total 5 patients received at least 1 dose of the study treatment. Four patients were included in the flat dosing cohort and one patient in the age/weight-based cohort. Patients were aged 13 - 17 and were diagnosed with R/R cHL (n=3) or R/R NHL (n=2). Overall, the study population is very small and only adolescents were included.

## Efficacy

One complete metabolic response and two partial metabolic responses were observed. Therefore, Opdualag resulted in anti-tumour efficacy, although the efficacy data are considered too limited to draw further conclusions.

## Safety

No DLTs were observed, however no conclusions can be drawn about the optimal dose, as the first dose cohort was not fully enrolled before termination of the study.

The observed drug-related TEAEs were all low grade and in line with the known safety profile of relatlimab in combination with nivolumab. One fatal TEAE was observed of malignant neoplasm progression. This event occurred 55 days after the last dose was received and it is agreed that due to the timing and nature of the adverse event the fatal TEAE was most likely not drug-related. Three subjects reported SAEs and after assessment of the narratives it is agreed that none of these SAEs were drug-related. Therefore, it can be concluded that the data did not reveal any new safety concerns.

## Pharmacokinetics

Four paediatric subjects (&gt;12 years, &gt;40 kg) received the same dose as approved in adults (i.e., relatlimab 160 mg Q4W + nivolumab 480 mg Q4W) for 5 to 8 cycles. In adults, steady-state is reached by 16 weeks (i.e., 4 cycles). Nivolumab Ctrough geometric mean (CV%) at Cycle 4 Day 1 was 79.80 (53) μg/mL and relatlimab Ctrough was 20.52 (66) μg/mL. Ctrough concentrations at steady-state are somewhat higher compared to adults (i.e., Relatlimab Cmin 15.3 (64.3) μg/mL and nivolumab Cmin 59.7 (58.6) μg/mL. The paediatric nivolumab data cautiously supports the finding that nivolumab clearance in adolescent subjects is lower than those of adults.

## SmPC

The MAH proposes no changes to the Opdualag SmPC based on the efficacy and safety data without providing a justification. Opdualag is approved in adolescents with advanced melanoma, this indication was entirely based on extrapolation from adult data and therefore there are no paediatric data in the SmPC (EMEA/H/C/005481/0000). It is proposed to update the paediatric sub-section in 4.2 and 5.2, and to add a paediatric sub-section in section 4.8 and 5.1 (see proposal below).

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

The MAH submitted the final report for study CA224069 in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006. The study was terminated early. Study CA224069 was a phase I/II study aimed at dose finding in paediatric subjects with R/R cHL or R/R NHL. No conclusions can be drawn about the optimal dose, as the first dose cohort was not fully enrolled before termination of the study. The efficacy data are considered insufficient as only 5 patients were included. The observed safety was in line with the known safety profile of relatlimab in combination with nivolumab.

The MAH proposes no changes to the Opdualag SmPC based on the efficacy and safety data, but a suggestion for updates to SmPC section 4.2, 4.8, 5.1, and 5.2 is provided. After agreement of the wording for the SmPC, the MAH is requested for a commitment to submit a variation shortly after finalisation of the procedure to implement the changes into the PI, as it is not possible to update the PI during a P46 procedure. Acknowledging the limited number of patients in study CA224069, including the paediatric data is in line with the SmPC guidance.
