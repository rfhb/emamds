## Overview

Zeydovio is a medicine used to treat adults with short bowel syndrome, a condition in which a large part of the small intestine has been removed by surgery, for example because of a disease. As a result, the remaining intestine may not absorb enough nutrients and liquids from food and drinks and patients may need fluid and nutrition support. Patients should be stable after their intestine has had time to adapt following surgery.

Zeydovio contains the active substance glepaglutide.

### How is Zeydovio used?

The medicine can only be obtained with a prescription. Treatment must be started under the supervision of a doctor experienced in treating short bowel syndrome.

Zeydovio is given by injection under the skin of the belly or thigh twice a week. There should be an interval of three or four days between injections. Patients can inject Zeydovio themselves using the pre-filled pen once they have been trained to use it correctly.

Before treatment starts, the doctor will check the patient's stomach, small intestine and large bowel and remove any polyps, which are small abnormal tissue growths.

The doctor will assess how well treatment is working after six months. If the patient's condition has not improved, the doctor will consider switching to another treatment or stopping treatment.

During treatment, the doctor will closely monitor the patient's weight and nutritional status. Examinations of the stomach and intestines are recommended once a year during the first two years of treatment and then at least every five years.

For more information about using Zeydovio, see the package leaflet or contact your doctor or pharmacist.

### How does Zeydovio work?

The active substance in Zeydovio, glepaglutide, is similar to glucagon-like peptide-2 (GLP-2), a natural hormone that helps maintain the structure and function of the intestine.

Glepaglutide acts like GLP-2 by attaching to GLP-2 receptors, which are targets found in the intestine. This promotes the growth of the intestinal lining and increases its thickness and surface area. It also increases blood flow in the intestine and slows movement through the intestine. As a result, the intestine can absorb more fluids and nutrients.

Glepaglutide is long acting, meaning that after injection under the skin, it is slowly absorbed into the body and its effects last longer.

### What benefits of Zeydovio have been shown in studies?

A study involving 106 adults with short bowel syndrome who needed fluids and nutrients given directly into a vein compared Zeydovio with placebo (a dummy treatment) over 24 weeks. The main measure of benefit was the change in the weekly amount of fluid and nutrients given into a vein since the start of treatment.

At the end of the study, the amount of support compared to the start of treatment had fallen by an average of 5.1 litres per week in patients receiving Zeydovio twice weekly, compared with 2.9 litres per week in patients receiving placebo. In addition, 14% of patients treated with Zeydovio twice weekly no longer needed fluids and nutrients given into a vein, while all patients receiving placebo still needed it.

Studies carried out with Zeydovio are described in more detail in the medicine's assessment report.

### What are the side effects and restrictions with Zeydovio?

For the full list of side effects and restrictions with Zeydovio, see the package leaflet.

The most common side effects with Zeydovio (which may affect more than 1 in 10 people) include injection site reactions, problems affecting the stoma (an opening in the abdomen for removing waste from the body) in patients with a stoma, abdominal (belly) pain, nausea (feeling sick), vomiting, fluid retention (a build-up of fluid in the body) and swelling or bloating of the abdomen.

Zeydovio must not be used in people who have active or suspected cancer. It must also not be used in people who have had cancer of the stomach or gut, liver, gallbladder, bile ducts or pancreas within the previous five years.

### Why is Zeydovio authorised in the EU?

Zeydovio reduced the amount of fluids and nutrients that adults with short bowel syndrome needed to receive directly into a vein. Some patients were able to stop it completely. Reducing this support is important because it can take up a considerable amount of patients' time and can cause complications.

The main study included a limited number of patients, which is acceptable because short bowel syndrome is rare. Information beyond two years of treatment was limited at the time of authorisation.

Most side effects with Zeydovio were mild to moderate. Some serious side effects occurred, including blockage of the bowel and other problems affecting organs in the belly. Complications affecting a stoma and polyps in the gut were also reported. These risks are considered manageable with the precautions and monitoring described in the product information and acceptable considering the benefits of the medicine.

The European Medicines Agency decided that Zeydovio's benefits are greater than its risks and it can be authorised for use in the EU.

### What measures are being taken to ensure the safe and effective use of Zeydovio?

Recommendations and precautions to be followed by healthcare professionals and patients for the safe and effective use of Zeydovio have been included in the summary of product characteristics and the package leaflet.

As for all medicines, data on the use of Zeydovio are continuously monitored. Suspected side effects reported with Zeydovio are carefully evaluated and any necessary action is taken to protect patients.

### Therapeutic indication

Zeydovio is indicated for the treatment of adults with Short Bowel Syndrome (SBS). Patients should be stable following a period of intestinal adaptation after surgery.
