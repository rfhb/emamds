## 1.8.3. Opinion

Based on the CAT review of data on quality, safety and efficacy for Tacquell in the treatment of adult patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma following prior anti-Programmed Death-1 (PD1)-based checkpoint inhibitor treatment given either as first line treatment for unresectable stage III or IV disease, or given as (neo)adjuvant therapy, the CAT considers by consensus that the quality, safety and efficacy of the above-mentioned medicinal product is not sufficiently demonstrated, and, therefore, recommends the refusal of the granting of the marketing authorisation for the abovementioned medicinal product.

The CAT considers that:

- The comparability between the clinical batches from the manufacturing sites , and between the M14TIL clinical trial batches and the commercial batches is not established.
- EU GMP compliance, including manufacturing authorisations and GMP certificates, for all the sites responsible for manufacture and quality control of the commercial active substance and finished product, is incompletely documented.
- The control strategy lacks the necessary robustness to ensure consistent quality, safety, and efficacy of the commercial medicinal product.
- The control strategy regarding the non-proliferation of feeder cells is inadequate, and aseptic process validation does not encompass the full feeder cell manufacturing process.
- The GCP inspection identified critical GCP compliance concerns in the pivotal clinical trial which impact the reliability, integrity, traceability, and re-constructability of the clinical data package.
- Multiple methodological and statistical concerns limit the interpretability of the pivotal study. These include inadequate original pre-specification of censoring rules and the handling of intercurrent events, the lack of an appropriate mitigation strategy for investigator-assessed PFS in an openlabel study and key modifications to the primary endpoint framework and analysis strategy,
- The post-hoc restriction of the randomized comparison and the exclusion of a substantial proportion of patients from the primary analysis deviate from the ITT principle, reducing the robustness and generalisability of the retained dataset, particularly in the context of an MAA based on a single pivotal randomized trial.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

- A comprehensive safety assessment is restricted by how the safety profile was characterized, in particular by limited safety data collection and follow-up, potential misclassifications of SAEs as AEs, and lack of detailed death narratives.

In the context of a potential future application, the applicant should consider the post-approval efficacy and safety follow-up according to Article 14 of Regulation on advanced therapy medicinal products (EC Regulation 1394/2007).

Due to the aforementioned concerns a satisfactory summary of product characteristics, labelling, package leaflet, pharmacovigilance system, risk management plan and post-authorisation measures to address other concerns as outlined in the list of outstanding issues cannot be agreed at this stage.

Furthermore, following the review of the available data in the context of the applicant's claim of new active substance status, the CAT/CHMP position at the time of this report is reflected in appendix 1.

Based on the draft CHMP opinion review of data on quality, safety and efficacy for Tacquell in the treatment of adult patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma following prior antiProgrammed Death-1 (PD-1)-based checkpoint inhibitor treatment given either as first line treatment for unresectable stage III or IV disease, or given as (neo)adjuvant therapy, adopted by the CAT the CHMP considers by consensus that the quality, safety and efficacy of the above-mentioned medicinal product is not sufficiently demonstrated, and, therefore, recommends the refusal of the granting of the marketing authorisation for the above-mentioned medicinal product based on the same grounds for refusal as adopted by the CAT.

## 2. Introduction

## 2.1. Therapeutic context

Cutaneous melanoma is a cancer type that derives from melanocytes.

Disease staging is based on a four-tiered staging system defined by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) depending on tumor thickness, presence of mitosis and ulceration in the primary stages and regions of metastases in the later ones. Once the melanoma is locally advanced and has spread to the lymph nodes (stage III) or is spread to organs or other parts of the body (stage IV), it is called advanced stage disease. Patient prognosis is related to the disease stage.

Cutaneous melanoma is one of the most common malignancies, accounting for 1.7% of global cancer diagnoses according to GLOBOCAN. The risk of developing melanoma increases with age, possibly related to a greater cumulative exposure to UV radiation. However, melanoma can also develop on areas of the skin that are not UV-exposed.

Melanoma has an estimated yearly increasing incidence of &gt;150.000 newly diagnosed cases per year in Europe (Dyba et al., 2021). The global age-standardized incidence rate is 3.8/100,000 for males and 3.0/100,000 for females. In Europe, the incidence rate is 10 - 25 new melanoma cases per 100,000 inhabitants, with a dramatic increase seen in the last years in many parts of Europe for all age groups. Most diagnoses are made prior to the development of lymph node or distant metastases (stage III to IV, advanced disease). The risk of developing melanoma increases with age, possibly related to a greater cumulative exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation. However, melanoma can also develop on areas of the skin that are not UV-exposed (Dennis et al., 2008; Rozeman et al., 2018; Ting et al., 2007).

Surgical resection is the preferred first line of therapy for patients with localized melanoma (stages I to III). However, despite resection of the primary tumor, the disease metastasizes in about 14% of patients. Previously, patients with stage IIIC to IV melanoma had a poor prognosis, with a 10-year survival of less than 5%, due to the very limited effect of traditional first-line therapies like radiation and chemotherapy (Balch et al., 2009; Bhatia &amp; Thompson, 2012).

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

In the past years, different immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) or anti-T-cell coinhibitory receptor CTLA-4 antibodies, have been developed with the aim of reactivating pre-existing anti-tumor T-cell reactivity. Moreover, targeted therapies directed against aberrant molecules or pathways that drive uncontrolled growth of tumor cells (e.g. BRAF gene mutation) have been developed to inhibit tumor cell growth. These developments have significantly improved the prognosis of patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma.

The anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4) antibody ipilimumab (IPI) was the first immune checkpoint inhibitor to achieve a significant improvement in survival of advanced stage melanoma and was approved as a Standard of Care (SoC) for the management of those patients in Europe in 2011. A switch in clinical practice occurred with the approval of molecules targeting PD-1 as first-line therapy in 2015 and as adjuvant therapy for patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma in 2018. The current European Society's for Medical Oncology (ESMO) clinical practice guideline (Amaral et al, 2024) for the management of cutaneous melanoma indicates 'current first-line SoC treatment options for unresectable stage III/IV melanoma are PD-1 blockade (nivolumab, pembrolizumab), PD-1 blockade combined with CTLA-4 blockade (nivolumab-ipilimumab), PD-1 blockade combined with lymphocyte activation gene-3 (LAG-3) blockade (nivolumab-relatlimab)'.

After failure of first-line treatment with anti-PD-1 antibodies, second-line treatment with other immune checkpoint inhibitors or targeted therapy leads to objective response rates (ORR) of 28% to more than 50%, however of relatively short duration. Due to this limited clinical benefit of second-line treatment with the currently available immune checkpoint inhibitors and targeted therapies, there is a clear unmet medical need for novel treatment modalities for relapsed/refractory patients with advanced stage melanoma.

## 2.3. Description of the product

Tacquell cell dispersion for infusion is an autologous cell-based immunotherapy product that contains viable autologous CD45+CD3+ T-cells, isolated and expanded ex vivo from a melanoma metastasis with the goal of generating immune reactions against the patient's own tumor.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The mechanism of action of the autologous tumor infiltrating lymphocytes relies on anti-tumor reactivity mediated by CD45+CD3+ T cells. Anti-tumor reactivity is mediated by CD45+CD3+ T cells and by both CD4+ and CD8+ subpopulations, and that there is long-term persistence of tumorreactive T cells in peripheral blood of patients after product infusion. In addition, it was shown that the anti-tumor reactivity is directed against both patient-specific neoantigens and tumor-associated antigens.

Tacquell is intended for autologous use and should only be administered as a single dose via intravenous infusion. The proposed recommended dose of Tacquell is 10 × 10 9 - 1 x 10 11 viable CD45+CD3+ viable cells dispersion for infusion.

Tacquell must be administered in a qualified treatment centre by a physician with experience in adoptive cellular therapy and trained for administration and management of patients treated with Tacquell.

## 4. Non-clinical aspects

## 4.1. Introduction

## 4.3. Pharmacology

## 4.3.1. Pharmacodynamics

## 4.3.1.1. Primary pharmacodynamics

Tacquell (TM001) is an autologous, patient-specific T cell product composed of viable CD45+CD3+ T cells expanded ex vivo from tumor lesions. The product aims to induce broad immune responses against tumor-specific antigens, including neoantigens and tumor-associated antigens.

Due to limited availability of autologous tumor material, nonclinical pharmacology studies rely on ex vivo analyses of patient-derived samples from clinical trials and literature data. These studies have demonstrated that TM001 TILs recognize tumor antigens, as shown by CD137 expression and IFNγ production when co-cultured with autologous tumour cell lines. Both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells contribute to this tumor reactivity, with CD8+ T cells being predominant.

The T cell subpopulations in TM001 batches of the confirmatory phase III trial were described but the characterization of these TILs IMP is not detailed.

Following infusion, tumor-reactive T cells, initially absent in patients' peripheral blood, expanded markedly and persisted, indicating sustained immune activation.

Mapping of CD8+ T cells recognizing neoepitopes in TIL infusion products and blood samples from treated patients led to identification of 106 neoepitopes within the TIL infusion products (Kristensen et al, 2022).

The investigators have addressed the specificity for neoantigens and tumor-associated antigens in a selection of TIL-IMP batches from the confirmatory phase III trial. They found that the mechanism of action of TM001 relies on the presence of anti-tumor-reactivity of CD45+CD3+ T cells, reactive against neoantigens or tumor-associated antigens.

Published literature supports these findings (Alfei F et al. 2021, Sun J, Zhang J, Hu H, et al. 2021, Dhodapkar MV, Dhodapkar KM. Et a. 2020). Studies have reported response rates around 50%, including durable complete responses, with persistent neoantigen-specific T cells and manageable safety profiles, particularly in patients resistant to anti-PD-1 therapy (Verdegaal EME et al. 2020). Other reports confirm long-term persistence of tumor-reactive T cells and show correlations between neoantigen-reactive T cell diversity and improved progression-free and overall survival (Kristensen et al. 2022)

## 4.5. Overall discussion and conclusions on non-clinical aspects

## 4.5.1. Discussion

The non-clinical package is mainly based on literature references with the aim to demonstrate that TM001 presents with the key characteristics of an active TIL preparation. This approach is acceptable taking into account the nature of the product and the low relevance of in vivo models for highly patient specific products as well as the availability of published results studying TILs in general.

The mechanism of TM001 was investigated through ex vivo analyses of tumor-reactive T cells in samples from patients participating in the confirmatory phase III trial (M14TIL). The T cells expressed activation markers and immune checkpoint molecules. This may reflect both activation and exhaustion in the tumor microenvironment. Variations in T-cell populations were observed between patients who responded well to the treatment and those who did not. However, no direct correlation between these variations and clinical response was demonstrated.

It was confirmed that TIL-IMP contains tumor-reactive T cells, but the proportion of such cells varies between patients, and the specific roles of different TIL subpopulations are not fully defined.

From a non-clinical perspective, and in line with the guidelines, the absence of PK and biodistribution studies is considered acceptable. Considering that T cells are naturally trafficking cells, their ability to circulate and migrate throughout the body as part of their immune function is well established. Moreover, given that TILs are fully differentiated cells, the risk of ectopic implantation is low. Overall, the justification provided is considered appropriate and consistent with regulatory expectations for this type of product.

Given the nature of the product and the absence of a suitable animal model, to assess safety the applicant has relied on clinical data and literature. Evidence from earlier studies (Muul et al, 1987); Topalian et al, 1987) supports the absence of tumor cells in expanded TIL preparations and their selective cytotoxicity toward tumor cells only.

As TM001 is a single-dose treatment for adults with advanced melanoma, repeated-dose and juvenile toxicity studies are not required. The absence of developmental and reproductive toxicity studies is considered acceptable given both the therapeutic indication, and the autologous, patient-specific nature of the product challenging the relevance of conventional experimental models (EMEA/CHMP/410869/2006; ICH S9//2018). In addition, treatment with Tacquell is preceded by a conditioning regimen with cyclophosphamide (2 days) followed by fludarabine (5 days). Both substances were shown to induce embryo-foetal lethality and malformations in animal studies. Adverse reproductive outcomes were reported in humans with cyclophosphamide (incl. malformations, foetotoxicity, intrauterine death) which is contraindicated in pregnant women and in women of reproductive potential not using contraception, while it is generally reported that fludarabine should not be used during pregnancy unless clearly necessary. Contraception is also requested during treatment with IL-2 which is recommended for 5 days following Tacquell infusion. Therefore, it is expected that measures will be already in place to avoid foetal exposure for any woman of childbearing potential treated with Tacquell, strengthened further with a recommendation to verify the pregnancy status for females of childbearing potential prior Tacquell therapy.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Given the nature of the infused T cells, carcinogenicity studies are not considered necessary as the tumorigenic potential of T cells is low (EMEA/CHMP/410869/2006). Furthermore, it was previously demonstrated that culture-expanded human TILs able to perform autologous tumor cell killing were free from tumor cells (Muul et al, 1987; Topalian et al, 1987), therefore not causing any potential carcinogenic risk. The absence of tumor cells and B cells is confirmed for each TM001 batch. Lastly, the available clinical data for autologous TILs do not indicate any carcinogenicity issues (Chesney et al, 2022).

Due to the nature of the product, additional studies on genotoxicity, environmental risk or other toxicity are not warranted (EMEA/CHMP/410869/2006). Moreover, no safety/toxicity studies were performed for the other products used in the TM001 therapy (namely preparative non-myeloablative lymphocyte depleting chemotherapy and supportive IL-2) because these products are approved/used as previously described in the literature and in accordance to their use in the clinical setting (Dafni et al, 2019; Warner et al, 2023). Nonclinical pharmacodynamic drug interactions studies have not been performed due to lack of relevant animal models.

The CHMP endorses the CAT discussion on the non-clinical aspects as described above.

## TIL-IMP therapy

The dose of TIL-IMP administered in this study was within the standard dosage range of TIL products that have been used in previous phase I/II studies (Andersen et al, 2016, van den Berg et al, 2020).

## 5.2.5.1. Discussion

Tacquell consists of autologous viable CD45+CD3+ T cells, isolated and expanded ex vivo from a melanoma metastasis, also referred to as tumor infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs).

No biopharmaceutics studies have been performed for TM001, as this class of autologous somatic cell therapy products administered intravenously does not require bioavailability studies (EMEA/CHMP/410869/2006).

No specific clinical pharmacokinetic studies were performed for TM001 (EMEA/CHMP/410869/2006). Pharmacodynamics, including a description of the mechanism of action of TM001, is presented in the non-clinical section 4.3. As regards the TIL dose, no justification was provided for the dose selected for the marketing authorisation. The applicant proposed a minimum dose of 10E9 viable CD45+CD3+ cells. Compared to the cell doses administered to patients in studies M14TIL and H23TIL, 10E9 viable CD45+CD3 cells is outside of the 1.5 IQR. Considering the lack of a clear correlation between cell dose and clinical response in combination with the lowest viable CD45+CD3 cell dose administered to patients that had an objective response, the minimum dose of 10E9 viable CD45+CD3+ cells is insufficiently justified.

No upper dose was proposed by the applicant under the clinical aspects, which is not in line with the data provided. The highest cell dose administered to patients in the target population of the claimed indication is 46E9 in study M14TIL and 86E9 in the H23TIL HE program. An appropriate choice of a maximal dose should be based on clinical outcome in the target population.

Since IL-2 high dose may be associated with increased toxicity further, clarification has been provided regarding the administration scheme and the number of doses received by the patients in the clinical trial with an objective response (CR or PR) after Tacquell therapy. The dosing regimen for NMA-LD and IL-2 are in line with those used in previous phase I/II TIL studies and in line with their registered dosing schedule.

## 5.2.5.2. Conclusions

As regards the TIL dose, the Applicant proposed a dose range of ≥10 × 10^9 to 1 x 10^11 viable CD3+CD45+ cells/ which is considered too wide for the reasons provided in the discussion above. The posology is therefore not acceptable at this stage.

## TM001 Dose Recommendation summary efficacy

In the confirmatory phase III trial, TM001 was dosed based on the total number of viable cells within a preset range of 5E9 and 2E11 cells. This dose was established following phase I/II feasibility studies (Andersen et al, 2016, van den Berg et al, 2020). Based on the results from the batches in the confirmatory phase III trial (M14TIL), the dose has been set on ≥10E9 viable CD45+CD3+ T cells, the active moiety in TM001.

It has been evaluated if a relationship between dose and response could be established. Within the Per Protocol Analysis Set (PPS which consisted of all patients that received treatment) of anti-PD-1 pretreated patients who received TM001 therapy at the NKI site.

The Odds Ratio (OR) is reported as just above 1 for the analyses on the M14TIL and H23TIL. Significant p value of &lt;0.05 is only observed for the analysis on the H23TIL cohort whereas no significant p-value is provided for the analysis of the M14TIL Tacquell target population.

To further assess the relation between the Tacquell viable CD45+CD3+ cell dose range and efficacy data for the primary and selected efficacy endpoints (ORR, PFS and OS) for the Tacquell target population within M14TIL, a categorical analysis was performed by dividing patients into high versus low dose groups based on the cohort median of infused Tacquell viable CD45+CD3+ dose. In addition, a continuous analysis was performed using Cox proportional hazards models in which the Tacquell viable CD45+CD3+ cell dose was modelled per 10-unit increase to capture linear dose-outcome relationships across the full dosing range. Relation with objective response rate (ORR) was assessed using logistic regression for objective response (CR/PR) versus no objective response (SD/PD) in a categorical analysis for Tacquell dose (&gt; median vs ≤ median), whereas relation with progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) was analysed using Kaplan-Meier estimates and Cox regression with right-censoring incorporated via event indicators for progression and death.

In the categorical analysis, patients receiving a Tacquell viable CD45+CD3+ cell dose above the median demonstrated a significantly higher ORR compared with those receiving a dose equal to or lower than median. The effect of the Tacquell dose on PFS and OS did not reach statistical significance with p values &gt;0.05. The continuous-dose analyses did not reach statistical significance.

In addition, a logistic regression analysis of viable CD45+CD3+ cells Tacquell dose administered versus best overall response (CR, PR) was performed for patients in the Tacquell target population in the confirmatory phase III trial (M14TIL). The response probability increases with increasing dose. The logistic regression model suggests that for doses under 20E9 viable CD45+CD3+ cells, there may still be a probability of response. In view of this response potential and taken into consideration the manufacturing consistency of the phase III trial batch data, a minimal dose has been defined with an acceptance criterium set at ≥10E9 viable CD45+CD3+ cells.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The applicant proposes maintaining the minimum dose of 10E9 viable CD45+CD3+ cells and proposes monitoring dosages (batch sizes) as part of the continuous process validation (CPV). The applicant proposes to re-evaluate the specifications once a statistically significant number of batches have been manufactured using the commercial process.

## 5.3.2.1.1. Study title

Randomized phase III study comparing a non-myeloablative lymphocyte depleting regimen of chemotherapy followed by infusion of tumor infiltrating lymphocytes and interleukin-2 to standard ipilimumab treatment in metastatic melanoma

## 5.3.2.1.2. Study design

This was a Phase III multicenter, open-label, randomized trial to evaluate TIL-IMP therapy or anticytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4) therapy (ipilimumab) administered intravenously in patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma. For patients in the ipilimumab group, follow-up assessments were scheduled at 12 weeks after first ipilimumab infusion and then at 18 and 24 weeks, Thereafter, every 3 months for 2 years, one year every 4 months, 2 years every 6 months, and yearly thereafter until disease progression, start of subsequent line of therapy or formal end of study.

## Statistical methods for estimation and sensitivity analysis on primary estimand&lt;s&gt;

The primary efficacy analysis was based on the Full Analysis Set (FAS), defined as all randomized patients analysed according to their allocated treatment. The primary estimand targeted the treatment effect on progression-free survival (PFS) from randomization to the earliest of disease progression or death, assessed per RECIST 1.1. Intercurrent events, including treatment discontinuation and initiation of new anti-cancer therapy, were handled using a treatment policy strategy. A formal estimand table was prespecified in the SAP.

The primary analysis used a stratified log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards model adjusted for stratification factors (BRAF mutation status, treatment line, and center). Additional methods included Fleming-Harrington weighted log-rank tests and Restricted Mean Survival Time (RMST) analysis, although key parameters (e.g. weight function, truncation time) were not prespecified.

No formal multiplicity adjustment was applied. Missing data were not imputed; a complete-case approach was used. No prespecified sensitivity analyses were conducted to explore the robustness of results under alternative estimand strategies or missing data assumptions. A supportive analysis was performed in the Per Protocol Set (PPS), excluding patients with early progression using a week-5 landmark approach.

## Statistical methods for estimation and sensitivity analysis on the secondary estimands

Secondary analyses were conducted using standard statistical methods. PFS at 6 months, ORR, and CRR were analysed as binary endpoints using Fisher's exact test. No formal multiplicity adjustment or hierarchical testing strategy was applied. Missing data were handled via a complete-case approach without imputation. No sensitivity or supplementary analyses were performed to assess the robustness of the secondary estimands.

Of 168 patients randomized 1:1, 143 responded to the baseline questionnaire (88% response rate). Of

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

these, 75 (89%) were in the TIL-IMP arm and 73 (87%) in the IPI arm. ## 5.3.4.3. Additional M14TIL-SWOG S1616 comparison provided at the second round

During the assessment, the Applicant provided an additional M14TIL-SWOG S1616 indirect comparison to contextualise the M14TIL TIL/Tacquell results against an external dataset including patients treated with ipilimumab or nivolumab plus ipilimumab.

The applicant created a virtual comparator dataset for Tacquell versus IPI monotherapy and IPI/NIVO combination therapy by applying exclusion criteria of the confirmatory phase III trial for Tacquell (M14TIL trial) on the SWOG1616 dataset (i.e. excluding patients with mucosal melanoma, a clinical ECOG performance status of &gt;1, or with LDH levels &gt;2 times the upper limit of normal). The patient group selected from the SWOG1616 dataset comprised 19 patients treated with IPI monotherapy and 52 patients treated with IPI/NIVO combination therapy.

The report describes four analyses: an unadjusted Cox analysis, a multivariable Cox regression, propensity score full matching, and propensity score weighting. The propensity score model included age, sex, WHO performance status, brain metastases, liver metastases and metastatic category. The report also notes baseline imbalances between the M14TIL and SWOG populations, including older patients in the SWOG population

A propensity score matching model was employed to reduce potential confounding and to achieve maximal comparability between the M14TIL-Tacquell patient cohort and the SWOG1616-IPI/NIVO cohort. Propensity scores, which represent the probability of a patient receiving one treatment versus the other based on observed baseline covariates, were estimated using a logistic regression model including age (dichotomized as age 75), sex, WHO performance status, presence of brain metastases, liver metastases, and metastatic category. A full matching method was applied for pairing each patient in the M14TIL-Tacquell group with a weighted sum of similar patients from the SWOG1616-IPI/NIVO

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

group based on their propensity scores. The results showed a median PFS after Tacquell therapy versus after IPI/NIVO combination therapy, with a 6-months PFS.

## 5.3.5. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical efficacy

## 5.3.5.1. Discussion

Tacquell consists of autologous viable CD45+CD3+ T cells, isolated and expanded ex vivo from a melanoma metastasis (TM001DS), with the goal of generating immune reactions against the patient's own tumour.

The sought indication is: treatment of adult patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma following prior anti-Programmed Death-1 (PD-1)-based checkpoint inhibitor treatment given either as first line treatment for unresectable stage III or IV disease, or given as (neo)adjuvant therapy.

The clinical development program included 2 phase I/II and a phase III study M14TIL. Supportive data come from a side-study of the pivotal phase III trial (4 subjects) and a hospital exemption program (91 subjects).

The Applicant submitted the publications by Andersen et al, 2016 and van den Berg et al, 2020 of the two phase I/II clinical trials. These studies were carried out using developmental TIL products that contributed in principle to the design and final manufacturing process applied in the confirmatory phase III trial and were performed in the same clinical sites. They are therefore considered supportive. As the applied manufacturing processes in the phase I/II feasibility studies differ from the commercial process, no formal study reports are available, and only corresponding publications have been provided. The manufacturing process of the TIL was mainly based on established methods published.

Since the results of the phase I/II feasibility studies are not expected to have an impact of the efficacy results of the phase III M14TIL study, no concerns are raised in this regard.

The proposed minimum dose of 10E9 viable CD45+CD3+ cells (see also discussion on clinical pharmacology) has not been exhaustively justified. The lower range of the cell dose of viable CD45+CD3 cell administered to patients should be the lowest number of cells administered to patients that had an objective response in the confirmatory phase III study. A provided logistic regression model may suggest that for lower doses, there may be a probability of response. At the stage of a MAA, it is not accepted to claim an efficacy dose for a medicinal product based on probability of response and the dose should be clinically qualified. To overcome this aspect the applicant has proposed to monitor dosages (batch sizes) as part of the continuous process validation (CPV) which is not endorsed as an acceptable argument to set this minimal dose. As well, no upper dose limit was proposed in the clinical aspects which is also not acceptable.

In all studies the administration of the TIL was preceded by a preparative standard non-myeloablative lymphodepleting chemotherapy regimen and was followed by supportive intravenous administration of IL-2. The non myeloablative conditioning regimen consists of Cyclophosphamide 60 mg/kg/day which is administered on days -7 and -6 IV and of Fludarabine 25 mg/m2/day which is administered from Day -5 to Day-1 IV. The interleukin-2 (IL-2) 600,000 IU/kg IV is used from Day 0 to Day 5.

These dosing regimen for the preparative chemotherapy were used in line with their authorisation. As regards to the IL-2 dose, the Applicant stated that these regimens were used in previous phase I/II TIL without specifying if this corresponds to the Applicant's study or to other clinical studies. It is noteworthy that IL-2 (Aldesleukin) is authorized by US FDA for the treatment of adults with metastatic melanoma. Although no formal demonstration was made to attribute the antitumour activity to the TIL

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

and not the IL-2 and to isolate the IL-2 activity form the TIL activity, different literatures show the use of IL2 for the expansion and the survival of the lymphocytes (Diaz-Cano et al, 2022). The IL-2 administration is regarded as supportive to promote persistence and outgrowth, rather than as the main driver of the clinical efficacy (Rosenberg et al, 2009). In addition, tables summarising number of IL-2 administrations stratified by BOR for study M14TIL and H23TIL respectively have been provided and patients with an objective response (CR or PR) after Tacquell therapy have received between 1 and 5 doses of supportive IL-2. This number is in line with results from Goff SL et al 2016, showing data from 99 patients with melanoma treated with TIL. The numerically highest ORR was observed in the subgroup of patients who received 3 to 5 doses of IL-2.

In study M14TIL study the comparator, ipilimumab, YERVOY, is an authorised medicinal product in the EEA (EU/1/11/698/001-002), indicated for the treatment of advanced (unresectable or metastatic) melanoma in adults and adolescents 12 years of age and older as monotherapy or in combination with nivolumab. The dose and regimen used are in line with the marketing authorization.

## Design and conduct of clinical studies

Main study: M14TIL

Study M14TIL is a Phase III multicenter, randomized, open-label, trial to evaluate the efficacy and the safety of TIL-IMP therapy, compared to ipilimumab (IPI) in patients with histologically confirmed American Joint Committee of Cancer (AJCC) version 7 unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma.

The 5-year follow-up of the pivotal trial is considered as sufficient to assess the efficacy of the TIL in this indication however the threshold of &gt;50% is further discussed in the safety section.

Based on the design of the Phase III, multicenter, open-label, randomized, controlled trial, the primary objective which is the comparison of progression-free survival according to RECIST 1.1 between ipilimumab arm and TIL-IMP arm is endorsed. It was calculated in the efficacy analysis set (FAS) from the date of randomization to the date of progression or death (whatever came first). This definition aligns with the recommendations outlined in the Guideline on the evaluation of anticancer medicinal products in man (EMA/CHMP/205/95 Rev.6) and is considered appropriate for studies in this indication. The secondary objectives, PFS at 6 months (according to RECIST 1.1), PFS according to irRC, Overall response rate, Complete response rate and Overall survival were adequately defined so as to support the results of the median PFS and to demonstrate additional effects on the disease. PFS at 6 months (according to RECIST 1.1) was initially the primary endpoint and, when reclassified, was retained as a secondary endpoint, which is the recommended approach. Taking into account the targeted therapeutic indication, ie. unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma, the overall survival could have been proposed as a key secondary endpoint. Several amendments have been made to the primary endpoint between 2014 to 2021, with total PFS listed as the primary endpoint in protocol version 1 (Feb 2014), then PFS at 6 months listed in protocol version 2 (Dec 2014) and then total PFS again in protocol version 7 (23 Apr 2021). PFS at six months became a secondary objective in protocol version 7 [E29.1]. Although the final primary endpoint chosen may make sense methodologically, the integrity of the study is not considered preserved, and no reassuring justification has been provided on such terms.

During the conduct of the study the approval of anti-PD1 based immune check-point inhibitors in 2016 changed the standard of care for patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma from Ipilimumab to anti-PD1 treatment in the 1st line setting and this would have been the appropriate control arm in the proposed indication. To address this issue, the applicant presented an indirect comparison (study S1616) of Tacquell with IPI-NIVO in the second-line setting, referring to the SWOG1616 trial, which evaluates IPI and IPI-NIVO in patients refractory/resistant to PD1 (see section 5.3.5). However, the indirect comparison is limited by cross trial differences, residual baseline imbalances, and lack of alignment in PFS assessment. To address confounding factors, the Applicant performed an unadjusted Cox analysis and adjusted analyses using a multivariable Cox model and propensity score based on full matching with weighting (see section 5.3.5). The propensity score model included age dichotomised at 75 years, sex, WHO performance status, presence of brain metastases, presence of liver metastases, and metastatic category. Across analyses, PFS results numerically favoured Tacquell, but estimates were imprecise. The unadjusted analysis and the propensity score weighted analyses showed similar effect estimates with wide confidence intervals overlapping unity. Given the limited size of the external comparator cohort and the use of weighting, the robustness of these estimates is uncertain. Key information required to assess the validity of the propensity score approach is insufficiently documented, including balance diagnostics, assessment of overlap and positivity, distribution of weights, and effective sample size. Thus, based on the answer provided it is still entirely unclear if the treatment effect holds in the current treatment landscape. Of further note, patients in the SWOG1616 comparator dataset were relapsed/refractory to PD-1, and most had received anti-PD1 as first-line therapy for unresectable stage IIIC-IV melanoma, i.e. they received IPI-NIVO in a second-line setting. While indeed IPI-NIVO may be used after first-line anti-PD1 according to the ESMO guidelines, the additional benefit of adding nivolumab to ipilimumab in this setting may be expected to be less than when IPI-NIVO is given in the first line treatment, only after prior (neo)adjuvant anti-PD1. Of note, the sought indication for Tacquell would also permit use in the 'first line' after only prior (neo)adjuvant anti-PD1 and an indirect comparison versus IPI-NIVO in this setting has not been carried out. Therefore, based on the above, to draw robust conclusions, a prospective, randomized trial directly comparing TIL therapy with IPI/NIVO in this population would be the most appropriate approach. In the total population of study M14TIL, it appears that only 17.9% (ipilimumab arm) and 16.7% (TIL arm) received the trial treatment as their first line of treatment. An incorrect use of 'first line treatment' definition was applied using such parameter for stratification of patients and affecting the randomisation. Despite the applicant's justification indicating that the stratification variable was misinterpreted consistently across sites and did not impact the randomization process or study integrity, this is considered incorrect: these patients would be randomised according to an incorrect value for this stratification factor. In addition, the applicant clarified that the term \"second line\" was used for both patients previously treated with adjuvant anti-PD1 and those treated with prior anti-PD1 in the first-line setting, thus 'first line treatment' refers only to patients with no prior exposure to anti-PD1. Only 19 patients received the TIL in this 'first line' setting. Therefore, the vast majority of subjects had already received a prior 'first-line' of therapy and received the trial treatment as 'second-line therapy'. Considering the major shortcomings of the dossier on the conduct of the study and the relevant uncertainties in assessing the benefit/risk balance of Tacquell, the definition of the proper indication with respect to treatment line or prior therapies is not possible. A complete estimands table aligned with ICH E9(R1) for the primary endpoint, including a clear classification and handling strategy for each intercurrent event was provided. The intercurrent events and the handling of these events are appropriate for the population included in the M14TIL clinical study. Supplementary and sensitivity analyses for the primary estimand were performed, including the Fleming-Harrington weighted log-rank test, a landmark analysis and the restricted mean survival time (RMST) approach. These analyses constitute sensitivity analyses, as they rely on the same primary estimand while applying alternative estimators or analytical approaches. No supplementary estimands, as defined in ICH E9(R1), were introduced. An overall administrative censoring rule defined as the earliest of last date known alive or data cut off, together with a statement that non administrative censoring was limited to patients who did not receive treatment, with no loss to follow up or withdrawals, and a limited description of two intercurrent events handled under a treatment policy strategy was provided. This does not constitute a complete, operationally reproducible specification for a RECIST based PFS derivation, where censoring is typically anchored to the date of the last adequate tumour assessment. Moreover, the provided patient listing shows at least one apparent internal inconsistency, for example a patient coded as died 0 and progressed 0 but not flagged as administratively censored, suggesting either listing errors or missing censoring categories not described. Finally, in the inspection context of incomplete or unclear audit trails (see section 2.4.3), post data cut off changes and the inability to confirm the integrity of CSR data outputs, the reliability of event and censoring derivations cannot be supported by a binary flag table alone. The Applicant stated that these sensitivity analyses were pre-specified, however, the estimand framework was not used in the SAP but implemented during the analytical phase post-hoc. The censoring rules were not completely described in the SAP and CSR which could jeopardize the integrity of the study and is considered of post-hoc nature. As well, death for other reason than melanoma was not used as censoring reason for PFS in the confirmatory phase III trial (M14TIL). Given the extent of issues uncovered during the GCP inspection (see section 2.4.3) and the open-label nature of the trial, this is seen as problematic.

The inclusion and exclusion criteria are overall adequate. Randomisation was stratified based on BRAF V600 mutation, line of treatment and treatment center which is appropriate. The reasons for longer intervals between randomisation and TIL infusion in some patients, mainly identified in metastasectomy related complications as described in section 5.3.2.1.2 can be understood.

In addition to the scans described in the study design above, in the IPI arm, a CT scan is performed prior to the 3rd ipilimumab infusion, approximately 5 weeks after the first dose, but it is not used for tumor response assessment. This scan can lead to early discontinuation of treatment if disease progression is detected, which may affect the apparent benefit of the ipilimumab control arm, as only 47.62% of patients completed all four cycles of treatment. The applicant noted that early discontinuation due to disease progression only occurred in 9 patients, based on clear signs of progression, but the specific criteria and potential impact on response rates are unclear. In addition, the applicant didn't provide a timeline of CT scans from the time of randomisation in the IPI arm. In the IPI arm, there was a 'baseline scan' performed at screening, within 4 weeks prior to randomisation. Yet, given that the article published on the pivotal study (Rohaan et al., 2022) states that the CT scan closest to start of treatment was used as baseline in both treatment groups, this could imply that the 5-week scan was used as baseline in some patients, if closer to the start of treatment than the 'baseline scan'. The issue remains unclear. In the Tacquell arm, patients undergo evaluations for response at 6, 12, and 18 weeks after Tacquell infusion, and then every 3 months thereafter. The scan conducted before Tacquell therapy ((before start of the preparative lymphodepleting chemotherapy) serves as the baseline for assessing tumor response in the Tacquell arm. Overall, the timeline of baseline and evaluation scans in the IPI arm is still unclear and remains an uncertainty.

The study was not blinded, and assessment of tumour response was per investigator. The Dutch National Health Care Institute (Zorginstituut Nederland) advised at an early stage that an independent radiologist should perform blinded central review of CT scans for determination of the primary endpoint. Then, between protocol versions 7 and 8, the study protocol was amended to indicate that the radiologic response was investigator assessed, as patients randomised for TIL-IMP therapy all underwent surgical resection of a tumour lesion, and the radiologist could therefore not be treatment blinded. A fully blinded central review is claimed infeasible because surgical resection for Tacquell manufacturing functionally identifies the treatment arm, a feasible independent mitigation to strengthen endpoint credibility (e.g. independent central review and/or independent adjudication/confirmation of progression events and dates under a predefined charter) has not been provided. Therefore, this major issue is not considered resolved.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

As regards to the concomitant medications, it was only specified that filgrastim could be used to treat the neutropenia and the concomitant medication prescribed for the treatment-related adverse events were recorded throughout the study. Moreover, the use of systemic corticoids for the treatment of AEs after Tacquell infusion should be avoided, unless absolutely required from a safety perspective. Antibiotics were allowed and were used after Tacquell infusion. For both arms, co-interventions were permitted, namely palliative (limited-field) radiation therapy for pain control to sites of bone disease present at baseline that were not target lesions, as well as surgical resection for palliative reasons for non-target lesions present at baseline. The primary analysis used standard methods for time-to-event outcomes, including Kaplan-Meier estimates, stratified log-rank test, and Cox proportional hazards regression. However, these were not explicitly linked to a predefined estimand, and the sensitivity analyses presented (including RMST and Fleming-Harrington weighted tests) were not described in a way that allows assessment of their relevance to missing data, intercurrent events or potential informative censoring. In particular, the use of a complete-case approach without justification raises concerns regarding potential bias from informative censoring. Additional analyses such as RMST and Fleming-Harrington weighted tests were performed but were not prespecified in the SAP. The absence of formal multiplicity control for secondary endpoints further limits confirmatory strength. Overall, while the statistical methods applied are standard, they fall short of current regulatory expectations in the absence of fully defined estimands and supporting sensitivity analyses. In addition, in the context of the amendments made to the protocol and the open label nature of the study, these changes and the methodological considerations regarding the use of the weighted-log rank analysis and the RMST approach lacks of justification and contextualisation (for instance in light of any exchanges with the DSMB) to ensure that the integrity of the study was maintained at all times. Overall, the open-label design of the study is acknowledged. Nonetheless, several important changes occurred during the study, including the restriction of the primary analysis to the TM001 subgroup introduced post hoc due to comparability issues. These adaptations, while possibly justified, reduce the confirmatory nature of the trial and impact the generalizability of the findings. In addition, the routine GCP inspection conclusion adds to the difficulty to interpret the results. Indeed, the GCP inspection reported multiple findings, affecting the quality of the data reported (see section 2.4.3 for the specific criticalities and major findings). There were multiple serious and significant shortcomings in the data management processes, a general lack of control over key computerised systems used in the trial as well as significant shortcomings in the statistical analysis processes. At the sponsor location, 'not all key clinical trial processes were governed by QMS documents such as SOPs. For multiple key processes, SOPs had only been put in place late, well after the start of the trial.' At the investigator site, Inadequate Deviation Management and Documentation was observed. Altogether, these findings make impossible for the inspectors to confirm integrity of the data represented in the CSR and warned that the trial results could be used in an MAA-procedure only with major reservations. Following this inspection outcome, the committee considered that there are major concerns in the pivotal clinical trial which impact the reliability, integrity, traceability, and reconstructability of the clinical data package and finally that the quality of the data cannot be acceptable. To mitigate these shortcomings the applicant provided additional source data verification activities for CAPA measures and other corrective actions including replication of the statistical analysis of the efficacy and safety parameters by an independent statistician. However, this exercise does not fully alleviate concerns regarding data integrity in the eCRF; the reliability of the pivotal evidence

package remains not established.

From 199 patients who were screened at the start of the phase III study, 168 patients were randomized with 84 patients who were assigned to the TIL arm and 84 patients to the IPI arm, this was the total FAS population. The Full Analysis Set population corresponds to the randomized patients and is the same as the intention-to-treat (ITT). The Per Protocol Analysis Set (PPS) consisted of all patients that received treatment. Given the proposed indication, the applicant has also defined an FAS including patients with prior anti-PD-1 treatment, which comprises 145 patients in total (117 at the NKI site and 28 at the CCIT site). Considering the patients who received a prior anti-PD-1 treatment at the NKI sites, 58 were assigned to ipilimumab arm of which 57 were treated and 59 were assigned to the TIL arm of which 57 were infused with TM001. Compared to clinical trial with other immunotherapies the numbers of patients randomised per arm is limited. Nevertheless, considering the nature of the medicinal products and the limitation related to the manufacturing process, no concerns will be raised.

The protocol deviation log omitted multiple protocol deviations, as also highlighted in the GCP inspection report, and hence the CSR contains insufficient information on potentially significant protocol deviations that occurred during the trial. In some cases contradictory information have been provided on same patient in regards to screening (patient with screening failure but with metastasectomy done to be save for later and the patient was not included or randomised), randomisation to the respective arm (a patient was initially reported as randomised to IPI arm of the study when later was confirmed to have been randomised to the TIL Arm), etc. Therefore, the conflicting information provided regarding these patients illustrates the lack of data control and concerns remain related to the screening and randomization process, as well as the handling of protocol deviations.

Following a post-trial comparability exercise performed by the Applicant, it was concluded that the cells manufactured on the Dutch sites were not similar to the cells manufactured on the Danish site. The Applicant therefore decided to separate data from the Dutch sites and the Danish site and present data from the Dutch sites as pivotal data for this MAA. Separate clinical results for each manufacturing site and per treatment centre (NKI and CCIT) for patients who received prior anti-PD1 treatment as well as the pooled data of the patients who received prior anti-PD1 treatment (N=145) were requested during the assessment. Further, the Applicant was asked to submit comparative KM curves for PFS and OS versus the Dutch site, NKI and discuss any deviation between sites. while the non-comparability is acknowledged. To justify this exclusion, the applicant claims that, as the sub-analysis (of Dutch patients with prior anti-PD-1 treatment) was performed after completion of patient enrolment, influence of potential bias in the execution of the trial was limited. However, this does not address the inspection-related concerns on data integrity and reconstructability of key analysis datasets and outputs and this statement does not include a specific reconciliation showing that the key efficacy endpoints and analysis datasets underpinning the restricted claim can be considered accurate and complete given the inspection conclusions affecting the full data package and the CSR outputs, including data management and statistical analysis processes.

Overall, the credibility of the efficacy demonstration remains methodologically poor. While it is acknowledged that the non-comparability between the Danish and the Dutch sites and the changing therapeutic landscape are arguments for the performed subgroup analysis, the justification for breaking the ITT principle is considered not acceptable as indeed the full ITT (n=168) is considered the main efficacy population. Furthermore, the lack of comparative efficacy data between the sites is considered highly concerning.

## Efficacy data and additional analyses

Main study M14TIL

In the full ITT population (N=168), patient characteristics were well-balanced between the arms. Similarly, the baseline characteristics were mostly similar between the TM001 and IPI treatment arms at the NKI site (N=117). No information on the race or ethnic background of patients in the confirmatory phase III trial (M14TIL) and hospital exemption program (H23TIL) was collected. The majority of the patients had stage IV melanoma, with only 2 patients in each study arm with unresectable IIIC melanoma at study entry. Since there is no biological rationale for expecting less response to immunotherapy in these patients than in patients with stage IV melanoma, and that treatment guidelines also generally recommend the same first-line treatment approach in these patients, the extrapolation of data from stage IV patients to stage IIIC is acceptable. Treatment arms were balanced in terms of baseline brain and liver metastases, M-category and LDH levels.

Patients were stratified by BRAF mutation status. Among patients who received prior anti-PD1 therapy, 27 (45.76%) patients in the TM001 arm and 23 patients (39.66%) in the IPI arm had a BRAF V600 mutation melanoma. While the CSR only mentioned anti-PD1 as prior systemic therapies, based on the list of prior systemic therapies and in the total FAS, some patients were treated with BRAF inhibitor with or without MEK inhibitors.

In the ITT population of the M14TIL trial trial, four patients (two per arm) had prior BRAF/MEK inhibitor therapy; none were in the target (mITT) population, where anti-PD-1 was the only permitted prior treatment. Most patients received TIL before BRAF/MEK inhibitors. Two patients (4%) received BRAF/MEK inhibitors after adjuvant anti-PD-1 and before starting the Tacquell therapy, but it is unclear whether others had multiple prior lines (e.g., anti-PD-1 followed by IPI/NIVO).

Very scant information on the use of BRAF/MEKi as first-line therapy in the unresectable Stage III/IV setting prior to Tacquell therapy from both the M14TIL study and the H23HE programme are available. As rationale to treat patients with Tacquell before the inhibitors, the Applicant cited some publications (Dennison, 2021; Dushyanthen, 2017; Liu et al., 2015; Vella et al., 2014) showing that MEK inhibitors impaired activation of TILs in in vitro and in vivo settings.

The ORR of BRAF/MEKi as the first line of treatment after Tacquell therapy was 10/14 (71%) and after IPI treatment was 18/19 (95%), results that the applicant does not consider substantially different given the low patients numbers. These ORR rates are also in line with what would be expected for BRAF/MEKi in these patients. A retrospective study (Pedersen 2021) showing similar OS/PFS in patients receiving BRAF/MEK inhibitors post-Tacquell versus those without prior TIL therapy was provided. However, the indirect comparison lacks statistical rigor. No proper clinical trial was performed to establish non inferiority or superiority of TIL over MEK inhibitor /BRAF inhibitor after antiPD1 progression in patients with BRAFV600 mutated melanoma.

Overall, the available evidence does not exclude a potential loss of treatment opportunity for patients with BRAF-mutant melanoma treated with TACQUEL before BRAF/MEK inhibitors.

## Outcomes and estimation

At the DCO 31-Dec-2023, the median follow-up time in the FAS/ITT (n=168) was 52.42 months (95% CI 46.14 - 58.56) being 55.80 months (95% CI, 45.52 - 70.92) and 49.95 months (95% CI, 42.37 to 58.56) in the TIL-IMP and IPI arm, respectively. The follow-up time is considered adequate for the claimed indication.

In the FAS (ITT population), the median PFS assessed according to RECIST was 6.81 months (95% CI, 4.19 - 12.47) in the TIL-IMP arm, compared to 3.06 (95% CI, 2.97 - 4.32) months in the IPI arm; the analysis of the curves showed an HR between ipilimumab and TM001 arms of 0.52 [95% CI 0.37-0.73; P-value &lt;0.001]). The proportional hazards assumption was found not to be violated. The statistical analysis plan specified that PFS was to be compared between the ipilimumab and TIL arms using a logrank test, and the Fleming-Harrington weighted log rank test putting more weight on events that occur later in time, as well as the Restricted Mean Survival Time (RMST) analysis as sensitivity analyses. A significant difference in PFS between ipilimumab and TIL in favor of TIL (χ2 = 8.69, p = 0.003) was observed when using the Fleming-Harrington weighted log rank test. In the RMST analysis, the restricted mean survival time for the ipilimumab arm was 7.9 months and for the TIL arm was 16.7 (p = 3.96 × 10-4). Thus, the primary endpoint can be said to be met, again bearing in mind the methodological concerns described above. In the FAS/ITT, the 6-month PFS event-free rate was 21.4% (95%CI: 14.23-32.27) in the ipilimumab arm and 52.4% (95%CI: 42.72-64.23) in the TIL arm. PFS at six months for the PPS population was 52.5% (95% CI, 42.62 - 64.67) in the TIL-IMP arm and 19.5% (95% CI, 12.57 - 30.28) in the IPI arm. PFS according to irRC was similar to PFS per RECIST. In the modified ITT population (ie the patients from the NKI site, previously treated with anti-PD-1), at time of data cut-off on 31 December 2023, the overall median follow-up time was 45.52 months (95% CI = 38.62 - 54.82 being 45.52 months (95% CI, 38.98 - 60.37) and 42.37 months (85% CI, 37.20 - 57.64) in the TM001 and IPI arm, respectively The primary endpoint, progression free survival (PFS) according to RECIST 1.1, showed that the median PFS is 2-fold longer after the TIL treatment compared to ipilimumab arm (6.65 months (95% CI, 4.06 - 12.47) in TIL arm, compared to 3.08 months in the IPI arm (95% CI, 3.01 - 4.48)). The analysis of the curves showed an HR between ipilimumab and TM001 arms of 0.49 [95% CI 0.32-0.73; P-value &lt;0.001]). In general, PFS was shown to be largely consistent across subgroups based on gender, age, site, type of prior therapy, BRAF mutation, WHO status, LDH (&lt;ULN), disease stage, brain metastasis, liver metastasis, and follow-up systemic therapy. For the patients from the NKI site, previously treated with anti-PD-1, the PFS rate at 6 months was of 50.85% (95% CI, 39.57 - 65.35) in TM001 arm compared to 18.97% (95% CI, 11.14 - 32.28) in IPI arm. Assessment of PFS according to irRC also showed a PFS of 5.4 months (95% CI, 3.96 - 12.83) in patients treated with TM001 compared to 3.55 months (95% CI, 3.03 - 4.84) in patients treated with IPI which is line with the mPFS assessed according to RECIST 1.1. These results suggest a clinical response for the patients treated in the TIL. However, bearing in mind the methodological concerns described above, no conclusion will be drawn with regards to clinical benefit. The ORR in the FAS/ITT population was 50.0% (95% CI, 38.9 - 61.1) in the TIL-IMP arm, as compared to 21.4% (95% CI, 13.2 - 31.7 [p&lt;0.001]) in the IPI arm. The ORR results in the IPI arm are equivalent to those seen in comparable trials. Thus, response rates in the TIL arm appear substantially higher than those in the control arm, while duration of response rates are more comparable between the two arms. For the patients from the NKI site, previously treated with anti-PD-1, the percentage of patients with an objective response according to RECIST 1.1 was found to be significantly higher (p&lt;0.001) in

patients that received TM001 therapy, compared to patients that received the comparator IPI

## Supportive studies

M14TIL side study: The M14TIL-side-study included 4 patients, and based on the very limited information provided, the side-study is not considered to provide further evidence to the dossier.

Hospital Exemption program: The median PFS assessed according to RECIST 1.1 was 3.48 months after the TIL infusion for the 50 TM001-treated patients included in this analysis and the PFS at six months after TM001 infusion was 39.96% (95% CI 27.17 - 58.79) for these patients. This result of 3.48 months is different to the median PFS of 6.665 months obtained in the pivotal study because PFS in the hospital exemption program was calculated from the day of TM001 infusion, whereas in the confirmatory phase III trial PFS was calculated from the day of randomization, with a median time between randomization and infusion of 49 days (range 29 - 356 days).

The overall survival was also shorter than in the pivotal study, at 13.93 months, as compared to 25.04 months in the TIL arm in the pivotal study. However, the median follow-up for OS was only median 9.36 months. Response rates were also somewhat lower than in the pivotal study, with an ORR of 34%, including 8% with a CR. The efficacy results from the HE programme are numerically somewhat lower than those in the pivotal study but still provide some corroboration of the results seen in the pivotal study.

Updated results were provided for 91 patients treated in the hospital exemption program (data cut-off 08OCT2025) showing similar results as for the first analysis of 50 Patients. Updated PFS and OS results of patients who received the IPI/NIVO in first-line setting were also provided, showing same trends as the results from patients who received first-line anti-PD-1 and adjuvant anti-PD1.

## 5.3.5.2. Conclusions on the clinical efficacy

The routine GCP inspection (EMA/IN/0000276203) concluded that multiple significant findings constituting (often significant) non-compliance with ICH-GCP E6 R2 have been raised. Given the inspection conclusion that data integrity, completeness and correctness of the CSR could not be unambiguously established, it remains insufficient to re-establish the reliability of the pivotal evidence package.

The several methodological and statistical issue as described above which are supported by the GCP inspection, prevents from providing clear evidence of efficacy for Tacquel. Indeed, the methodological explanations provided do not reconcile the primary efficacy endpoints and analysis outputs with the documented limitations affecting computerised systems, data management/audit trail, and reconstructability of statistical outputs. Furthermore, given the changing treatment landscape during the extended time period in which the clinical trial was run, it is not clear whether, and in which setting the product might provide a clinical benefit. Further, given the concerns with GCP and study methodology in general, including the violation of the ITT principle through the exclusion of data from the Danish site, the reliability of the data from the study is in question.

In this context, it remains to be demonstrated, for the pivotal efficacy evidence package, that the key efficacy source data and derived endpoints (notably PFS events and censoring) are reliable and consistently derived from traceable source documentation and final locked datasets.

All in all, no positive efficacy outcome has been shown in the sought indication.

The CHMP endorses the CAT conclusion on clinical efficacy as described above.

## 5.4. Clinical safety

## 5.4.1. Safety data collection

Overall, 169 patients were exposed to TIL throughout the clinical development: 35 patients in 2 phase 1/2 studies, 80 in the M14TIL, 4 in the side study, and 50 in the hospital exemption program H23TIL program. Only data from study M14TIL, where 80 patients received TIL (64 at NKI and 16 at CCIT) is considered for assessing safety.

The safety data was collected at a reasonable frequency (during chemotherapy, at TIL infusion, and during IL2 treatment, with complete AE assessment at 6, 12, 18, 24 weeks, then every 3 months up to 3 years and every 6 months up to 5 years or end of study. Causality was assessed by investigator by phase of treatment and overall. Nevertheless, the 3 treatment phases cannot be dissociated and the whole regimen is to be considered for safety assessment.

The first patient was enrolled in the study on 23 September 2014 and end of study was 31 December 2023.

## 5.4.2. Patient exposure

## Phase 3 confirmatory Study: M14TIL

Patients assigned to the TIL-investigational medicinal product (TIL-IMP) therapy group received a single intravenous TIL-IMP infusion containing 5E9 to 2E11 viable cells. The TIL-IMP infusion was preceded by a preparative non-myeloablative lymphodepleting chemotherapy regimen and followed by supportive intravenous administration of IL-2.

Preparative chemotherapy consisted of a NMA-LD regimen of cyclophosphamide (60 mg/kg/day x 2 days IV and Mesna 300mg/m 2 ) and fludarabine (25 mg/m 2 /day x 5 days IV). After TIL-IMP infusion, in-vivo outgrowth of T-cells from the TIL-IMP was supported by high dose IL-2 IV (600,000 IU/kg/dose every 8 hours) for a maximum of 15 times.

Patients assigned to the IPI group received 3 mg/kg IPI administered intravenously every 3 weeks, for a maximum of 4 doses, following SmPC recommendations of Yervoy.

For the phase 3 study, TIL was manufactured at 3 sites. Clinical outcome of the confirmatory phase III trial was positive and there appeared no differences between the clinical sites in clinical outcome parameters, however, a post-trial product quality comparability analysis showed the manufacturing process differences between the Dutch manufacturing sites and the Danish manufacturing site. For this reason, TIL-IMP includes any TIL product administered in the confirmatory phase III trial, while TM001 is the TIL-IMP product manufactured by the Dutch sites that is subject to this MAA.

## 5.4.11. Overall discussion and conclusions on clinical safety

## 5.4.11.1. Discussion

## 5.4.11.1.1. Overall assessment of available safety data

The safety analysis is based on the data from the phase 3 pivotal study (M14TIL), a multicenter, openlabel, randomized phase III trial comparing TM001 to standard ipilimumab treatment in patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma. Data from the M14TIL 'side study' and the hospital exemption program H23TIL in The Netherlands are supportive. According to the Applicant, number and severity of AEs from the H23TIL hospital exemption programme is in line with the previously gained knowledge on TIL therapy. However, this cannot be assessed at present due to the uncertainties of the safety data from the phase III trial.

The treatment regimen consisted of Cyclophosphamide on D-7 and D-6 and Fludarabine on D-5 to D-1, TIL infusion on D0, and interleukin IL2 on D0 to D5. Cyclophosphamide and Fludarabine dose was as recommended in the SmPC (60mg/kg/day and respectively 25mg/m2/day). Proleukin planned dose was 600,000UI/Kg every 8 hours, in line with the product label approved by FDA, but considerably higher than recommended in the SmpC (18 *106UI/m2/day) in the EU labelling (of note, Proleukin is not centrally authorized in the EU). Adverse events were collected and graded by the investigator by phase of treatment and overall. Nevertheless, the treatment phases cannot be dissociated and the whole regimen is to be considered for safety assessment.

Overall, 169 patients were exposed to TIL throughout the clinical development: 35 patients in 2 phase 1/2 studies, 80 in the M14TIL, 4 in the side study, and 50 in the hospital exemption program H23TIL. For the phase 1/2 studies, the applicant states that no CSRs were compiled as the data was reported in publications and therefore safety data does not contribute to this MAA.

The pivotal phase 3 study M14TIL was conducted at 2 sites (CCIT in Denmark and NKI in the Netherlands). The first patient was enrolled on 23SEP2014 and the end of study was 31DEC2023.

The safety data in the application is focussed on the M14TIL study and is therefore limited. In study M14TIL, 80 patients received TIL (64 at NKI and 16 at CCIT). While the manufacturing process of TIL differed in terms of quality characteristics between the two sites, no difference in terms of safety is observed.

The duration of follow-up for the M14TIL study was 54.4 months (46 - 58 months). Data cut-off was 31DEC2023 in the 'side study' and 06NOV2024 in the H23TIL program. The applicant states that the standard safety follow-up period in the M14TIL trial was 28 days following the last study treatment (defined as the day of discharge in the Tacquell arm or the day of administration of the last IPI dose in the IPI arm) for AEs and 100 days following the last study treatment for SAEs and AEs clearly related to the study treatment. Unresolved AEs within the 28-day period were monitored until resolution, up to 100 days post-treatment. The lack of systematic follow-up for AEs beyond 100 days is an important limitation to the ability to fully characterize the long-term safety of Tacquell. The absence of detailed data on mean, median, minimum, and maximum safety follow-up durations remain a gap in the safety assessment.

In terms of dose of TIL, the target dose range in the M14TIL, 'side study', and H23TIL was 5E9 to 2E11 viable cells. The median dose administered in the M14-TIL was 40.90 E9 with a range of 4.90 - 110.40 E9 cells. There seems to be no relation between the TIL dose and the safety profile.

## Adverse events

MedDRA version 28.1 has been used for coding adverse events.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The safety analysis set is the PPS and consists of all patients treated with Tacquell (n=80). In the TIL arm, it is noted that the frequency of adverse events is comparable between the patients with prior anti-PD1 treatment (n=69) and the total number of patients treated (n=80).

The summary of clinical safety (SCS) submitted by the Applicant is not considered acceptable. The document contains very few organised data. Usual tabulated overview of all AEs/SAE regardless of causality, by SOC frequencies and at the PT level (all and grade 3-4) are not included in the SCS or in the Clinical study report (CSR). Tabulated treatment-related AEs with onset prior to TIL infusion and respectively after the TIL infusion, and by stage of the treatment regimen (tumour cell harvesting, NMA-LD, TIL-infusion or IL-2), by SOC and PT and by grade are not included as well. Only raw data are available, with the exception of data specific to AEs that occurred following the tumour harvest, which are not suitable for the expected assessment.

Clinically significant abnormal findings or other abnormal assessments that were associated with the disease being studied, unless considered by the investigator as more severe than expected for the patient's condition, or that presented or were detected at the start of the study and did not worsen, were not reported as AEs or SAEs. Events that clearly occurred due to progressive disease were not reported as an SAE. It remains unclear if the AE/SAE were reported in line with the ICH scientific guidelines, which states: 'All adverse events occurring after initiation of study treatments (including events likely to be related to the underlying disease or likely to represent concomitant illness, unless there is a prior agreement with the regulatory authority to consider specified events as disease related) should be displayed in summary tables.' While the applicant argues that the exclusion of disease-related events does not lead to the underreporting of treatment-related (S)AEs or inaccurate ADR frequencies, this assumption is not substantiated. The exclusion of events such as liver dysfunction, respiratory compromise, and malignant effusions, despite being attributed to disease progression, could theoretically overlap with known toxicities of Tacquell therapy. As such, the applicant's approach to AE/SAE reporting is not supported and does not comply with EMA guidelines.

The figures included in this report are based on the CSR dated 10DEC2025, unless otherwise specified.

Overall in the TIL-IMP arm 80 patients reported a total of 3852 AEs, with 3 times higher frequency than in the IPI-arm (82 patients reported a total of 1090 AEs). The frequency of any TEAE was the same in both arms (100%); the frequency of Grade &gt;=3 TEAE and Grade &gt;=3 treatment-related TEAE was higher in the TIL than IPI arm (100% vs 71.95% and 100% vs 57.3%). The frequency of SAE and treatment-related SAE was higher in the IPI than TIL group (43.9% vs 16.3% and 26.8% vs 15%); this imbalance should be interpreted with caution due to potential misclassification of SAE in the TIL arm.

As stated above, only the most common 'treatment related TEAE' (i.e. whole regimen) are reported in the most recent SCS and include, amongst others by PT: neutrophil count decreased 100%[EA1.1][STeo1.2], fever 93%, anaemia and nausea 91% each, febrile neutropenia 89%, chills 84%, platelet count decreased 83%, fatigue 75%. Information on the time to onset and duration/time to resolution of events has not been provided. According to the applicant, 'the majority of AEs are resolved before discharge of the patients from the hospital and within the first 1-3 months after Tacquell therapy' This raise concerns on the consistency and adequacy of the safety follow-up.

By phase of treatment, in the TIL arm, frequency of adverse events reported as related to the NMA-LD treatment (cyclophosphamide and fludarabine) was 100%, to IL-2 98.75%, to TM-001 95%, and to TM-001/IL2 100%. However, one cannot distinguish to which specific treatment the events are related.

Most common TEAE reported before TIL infusion includes by PT: neutrophil count decreased, platelet count decreased, anaemia, febrile neutropenia, nausea, and white blood cells decreased, consistent with the chemotherapy known safety profile.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The most common TEAE reported after the TIL infusion were anaemia, fatigue, fever, rash, febrile neutropenia, dyspnoea, nausea, hypoalbuminemia, and sinus tachycardia.

The study protocol allowed up to 15 doses of IL2; however, in practice only a median of 5 doses could be administered due to toxicity. There was no relation between the dose of TIL, the number of doses of IL2 and the safety profile. The most common TR-TEAE reported as related to IL2 were fever, chills, dyspnea, febrile neutropenia, hypophosphatemia, fatigue. Most events were of Grade 1-3.

In the IPI arm, the most common treatment-related TEAE are: diarrhoea and fatigue (45.1% each), pruritus (41.6%), nausea (36.6%), consistent with the known safety profile of IPI. Most of the events were Grade &lt;= 3.

In the updated SCS, the most common Grade &gt;=3 TR-TEAE reported in the TIL arm were neutrophil count decreased 95%, febrile neutropenia 89%, platelet count decreased 80%, WBC decreased 71%, hypophosphatemia 66%%, fever 45%, anaemia 20%.

The most common Grade 4 treatment related TEAE were neutrophil count decreased 91%, WBC decreased 70%, platelet count decreased 60%. These are consistent with the safety profile of cyclophospamide, fludarabine, and IL2. Median duration of Grade 3-4 neutrophil count decreased was 7 days (2-58 days).

In the IPI arm, the Grade 3 AE with highest frequency were colitis 18.29%, diarrhoea 14.63%, elevated ALT/AST 8.54/7.32%. In total 9 AE were Grade 4 (elevated ALT/AST, colitis, GGT increased, lipase increased, and hyperglycemia).

In the supportive EAP program H23TIL, the safety profile appears consistent with the pivotal M14TIL study (data not shown), however due to uncertainties on safety data collection it is not possible to consider these data as contributing to the assessment.

## Adverse drug reactions (ADR)

ADRs are considered those related to any component of the treatment regimen (tumour harvest, NMALD, TIL and IL-2. It is not possible to isolate the events really related to TIL alone due to the fact that IL2 treatment starts the same day as TIL infusion. In addition, since the IL2 dose is considerably higher than in the Proleukin product authorized in some EU countries, the ADR will capture the TIL and the likely higher toxicity of IL2.

## AESI (adverse events of special interest)

No AESI were pre-planned. It is stated that autoimmune toxic effects associated with melanocytedirected reactivity are expected. The applicant states that expected (due to the mechanism of action) immune related side effects (skin/hair colour hypopigmentation, impaired vision and uveitis, impaired hearing) occurred in few patients (data not shown). Cytokine release syndrome (CRS), typically observed with CAR-T cell therapies, was not observed with Tacquell. However, it should be noted that CRS has been observed with other TILs, and its absence with Tacquell may be due to the fact that it was not specifically investigated or monitored in the study.

According to the provided appendices, elevation of IL-6 was reported during the study, which may be a symptom of cytokine release syndrome (CRS). However, levels of IL-6 were not systematically measured and CRS was not systematically assessed. This may give rise to underestimation of this effect.

Capillary leak syndrome (CLS) is reported in 30% of patients in the M14TIL study and is a potentially life-threatening AE considered related to treatment with IL-2. Uveitis has been reported in 6 patients, including 2 SAE considered related to the TIL infusion. In one of the phase 2 studies, vitiligo was reported in 37% of patients, and hypopigmentation was reported in 9/80 patients in M14TIL study. Immune-related side effects associated with the mechanism of action of Tacquell, especially recognition of healthy melanocytes in organs like the eye, the skin and the ear, that were observed after Tacquell therapy in a minority of the M14TIL and H23TIL Tacquell target population, is to be considered among the selected ADRs.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

SAE (serious adverse events) In study M14TIL, the number of SAE in the IPI arm was almost 3 times higher than in the TIL arm. The incidence of SAEs in the TIL-IMP arm is unexpectedly low, given that the treatment protocol involves tumour harvest, chemotherapy and IL-2. One would anticipate a higher frequency of SAEs, such as infections and cytopenias, with febrile neutropenia also being a potentially expected SAE. Since patients were already hospitalized for the procedure, only AE which led to transfer to ICU, resulted in prolonged hospitalization or death were considered SAE. The median duration of hospitalization was 17 days (12-38 days) and 8 patients (10%) were admitted to ICU for a median duration of 4 days (1-24 days). Hospitalization was not considered an SAE and the applicant states that there were no AEs which resulted in prolonged hospitalization. However, AE which would have required hospitalization (e.g. Grade 4 AE) if the patient was not already hospitalized were not reported. This induces important uncertainties on the classification and reporting of SAE and the rate of SAE cannot be reliably estimated. With this caveat, the SAE considered by the applicant related to TIL were Grade 3 and Grade 4 (1 patient each) acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), Grade 3 pleural effusion (1 patient), and Grade 2 and Grade 3 (1 patient each) uveitis, Grade 3 pneumonitis. According to the narratives, the Grade 4 ARDS has not resolved; all other Serious ADR either stabilized or resolved. In the IPI arm, the majority of SAE were Grade 3 and occurred in 1-2 patients each. There were 4 Grade 4 AE: colitis, pulmonary thromboembolism, and Stevens-Johnson syndrome (1 patient each) and hyperglycaemia (2 patients). There was 1 SAE of Grade 5: intracranial haemorrhage. Of the 58 SAE reported, 34 were considered related to IPI. Deaths With respect to deaths, the safety data provided are inconsistent and incomplete. The clinical study report (CSR) indicates that only one patient died during the study (TIL-IMP arm), but the SAE narratives indicate a total of four deaths, which also appears to be inaccurate when compared to the provided overall survival (OS) data.

## 5.4.11.2. Conclusions on clinical safety

Overall, there are significant uncertainties related to limited follow-up, classification of SAE and absence of death narratives. The collection methodology, which excluded AEs not classified as \"clearly related\" beyond 28 days post-discharge, is an important limitation to the completeness of the safety data. The protocol-defined follow-up periods of 28 days for AEs and 100 days for SAEs are not considered sufficient to capture delayed-onset adverse events, which are relevant for assessing the long-term safety of Tacquell. The definition of «treatment related» is not clear, and a proper assessment of causality has not been provided. Consequently, at this time, the ADRs being proposed by the Applicant do not allow to conduct a thorough safety assessment. SAEs were misclassified. The lack of detailed narratives for deaths is highly concerning as they are critical for conducting a thorough benefit-risk assessment. In addition, there are numerous unresolved issues which in totality raise concern about the suitability of the safety clinical package to support a MA dossier.

Furthermore, irrespective of these methodological aspects consideration, the safety profile of Tacquell is considered unfavourable, with very high frequency of adverse events and Grade &gt;=3, considerably higher than the comparator Ipilimumab. The high toxicity is driven by the pre-TIL chemotherapy (which is mandatory prior to TIL cells infusion) and high doses of IL2 treatment (needed for the TIL in vivo persistence and outgrowth). The toxicity of TIL in itself is not possible to be characterized, due to concomitant IL2 treatment and the complex treatment regimen. In the context of this high toxicity, cases of death attributed to progression of disease but potentially due to adverse events represent an important concern.

In conclusion, the significant uncertainties in the methodology, in particular limited safety data collection and follow-up, potential misclassifications of SAEs as AEs, and lack of detailed death narratives, preclude the assessment of the safety profile of Tacquell.

The CHMP endorses the CAT conclusion on clinical safety as described above.

## 8. Benefit-risk assessment

## 8.1. Therapeutic context

The proposed indication for Tacquell is the treatment of adult patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma following prior anti-Programmed Death-1 (PD-1)-based checkpoint inhibitor treatment given either as first line treatment for unresectable stage III or IV disease, or given as (neo)adjuvant therapy.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

## 8.1.1. Disease or condition, proposed therapeutic indication

Melanoma is a cancer type that derives from melanocytes. Once the melanoma is locally advanced and has spread to the lymph nodes (stage III) or is spread to organs or other parts of the body (stage IV), it is called advanced stage disease. Patient prognosis is related to the disease stage. Cutaneous melanoma is one of the most common malignancies, accounting for 1.7% of global cancer diagnoses according to GLOBOCAN. The risk of developing melanoma increases with age, possibly related to a greater cumulative exposure to UV radiation.

Melanoma has an estimated yearly increasing incidence of &gt;150.000 newly diagnosed cases per year in Europe (Dyba et al., 2021). In Europe, the incidence rate is 10 - 25 new melanoma cases per 100,000 inhabitants, with a dramatic increase seen in the last years in many parts of Europe for all age groups. Most diagnoses are made prior to the development of lymph node or distant metastases (stage III to IV, advanced disease). The risk of developing melanoma increases with age, possibly related to a greater cumulative exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation. However, melanoma can also develop on areas of the skin that are not UV-exposed (Dennis et al., 2008; Rozeman et al., 2018; Ting et al., 2007).

## 8.1.2. Available therapies and unmet medical need

Patients with stage IIIC to IV melanoma were reported to have a poor prognosis in the past before approval of immunotherapy, with a 10-year survival of less than 5%, due to the very limited effect of traditional first-line therapies like radiation and chemotherapy (Balch et al., 2009; Bhatia &amp; Thompson, 2012).

The anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4) antibody ipilimumab (IPI) was the first immune checkpoint inhibitor to achieve a significant improvement in survival of advanced stage melanoma and was approved as a Standard of Care (SoC) for the management of those patients in Europe in 2011. A switch in clinical practice occurred with the approval of molecules targeting PD-1 as first-line therapy in 2015 and as adjuvant therapy for patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma in 2018.

Nevertheless, after failure of first-line treatment with anti-PD-1 antibodies, currently there are no therapies approved by the EMA specifically for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic melanoma that has progressed after treatment with ICIs/ targeted treatments. The recent ESMO guideline on cutaneous melanoma (T. Amaral et al, 2025), indicates that patients who do not respond to PD-1based immunotherapy have very limited therapeutic options available (which includes also experimental TILs treatment as potential therapy) and recommends clinical trials as the best option in this setting. This guideline also states that chemotherapy should be considered as a last-line treatment. Due to this limited clinical benefit of second-line treatment with the currently available immune checkpoint inhibitors and targeted therapies, there is a clear unmet medical need for novel treatment modalities for relapsed/refractory patients with advanced stage melanoma.

## 8.2. Main clinical studies

M14TIL is a Phase III multicenter, open-label, randomized trial to evaluate TIL-IMP therapy or ipilimumab administered intravenously in patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma. A total of 168 patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either ipilimumab (administered IV 3 mg/kg once every 3 weeks, maximal 4 times as described in the SmPC of Yervoy) or TIL-IMP therapy (a dose of minimum 5E9 up to a maximum of 2E11 viable cells administered IV over 20 to 30 min). Patients that were assigned to the TIL-IMP group underwent a metastasectomy to harvest tumour material required for the manufacturing of TIL-IMP. One week prior to the TIL-IMP infusion, patients were admitted to the hospital for administration of preparative non-myeloablative lymphodepleting chemotherapy intravenously (cyclophosphamide/fludarabine), followed by the TIL-IMP infusion and subsequent highdose (600,000 IU/kg IV over 15 min every 8 hours continued for up to 5 days) interleukin-2 (IL-2) intravenously to support the in-vivo outgrowth of T-cells from the TIL-IMP.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

At the time of study development, treatment with IPI was standard of care for patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma. For that reason, IPI was chosen as comparator for the phase III trial. However, during the course of the trial (2014-2023) the standard of care changed with the approval of anti-programmed death-1 (PD-1) immune checkpoint inhibitors in June 2015, resulting in the majority of patients included in the phase III trial being pre-treated with anti-PD-1-based checkpoint inhibitors. In addition, TIL-IMP products from one of the three manufacturing sites was found to be non-comparable. Therefore, the Applicant considered a subset of the data of the confirmatory phase III trial dataset as relevant to evaluate and this results in 59 patients in the TIL arm and 58 patients in the control arm.

The formal end of study was set to 31 December 2023 based on an event-driven decision (&gt;50% patients died). At this DCO, the overall median follow-up time for all 168 patients (FAS) was 52.42 months (95% CI, 46.14 - 58.56), being 55.80 months (95% CI, 45.52 - 70.92) and 49.95 months (95% CI, 42.37 to 58.56) in the TIL-IMP and IPI arm, respectively. For the patients from the NKI site, previously treated with anti-PD-1, at time of data cut-off, the overall median follow-up time was 45.52 months (95% CI, 38.62 - 54.82), being 45.52 months (95% CI, 38.98 - 60.37) and 42.37 months (85% CI, 37.20 - 57.64) in the TM001 and IPI arm, respectively.

## 8.3.1. Uncertainties and limitations about favourable effects

A routine GCP inspection (EMA/IN/0000276203) concluded that from a GCP perspective, reliability, integrity, traceability, and re-constructability of the clinical data package and therefore completeness and correctness of the CSR could not be unambiguously established, and that the trial results could only be used with major reservations and they remain insufficient to provide back the reliability of the pivotal evidence package. Although the applicant presented further actions, undertaken to mitigate the findings from the inspection, including source data verification and repetition of the statistical analysis by an independent statistician, this exercise has not been considered to fully alleviate concerns regarding data integrity in the eCRF. These elements did not alter the assessment conclusion, and the reliability of the pivotal evidence package remains not established.

In addition, the documented limitations affecting computerised systems, data management/audit trail, and re-constructability of statistical outputs further undermine any methodological explanations provided and would not strengthen the primary efficacy endpoints and analysis outputs. In this context, it remains to be demonstrated that the pivotal efficacy data are reliable and traceable to source documentation, and that endpoints (notably PFS events and censoring) are consistently and reproducibly derived from these data and the final locked datasets.

Further, in view of multiple amendments to the primary endpoint definition/analysis approach and the description of DSMB closed-session review including survival information by treatment arm, it remains unclear which comparative efficacy information was reviewed, when this occurred, and how confirmatory interpretability was preserved. Finally, in this open-label trial with investigator-assessed PFS, where a fully blinded central review is deemed infeasible, adequate independent mitigation measures (e.g. independent central review and/or independent adjudication/confirmation of progression events and dates under a predefined charter) have not been put in place to ensure the credibility and robustness of the endpoint. Furthermore, inadequate original pre-specification of censoring rules and the handling of intercurrent events, in an open-label study and key modifications to the primary endpoint framework and analysis strategy provide further level of methodological and statistical concerns limiting the interpretability of the pivotal study,

Therefore, the efficacy results are hampered by major methodological concerns and uncertainties.

Following a post-trial comparability exercise performed by the Applicant, it was concluded that the cells manufactured on the Dutch sites were not similar to the cells manufactured on the Danish site. The Applicant therefore decided to separate data from the Dutch sites and the Danish site and present data from the Dutch sites as pivotal data for this MAA. The indication sought was hence based on a subgroup of the patients in the pivotal trial, specifically those treated at the Dutch site and only those with prior anti-PD1 therapy, given either in the (neo)adjuvant or first-line setting. The ITT principle is not considered to have been followed since data from the Danish site are excluded. No separate clinical results of the patients treated at NKI and at CCIT were submitted and this lack of comparability raises concerns regarding the inability to assess whether efficacy results differ between the Danish and the other sites adding on the fragility of the rationale for the exclusion of the Danish data and the ensuing ITT violation. This also contributes in reducing the robustness and generalisability of the retained dataset, particularly in the context of an MAA based on a single pivotal randomized trial.

Given the start date of this trial, it is acknowledged that the sponsor chose ipilimumab in the control arm which was an appropriate comparator at the time of trial initiation. With ipilimumab/nivolumab becoming standard of care in the first line, the use of ipilimumab in the second line setting has been reduced. Therefore, the absence of a control arm in line with current treatment landscape leads to a challenging interpretation of the magnitude of the treatment effects with regards to the current available therapies used to treat adult patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma following prior anti-PD-1-based checkpoint inhibitor treatment given either as first line treatment for unresectable stage III or IV disease, or given as (neo)adjuvant therapy.

In the impossibility to define a clear benefit of the product due to the methodological and statistical aspects described above the efficacy of the proposed minimum dose has not been substantiated, and the proposed dose range still has to be justified.

Overall, 169 patients were exposed to TIL throughout the clinical development: 35 patients in 2 phase 1/2 studies, 80 in the M14TIL, 4 in the side study, and 50 in the hospital exemption program H23TIL. Only data from study M14TIL, where 80 patients received TIL (64 at NKI and 16 at CCIT) is considered for assessing safety.

Overall in the TIL-IMP arm 80 patients reported a total of 3852 AEs, with 3 times higher frequency than in the IPI-arm (82 patients reported a total of 1090 AEs). The following analysis is based on the M14TIL clinical study report for 80 patients treated with Tacquell.

The frequency of any TEAE was the same in both arms (100%); the frequency of Grade &gt;=3 TEAE and Grade &gt;=3 treatment-related TEAE was higher in the TIL than IPI arm (100% vs 71.95% and 100% vs 57.3%).

The Grade 4 events related to IL2 were hypophosphatemia and CPK increased (3 patients each).

In the TIL arm, 13 patients (16.25%) reported 26 SAEs and 12 patients (15%) reported at least 1 treatment related SAE compared to 22 patients (26.83%) in the IPI arm. All but one SAE (1 case of uveitis Grade 2) were Grade &gt;=3.

## 8.6. Benefit-risk assessment and discussion

## 8.6.1. Importance of favourable and unfavourable effects

The efficacy results presented from pivotal study M14TIL in patients with unresectable or metastatic melanoma may appear promising if taken in isolation from the methodological and statistical concerns. There is a relevant PFS difference between the study arms which pertains to the proportion of longer term non-progressors. Median PFS is roughly twice that in the ipilimumab control arm, and the median OS is also numerically improved relative to the control arm. In addition, ORR is higher in the TIL arm than the control arm, though the duration of response is comparable. The observed efficacy results could be considered clinically relevant. However, according to the GCP inspection, data integrity, completeness and correctness of the CSR could not be unambiguously established, it remains insufficient to ensure the reliability, integrity, traceability, and re-constructability of the pivotal evidence package.

Indeed, given the documented limitations in computerized systems, data management/ audit trails, and the re-constructability of statistical outputs, the reliability of the key efficacy source data and derived endpoints (including PFS events and censoring) could not be adequately demonstrated. Furthermore, these data could not be consistently verified against traceable source documentation and the final locked datasets.

Further, taking into account the several amendments to the primary endpoint definition/analysis approach and the description of DSMB closed-session review including survival information by treatment arm, it is difficult to derive a correct evaluation of efficacy. Moreover, in the frame of an open label trial, inadequate original pre-specification of censoring rules and the handling of intercurrent events, and the lack of an independent evaluation of PFS data (e.g. through central review and/or independent adjudication/confirmation of progression events and dates under a predefined charter) does not strengthen the endpoints' credibility.

Furthermore, the limitation of the PFS results is all the more marked by the fact that the enrolled patient population was treated under conditions (use of ipilimimab as comparator) that no longer reflect the current therapeutic setting, as the field in the treatment of melanoma has evolved since the randomised pivotal study started.

All in all, multiple methodological and statistical concerns limit the interpretability of the pivotal study. These include inadequate original pre-specification of censoring rules and the handling of intercurrent

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

events, the lack of an appropriate mitigation strategy for investigator-assessed PFS in an open-label study and key modifications to the primary endpoint framework and analysis strategy, as well, the post hoc restriction of the randomized comparison and the exclusion of a substantial proportion of patients from the primary analysis deviate from the ITT principle, reducing the robustness and generalisability of the retained dataset, particularly in the context of an MAA based on a single pivotal randomized trial.

The extent and severity of uncertainties regarding the safety are deemed critical and highly concerning. The absence of data on deaths, misclassification of SAE, limited safety data collection and the duration of safety follow-up impede an adequate assessment of the safety profile of Tacquell. There are therefore numerous unresolved concerns which in totality raise doubts on the adequacy of the clinical safety package in support of the MA dossier. Nevertheless, even if the safety profile provided is taken in isolation from the methodological concerns reported above, the data suggest an unfavourable safety profile, with very high frequency of adverse events and Grade &gt;=3 adverse events, considerably higher than the comparator Ipilimumab. The most common Grade 3 and 4 events were febrile neutropenia, neutropenia, platelet count decreased, hypophosphatemia, and CPK increased. The high toxicity is driven by the pre-TIL chemotherapy (which is mandatory prior to TIL cells infusion) and IL2 treatment (needed for the TIL in vivo persistence and outgrowth). The toxicity of TIL in itself is not possible to be characterized and isolated, due to concomitant IL2 and pre-TIL chemotherapy treatments. In the context of this high toxicity, cases of death due to adverse events are not clear and death narratives are not available.

All the above should also be seen in the frame of the quality issues which concerns: comparability between the clinical batches from the manufacturing sites, and between the M14TIL clinical trial batches and the commercial batches which is not established; incomplete documentation for the GMP compliance; the control strategy lacks the necessary robustness to ensure consistent quality, safety, and efficacy of the commercial medicinal product and furthermore the control strategy regarding the non-proliferation of feeder cells is inadequate, and aseptic process validation does not encompass the full feeder-cell manufacturing process.

Overall, due to the uncertainties and limitations of the design of the phase III trial, the limited data and the extent and severity of uncertainties regarding efficacy, and safety, together with the major concerns raised in the quality aspect of the dossier, data are not considered sufficient to support positive benefit/risk balance.

## 8.6.2. Balance of benefits and risks

As described in detail above, based on i) non-established comparability, ii) incomplete documentation about EU GMP compliance, iii) inadequate and non-robust control strategy to ensure consistent quality, safety and efficacy, iv) the critical results of the GCP inspection, v) the multiple methodological and statistical shortcomings, vi) the breaking of the ITT principle and vii) the non-comprehensive safety data set provided, it is currently considered that an adequate assessment of the potential clinical benefit over a not characterized safety profile is not possible at this stage and the clinical benefit of Tacquell in the claimed indication has not been demonstrated.

## 8.7. Benefit-risk conclusions

Due to all the above-described concerns the benefit-risk balance of Tacquell in the treatment of adult patients with unresectable stage IIIC to IV melanoma following prior anti-Programmed Death-1 (PD1)-based checkpoint inhibitor treatment given either as first line treatment for unresectable stage III or IV disease, or given as (neo)adjuvant therapy is negative.
