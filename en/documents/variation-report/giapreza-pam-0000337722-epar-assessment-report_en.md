## 1. Introduction

On 10 March 2026, the MAH submitted available data from paediatric study for Giapreza (study CRH04), in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

Study CRH04 was terminated early due to internal sponsor business reasons. At the time of termination, nine patients had been enrolled, of whom eight received study treatment (LJPC-501 or placebo). As enrolment was less than 10% of the planned sample size, the results are limited to descriptive summaries without statistical analyses.

A short critical expert overview has been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

Giapreza (angiotensin II) is a vasopressor that activates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), a well-characterised physiological system regulating vascular tone and blood pressure. The active substance is angiotensin II (as acetate). Giapreza has been assigned the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) code C01CX08, classifying it within \"other cardiac stimulants\".

Giapreza was authorised in the European Union via the centralised procedure in August 2019 for the treatment of refractory hypotension in adults with septic or other distributive shock who remain hypotensive despite adequate volume repletion and the use of catecholamines and other available vasopressor therapies.

At the time of the initial authorisation, a post-authorisation efficacy study (PAES) (study LJ501-CRH06) was included in Annex II, with a due date for submission of final results in June 2024, to provide further controlled data on morbidity, mortality, and ischaemic and thromboembolic events associated with angiotensin II. Following the change of MAH to PAION, the MAH considered the originally agreed PAES design not feasible to conduct as planned.

In this context, the renewal of the marketing authorisation was granted on 16 May 2024 by the European Commission, with a commitment by the MAH to present a feasible and scientifically sound clinical study design to the Scientific Advice Working Party (SAWP).

In the scientific advice requested in October 2024 (Procedure EMA/SA/0000154991), the Applicant proposed to modify the agreed PAES to a non-interventional, multicentre study consisting of a Giapreza treatment arm (combining retrospectively and prospectively included patients) and a retrospective historical-control arm receiving standard of care prior to giapreza availability.

However, the replacement of the prospective PAES by a non-interventional retrospective/prospective design using historical controls, as proposed by the Applicant, was considered not acceptable by the SAWP. Consequently, a prospective study remains required under the PAES post-marketing commitment to adequately characterise the clinical role of angiotensin II and to address existing safety concerns (including ischaemic and thromboembolic risks). At the time of this assessment, it is unknown whether the MAH has initiated the required PAES.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The paediatric development for Giapreza has been conducted in accordance with the agreed PIP (EMEA-001912-PIP02-16-M03). The paediatric clinical programme includes the following clinical studies:

Study 2 - LJ501-CRH04 (study under review in the current procedure): Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of LJPC-501 in paediatric patients from 2 to &lt;18 years of age with distributive shock who remain hypotensive despite fluid therapy and vasopressor therapy.

Study 3 - LJ501-CRH05: Open-label, randomised, active-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of LJPC-501 in paediatric patients from birth to &lt;2 years of age with distributive shock who remain hypotensive despite fluid therapy and vasopressor therapy.

In December 2025, the Applicant submitted a PIP modification request (EMA/PE/0000313565) to combine Study 2 (CRH04) and Study 3 (CRH05) into a single study (study 4). In March 2026, the PDCO adopted the Opinion on the acceptance of a modification of an agreed Paediatric Investigation Plan (EMA/PE/0000313565), replacing the previously agreed CRH04 and CRH05 studies with a single, double-blind, randomised placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of angiotensin II in paediatric patients from birth to less than 18 years of age with refractory hypotension in distributive shock receiving fluid resuscitation and standard-of-care (SOC) vasopressors (study 4). The PIP completion is deferred until June 2029.

The present report pertains to Study 2 (CRH04) under the agreed PIP, which the Applicant terminated early for internal business reasons. The available study results are submitted within this procedure.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

LJPC-501 (angiotensin II) was supplied as an aqueous solution for intravenous infusion in single-use vials containing 2.5 mg/mL angiotensin II and 25 mg/mL mannitol as excipient. Prior to administration, the investigational product was diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride (normal saline). The comparator (placebo) consisted of 0.9% sodium chloride solution and was provided by the study site.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## 2.3.1. Introduction

The MAH submitted an abbreviated clinical study report for:

-Study LJ501-CRH04: a randomised, placebo-controlled, double blind, multicentre efficacy and safety study of ljpc-501 in paediatric patients &gt; 2 to 17 years of age with catecholamine-resistant hypotension associated with distributive shock.

## 2.3.2. Clinical study

Study LJ501-CRH04: \"A randomised, placebo-controlled, double blind, multicentre efficacy and safety study of ljpc-501 in paediatric patients &gt; 2 to 17 years of age with catecholamine-resistant hypotension associated with distributive shock\".

The study was designed as a randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind, multicentre efficacy and safety study in paediatric patients aged 2 to 17 years of age with catecholamine-resistant hypotension associated with distributive shock.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

For the identification and initial management of shock, sites followed the pertinent most recent guidelines (Davis et al., 2017; Weiss et al., 2020) as much as possible, including fluid resuscitation, choice of vasopressors, and monitoring. Screening included a titration of standard-of-care vasopressors for a minimum of 2 hours to a maximum of 48 hours to achieve a patient-appropriate mean arterial pressure (MAP). Once MAP was stabilized, all baseline standard of care (SOC) vasopressor doses were converted to norepinephrine equivalents and summed to obtain the screening norepinephrine-equivalent dose (NED). The baseline MAP was defined as the average of 3 recorded MAP values documented within 15 minutes prior to initiation of study drug (i.e, -15 minutes, -5 minutes, and 0 minutes or just prior to initiation of study drug administration). Baseline NED was NED

at time of initiation of study drug.

Eligible patients were randomised 2:1 to receive LJPC-501 or placebo, with stratification by age (&gt; 2 to &lt; 6 years, ≥ 6 to &lt; 14 years, and ≥ 14 to &lt; 18 years) and by screening NED (&lt; 0.2 μg/kg/min and ≥ 0.2 μg/kg/min). To ensure adequate representation across all ages, at least 25 patients were planned to be randomised within each of the 3 age subgroups. Study drug (LJPC-501 or placebo) administration began at a dose rate of 5 ng/kg/min.

During the first 3 hours of treatment, study drug was supposed to be titrated to maintain baseline MAP while reducing doses of SOC vasopressors. Study drug infusion rates could be increased to a maximum of 80 ng/kg/min or decreased to a minimum of 1.25 ng/kg/min.

Unless adjustment was required for safety reasons, SOC vasopressor doses should be decreased, and not increased, during the first 3 hours. The planned treatment duration was 3 to 48 hours; but could continue for up to 7 days total if the patient was responding, tolerating treatment, and likely to benefit from continued vasopressor support in the opinion of the investigator.

## Treatments

## Study medication (LJPC-501/placebo)

Patients were randomised by a centralised block procedure in a 2:1 ratio to treatment with LJPC-501 or placebo. Stratification variables were age (2 to &lt; 6 years, ≥ 6 years to &lt; 14 years, and ≥ 14 years to &lt; 18 years) and screening NED (&lt; 0.2 µg/kg/min and ≥ 0.2 µg/kg/min). At least 25 patients were planned to be randomised and receive any amount of the study drug within each of the 3 age groups.

Patients were planned to begin study drug treatment by intravenous (IV) infusion using a central line at a dose rate of 5 ng/kg/min. During the first 3 hours, the dose rate could be escalated up to a maximum of 80 ng/kg/min in order to maintain the age-appropriate Baseline MAP while decreasing Baseline standard-of-care vasopressor dose(s). Treatment duration was 3 to 48 hours; however, treatment could continue for up to 7 days total if patient was responding, tolerating treatment, and if needed continued vasopressor support. For the duration of treatment, study drug could be titrated between 1.25 and 80 ng/kg/min in steps of no more than 10 ng/kg/min to avoid transient hypertension or hypotension. To discontinue study drug, the dosage was down titrated in steps to avoid hypotension. Prior to and during treatment, patients received supportive therapy consistent with Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria, protocol methods, and the 2020 guidelines (Weiss 2020).

## Titration of Study Drug and Other Vasopressors to Maintain MAP

Baseline MAP was assessed by 3 measurements documented within 15 minutes prior to initiation of study drug (ie, approximately -15 minutes, -5 minutes, and just prior to initiation of study drug administration). Standard-of-care vasopressor doses should not be changed during that time. Baseline NED is the NED at the time of initiation of study drug. Over the first 3 hours of study drug treatment, the goal was to maintain Baseline MAP and to reduce the sum NED by at least 50% from Baseline to Hour 3 while titrating study drug dose. Study drug dose was increased to increase MAP. As the MAP increased, the NED was carefully down titrated while maintaining MAP at or above the Baseline MAP. After Hour 3, the goal was to maintain Baseline MAP, continue to reduce reliance on other vasopressors (to discontinuation, if possible), and continue study drug until Hour 48. Study drug treatment could continue for up to 7 days total if patient was responding, tolerating treatment, and could benefit from continued vasopressor support, in the opinion of the Investigator. Study drug could be discontinued after Hour 3 (or earlier for safety reasons) and prior to Hour 48 if the patient no longer needs vasopressor support or for safety reasons. Treatment with the study drug could be reinitiated after completion within 6 hours if further haemodynamic support was needed in the opinion of the investigator, and if the patient had received &lt; 7 days of study drug treatment. For the duration of study drug treatment, study drug could be titrated between 1.25 and 80 ng/kg/min, in steps of no more than 10 ng/kg/min to avoid transient hypertension or hypotension. Study drug and standard-ofcare vasopressor titrations are summarised in Table 3.

## Objectives

The primary objective is:

- To evaluate the effect of LJPC-501 infusion on mean arterial pressure (MAP) as assessed by standard-of-care vasopressor dose reduction in paediatric patients with catecholamine-resistant hypotension (CRH) associated with distributive shock.

Secondary objectives are:

- To evaluate change in the Paediatric Logistic Organ Dysfunction-2 (PELOD-2) score.
- To evaluate changes in catecholamine and other vasopressor doses over time.
- To evaluate the change in MAP over time.
- To establish the safety and tolerability of LJPC-501 in paediatric patients with CRH.

Exploratory objectives are:

- To evaluate mortality at Day 30.
- To evaluate changes in heart rate, cardiac index, ScvO2, capillary refill time, peripheral/central pulse differential, serum lactate and urine output.
- To evaluate PICU length of stay.
- To evaluate need for utilisation of renal replacement therapy (dialysis), ventilator, and vasoactive support.

## Outcomes/endpoints

The primary efficacy endpoint is:

- The proportion of patients in each treatment group who have achieved ≥ 50% reduction of vasopressor dose as measured by NED from baseline to Hour 3.

Response is defined as a ≥ 50% reduction in vasopressor doses expressed as NED from baseline to Hour 3 provided Hour 3 MAP is no more than 3 mmHg lower than the Baseline MAP.

Secondary efficacy endpoints are:

- Change in PELOD-2 score from Baseline to Hour 24 and Hour 48
- Change in catecholamine and other vasopressor doses from Baseline to Hour 24 and Hour 48
- Change in MAP from Baseline to Hour 3, Hour 24, and Hour 48

Exploratory endpoints are:

- Mortality at Day 30
- Changes in heart rate, cardiac index, ScvO2, capillary refill time, peripheral/central pulse differential and serum lactate from Baseline to Hour 3, Hour 24 and Hour 48.
- Urine output during Baseline, Hour 0 to 3, Hour 3 to 24, Hour 24 to 48, then 24-hour periods to EOT.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Central randomisation via IRT was used. Stratification variables were age (&gt; 2 to &lt; 6 years; ≥ 6 to &lt; 18 years) and Screening NED (&lt; 0.2 μg/kg/min and ≥ 0.2 μg/kg/min). Blocked randomisation within strata with a 2:1 schedule of LJPC-501 to placebo was used. The Sponsor and all study site personnel, with the exception of the unblinded pharmacist(s), remained blinded to study drug assignment. Once prepared, the tubing, bag, label, solution colour, and all other properties of the final study drug product and delivery system were identical to maintain the blind. The study blind could be broken in emergency situations.

## Patient narratives

## Patient 1

Patient 1 was an 2-11-year-old female with a known medical history of (unknown date of diagnosis, ongoing), now presenting with toxic shock. The patient was screened on. The initial physical exam showed warm extremities, diffuse abdominal tenderness without muscle guarding or contractures, with audible peristalsis. Neurological examination was reported as a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) 3 in a sedated, intubated, and ventilated patient. ECG did not show any abnormalities. ## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

Giapreza (angiotensin II) is a vasopressor that activates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), a well-characterised physiological system regulating vascular tone and blood pressure.

This Article 46 procedure under Regulation (EC) No 1901/2006 concerns the submission of Study CRH04, titled: \"A randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind, multicentre efficacy and safety study of LJPC-501 in paediatric patients &gt;2 to 17 years of age with catecholamine-resistant hypotension associated with distributive shock.\" The study is part of the European pediatric clinical development programme (study 2), in accordance with the agreed Pediatric Investigation Plan (PIP) EMEA 001912 PIP02 16 M03. Of note, in March 2026, the PDCO adopted the Opinion on the acceptance of a modification of an agreed Paediatric Investigation Plan (EMA/PE/0000313565), replacing CRH04/CRH05 (studies 2 and 3) with a single double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled pediatric trial (Study 4) with PIP completion deferred to June 2029.

## CRH04 study

Study CRH04 (Study 2 of the agreed PIP) was terminated prematurely by the Applicant due to internal business reasons. The available study results have been submitted within this procedure. However, given the early termination, with enrolment of only approximately 10% of the planned population, the data are considered descriptive only and do not permit robust conclusions.

Study CRH04 was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre efficacy and safety study in pediatric patients aged ≥2 to &lt;18 years with catecholamine-resistant hypotension associated with distributive shock. LJPC-501 was supplied as an aqueous solution for intravenous infusion in vials containing angiotensin II 2.5 mg/mL and was diluted prior to administration. During the screening, SOC

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

vasopressors were titrated for ≥2 to ≤48 hours to achieve a patient appropriate MAP. Once stabilised, baseline SOC vasopressor doses were converted to NED. Eligible patients were randomised 2:1 (LJPC-501:placebo), with stratification by age (&gt;2-&lt;6, ≥6-&lt;14, ≥14-&lt;18 years) and screening NED (&lt;0.2 vs ≥0.2 µg/kg/min). Study drug started at 5 ng/kg/min and, during the first 3 hours, was titrated to maintain baseline MAP while reducing doses of SOC vasopressors. Infusion rates could be increased to a maximum of 80 ng/kg/min or decreased to a minimum of 1.25 ng/kg/min. The planned treatment duration was 3-48 hours, with continuation permitted for up to 7 days if, in the investigator's judgement, the patient was responding, tolerating treatment, and likely to benefit from continued vasopressor support. Study population At termination (for business reasons), nine patients were enrolled, of whom eight received study treatment (LJPC-501 n=6; placebo n=2). One patient randomised to LJPC-501 was not treated because MAP had recovered prior to dosing. By treatment group, the LJPC-501 arm comprised seven pediatric patients with a mean age of 9 years (4 males, 3 females) with a mean body weight of 32.6 kg, and a mean height available for 6/7 patients of 139.2 cm. The placebo group comprised two pediatric patients with a mean age of 4 years (1 male, 1 female), with a mean body weight of 17.5 kg and a mean height of 103.5 cm respectively. At baseline, the LJPC-501 arm included toxic shock, haemorrhagic shock following, and septic shock due to identified pathogens (e.g. Streptococcus pyogenes, Morganella morganii, Staphylococcus aureus) with frequent chest-X-ray abnormalities (pulmonary oedema/infiltrates or consolidations). The placebo arm comprised vasoplegia after with life-threatening haemoperitoneum (onset prior to infusion) and septic shock due to Pseudomonas aeruginosa. All patients were receiving SOC vasopressors, quantified as norepinephrine-equivalent dose (NED): in LJPC-501, baseline NED ranged 0.19-53.33 µg/kg/min (one patient with markedly elevated requirements), whereas in placebo one patient had 0.15 µg/kg/min and the other had missing baseline norepinephrine recording. Baseline MAP ranged 63-88 mmHg (LJPC-501) and 66-75 mmHg (placebo). Baseline characteristics were generally consistent with a pediatric population with catecholamine dependent distributive shock. However, clinically relevant imbalances (very small placebo sample, younger age distribution, differing aetiologies, and one missing baseline NED value) limit the comparability between groups and the interpretation of cross-arm analyses. Of note, regarding patient Patient1, the narrative indicates a medical history of, although was an exclusion criteria. Efficacy The paediatric efficacy dataset for LJPC 501 is not interpretable for robust conclusions due to premature trial termination with an extremely small sample (9 randomized; 8 treated; 6 active vs 2 placebo) and one non-evaluable placebo patient because of missing baseline NED. The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients in each treatment group who achieved a ≥50% reduction in vasopressor dose (expressed as norepinephrine-equivalent dose, NED) from baseline to Hour 3, provided that the Hour-3 mean arterial pressure (MAP) was no more than 3 mmHg below the baseline MAP. The 3-hour primary endpoint was achieved in 2/6 (33%) LJPC-501 patients and 1/2 (50%) placebo patients (the second placebo patient lacked a valid baseline NED and was therefore non-evaluable for the primary endpoint). Interpretation of results is constrained by the very small sample sizes, and heterogeneity in aetiologies and baseline vasopressor requirements, which preclude

any meaningful comparative inference.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Notably, patient profiles showed that in many cases MAP was maintained without substantial up-titration of study drug. Specifically, in four patients (all in the LJPC-501 group) NED had already been profoundly reduced during screening prior to study-drug initiation, with LJPC-501 started at 5 ng/kg/min and subsequently barely up-titrated. Under these circumstances, a treatment effect on NED reduction cannot be discerned. Establishing such an effect in children with catecholamine-resistant hypotension would require that the study-drug dose be increased while NED is decreased to maintain MAP.

Treatment group summaries for secondary (PELOD-2, vasopressor/catecholamine changes, MAP) and exploratory endpoints (Day-30 mortality, PICU stay, renal replacement therapy, mechanical ventilation, vasoactive support) were not provided in the Applicant's report. Instead, relevant information was presented only within individual patient narratives.

Review of such narratives indicates that, regarding vasopressor use through hours 24/48, all six LJPC-501-treated patients experienced a ≥50% reduction in norepinephrine-equivalent dose (NED) at some timepoint within 0-29.7 hours post-initiation. In the placebo group this occurred in 1/2 patients (at 1.25 hours; the other placebo patient was non-evaluable for baseline NED). Across LJPC-501 cases, NED decreased while MAP was maintained per protocol targets in the individual profiles. Additionally, one patient discontinued all SOC vasopressors approximately 4.5 hours after LJPC-501 initiation(Patient 6).

Although several LJPC-501 cases showed ≥50% vasopressor reductions beyond 3 hours (up to 29.7 hours), suggesting a potential effect over a longer time window, this pattern cannot be reliable distinguished from the natural disease evolution or the effects from co-interventions in such a limited dataset. Likewise, the only evaluable placebo patient also achieved a ≥50% reduction in vasopressor requirements at 1.25 hours, underscoring the aforementioned uncertainty.

Day-30 mortality was 1/6 (16.7%) in the LJPC-501 group (one fatal SAE of severe distributive shock in (Patient 2) considered not related to study drug) versus 0/2 in the placebo group. Nonetheless, sample size is far too small to interpret safety or mortality trends.

Overall, the current evidence does not allow robust efficacy conclusions for LJPC-501 in the pediatric population. Meaningful assessment would require a larger, adequately powered pediatric study and efficacy of LLJPC-501 in children remains to be established.

## Safety

## Exposure

The safety population comprised the 8 treated patients (LJPC-501 n=6, placebo n=2) enrolled. However, as it was mentioned before, the study terminated prematurely, therefore, analyses are descriptive only and do not allow for statistical comparisons between groups.

Exposure of the safety population was highly heterogeneous, reflecting the clinical instability inherent to the target population and the need for individualized hemodynamic support. In the LJPC 501 arm, norepinephrine (NED) requirements varied widely (0.01-66.67 µg/kg/min), translating into cumulative NED exposure of 0.01-361.6 mg/kg (mean 61.57 mg/kg; median 0.67 mg/kg). By comparison, patients in the placebo arm received lower NED rates (0.01-9.62 µg/kg/min) and correspondingly lower cumulative NED exposure (0.01-0.93 mg/kg; mean and median 0.47 mg/kg).

LJPC 501 administration dosage was likewise variable. Infusion rates ranged from 0.89 to 130.00 ng/kg/min, with cumulative dose of study drug of 0.02-0.43 mg/kg (mean 0.10 mg/kg; median 0.02 mg/kg) over 2.08-3.37 days. Placebo infusions were administered at 2.50-30.00 ng/kg/min, with cumulative dose of 0.005-0.01 mg/kg and shorter durations (0.25-0.68 days). Notably, the highest LJPC 501 infusion rate observed was 130 ng/kg/min.

Consequently, comparisons of safety outcomes between arms should be interpreted with caution due to the limited sample size, unequal exposure duration, and potential confounding by disease severity and concomitant vasopressor use.

## Adverse events

As expected in this critically ill population, AEs occurred in all patients. Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were reported in 6/6 LJPC-501 patients and 1/2 placebo patients. No AE was considered related to LJPC-501 by investigators. Across MedDRA system organ classes, the LJPC-501 group reported 21 TEAEs vs 1 TEAE in placebo, with varied preferred terms and no recurrent pattern pointing to a specific organ system within this small dataset. Reported TEAEs in LJPC-501 included anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hypothyroidism, impaired gastric emptying, constipation, aphthous ulcer, pyrexia, hypothermia, veno-occlusive liver disease, stoma site Candida infection, infectious croup, hypoalbuminaemia, dizziness, aphonia, somnolence, pulmonary oedema, pleural effusion, bronchospasm, and distributive shock. The single TEAE in placebo was hypoalbuminaemia. Investigators assessed none of these TEAEs as study-drug related. Two SAEs occurred: one fatal SAE of severe distributive shock LJPC-501 group. Care was limited (including cessation of all medications, LJPC-501 inclusive) and the patient died; the event was considered unrelated to study drug. The cumulative LJPC-501 dose was 0.43 mg/kg, maximum rate 130 ng/kg/min, over 2.08 days. The other SAE was one hemoperitoneum in Patient 8 (placebo group), starting before study-drug initiation and resolved with surgical intervention. As thi SAE began prior to study-drug initiation it was not counted as TEAE. No AESI were reported: specifically, no embolic/thrombotic events, no ischaemia/vasoconstriction, no cardiac arrhythmias or TdP/QT prolongation, no hypertension, and no acute kidney injury were observed in either group. No additional AEs were identified from laboratory, vital signs or physical examination datasets. Within the limitations of this prematurely terminated study, no unexpected safety signals for LJPC-501 were identified at infusion rates up to 130 ng/kg/min and the AE profile was consistent with the underlying severity of catecholamine-resistant hypotension in distributive shock. However, safety interpretation is constrained by premature termination of the study (&lt;10% of the planned pediatric sample), very small safety database (6 vs 2), and heterogeneous exposure to vasopressors and LJPC-501. Consequently, only descriptive observations are appropriate and robust conclusions on comparative safety cannot be drawn. Notably, at the time of the initial authorisation, a post-authorisation efficacy study was required to provide further characterisation of key safety risks, including thromboembolic events and ischaemic. In this context, it is reassuring that no events of ischaemia/vasoconstriction or embolic/thrombotic events were identified in the pediatric CRH04 study. However, this observation should be interpreted with caution, given the very small sample size and the protocol requirement that patients should receive venous thromboembolism prophylaxis per institutional guidelines, if indicated, before starting study drug, which may have mitigated event detection in this dataset.

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

Given the premature termination of Study CRH04 and the resulting enrolment of only a small fraction of the planned pediatric sample (less than 10%), both efficacy and safety data are descriptive in nature and therefore do not allow for robust conclusions. Study CRH04 provides only limited supportive information and does not adequately address the

outstanding uncertainties regarding the efficacy and safety profile of LJPC-501 in the pediatric population. Efficacy and safety of LLJPC-501 in children remains to be established.

Further pediatric evidence is needed to be generated within the pediatric development programme under the agreed PIP. Of note, in March 2026, the PDCO adopted the Opinion on the acceptance of a modification of an agreed Paediatric Investigation Plan (EMA/PE/0000313565), replacing CRH04/CRH05 (studies 2 and 3) with a single double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled pediatric trial (Study 4), with PIP completion deferred to June 2029.

Based on the current assessment, no changes to the SmPC are required at this stage, as the submitted data are not considered relevant for inclusion.
