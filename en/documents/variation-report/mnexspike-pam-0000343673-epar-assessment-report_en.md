## 1. Introduction

On 27 April 2026, the MAH submitted a completed paediatric study for mNEXSPIKE, in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

These data are also submitted as part of the post-authorisation measure. Reference is made to the letter of undertaking for mNEXSPIKE (dated 05 Dec 2025) in which the MAH committed to submitting the final CSR of the study mRNA-P301-Japan study (study P301-Japan).

A short critical expert overview has also been provided. this clinical overview presents a summary of the immunogenicity and safety results from the study P301-Japan through 12 months of follow-up to fulfil this commitment. For clarification, there is not a stand-alone final study P301-Japan CSR, rather the final data from Study P301-Japan are included in the overall mRNA-1283-P301 final CSR included in the submission.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that study title \"A Randomized, Observer-blind, Active-controlled Phase 3 Study to Investigate the Safety, Immunogenicity, and Relative Vaccine Efficacy of mRNA-1283 Compared With mRNA-1273 in Participants Aged ≥12 Years for the Prevention of COVID-19 - Part 3\" and number mRNA-1283-P301 (Part 3) is part of a clinical development program.

Part 1 was a pivotal Phase 3 randomised, observer-blind, active-controlled, single-dose, multicentre study to evaluate the safety, immunogenicity, and rVE of mRNA-1283 in individuals ≥12 years of age for the prevention of COVID-19.

Part 3 was an independent study part conducted in the United States to assess reactogenicity, safety, and immunogenicity of mRNA-1283 in participants ≥12 years of age who had a prior COVID-19 vaccination status of either unvaccinated or vaccinated.

Lastly, the Japan cohort (Study P301-Japan) was an independent study part to evaluate the safety and immunogenicity of mRNA-1283 in Japanese individuals ≥12 years of age for the prevention of COVID19. The MAH submitted the final CSR from P301 in order to fulfil the post-authorisation measure to submit final results from the study P301-Japan.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Participants were randomised 1:1 to receive mRNA-1283.815 10 μg (mNEXSPIKE XBB.1.5 variant) or mRNA-1273.815 50 μg (Spikevax XBB.1.5 variant) as a single dose. These are the approved doses of mRNA-1283 and its variant adapted vaccines and mRNA-1273 and its variant adapted vaccines.

## Clinical study

mRNA-1283-P301: A Randomized, Observer-blind, Active-controlled Phase 3 Study to Investigate the Safety, Immunogenicity, and Relative Vaccine Efficacy of mRNA-1283 Compared With mRNA-1273 in Participants Aged ≥12 Years for the Prevention of COVID-19

## Description

To address the need for updated vaccination strategies, the MAH has used its messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA)-based platform to develop a custom SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccine candidate, mRNA-1283, which encodes the N-terminal domain (NTD) and receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the spike protein (S protein) of SARS-CoV-2. Animal challenge and protection studies have indicated protective responses elicited by RBD-based vaccines. mRNA-1283 is approved in the EU as mNEXSPIKE for individuals aged 12 years of age and older. The current study of mRNA-1283 (mRNA-1283-P301) was designed to evaluate the safety and immunogenicity of mRNA-1283 (variant composition) including in previously unvaccinated individuals. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the immunogenicity, reactogenicity, and safety of mRNA 1283.222 in Part 1 and of mRNA-1283.815 in Parts 3. Part 3 is designed to generate additional clinical experience with commercially representative investigational product in adolescent, unvaccinated, and adult populations.

## Methods

## Study participants

Study mRNA-1283-P301 (hereinafter Study P301) is an multicentre study evaluating mRNA-1283 variant-containing formulations in participants ≥12 years of age. CSRs reporting on analyses of Study P301 and Study P301-Japan have been provided previously. Study P301 Part 3 was designed as an observer-blind, active-controlled, single dose, Phase 3 study in up to 1000 adolescent participants, 600 previously unvaccinated participants, and 500 adult participants who were randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive a single 10 μg dose of mRNA 1283.815 or a single 50-μg dose of mRNA-1273.815. Study P301-Japan included 689 subjects in the Safety Set and 685 subjects in the Per Protocol Immunogenicity Set (PPIS). Randomisation in study P301 Part 3 was stratified by age groups (adolescents [12 to &lt;18], 18 to &lt;65, and ≥65 years) and by previous COVID-19 vaccination status (Yes/No), with the goal to enrol ~1000 participants in the adolescent (12 to &lt;18 years) group, ~500 adults, and ~600 prior COVID-19 unvaccinated participants. Participants were to be followed for 6 months. Prior COVID-19 vaccination was not required to enrol in Part 3 of the study. All participants with prior medical conditions that were medically stable were allowed to enrol. Prior SARS-CoV-2 infection and COVID-19 vaccination (with up to 5 approved/authorized doses of which none were XBB-containing) were allowed unless an infection or vaccination had occurred within 90 days of enrolment.

## Treatments

mRNA-1283.815 is a monovalent vaccine composed of the RNA-LNP encoding the linked NTD-RBD of the Spike protein of the XBB.1.5 variant of SARS-CoV-2. In comparison, mRNA-1273.815 is an approved monovalent vaccine composed of the RNA-LNP encoding the full-length Spike protein of the XBB.1.5 variant. In both vaccines, the mRNA is formulated in a mixture of 4 lipids common to the Sponsor's mRNA vaccine platform: SM-102, cholesterol, DSPC, and PEG2000-DMG.

Participants were randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive a single 10 μg dose of mRNA-1283.815 or a single 50-μg dose of mRNA-1273.815.

## Objectives

For P301-Japan the primary Immunogenicity objective was to demonstrate a non-inferior neutralising antibody response of mRNA-1283.815 10 μg compared to the antibody response of mRNA-1273.815 50 μg Japan cohort based on GMR at Day 29 after the study injection.

The primary safety objective was to evaluate the safety and reactogenicity of mRNA-1283.815 10 μg study injection.

Secondary objective was to characterise the neutralising antibody response against Omicron XBB.1.5 and the ancestral SARS-CoV-2 D614G (mRNA-1283.815) at all timepoints.

The primary objective for study P301-Part 3 was to evaluate the safety and reactogenicity of 10 μg mRNA-1283.815.

Co-secondary objectives were:

- To evaluate the neutralising antibody response against Omicron XBB.1.5 (mRNA-1283.815 vs mRNA-1273.815) at Day 29 in previously unvaccinated study participants
- To characterize the neutralising antibody response against Omicron XBB.1.5 (mRNA-1283.815 and mRNA-1273.815) at Day 29 in all study participants

## Outcomes/endpoints

Primary endpoints for P301-Japan were:

- GMR of Omicron XBB.1.5 GM level in the Japan cohort who received mRNA-1283.815 10 μg over the Omicron XBB.1.5 GM level in the Japan cohort who received mRNA-1273.815 50 μg at Day 29 after the study injection.
- Solicited local and systemic ARs during a 7-day follow-up period after the study injection.
- Unsolicited AEs during the 28-day follow-up period after the study injection.
- SAEs, MAAEs, AEs leading to withdrawal, AESIs from Day 1 to EOS.

Secondary endpoints were:

- Omicron XBB.1.5 and ancestral SARS-CoV-2 D614G GM levels at all planned timepoints.
- SRR against Omicron XBB.1.5 and ancestral SARS-CoV-2 D614G at all planned timepoints.

Primary Endpoints for study P301-Part 3 were the collection and comparison of:

- Solicited local and systemic ARs during a 7-day follow-up period after the study injection

## Sample size

The sample size of P301-Part 3 is not based on statistical hypothesis testing for immunogenicity. Participants in each group were randomised 1:1 into mRNA-1283.815 or mRNA-1273.815.

- Up to approximately 1000 adolescents
- Up to approximately 600 prior COVID-19 unvaccinated participants
- Up to approximately 500 adults

With this sample size, there was at least a 90% probability to observe at least 1 participant reporting an AE if the true rate of AEs is 1.0%, with at least 1050 participants receiving the mRNA-1283 variant formulation.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

All participants were centrally assigned/randomised to investigational intervention using an IRT.

This study is observer-blind as to treatment assignment. Dose preparation, administration and accountability is to be performed by designated unblinded site personnel who will not participate in any of the clinical study evaluations. Neither the participant nor participant's parent(s)/legal guardian(s) nor the investigator nor clinical staff responsible for study assessments/safety will have access to the treatment assignment during the conduct of the study. The laboratory personnel in charge of immunogenicity testing will be blinded to the treatment assignment of the samples tested throughout the course of the study. The Sponsor will remain blinded to treatment assignment through the final efficacy analysis. For the safety and immunogenicity (Day 29) interim analysis in Part 1, a preidentified Sponsor team was unblinded to review group-level results. The DSMB may also review unblinded safety or efficacy information at closed sessions (without the Sponsor's participation). Study sites will remain blinded.

Except in the case of medical necessity, a participant's treatment should not be unblinded without the approval of the Sponsor. If a participant becomes seriously ill or pregnant during the study, the blind will be broken only if knowledge of the treatment assignment will affect that participant's clinical management. In the event of a medical emergency requiring identification of individual treatment assignment, the investigator will make every attempt to contact the CRO medical monitor to explain the need for unblinding within 24 hours of opening the code. The investigator will be responsible for documenting the time, date, reason for unblinding, and the names of the personnel involved. The investigator (or designee) will have access to unblind participants within IRT. All unblinding will be tracked via an audit trail in IRT and documented in the final study report.

## Statistical Methods

For P301-Japan the Null hypothesis (H0): Antibody GM level against Omicron XBB.1.5 after the mRNA-1283.815 dose (10 μg) in the Japan cohort is inferior to that after mRNA-1273.815 dose (50 μg) in the Japan cohort, based on the GMR defined as the ratio of GM level against Omicron XBB.1.5 of mRNA 1283.815 at Day 29 vs the GM level against Omicron XBB.1.5 of mRNA-1273.815 at Day 29. The prespecified noninferiority margin was 1.5 or 0.667 (1/1.5).

With approximately 622 evaluable participants (311:311 for mRNA-1283.815 vs. mRNA-1273.815) in the Japan cohort, there was approximately 80% power to demonstrate non-inferior antibody responses of mRNA-1283.815 vs. mRNA-1273.815 for the primary endpoint at 2-sided alpha of 0.05. It was also expected that ~10% participants might be excluded from the PPIS (for reasons such as missing immunogenicity samples); hence the target enrollment for the Japan cohort was 692 participants (346 participants per arm).

The primary objective for P301-Part 3 was to evaluate the safety and reactogenicity profile of mRNA 1283.815 at the 10 μg dose level administered as a single dose. Reactogenicity included local and systemic ARs within 7 days of study injection. Safety included any unsolicited AEs within 28 days of study injection. SAEs including deaths, MAAEs, AEs leading to discontinuation, and AESIs were collected for the entire study duration.

The safety and reactogenicity evaluation included the frequencies of solicited ARs (local and systemic), unsolicited AEs including SAEs, MAAEs, AEs leading to discontinuation, and AEs of interest (including both Investigator-reported AESIs and programmed SMQ analyses by medical concept). Unsolicited AE summaries were to include solicited ARs if any solicited AR met seriousness criteria. Although there was no statistical comparison for reactogenicity and safety between the 2 vaccine groups, the mRNA1273.815 50 μg group safety and reactogenicity provided reference to the mRNA-1283.815 results.

## Recruitment

For P301-Japan the Randomisation Set consisted of all participants who were randomised, regardless of the participant's treatment status in the study. Participants were analysed according to the treatment group to which they were randomised (344 and 348 participants in the mRNA-1283.815 and mRNA-1273.815 groups, respectively).

The FAS consisted of all participants who received study vaccine (343/344 [99.7%] and 346/348 [99.4%] participants in the mRNA-1283.815 and mRNA-1273.815 groups, respectively).

The PPIS consisted of all participants from the Japan cohort in the FAS who received the planned dose of study injection, have Baseline and Day 29 (occurring between 21 and 42 days after vaccination) nAb data against XBB.1.5, and had no major protocol deviations that impact immunogenicity data. Of the 689 participants in the FAS, 685 (99.0%) met criteria for inclusion in the PPIS. Reasons for exclusion from the PPIS included did not have Baseline or Day 29 (occurring between 21 and 42 days) Immunogenicity Data (n = 2) and Major Protocol Deviations that Impact Key or Critical Data (n = 2). The PPIS is the primary analysis set for analyses of immunogenicity.

The PPSE consisted of all participants in the FAS who receive the planned dose of IP and have no major protocol deviations that impact vaccine efficacy data. Of the 689 participants in the FAS, 688 (99.4%) received vaccine and had no major protocol deviations that impacted vaccine efficacy data. Reasons for exclusion from the PPSE included Major Protocol Deviations that Impact Key or Critical Data (n = 1).

The Safety Set consisted of all randomised participants who received the study vaccine (343 and 346 participants in the mRNA-1283.815 and mRNA-1273.815 groups, respectively). The Solicited Safety Set included all randomised participants who received study vaccine and contributed any solicited AR data (343/343 [100%] and 346/346 [100%] participants in the mRNA-1283.815 and mRNA-1273.815 groups, respectively, as a percentage of the total Safety Set). The Safety Set was used for all analyses for safety except for solicited ARs, for which the Solicited Safety Set was used.

One participant in the mRNA-1273.815 group had an SAE of endocarditis assessed as related to study injection by the Investigator, which is described below:

- Endocarditis (mRNA-1273.815): An adult with a relevant history of hypertension, mitral valve incompetence, dyslipidemia, on polypharmacy had an SAE of endocarditis (verbatim: infective endomyocarditis) on Day 368 and was hospitalised on the same day. No other relevant details surrounding the event were known as the participant reported the event to the Study Investigator via email with very limited details. It was unknown if any cardiology consultation was obtained. The participant while on study had a prior SAE of angina pectoris, which started on Day 333 (deemed as not related to the study injection by the Investigator; outcome resolving). The participant was reported as lost to follow-up. The Investigator assessed the event of endocarditis as related to the study injection as the drug may have caused the participant to become easily infected. According to the Investigator, although the event was not a direct bacterial infection, the possibility that it was a factor in the development of the disease could not be ruled out, and a causal relationship to the study injection was considered possible by the Investigator. The Sponsor assessed the endocarditis as not related to the study injection based on the strength of evidence particularly given the considerable long latency (&gt;1 year) of events and the known medical history of heart valve disease in this elderly participant with other comorbidities (immunocompromised status) which together constitute significant risk factors and were potentially contributory.

## Discussion on clinical aspects

The MAH submitted the final CSR from P301 in order to fulfil the post-authorisation measure to submit final results from the study P301-Japan. CSRs for this study has been submitted earlier including primary immunogenicity results and short-term follow-up safety results. Since the studies included adolescent subjects 12 - 17 years of age this needs to be considered for the assessment. For P301Japan the primary Immunogenicity objective was to demonstrate a non-inferior neutralising antibody response of mRNA-1283.815 10 μg compared to the antibody response of mRNA-1273.815 50 μg Japan cohort based on GMR at Day 29 after the study injection. This had been demonstrated earlier. Immunogenicity results with longer follow-up period for up to 12 months are provided with this submission. The observed GM nAb level at Days 91, 181, and 365 for Omicron XBB.1.5 as well as the GMFR between Predose and Days 91, 181, and 365 for both groups are provided. While an overall reduction of neutralising Ab titres is determined over time nAb titres after vaccination with mRNA-1283 are constantly numerically higher over time as compared to titres after vaccination with mRNA-1273. Titres after 1 year are still elevated as compared to baseline titres in both groups. Incidence rates of COVID-19 infections are very similar in both groups. Neutralising Ab titres are highest in the adolescent group which is expected.

The pre-specified exploratory endpoint for P301 - Part 3 followed up for up to 6 months and confirms the same trend of numerically higher nAb titres after vaccination with mRNA-1283 and elevated nAb titres for up to 6 months as compared to baseline titres. Also in this study titres from adolescents were higher than in the older age groups.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

Subjects in P301-Japan were followed-up for safety data collection for nearly 12 months in the median. Solicited AEs have been submitted and assessed earlier for both P301-Japan and P301 - Part 3. Incidences of solicited local and systemic ARs were similar when compared within the different studies. Incidences of grade 3 solicited ARs were also very similar between the mRNA-1273 and mRNA-1283 groups.

There were no deaths in any study P301-Japan and 1 death in study P301-Part 3 (gunshot wound). There were 4 and 5 SAEs in study P301-Japan in the different groups. One of these SAEs was considered related to study treatment in mRNA-1273 group which was an individual who developed endocarditis on Day 368. The MAH assessed the endocarditis as not related which is agreed. Other SAEs are not considered related by the investigator. The incidence of SAEs was 15/1052 (1.4%) participants in the mRNA-1283.815 group and 20/1039 (1.9%) participants in the mRNA-1273.815 group, all of which were assessed as not related to study injection by the Investigator which is agreed.

Incidences of unsolicited AEs are very similar between the groups in both studies. The most frequently reported unsolicited AEs in both groups by PT, regardless of causality, were in the infections and infestations SOC (24/70 [34.3%] participants in the mRNA-1283.815 group and 18/70 [25.7%] participants in the mRNA-1273.815 group) and all were assessed as not related to study injection by the Investigator. This was consistent with the overall study population.

In the mRNA-1283.815 group, 1 AESI was reported, which was assessed as not related to study injection by the Investigator (facial paralysis on Day 157). The incidence of MAAEs in adolescents was similar between groups. Of the total MAAEs, 1/70 (1.4%) in the mRNA-1283.815 group (urticaria on Day 5) and none in the mRNA-1273.815 group were assessed as related to study injection by the Investigator.

The incidence of all unsolicited AEs was balanced between mRNA-1283.815 and mRNA-1273.815 for the study population overall. The assessment of unsolicited AEs by age-subgroups did not identify safety concerns.

## 3. CHMP overall conclusion and recommendation

Results confirm the initial finding of non-inferiority of nAb GM titres after vaccination with mRNA1283.815 as compared to mRNA-1273.815. While titres drop over time neutralising titres remain elevated in both groups up to 12 months as compared to baseline titres. No new safety signals have been identified neither in adolescents nor in adults. There are no changes to the SmPC as a result of this assessment. The B/R remains positive for mNEXSPIKE.
