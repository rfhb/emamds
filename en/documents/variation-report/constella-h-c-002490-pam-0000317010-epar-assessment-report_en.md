## 1. Introduction

On 4 December 2025, the MAH submitted one completed paediatric study for Constella (linaclotide), in accordance with Article 46 of Regulation (EC) No1901/2006, as amended.

A short critical expert overview has also been provided.

## 2. Scientific discussion

## 2.1. Information on the development program

The MAH stated that Study M21-862, \"Functional Constipation: A Phase 2 Dose Finding Study Evaluating the Safety and Efficacy of Linaclotide in Pediatric Subjects 6 Months to Less Than 2 Years of Age\", is part of the EU PIP EMEA-000927-PIP01-10-M08, Decision No. P/0207/2024. In the PIP decision, M21-862 is listed as Study 5 under the clinical studies for treatment of functional constipation. No updates to the product information have been proposed as part of this Article 46 submission. The clinical overview states that linaclotide was safe and well tolerated, that the safety profile was consistent with prior paediatric and adult linaclotide studies for FC, and that no new safety risks were identified in Study M21-862.

Linaclotide is a first-in-class, 14-amino acid peptide that acts on the apical surface of epithelial cells in the intestinal lumen to stimulate guanylate cyclase subtype C (GC-C) receptors. By activating GC-C, orally administered linaclotide increases intestinal fluid secretion and intestinal transit and decreases visceral abdominal pain. Linaclotide has minimal oral bioavailability (0.2% or less) in several animal species and is minimally absorbed with low systemic availability in adults.

Linaclotide is approved in several regions of the world and for different indications:

- in the United States for the treatment of irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C) in adults and paediatric patients 7 years of age and older, chronic idiopathic constipation (CIC) in adults, and functional constipation (FC) in paediatric patients 2 years of age and older;
- in Canada for the treatment of IBS-C and CIC in adults, and FC in paediatric patients 6 to 17 years of age;
- in Japan, Australia, New Zealand, the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, and Mexico for the treatment of IBS-C and CIC, or chronic constipation in Japan, in adults;
- in the United Kingdom, China and Switzerland for the treatment of IBS-C in adults.

In the EU, the product is currently authorised under the brand name Constella for the following indication:

\"Constella is indicated for the symptomatic treatment of moderate to severe irritable bowel syndrome with constipation (IBS-C) in adults.\"

Functional constipation (FC) is a common healthcare problem in children of all ages, with a worldwide prevalence ranging from 0.7% to 29.6%. Symptoms suggestive of FC include infrequent bowel movements, defined as two or fewer bowel movements per week, hard or large-diameter stools, painful defaecation, retentive posturing and faecal incontinence. If left untreated or insufficiently controlled, FC may have clinical consequences including poor appetite, failure to thrive, encopresis, anal fissures and cystitis. The submitted background information also describes a severe impact on quality of life, school absenteeism and substantial healthcare-utilisation costs.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The pathophysiology of paediatric FC is multifactorial and includes genetic predisposition, inadequate fibre and fluid intake and immobility. Especially in younger children, withholding behaviour after painful or frightening stool evacuation is described as the most common aetiological factor. This can lead to a self-reinforcing cycle in which voluntary stool retention increases water absorption from the faeces, resulting in harder stools and further difficult and painful defaecation. The submitted information further describes colonic dysmotility as a possible cause of FC, particularly in adults, and also as a possible consequence of longstanding faecal stasis in chronic FC.

Initial non-pharmacological interventions include education, dietary advice with sufficient fibre and fluid intake, and behavioural modifications. In older children, toilet training, reward systems and stool diaries may be added. If a faecal mass is suspected, disimpaction may be attempted with rectally administered enemas or high doses of oral polyethylene glycol (PEG), followed by maintenance treatment and eventual weaning after sustained symptom relief. The CSR cites submitted literature indicating that approximately 40% of children with FC referred to a paediatric gastroenterologist remain symptomatic after 5 years and 20% still have symptoms after 10 years of treatment, with persistence in some cases into adolescence or adulthood.

The submitted literature cited in the CSR also suggests that persistence of paediatric FC may be related to suboptimal dosing, adherence and treatments that do not address the underlying pathophysiological mechanism. In the European Union, no pharmacological therapies are approved for paediatric FC and PEG is used off-label, while Constella is authorised only for adult IBS-C. This supports the clinical relevance of evaluating linaclotide in children aged 6 months to less than 2 years with FC, particularly because linaclotide has a defined GC-C-mediated intestinal mechanism that may address secretory and motility-related components of the disease. Study M21-862 was a Phase 2 dosefinding study intended to inform dose selection, safety, tolerability and exploratory efficacy in this younger paediatric population; no formal hypothesis was specified.

A line listing of all the concerned studies is annexed.

## 2.2. Information on the pharmaceutical formulation used in the study

Linaclotide was supplied as 72 µg capsules for oral administration in Part 1 and Part 2 (Table 1).

For dose preparation, the entire capsule contents were used to prepare an oral solution to achieve the assigned dosage strength per cohort. The LAR/parent/guardian prepared the dose before study-drug administration. Complete dosing instructions were provided to the LAR/parent/guardian. Two dose preparation options were available. Option A was to dilute the capsule contents according to the dilution instructions provided by the study staff and to administer the prepared liquid to the child. The allowable liquids were 100% purified bottled water only (no alkaline water, tap water, or water with flavour additives) or 100% apple juice (pasteurised, unflavoured). Option B was to dilute the capsule contents according to the dilution instructions provided by the study staff and to mix the prepared liquid with 100% applesauce (unsweetened, unflavoured) before administration to the child.

## 2.3. Clinical aspects

## 2.3.1. Introduction

The MAH submitted one final report for:

- Study M21-862, Functional Constipation: A Phase 2 Dose Finding Study Evaluating the Safety and Efficacy of Linaclotide in Pediatric Subjects 6 Months to Less Than 2 Years of Age.

The study is submitted with a complete study report, protocol, and necessary appendices and documents. However, the study report contains mainly raw data tables only, which are not very convenient for reading.

## 2.3.2. Clinical study M21-862

## Description

Study M21-862 was a Phase 2, two-part study in paediatric subjects 6 months to less than 2 years of age who met Rome IV criteria for childhood functional constipation. Part 1 was an open-label, ascending, multiple-dose dose-finding part to assess the safety of linaclotide. Part 2 was randomised, double-blind and placebo-controlled to evaluate the safety and efficacy of linaclotide. Part 1 was planned as three sequential open-label cohorts with linaclotide administered orally once daily at 9 µg, 18 µg and 36 µg for Cohorts 1, 2 and 3, respectively. Dose selection was based on extrapolation of efficacy and safety data from paediatric Phase 2 FC studies in ages 2-17 years, adult Phase 3 CIC studies, and a paediatric GC-C ontogeny study. In each Part 1 cohort, at least one subject was to be ≤ 12 months of age. A linaclotide dose was to be selected for Part 2 based on the overall benefit-risk profile of the Part 1 open-label cohort(s) including absence of treatment-related adverse events of special interest (AESI) and improvement in spontaneous bowel movement (SBM) frequency with or without improvement of at least 1 point in the Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS). The Internal Dose Escalation Review Committee (IDERC) monitored Cohorts 1 to 3 and was to recommend dose escalation to the next cohort, stopping, or dose selection (Figure 1).

After completion of Cohort 1 (linaclotide 9 µg), the IDERC recommended not to escalate the dose to Cohorts 2 or 3 and advised to proceed directly to the Part 2 final cohort with linaclotide 9 µg (Figure 1) because two subjects had diarrhoea adverse events (AEs) requiring treatment interruption while the remaining subjects showed SBM-frequency improvements.

Part 2 was initiated as a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled final cohort in which subjects were randomised 1:1 to linaclotide 9 µg or matching placebo. An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) monitored unblinded interim safety data and assessed the overall safety profile of subjects in the final cohort.

All cohorts were 7 to 12 weeks in duration and included a 0- to 4-week Screening Period (the minimal of 0 to 4-week period was applied only to those subjects that needed wash out from prohibited medications), a 2- to 3-week Preintervention Period, a 4-week Study Intervention Period, and a 1-week Postintervention Period (Figure 2).

## Methods

## Study participants

Study M21-862 targeted male and female paediatric subjects with functional constipation (FC) in the 6 months to less than 2 years age range. The protocol eligibility criterion specified 6 months to less than 1 year and 11 months at the time the legally authorised representative (LAR)/parent/guardian signed informed consent.

For the disease-activity criterion, subjects had to meet Rome IV criteria for FC for at least 1 month before Screening, with at least two of the following:

- 2 or fewer defecations per week, each without laxative, suppository, or enema use during the preceding 24 hours;
- History of excessive stool retention;
- History of painful or hard bowel movements;
- History of large-diameter stools;
- Presence of a large faecal mass in the rectum.

The protocol also required discontinuation of laxatives before the Preintervention Visit in favour of protocol-permitted rescue medicine. Based on eDiary data, the subject could not have more than one loose or mushy stool, no watery stool with any spontaneous bowel movement (SBM) occurring without laxative use in the specified 14-day window, an average of ≤ 2 SBMs per week during that window, and daily eDiary completion on 10 of the 14 days immediately preceding enrolment/randomisation.

Key exclusion criteria were non-exhaustive and included the following categories:

- conditions that could interfere with drug absorption or make the subject unsuitable for study drug;
- faecal impaction at Preintervention after failed outpatient clean-out or at enrolment/randomisation, and manual disimpaction within 6 months before enrolment/randomisation;
- clinically significant alarm symptoms, systemic signs of infection or colitis, neoplastic process, relevant gastrointestinal surgery, mechanical bowel obstruction or pseudo-obstruction, allergy or significant sensitivity to the study intervention, and organic or confounding causes of constipation such as celiac disease, cystic fibrosis, untreated or unstable hypothyroidism, chromosomal disorders, active anal fissure as primary cause, anatomic malformations, Hirschsprung disease or other intestinal nerve/muscle disorders, neuropathic conditions, lead toxicity, hypercalcemia, inflammatory bowel disease, or confounding lactose intolerance;
- active SARS-CoV-2 infection, recent or planned interventional study participation, recent investigational drug use, protocol-specified prohibited medicines, failure to meet stable-dose requirements, or rescue-medication use immediately before enrolment/randomisation.

## Assessor's comment:

The eligibility criteria are considered adequate to define a paediatric FC population for this age range. The protocol used Rome IV criteria, eDiary-based confirmation of bowel-movement frequency and stool consistency, rescue-medication restrictions, washout/prohibited-medication controls, and exclusions for organic, confounding, or safety-relevant conditions. It is noted that the age criterion is worded in the CSR synopsis as 6 months to less than 1 year and 11 months, while the protocol title/synopsis describe 6 months to less than 2 years and the demographic table reports ages up to 25 months. This appears to be a wording/presentation inconsistency rather than an eligibility concern affecting the assessment of the study population. No further issue is identified for this chapter based on the available CSR/protocol evidence.

## Treatments

In Part 1 Cohort 1, linaclotide 9 µg was administered orally once daily during the 4-week Study Intervention Period. In Part 2, linaclotide 9 µg or matching placebo was administered orally once daily during the 4-week Study Intervention Period. (Table 1)

The planned linaclotide 18 µg and 36 µg escalation doses were not administered.

## Objective(s)

Study M21-862 had separate objectives for Part 1 and Part 2. The Part 1 objective was to identify a tolerable, safe, and efficacious dose of linaclotide administered for 4 weeks in paediatric subjects 6 months to less than 2 years of age with functional constipation (FC) for Part 2.

The Part 2 objective was to evaluate the safety and efficacy of 4 weeks of study intervention with linaclotide in paediatric participants 6 months to less than 2 years of age with FC.

The CSR states that there was no formal hypothesis corresponding to the objectives.

## Outcomes/endpoints

The same endpoint framework was specified for Part 1 and Part 2. The key efficacy endpoints were:

- Change from baseline in 4-week overall spontaneous bowel movement (SBM) frequency rate, expressed as SBMs/week during the Study Intervention Period
- Change from baseline in 4-week stool consistency using the Bristol Stool Form Scale, reported by the legally authorised representative (LAR)/parent/guardian/caregiver during the Study Intervention Period
- Change from baseline in 4-week straining, reported by the LAR/parent/guardian/caregiver, during the Study Intervention Period during the Study Intervention Period

The additional efficacy endpoints for Part 1 and Part 2 were:

- Achievement of no longer meeting Rome IV criteria for FC at the end of the 4-week Study Intervention Period
- Global change in symptoms, and global severity of symptoms at each week during the Study Intervention Period

Safety endpoints/evaluations for Part 1 and Part 2 included frequency of adverse events (AEs) including adverse events of special interest (AESIs), clinical laboratory assessments (including haematology/complete blood count (CBC), clinical chemistry, and urinalysis), vital signs (including postural vital signs whenever possible according to age), ECG, physical examination, height, and weight as measures of safety and tolerability for the entire study duration.

## Sample size

Approximately 30 subjects were planned for Study M21-862, with a minimum of 18 subjects. The planned distribution was six subjects in each of the three Part 1 open-label cohorts and 12 subjects in the Part 2 final cohort, with six subjects planned for linaclotide and six for placebo in Part 2. In each cohort of Part 1, at least 1 subject was planned to be ≤ 12 months of age. The dossier states that the sample size was not driven by statistical considerations. The study was designed to enrol a sufficient number of paediatric subjects to observe clinical response trends and monitor safety.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

The analysed population comprised 19 subjects: 7 subjects in Part 1 (Cohort 1, linaclotide 9 µg), and 12 subjects in Part 2 (6 subjects treated with linaclotide 9 µg, and 6 subjects treated with placebo). Placebo was therefore part of Part 2 only; Part 1 Cohort 1 was open-label linaclotide only.

## Randomisation and blinding (masking)

Part 1 was open label. In Part 2, the final cohort was double-blind and subjects were randomised 1:1 using interactive response technology. The system assigned a randomisation number that encoded treatment group according to the randomisation schedule. During Part 2, sponsor personnel with direct oversight of trial conduct and management, except drug

supply management, as well as the investigator, study site personnel, and the subject's legally authorised representative, parent or guardian were to remain blinded to individual treatment assignment. Linaclotide and placebo capsules were identical in appearance. Emergency unblinding was available through interactive response technology, and an independent data safety monitoring board (DSMB) reviewed unblinded interim safety data with external statistical support to maintain sponsor blinding.

## Statistical Methods

All efficacy analyses were descriptive and were conducted on the intent-to-treat (ITT) population for each Part 1 cohort and by treatment group in Part 2. The ITT population included all subjects who received at least one dose of study drug, with subjects analysed according to the treatment group to which they were assigned or randomised. No formal hypothesis was tested, no statistical testing was performed, and no adjustment was made for multiple comparisons or multiplicity. Pooled summaries were also provided for the linaclotide 9 µg groups from Cohort 1 and the final cohort. Missing post-baseline efficacy data were handled using an as-observed approach without imputation. For the key efficacy estimands, spontaneous bowel movement data associated with rescue-therapy use were not used, data after premature study-drug discontinuation were not used, and data after studydrug interruption due to poor tolerability were included. Age subgroup analyses for 6 to 11 months and 12 to 24 months were descriptive. No formal statistical interim analysis was planned in the SAP, and no interim analysis was conducted. The unblinded DSMB safety reviews described under blinding were safety-monitoring activities and were not formal statistical interim analyses. Safety analyses were performed on the safety analysis (SA) population for Part 1 and Part 2. The SA

population included all subjects who received at least one dose of study drug. Subjects were analysed according to the study drug received, determined by the most frequent dose regimen received in the treatment period. Safety summaries were presented by cohort in Part 1 and by treatment group in Part 2. Treatment-emergent adverse events were summarized by Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) system organ class and preferred term, with summaries by severity and relationship to study drug. Clinical laboratory and vital-sign variables were summarized descriptively.

## Assessor's comment:

Study M21-862 had no formal hypothesis corresponding to its objectives and used a descriptive, nonconfirmatory statistical framework. Efficacy analyses were descriptive, with no inferential testing and no multiplicity adjustment. Missing post-baseline efficacy data were handled using an as-observed approach without imputation. Together with the small, non-statistically driven sample size and the direct progression from the 9 µg Part 1 open-label cohort to the Part 2 final cohort, the methods support descriptive characterisation of observed efficacy data but do not provide confirmatory statistical evidence of a treatment effect.

## Results

## Participant flow

The clinical study report (CSR) presents participant disposition for Cohort 1 in Part 1 and for the final cohort in Part 2. Overall, 36 subjects were screened, 17 were recorded as screen failures, and 19 subjects were enrolled in Part 1 or randomised in Part 2 and dosed (Table 2).

In Part 1, 7 subjects were enrolled and dosed with open-label linaclotide 9 µg. In Part 2, 12 subjects were randomised and dosed, 6 to placebo and 6 to linaclotide 9 µg. All enrolled or randomised subjects received assigned or randomised study treatment. No subject was recorded as never treated and no subject received study treatment other than assigned or randomised (Table 3).

Overall, 17 subjects completed study treatment: six of seven subjects in Cohort 1, all six subjects in the final placebo cohort, and five of six subjects in the final linaclotide 9 µg cohort. Two subjects discontinued study treatment: one subject in Cohort 1 due to an \"other reason\" and one subject in the final linaclotide 9 µg cohort due to withdrawal from treatment by subject. The remaining listed discontinuation reason categories were 0 in each reported group: adverse event, lost to follow-up, lack of efficacy, study terminated by sponsor, COVID-19 infection, COVID-19 logistical restrictions, and geo-political reasons (Table 3).

## Recruitment

Study M21-862 was conducted at 8 sites in the United States and the United Kingdom. The study period in the clinical study report (CSR) was from the First Subject First Visit on 27 April 2023 to the Last Subject Last Visit on 09 June 2025.

## Assessor's comment:

The recruitment experience indicates limited accrual in this very young paediatric population: 19 subjects were dosed over the CSR study period at 8 sites, with 17 screen failures among 36 screened subjects. This does not by itself create a separate conduct concern, but it reinforces that the results are from a small exploratory dose-finding study with separate Part 1 and Part 2 cohorts.

## Baseline data

Baseline analyses were based on the ITT population. For presentation of baseline data, the Cohort 1 linaclotide 9 µg group, N=7, and the total linaclotide 9 µg column, N=13, are presented as contextual information. The randomised baseline-comparability assessment is based on the Part 2 final-cohort comparison: placebo, N=6, and linaclotide 9 µg, N=6 (Table 4).

In the final cohort, demographic characteristics were generally comparable only descriptively because each treatment group contained six subjects. Both groups were predominantly male, with 4/6 males in the placebo group and 5/6 in the linaclotide 9 µg group. The paediatric age-category distribution was the same in the two final-cohort arms, with 1/6 subject in each arm aged 6 to &lt;12 months and 5/6 subjects in each arm aged 12 to 24 months. Race and ethnicity were visibly imbalanced: Hispanic or Latino ethnicity was reported for 5/6 placebo subjects and 2/6 linaclotide subjects; Black or African American race for 2/6 and 4/6, respectively; and White race for 4/6 and 2/6, respectively. Mean age was 15.8 months in the placebo group and 18.5 months in the linaclotide group. Height was numerically higher in the linaclotide group, while BMI was numerically higher in the placebo group. All final-cohort subjects were from North America (Table 4).

Baseline constipation measures in the final cohort were generally similar, again descriptively. Mean SBM frequency observed by the primary caregiver was 1.13 SBMs/week in the placebo group and 1.45 SBMs/week in the linaclotide 9 µg group. The all-reported SBM frequency corresponded within each arm to the primary-caregiver value, namely 1.13 SBMs/week in the placebo group and 1.45 SBMs/week in the linaclotide 9 µg group. Mean stool consistency was 1.94 in the placebo group and 1.88 in the linaclotide group, and mean straining was 1.63 and 1.33, respectively. These values show small numerical differences but no clear baseline disease-severity separation in the randomised final-cohort comparison (Table 5).

The medical-history summary was consistent with the enrolled constipation population. In the final cohort, constipation was reported as medical history in 4/6 placebo subjects and 3/6 linaclotide 9 µg subjects.

## Assessor's comment:

In the randomised final cohort, baseline disease characteristics were sufficiently similar for descriptive interpretation, but demographic balance cannot be regarded as robust because only six subjects were included in each treatment group and race and ethnicity were visibly imbalanced. The Cohort 1 linaclotide 9 µg group and the total linaclotide 9 µg column are contextual columns and should not drive the randomised baseline-comparability conclusion, which is based on the Part 2 final-cohort placebo versus linaclotide 9 µg comparison. The smaller numerical differences in sex, age and anthropometrics, and the minor differences in baseline SBM frequency, stool consistency and straining, do not identify a single baseline factor that invalidates the comparison. This contextual safetypopulation information does not alter the baseline comparability assessment based on the randomised ITT disease-characteristic measures. The final cohort included no European subjects, and only one subject in Cohort 1 was enrolled in Europe. This does not create an imbalance between the randomised treatment arms, but it limits the representativeness of the baseline dataset for the EU paediatric context. The efficacy findings should nevertheless be interpreted with caution appropriate to the small sample size and the observed demographic imbalances.

## Efficacy results

All efficacy analyses were conducted on the ITT population for each Part 1 cohort and by treatment group in Part 2. No statistical testing was performed; only descriptive statistics were provided. Pooled summaries for the linaclotide 9 µg dose groups from Cohort 1 and the final cohort were presented, and no adjustment was made for multiple comparisons or multiplicity.

The key efficacy endpoints were change from baseline in 4-week spontaneous bowel movement (SBM) frequency rate, stool consistency, and straining. In the Part 1 open-label Cohort 1 linaclotide 9 µg group (N=7), the mean changes from baseline were 3.181 SBMs/week for observed SBM frequency,

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

3.371 SBMs/week for all-reported SBM frequency, 0.845 for stool consistency, and -1.087 for straining. In the randomised Part 2 final cohort, the corresponding values were 2.503, 2.577, 1.062, and -0.821 with placebo (N=6) and 0.246, 0.895, 1.201, and -0.561 with linaclotide 9 µg (N=6) (Table 7). The CSR states that the Part 1 linaclotide 9 µg cohort had numerical improvements in SBM frequency and stool consistency and reduced straining, and that the Part 2 linaclotide 9 µg group did not replicate the numerical SBM-frequency trends observed in Part 1. In Part 2, stool consistency was numerically similar to, and slightly higher with, linaclotide than placebo, whereas the reduction in straining was smaller with linaclotide than placebo.

The pooled linaclotide 9 µg groups from Part 1 and Part 2 (N=13) had a numerically smaller mean change from baseline in 4-week SBM frequency than the Part 2 placebo group, with similar mean changes for stool consistency and straining. The CSR states that the small sample size limits interpretability of the study results.

For the additional Rome IV endpoint, 6/6 subjects (100.0%) in Cohort 1, 4/6 subjects (66.7%) in the Part 2 placebo group, and 1/5 subjects (20.0%) in the Part 2 linaclotide 9 µg group no longer fulfilled Rome IV criteria for functional constipation at the end of the 4-week treatment period. The pooled linaclotide result was 7/11 subjects (63.6%). The percentage denominator (N1) was defined as \"subjects with Rome IV assessments at both screening and end of study treatment\" and was therefore different from the ITT population (Table 7).

The global change and global severity endpoints were descriptive. At Week 4, global change in the child's constipation was rated as either much better or a little better for all evaluable subjects in Cohort 1, Part 2 placebo, and Part 2 linaclotide 9 µg. At Week 4, global severity in the child's constipation was none, mild, or moderate for 6/6 Cohort 1 subjects, 3/4 Part 2 placebo subjects, and 5/5 Part 2 linaclotide subjects.

Subgroup analyses by age group were conducted for the key efficacy endpoints, but no clear trends were observed because of the small sample size and because most subjects were aged 12 to 24 months.

## Assessor's comment:

The efficacy findings are descriptive and non-confirmatory. Overall, the results do not provide confirmatory evidence of efficacy in children 6 months to less than 2 years of age with functional constipation.

## Safety results

Safety analyses were performed on the SA population in the 4-week open-label period (Part 1) and the 4-week double-blind period (Part 2), with subjects analysed according to the study drug actually received (as treated). Exposure was short and similar across groups: mean treatment duration was 28.7 days in Cohort 1 linaclotide 9 µg, 29.7 days in Part 2 placebo, and 27.5 days in Part 2 linaclotide 9 µg. (Table 9)

Table 10 and Table 11 provide the adverse-event overview and TEAEs by preferred term. Overall, treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were reported in 3/7 subjects (42.9%) in Cohort 1 linaclotide 9 µg, 1/6 subjects (16.7%) in Part 2 placebo, and 3/6 subjects (50.0%) in Part 2 linaclotide 9 µg. Diarrhoea, influenza, and otitis media acute were the only TEAEs reported in at least 2 subjects during the study.

The applicant reports in the CSR that the only TEAEs considered to have a reasonable possibility of being related to study treatment were 2 diarrhoea events in 2 subjects (aged 12 - 24 months) in Cohort 1, linaclotide 9 µg. One event was grade 1 and one was grade 2 in severity. Study treatment was interrupted for both events, and both events were reported as recovered/resolved.

There were no grade 3 TEAEs, treatment-emergent serious adverse events (TESAEs), TEAEs leading to withdrawal of study treatment, adverse events of special interest (AESIs), or deaths reported during the study.

Table 12 provides the potentially clinically important laboratory values. The CSR reports no unexpected changes in clinical laboratory values, no clinically relevant shifts in individual laboratory parameters, no Grade 3 or Grade 4 laboratory abnormalities with grade worsening from baseline, and no subject meeting the laboratory criteria for potential Hy's Law. No clinically relevant vital-sign trends were observed. One Cohort 1 subject met a potentially clinically important standing-supine diastolic blood pressure criterion, and no other potentially clinically important vital-sign changes were reported. There were no unexpected clinically relevant ECG changes and no potentially clinically important ECG values.

## 2.3.3. Discussion on clinical aspects

Study M21-862 was a small paediatric Phase 2 dose-finding, safety and efficacy study in subjects aged 6 months to less than 2 years with functional-constipation. Overall, 19 subjects were enrolled (Cohort 1, Part 1) or randomised (final cohort, Part 2) and dosed. Following review by the IDERC, only linaclotide 9 µg was taken forward to the Part 2 final cohort.

Given the descriptive, non-confirmatory design, the efficacy results are interpreted as supportive observations only. The Part 1 open-label linaclotide 9 µg cohort showed numerical improvements from baseline in spontaneous bowel movement frequency rate, stool consistency, and straining, but the randomized Part 2 linaclotide 9 µg group did not replicate the Part 1 spontaneous bowel movement trend. The Rome IV assessment also does not provide supportive Part 2 evidence for linaclotide. The result was favourable in the open-label Part 1 cohort, where 6/6 assessed subjects no longer fulfilled Rome IV criteria for FC at the end of treatment. In contrast, in Part 2 only 1/5 assessed subjects receiving linaclotide no longer fulfilled Rome IV criteria, compared with 4/6 subjects receiving placebo. Taken together with the small sample size and absence of confirmatory statistical testing, these data do not support a paediatric efficacy claim.

From a safety perspective, the limited dataset is descriptive and did not reveal a new risk. The safety findings are consistent with the known safety profile of linaclotide and do not alter the regulatory conclusion for this Article 46 procedure. The population and disease setting differ from the current EU adult IBS-C indication, and the findings are therefore considered primarily for any potential impact on the established benefit-risk and product information rather than as evidence for an extension of indication.

<div style=\"page-break-after: always\"></div>

As no update to the product information has been proposed and the reviewed data do not indicate a need for additional regulatory consequences, the assessment does not identify any further clinical regulatory action arising from this Article 46 procedure.

## 3. Rapporteur's overall conclusion and recommendation

The submitted paediatric study is considered to fulfil the PAM. The descriptive data from Study M21-862 do not support a paediatric efficacy claim or a product-information update. No new safety risk was identified, and the benefit-risk balance for the authorised EU adult IBS-C indication remains unchanged. No further regulatory action is required based on the data reviewed in this Article 46 procedure.
